## Summary
- **Purpose.** The TSCC held its required hearing on the May 2025 bond measure ($1.83B, no tax-rate increase). Commissioners asked "questions that we think a typical community member might ask" [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:01:08].
- **Why the high schools cost so much.** Dan Jung read PPS's prepared answer [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:17:35]. The reasons given were:
  - prevailing wage, cost of living, taxes, the 1.5% green-energy mandate, limited contractor competition, Portland permit costs (some projects paid more than $4-5M in fees alone), and tight urban sites
  - PPS's climate policy, which requires all-electric buildings, and its equity-in-contracting goals
- **Cornerstone report.** Escalation-adjusted, the three high schools in design (Jefferson, Ida B. Wells, Cleveland) are about 25-30% more expensive than Lincoln (about $225M) [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:27:13].
- **Climate policy cost.** Commissioner Moore (a former director) asked whether the board ever weighed the climate policy's fiscal impact. Jung said that when the policy was approved "there was not a cost impact discussion" [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:36:19] ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OsyteRB1f9c&t=2179s)).
- **Capacity versus enrollment.**
  - **The question.** A commissioner cited Portland State University's (PSU) forecast of about 11,000 high-school students in 10 years. The three projects would bring high-school capacity to about 15,000. She called this an "if you build it, they will come" approach [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:40:54].
  - **Adams's answer.** The board directed building "for the next hundred years, not just for our current forecast". Extra space could hold other programs, and the enrollment campaign would help [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:41:50] ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OsyteRB1f9c&t=2510s)).
  - **Housing.** Adams acknowledged no housing-capacity analysis had been done [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:44:02].
- **Building smaller.** Moore asked about a smaller initial build with room to expand later, as at Roosevelt (1,500). Staff said this was "not the direction that has been chosen for this bond" [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:47:10].
- **Prior bonds.** About $700M remains from the 2017 and 2020 bonds, with work expected to finish around 2029-2030. Jefferson has $366M available from prior bonds [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:48:24].
- **Center for Black Student Excellence.** Nichole Watson said this 2020-bond project is "still very much active", that a site recommendation is coming soon, and that the delay "has taken too long" [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:52:42].
- **Tax rate.** CFO Morrison expects the bond tax rate to stay below $2.50 per $1,000. Debt service on the new bond starts in 2026-27 [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:53:59].
- **Elementary and middle schools.** Staff said there is no plan for another bond for about eight years. K-8 needs would rely on the bond's repair funds plus grants [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:55:57].
- **Closing statements.** Brim-Edwards, Wang, Greene and Splitt urged a yes vote [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:57:55].

## Decisions & votes
- None. TSCC takes no action and does not advocate [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:01:34].

## Rightsizing / closures
- **Capacity versus enrollment.** The main relevant exchange was the commission's challenge on capacity [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:40:54]. PSU projected about 11,000 high-school students in 10 years against about 15,000 seats of capacity. PPS answered with a 100-year design horizon, alternative uses of space and an enrollment campaign. It did not offer a consolidation or rightsizing plan.
- **Wang's capacity point.** A 1,700-seat school "would actually be at capacity safely" at about 1,400, roughly 80% [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@1:00:10].
- **Analysis:** these May 2025 statements are a baseline. PPS leadership publicly rejected downsizing high schools in response to the enrollment forecast. That bears on later rightsizing debates about how capacity and enrollment are measured.

## Bond / seismic / facilities
- **Cost reduction.** Staff described ongoing reductions and are reviewing CM/GC pre-construction services and general conditions. Jung said CM/GC remains the standard approach for projects this complex [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:31:12].
- **Contingencies.** The modernization allocation exceeds the three project estimates, and there is a program contingency. Staff called long-range forecasting "crystal ball" [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:39:14].
- **Greene** said he scrutinizes contractor costs so savings can flow to elementary and middle schools [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@1:02:12].
- **Seismic.** Seismic retrofits were not discussed at the hearing. The board took them up at the regular meeting the same evening (Res. 7109; see `2025-05-06_Uxh6Jm_xFYY.md`).

## Budget & enrollment
- PPS's tax-rate commitment is $2.50 per $1,000 or lower, and the district is currently below that [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:54:01].
- Morrison said bonds would be sold the next morning to cover debt service and avoid a drop-off in the rate [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:54:50].

## Public comment themes
- Three people spoke in person, and one written comment was received from Cascade Policy Institute [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:03:49].
  - A Libertarian Party of Multnomah County speaker said TSCC is "pointless". He argued the schools are "overbuilt for students who are never going to attend them" [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:05:27].
  - The Cascade Policy Institute president asked TSCC to "not certify or certify with objection" [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:09:10]. His criticisms:
    - enrollment data was not used to size the three high schools
    - an estimated $30M all-electric premium and about $50M in total "climate symbolism"
    - the Cornerstone report was read late [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:07:39]
  - A Franklin alumni-association member spoke as a grandparent and urged cursive instruction. This was off-topic for the bond [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:10:18].
