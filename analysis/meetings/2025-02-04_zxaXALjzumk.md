## Summary

- The superintendent introduced three new senior leaders: Candice Grose (Chief of Communications), Michelle Morrison (CFO, from Hillsboro SD) and Nichole Watson (Sr. Director of Engagement) [YT:zxaXALjzumk@0:36:07].
- **High School Cost Comparison (Cornerstone) report.** The board's first substantive discussion of cost cuts for the Cleveland, Jefferson and Ida B. Wells modernizations came after the $1.83B bond was referred. Staff asked the board for "values" on four big levers: square footage and capacity, climate-policy premiums, equity-in-contracting, and premium spaces such as health centers, teen parent centers and athletics [YT:zxaXALjzumk@1:44:18] [YT:zxaXALjzumk@1:56:29].
- The design scale in the Cornerstone report: Cleveland, Jefferson and Wells average about 320,000 sq ft for 1,700 students, versus about 300,000 for Lincoln and Beaverton. The extra area is worth about $24M [BB:3996231 p.3] [BB:3996231 p.5].
  - Escalated to June 2027, cost per sq ft is $1,213 (Cleveland), $1,296 (Jefferson) and $1,163 (Wells), versus $991 (Lincoln) and $832 (Beaverton) [BB:3996231 p.4].
- Armstrong (medium confidence) recommended a 300,000 sq ft target saving about $24M. She acknowledged that hoped-for $70–80M in savings is "just not going to happen" ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zxaXALjzumk&t=8115s)) [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:15:15].
- **Capacity versus enrollment split the board:**
  - Wang cited a PSU study: even with equal enrollment, high schools would hold "a little less than 1,200 each" ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zxaXALjzumk&t=9476s)) [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:37:56].
  - DePass warned against sizing to current enrollment because "Jefferson suffers" [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:12:18].
  - Greene and the student rep opposed building smaller [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:22:48] [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:33:36].
  - Brim-Edwards noted Wells and Cleveland have been over 1,500 for a decade [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:08:24].
- Brim-Edwards and Splitt urged speed: "the Bond is in Jeopardy". Brim-Edwards said the discussion "should have happened before the referral" [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:04:04] [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:21:02].
- Process friction:
  - Hollands and Greene wanted staff recommendations rather than board "values" and complained of unanswered questions [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:01:27] [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:28:03] [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:31:24].
  - Armstrong committed to answering within 48 hours and returning with recommendations; a bond work session was set for Feb 13 [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:47:36] [BB:3997974 p.4].
- **2025-26 budget overview:** a $40M shortfall, driven by rising costs, limited state revenue and declining enrollment.
  - The deck states PPS enrollment fell 9.5% since 2020 versus 6% statewide, and is "projected to continue to decline". It also says "We have fewer students and the same number of buildings" [BB:3988777 p.3] [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:51:33].
  - Proposed school-level cuts are about $30M. Central office cuts are about $12M for 2025-26 and $67M cumulative over four years [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:56:13].

## Rightsizing / closures

- There was no formal rightsizing item, but the high-school capacity debate is an important precursor:
  - Wang: "if we enrolled all our high schools equally … we wouldn't even be able to fill up our high schools to maybe about a little less than 1,200 each". He framed the trade-off as "30 empty classrooms" versus nicer or bigger spaces [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:37:56].
  - DePass: "I'd rather use uh population Pro projections" and do not use current enrollment, or "Jefferson suffers because Jefferson has a really low enrollment" [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:12:18].
  - Wang: "adamant about Jefferson being a full beautiful well- enrolled High School" [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:37:32].
  - Greene: building smaller "that's looking at right now and it's not thinking about um where we're going" [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:22:48].
  - Student rep: opposes "basing the school size on enrollment" [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:33:36].
  - Brim-Edwards: wants each school's enrollment history. She recalled pressure in 2017 to build Madison smaller, when it had about 900 students; "it's a good thing we didn't" because "now it's one of our larger high schools" [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:07:58].
- **Southeast Guiding Coalition:**
  - Brim-Edwards described Dunaway as one of the few SE schools "that wasn't impacted by the southeast guiding Coalition boundary changes in program closures".
  - She said proposed cuts to the coalition's licensed-staff "Program Balancing Pool" would break a five-year stabilization promise to Harrison Park, Marysville, Lane, Lent, Whitman and Vestal [YT:zxaXALjzumk@3:20:42] [YT:zxaXALjzumk@3:21:25] [BB:3988777 p.14].
  - Analysis: this is direct precedent for how the board weighs commitments made during earlier boundary and program consolidations.
- The budget deck links enrollment decline to "the same number of buildings" [BB:3988777 p.3].

## Budget & enrollment

- **Shortfall timeline:** $72M announced last April → $30M reduced → $42M remaining. A $32M PERS increase pushed the need to about $100M. The Governor's current service level (CSL) increase of about $20M brought it back to **$40M** for 2025-26 [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:53:07].
- **Enrollment:** the deck says PPS enrollment declined 9.5% since 2020 versus 6% for Oregon and "is projected to continue to decline" [BB:3988777 p.3].
  - School-level cuts include line items for "Licensed and Classified Staff Reduction Due to Enrollment Decline" [BB:3988777 p.13–14].
  - CFO Morrison said enrollment-driven reductions total "just over $2 million" in the line items, but there is "not a straight line" between lost students and the deficit [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:59:22].
- **Board requests:**
  - Line-item scale of central office cuts, contracts over $500K, and learning-app licenses (Brim-Edwards) [YT:zxaXALjzumk@3:16:08].
  - Protecting elementary and early literacy by cutting central office more (Hollands) [YT:zxaXALjzumk@3:07:44].
  - Staffing manual timing: Feb 25 [YT:zxaXALjzumk@3:22:59].
- Splitt and the superintendent emphasized state advocacy for full Quality Education Model (QEM) funding [YT:zxaXALjzumk@3:04:40] [YT:zxaXALjzumk@3:09:18].
