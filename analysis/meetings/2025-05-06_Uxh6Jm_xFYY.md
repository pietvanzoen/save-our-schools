## Summary
- **Seismic resolutions (headline outcome).** The board unanimously adopted two seismic resolutions:
  - **Res. 7109, "Urgent Need for Seismic Retrofitting of High-Risk School Buildings"** (7-0). Introduced by Hollands and co-developed with Brim-Edwards, Greene and parent advocates. It requires:
    - a districtwide Seismic Risk Reduction Plan by September 1, 2025
    - a $100M reserve for emergencies and end-of-life systems
    - a default presumption that remaining K-8 repair funds (about $366-398M, combining the $190M allocation and $176-208M in projected high-school savings) go first to seismic fixes at the "8-10 schools assessed to pose the greatest risk"
    - any decision to replace one of those schools to go through "the District's closure and consolidation policy and process" [BB:3997998 p.26-27]
  - **Res. 7110** (7-0, Wang-led). It assigns oversight to the School Facilities Improvement & Operations Committee (SFIOC), with at least six seismic-focused meetings a year. Its scope explicitly includes "potential school enrollment or consolidations changes related to seismic plans" [BB:3997998 p.28].
- **Jung on consolidation.** COO Dan Jung said the third step of seismic prioritization would apply a PPS lens, including whether money should go into "schools that could potentially be consolidated" [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@2:31:49] ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Uxh6Jm_xFYY&t=9109s)). His May 3 memo:
  - estimates $925.5M in construction cost for full seismic retrofits at all schools, or $129.3M to retrofit only unreinforced masonry (URM)
  - lists "anticipated impacts of potential school consolidation" as a PPS criterion [BB:3989487 p.2-3]
- **CBRC report.** The Community Budget Review Committee presented its budget report:
  - urged "more targeted structural reductions ... whether it's programs or locations" [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@1:38:11]
  - asked whether blended classrooms are a stop-gap that keeps low-enrollment schools open [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@2:02:58]
  - noted levy-funded teacher positions fell from 916 (2019-20) to 744, projected 718 [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@2:24:16]
- **Prophet Center relocation.** The board approved minimum criteria for relocating out of the Prophet Center headquarters (Res. 7103) 6-0, with Brim-Edwards abstaining over process [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@4:52:43]. AVT argued that redeveloping the site with about 1,000 housing units would help "stabilize enrollment at our neighborhood schools" [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@3:47:21].
- **Tension.** There was a sharp procedural exchange when a call for the question cut off Brim-Edwards' questions at about 11 pm. Another director objected that members must be allowed to ask questions [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@4:49:02].
- **Other items.** Heritage-month and educator-appreciation resolutions; Fund for PPS distribution formula (Res. 7102, 7-0); Ethics policy second reading postponed; 2025-26 board calendar previewed; consent agenda (7-0).

## Rightsizing / closures
- **Consolidation enters seismic planning.** Three formal documents tie seismic planning to possible consolidation:
  - Res. 7109 §3(b): if any of the 8-10 highest-risk schools "should be replaced entirely, any plan to build new schools or consolidate existing schools will be determined by the District's closure and consolidation policy and process" [BB:3997998 p.27].
  - Res. 7110 lists "potential school enrollment or consolidations changes related to seismic plans" as an SFIOC agenda topic. It also names "enrollment, building utilization" as factors for distributing limited bond funds [BB:3997998 p.28].
  - Jung's seismic memo names "anticipated impacts of potential school consolidation" as a PPS criterion [BB:3989487 p.3]. He said it aloud too [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@2:31:49].
- **Enrollment as a criterion.** A director asked whether the PPS lens would include "criteria such as enrollment". Jung said yes, with results expected around July [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@2:32:32].
- **Brim-Edwards (medium)** cautioned that state funding of buildings could mean the state deciding "we actually need you know 40 schools and not 80" [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@3:03:51].
- **CBRC points:**
  - School-level cuts were "aligned with enrollment largely" [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@1:38:01].
  - It recommended "more targeted structural reductions ... whether it's programs or locations" [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@1:38:11].
  - It questioned blended classrooms (18 new ones): "Are we keeping open schools um that have low enrollment and using or using this as a strategy to help us get by?" [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@2:03:00].
  - It asked for an asset strategy weighing "maintenance versus reuse or versus divestment", with "strategic sales" kept on the table [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@2:21:52].
- **Blended classrooms.** A director (possibly Brim-Edwards; low confidence) said blending saves about $2.5M and asked for outcome data before the budget vote [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@2:05:15].
- **Analysis:** this meeting is the earliest point in this batch where consolidation is formally written into board policy instruments, in the seismic resolutions. The CBRC independently raised closure-adjacent questions about "locations" and low-enrollment schools.

## Bond / seismic / facilities
- **Seismic status (Jung memo [BB:3989487 p.1-2]):**
  - average school age about 76 years
  - nine schools fully modernized, three in progress (Jefferson, Cleveland, Ida B. Wells)
  - six full seismic retrofits and 12 interior upgrades completed
  - about 75% of schools have received some seismic upgrade since 2012
- **Prioritization process.** The engineer's prioritization report is expected in summer (July). The third step, applying PPS's own criteria, has not started [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@2:30:48].
- **Board positions:**
  - **Hollands** (sponsor) described seismic needs as more than $130M within roughly $1B of deferred maintenance. He said the resolution "asks" rather than binds future boards [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@2:37:46].
  - **Brim-Edwards** (medium): "this creates the road map and an allocation" [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@2:40:03].
  - **Splitt:** strong yes, but warned that HVAC and heat problems will be deferred. She urged using state seismic grant dollars [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@2:45:00].
  - **Wang** (medium): the state bears ultimate responsibility. The 2025 bond's $200-400M for repairs is unprecedented [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@2:47:05].
- **Committee dispute (Res. 7110):**
  - Brim-Edwards (medium) said oversight must be substantive and include BAC (Bond Accountability Committee) reports. She noted the facilities committee had not met all calendar year [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@3:02:15].
  - Hollands (medium) wanted the scope broadened to all deferred maintenance and no fixed meeting count [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@3:05:39].
  - Wang said not holding committee meetings last summer was a deliberate choice [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@3:09:07].
  - Splitt pledged to act as BAC liaison if elected [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@3:11:08].
- **Prophet Center relocation (Res. 7103).** The nine minimum criteria are:
  - owned, not leased
  - within district boundaries
  - central location
  - freeway access
  - transit access
  - parking
  - meets functional and spatial needs
  - safety
  - all-in cost-neutral
  - **Background:** relocation is under Res. 6861 (Feb 2024) [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@3:22:39].
  - **Opposition:** DCU (District Council of Unions), PFSP (Portland Federation of School Professionals) and maintenance staff objected to reduced shop and warehouse square footage and to weak staff engagement.
  - **Chair's follow-up:** Wang asked the superintendent for a PEC (Prophet Education Center) staff-engagement plan by June 24 [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@3:54:13].
  - **Peer comparison:** Dana White noted PPS has in some cases "three times as much space as Seattle" [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@4:28:17].

## Budget & enrollment
- **CBRC recommendations** [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@1:31:48 to 2:28:15]:
  - a multi-year structural budget plan
  - a five-year sustainability plan
  - QEM (Quality Education Model) benchmarking of class sizes
  - Title I equity concerns about the 25% holdback
  - TAG (talented and gifted) funding cut to about $150K
  - concerns about the language line
  - replacing MAP with ODE's free interim assessment
  - enrollment marketing and exit interviews
  - attendance (about 36% chronic absenteeism)
  - revenue diversification
  - rebuilding reserves
- **Armstrong on Title I.** The 25% holdback avoids mid-year layoffs if federal funds are cut [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@1:47:40]. The budget already counts on the governor's current-service-level funding and a slight drop in PERS rates; otherwise "our reductions would have been twice" as large [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@1:54:09].
- **Adams** said the 9% high-school equity allocation is distributed by direct-certification (free and reduced-price meal) counts, not equally [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@1:59:58].
- Staff (likely Armstrong; not named) said that without QEM funding, class sizes will creep up as levy-funded teaching positions decline [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@2:26:43].

## Public comment themes
- **General public comment** (4 speakers per minutes, including late returns):
  - cursive instruction
  - Cleveland stadium lights and water
  - a Native-centered space for Indian Education, with a commenter proposing the vacant Meek School building [YT:Uxh6Jm_xFYY@1:16:11]

- **Res. 7103:** two staff commenters were concerned about minimum square footage for instrument-repair and maintenance spaces.
