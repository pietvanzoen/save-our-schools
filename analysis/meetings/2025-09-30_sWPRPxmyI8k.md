## Summary
- Annual federally required governance training: the board is the Head Start governing body and has fiduciary and legal oversight, sharing governance with a Parent Policy Council [YT:sWPRPxmyI8k@0:03:32] [YT:sWPRPxmyI8k@0:33:50].
- Program scope: capacity for 910 three- to five-year-olds in 51 pre-K classrooms at 14 sites, about 250 staff; 7 dual-language and 3 culturally specific classrooms [YT:sWPRPxmyI8k@0:04:34].
- PPS also gives free classroom space to more than a dozen community pre-K providers (Preschool for All partners) [YT:sWPRPxmyI8k@0:05:47]. Analysis: this is one existing use of surplus school space relevant to later capacity/rightsizing discussions.
- Funding is "braided" (federal Head Start, Oregon PreK, Preschool Promise, Preschool for All), which staff say cut "several million dollars out of the budget" [YT:sWPRPxmyI8k@0:07:41].
- Enrollment: 842 of 910 seats filled (93%), up 21% on the same point last year; every site at least 80% [YT:sWPRPxmyI8k@0:20:08] [YT:sWPRPxmyI8k@0:27:30].
- Chronic absence: about 64% of pre-K students would have counted as chronically absent last year [YT:sWPRPxmyI8k@0:30:29].
- A federal program review will occur this year (grant year 3 of 5) and will require board-member interviews [YT:sWPRPxmyI8k@0:40:38].
- Staff flagged possible federal funding instability and say mitigation plans exist; rising unionized salaries press against grant caps [YT:sWPRPxmyI8k@0:47:19] [YT:sWPRPxmyI8k@0:52:32].
- Superintendent (medium-confidence attribution) said kindergarten numbers came in above projection and that PPS has space and "an enrollment issue," framing pre-K as a feeder for K enrollment [YT:sWPRPxmyI8k@0:57:04] ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sWPRPxmyI8k&t=3424s)).
