## Summary
- The meeting's main item was the budget kickoff. Superintendent Armstrong presented a proposed 2025-26 budget of $2.04B (all funds), 15% below the prior year; the general fund is $868M. She described PPS as in its "third straight year of a significant structural deficit" [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@0:48:34] ([0:48:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-iIZmAv0aaE&t=2914s)).
- **Enrollment.** Armstrong cited a 10.8% enrollment drop since 2019-20 and weighted enrollment of 52,324 [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@0:50:54]. Staff compared this with a 6.5% statewide decline and said disproportionate decline can cut PPS's share of state funding [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@1:06:35]. The steepest declines are in kindergarten and 1st grade [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@1:05:54].
- **More cuts ahead.** Armstrong warned that 2026-27 reductions would be "at least 30 million" without new revenue [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@0:52:04]. The CFO later put the figure at "a minimum of $32 million" [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@1:19:53]. Staff raised the 2025-26 reduction from $40M to $43M to build fund balance [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@1:19:59].
- **Rightsizing language.** CFO Morrison said PPS is in a "structural ... deficiency" that needs "major course correction in order to rightsize our operations" [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@1:05:37] ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-iIZmAv0aaE&t=3937s)). She also said keeping a 5% fund balance will require "significant system changes" [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@1:21:04].
- **Bond assumed.** The budget assumes voters pass the May 2025 $1.83B bond, which would fund the Jefferson, Ida B. Wells and Cleveland modernizations plus safety, ADA, technology, curriculum, arts and athletics [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@0:52:56].
- **Staffing.** Cuts include 85.5 central-office FTE and more than 200 school FTE [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@1:25:50]. Elementary class sizes are projected to average 23.4, with 98.7% of classrooms under target; staff are promoting "blends" (multi-grade classes) in upper elementary and setting minimum class sizes in secondary [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@1:15:13].
- **Board views on funding.** Director Hollands (attribution medium) blamed state underfunding [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@1:29:15]. Director Brim-Edwards (medium) countered that the operating budget is essentially flat and that per-student resources are at a 20-25-year high [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@1:34:12]. She asked for detail on cut contracts and noted that outsourcing the print shop cost about $700K more [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@1:41:55].
- **Ideas on enrollment.** A director (likely Splitt, medium) said declining enrollment is spread thinly across grades. She asked for a state "off-ramp" and suggested voluntarily moving incoming kindergartners to neighboring schools [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@1:44:16].
- **Pay grade.** Resolution 7099 added a senior-chief pay grade ($224K-$239K) as part of Armstrong's central-office reorganization. It passed 6-1, with Brim-Edwards voting no [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@2:30:20].
- **Bond update dispute.** At the close, Brim-Edwards objected that the bond-update discussion required by Resolution 7067 (as amended) had been turned into a written memo. She noted that Native families had been told to raise the removal of Indian Education space from the Jefferson design at this meeting, and she filed a statement for the record [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@2:30:48]. Chair Wang replied that there was "disagreement on the interpretation of the words" [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@2:32:18].

## Bond / seismic / facilities
- The proposed budget includes cash flow for 2025 bond projects, which would be spent only if voters approve the bond [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@1:12:43].
- **Written update.** The agenda's bond item became a written staff memo, "High School Modernization Cost Analysis" (Dan Jung, COO; Stormy Shanks, Office of School Modernization; dated April 18, 2025). The memo says the three high-school designs are now about 295,000 sq ft each, and it forecasts combined savings "ranging from $67 million to $33 million" [BB:3996362 p.3]. Refined estimates were expected around June/July 2025 [BB:3996362 p.3].
- **Brim-Edwards' statement for the record** [BB:3997994 p.5]:
  - It quotes the April 8 staff memo, which projected a $176M-$208M balance in the bond's $1.15B modernization allocation.
  - She argues these funds could support seismic upgrades at elementary and middle schools built of unreinforced masonry.
  - She calls the earlier sample plan of retrofitting 1-2 schools for $5-10M "not a meaningful or enforceable commitment".
  - She asks for a May 6 agenda item to commit bond funds to seismic retrofits.
  - **Analysis:** this led to Resolution 7109 on May 6.
- Wang worried that cuts could cause deferred maintenance to grow "faster than we can handle" [YT:-iIZmAv0aaE@1:54:18]. Attribution: medium.
