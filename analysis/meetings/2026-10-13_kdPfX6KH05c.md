## Summary

- Wang's first reaction to the scenarios was "shock", mixed with "excitement" about stabilizing the system [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:01:59] [AUDIO:opb/2026-10-13_think-out-loud_pps-closures_eddie-wang@0:01:57].
- His core case was excess capacity plus equity:
  - a system "built for you know 60 to 80,000 students" now has 44,000 [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:02:53];
  - under-enrolled schools suffer a "slow decay" of librarians, specialists and grade strands [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:10:13];
  - in larger schools a student can join one of four bands, in small ones there is one, so the "zip code" decides [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:08:33].
- He conceded the board does not yet have all the impact information it asked for last week: "Uh, not not all of it. No." [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:14:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kdPfX6KH05c&t=882s).
- He gave two framings of money that sit in tension:
  - rightsizing is "reducing the expense of the district" so PPS can stop making cuts [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:19:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kdPfX6KH05c&t=1147s);
  - but "we're not um closing schools to save on the budget"; it is "the order of operations" (size the school count to the budget) [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:20:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kdPfX6KH05c&t=1208s).
- He rejected phasing or slowing down: "the longer we wait, the more severe the fix will have to be" [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:31:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kdPfX6KH05c&t=1860s).
- He described the current phase as "about tweaking the scenarios" [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:28:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kdPfX6KH05c&t=1725s). But he said he does not know how much the superintendent's November recommendation will differ from A/B/C [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:29:48].
- On closed buildings, he is "strongly against" selling them to private buyers. Reuse ideas (teacher housing, community centers, Preschool for All, bond swing sites) are "just conversations" [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:22:50] [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:24:24].
- On Sunnyside and focus options, he said the question "needs to be deeply explored", and that the board has asked staff why some schools were selected and others not [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:44:29].
- He did not dispute the host's framing that some families at thriving schools will lose what they have in order to reach baseline equity [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:45:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kdPfX6KH05c&t=2733s).
- He said the closure count fell from a floated "20" to 14 because of transition-support costs identified after the Green (SEGC equity audit) report [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:48:30] [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:48:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kdPfX6KH05c&t=2925s).

Analysis:
- The "20" figure matches the Aug 26 Willamette Week report that the district "Could Close Up to 20 Schools" [NEWS:NEWS-20260826-wweek-1]. The Green/Hanson audit is dated June 10 and was presented Aug 4 [BB:3997427](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/3997427?org=915) [MTG:2026-08-04/758088]. So the public "up to 20" figure came after the audit was presented. Wang's sequence (20 → audit → 14) may describe internal planning, but the archive does not document it.
- His capture-rate figures (87% → 71% → 73%) differ from Armstrong's on Oct 16 ("mid-80s" → "mid-70s") [YT:D3dVt33To7g@0:14:13]. Both are stated loosely.

## Positions & commitments

- **Pause / phasing:** ruled out in substance. "the longer we wait, the more severe the fix will have to be" [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:31:00]; "there's no more road left to kick the can" [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:31:23]. He did not address the host's region-by-region phasing idea directly [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:30:31].
- **Scenario A vs B:** he expressed no preference and said he does not know how much the superintendent's preferred option will change [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:29:48]. He said the feedback phase is "about tweaking the scenarios", including "things that um may have a bigger impact than we initially thought" [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:28:45], and that every tweak "has cascading effects that will bounce all the way across the district" [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:30:15].
- **Doing nothing (Scenario C):** he predicted it would drive more families out within five years [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:38:22] [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:38:53].
- **Information before the vote:** he conceded the board lacks some requested information [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:14:42]. His own priority questions are what a "well resource school" means, what can be offered under funding projections, and the equity impact [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:15:34]. He made no commitment on when the data would arrive.
- **Surplus property:** committed personally to keeping closed schools public and opposing sale [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:22:45] [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:22:50]. He gave no formal reuse plan [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:24:24].
- **DLI:** "I am a huge proponent of them and we'll support them forever", but programs must be sustainable [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:34:52] [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:35:03]. He cited Lent's whole-school DLI conversion under SEGC as a success [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:35:45].
- **Focus options / Sunnyside:** "deserves a robust discussion"; the board wants staff to show "why certain schools were selected and others weren't" [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:44:09] [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:44:29]. He made no commitment to change Sunnyside.
- **Community moves:** the board "prioritize[d] moving entire schools together" rather than redrawing boundaries [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:11:21].
- **District leadership:** he expressed confidence in the district's ability to manage the transition [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:47:41].
- **Feedback channels:** schoolboard@pps.net, the community sessions, and the PPS rightsizing feedback page [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:49:37].

Analysis: Wang avoided any statement that he would support deferral, phasing or a pause. That is consistent with his record (see `analysis/board_closure_stance/eddie-wang.md`). His concession that the board lacks requested data is the main new admission.
