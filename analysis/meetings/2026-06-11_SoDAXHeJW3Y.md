## Summary
- **Alternative and focus school overview.** This was framed explicitly as groundwork for rightsizing's "Educational Options" principle.
  - Staff said the 2003 Educational Options policy (6.10.022-P) "will inform our work in right sizing", with neighborhood schools as "the cornerstone" [YT:SoDAXHeJW3Y@1:01:46] ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SoDAXHeJW3Y&t=3706s)).
  - Lottery and transfer seats rose from 14% to 32% of kindergarten enrollment over Brennan's roughly 20 years, partly because enrollment fell while seats did not [YT:SoDAXHeJW3Y@1:13:41] ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SoDAXHeJW3Y&t=4421s)) [BB:3997345 p.12].
- **Calvert on location.** Rightsizing must weigh where options sit as well as what they are. Many focus options cluster in Southeast Portland, an issue the Southeast Guiding Coalition partly addressed by making Lent a whole-school Spanish DLI school [YT:SoDAXHeJW3Y@1:06:20].
- **Equity data.** Staff showed program-by-program demographics against district averages: 31.5% direct certification, 17.1% special education, 9.8% multilingual. Focus and alternative programs show "quite a bit of disparity". Staff also flagged that some programs draw heavily from particular neighborhood schools [YT:SoDAXHeJW3Y@1:16:49] [YT:SoDAXHeJW3Y@1:20:10] [BB:3997345 p.14-16].
- **Class sizes.** Splitt (medium confidence) asked for class-size comparisons, suspecting option programs have smaller classes [YT:SoDAXHeJW3Y@1:22:48].
