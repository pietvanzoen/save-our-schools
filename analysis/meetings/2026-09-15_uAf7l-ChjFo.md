## Rightsizing / closures (2:41:07–4:27:02)
- **Armstrong's opening.** Spreading resources "cannot become a permanent operating model even though it's been in place for at least eight years" [YT:uAf7l-ChjFo@2:41:47].
- **Equity process (Calvert)** [YT:uAf7l-ChjFo@2:48:31] [YT:uAf7l-ChjFo@2:51:16]:
  - Three SEGC lessons: balanced enrollment is not equity; being heard is not influence; change is cumulative.
  - An example equity checkpoint: a receiving school in a historically displaced area could not absorb students without investment and risked reinforcing advantage for affluent schools, so the team asked for a different receiving school.
- **Change load.** Magnitude, intensity and complexity of change, using Harrison Park's SEGC experience as the example. "scenarios affecting fewer schools is not necessarily more equitable or less disruptive" [YT:uAf7l-ChjFo@2:56:47] [YT:uAf7l-ChjFo@2:58:04].
- **KPIs (Smith)** [YT:uAf7l-ChjFo@2:59:28–3:04:11]:
  - schools above threshold, including Title I;
  - focal students in sustainable schools;
  - enrollment range (largest versus smallest);
  - building utilization;
  - walkability;
  - feeder continuity: seven middle schools currently split across 2–3 high schools; Bridlemile, Atkinson and Woodstock split at middle school;
  - percent of focal students changing schools;
  - Title I and CSI/TSI schools affected;
  - total students moved.
- **Cost of decline (Armstrong).** About 1,000 fewer students a year costs about $11.5M a year [YT:uAf7l-ChjFo@3:20:34]. The figure is garbled as "1.5 million" at [YT:uAf7l-ChjFo@3:09:19].
