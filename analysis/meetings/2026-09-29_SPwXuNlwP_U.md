## Summary
- **Purpose.** The board drafted its responses to the ten draft-audit recommendations directed at the board: Recs. 4 and 28–36 of the FY2024-25/2025-26 bond performance audit. The responses were due to the auditors by 10/1 [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@0:00:05] [BB:14479203 p.1].
  - Wang called it an "extremely awkward process": the board had to discuss a confidential document in public under meeting laws. The auditors asked that discussion stay on the responses only [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@0:01:28].
  - The internal auditor had drafted the responses at Audit Committee chair Sullivan's direction [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@0:00:46].
- **A consistent 4–3 split on bond oversight emerged:**
  - **Splitt, Sullivan, Engelsman and La Forte:** accept the auditors' recommendations as written, and add a bond-funded **internal** bond auditor to the Office of Internal Performance Audit (OIPA).
  - **Wang, DePass and Chase-Miller:** do a gap analysis of existing audit coverage first, prefer **external/independent** expertise, and evaluate the internal auditor before expanding her duties.
  - Analysis: this is the same 4-member grouping that pressed for more bond oversight elsewhere (La Forte's construction-auditor questions on 9/15; Engelsman's cost resolution). Wang, DePass and Chase-Miller were the board's strongest "go big" rightsizing voices on 9/1. It is a useful lens for predicting coalitions on the December closure vote, but this is inference.
- **Rec. 28** (OIPA assurance over the methodology for reducing district costs on Procedeo/Presidio program-management invoices).
  - DePass's partial-concur amendment ("needs further review of the capacity of OIPA and coverage provided by external sources") **failed 3–4**: Chase-Miller, DePass and Wang yes [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@0:58:06].
  - La Forte's amendment striking "depend on budget availability" **passed 4–3**: Chase-Miller and DePass no; Wang did not answer on the caption [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@1:00:07].
  - The amended response passed 7–0 [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@1:01:28].
- **Rec. 34** (hire an additional bond-funded auditor in OIPA).
  - Changing "audit employee" to "auditor" for flexibility passed 5–2: Engelsman and La Forte no [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@2:38:13].
  - Striking the budget-availability sentence passed 4–3: Chase-Miller, DePass and Wang no [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@2:42:18].
  - The amended response **passed 4–3**: Chase-Miller, DePass and Wang no [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@2:43:39].
- **Recs. 29 and 30** (memorialize fiscal-oversight responsibilities and update the board's "Expectations and Operating Protocols").
  - Wang's amendments to first seek advice from OSBA, the Council of the Great City Schools and urban peers were adopted 6–0 with strengthening edits (will update the protocols by June 2027) [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@1:15:05–1:24:43] [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@1:46:32–1:54:43].

## Bond / seismic / facilities
- The whole meeting concerned bond-program oversight.
- **Wang:** committing about "$2 million" over the bond's life to a new internal auditor "could be the difference between whether ... a school gets a seismic update or not" [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@0:32:58].
- **Engelsman:** "2 million out of four billion" is negligible. She cited Multnomah County's 9–10 auditors [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@0:38:21].
- **Splitt:** having moved bond management to an outside contractor (Presidio), the board "owe[s] it to ourselves to have that work checked by somebody internally" [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@0:35:01]. She also said the Bond Accountability Committee is effectively staffed by the contractor it is meant to evaluate [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@2:49:52].
- **La Forte:** "We're talking about $2 billion" and wants "somebody in the building" [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@2:33:27]. She noted that the new external bond auditor RFP had already been awarded over the summer [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@0:43:10].
- **DePass:** PPS spends about "$1.5 million" on additional auditing. She wants a gap analysis and asked whether the new external auditor's scope could be amended instead [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@0:53:24] [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@2:17:54].
