## Summary
- Dr. Terrance Green presented the equity audit of the 2020–23 Southeast Enrollment and Program Balancing (SEGC) initiative. This is the main "lessons learned" input for the 2026 rightsizing [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@0:42:11]. Findings:
  - Middle-school enrollment became more balanced: the gap between the largest and smallest school fell from about 330 to about 230 students [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@0:47:36].
  - Matched-student academic and discipline outcomes were comparable to peers [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@0:47:36].
  - Middle-school families perceived their schools much more negatively than elementary families did [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@0:49:39].
  - Twelve of the 20 schools enrolled fewer students than projected [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@0:48:58].
- **DLI trade-offs.** Converting one school to whole-school Spanish DLI displaced about 60% of neighborhood families, and Black enrollment there fell from about 10% to 2% [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@0:52:21]. Language programs reached about 86% of projected enrollment [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@0:51:00].
- **Staffing shock.** After the implementation year, 32% of staff at SEGC schools left their schools, compared with 19% elsewhere. Staff of color left at elevated rates [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@0:57:46] [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:29:06]. Promised FTE and resources often never arrived [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@0:58:26].
- **Trust.** Excluding inner-Southeast (whiter, higher-income) schools from the SEGC scope "severed lingering trust" [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:01:09]. Green's recommendations: strong equity commitments, clear decision criteria, transparent data, early engagement, receiving-school readiness, durable (about 3-year) transition supports, and tracking cumulative burden [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:08:38] [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:23:00].

## Rightsizing / closures
- **Framing.** Armstrong: "as we prepare to go deeper in the right sizing conversation, I think it's important for us to center lessons learned." [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@0:37:21] The board memo says each of the four rightsizing workgroups has incorporated the report, and that rightsizing will be judged by whether a change creates "stronger and more equitable learning environments" [BB:3997426 p.1].
- **Thresholds.** The memo uses 360 students for elementary and 500 for middle and K-8 (from the Quality Education Model), and 1,100 for high school (from the Jefferson process). It projects that by 2031-32, 69% of K-8s and 47% of middle schools will fall 100 or more students below these thresholds [BB:3997426 p.2].
- **Board questions to Dr. Green:**
  - **Family retention (Engelsman).** Families generally stayed with relocated DLI programs; a small, mostly English-speaking group left. Green noted that "rebalancing" is not "reversing" decline [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:10:39] [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:11:21] [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:12:03].
  - **DLI neighborhood versus lottery access (Splitt, Engelsman).** Calvert explained that DLI structures differ by language: Spanish is regional, Japanese, Vietnamese and Russian are district-wide, and the programs "have evolved over time" [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@0:53:41] [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:16:09].
  - **Receiving schools.** Students do better only when the receiving school is academically and culturally comparable or better. There are spillover effects on students already at receiving schools [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:18:50].
  - **Sufficient transition support.** Clear plans, acknowledging institutional "mourning", about 3 years of intensive support, co-planning time, and real-time indicators [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:21:37] [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:23:00].
  - **Models.** San Francisco for engagement; Philadelphia's Office of School Transition and San Antonio's "blending the familia" for transitions [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:27:05].
  - **Notice period.** Families felt SEGC was "prolonged and much longer than it needed to be" [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:33:10].
- **Why inner Southeast was excluded from SEGC.** Wang, a former SEGC member, recalled a capacity argument made before he joined. Franco said the perceived lesson is that "some of the loudest more most vocal voices got their way". He presented a "comprehensive plan enveloping the entire district" as the response to that lesson [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:43:31] [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:44:52].
- **QEM class sizes.** A director asked the district to commit to QEM class-size prototypes as well as school-size thresholds, and asked whether consolidation would save money if it did [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:46:16]. The speaker was not named; Analysis: the phrasing resembles Engelsman, but this is not confirmed. Armstrong said scenarios would arrive "within um weeks" and school names would come later [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:48:21]. Calvert said "there's going to be tensions" and that QEM is "one marker" [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:49:03].
- **Creston / Hosford split feeder.** About 10 students per year from Creston go to Hosford rather than Kellogg [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@1:51:44].
  - Staff (Franco, Haskins) opposed the exception. Franco cited split feeders at Tubman, Lane and Beaumont and the risk that "certain voices with access get pushed through" [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@2:03:52] [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@2:04:33].
