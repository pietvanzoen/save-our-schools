## Position summary

### Rightsizing / closures
- **Consistent, early advocate of districtwide consolidation.** In Sept 2025 he framed boundary work as "consolidating our resources where they're needed" [YT:dVcyfTroSSI@2:48:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dVcyfTroSSI&t=10120s) and asked which schools were "definitely safe from any consolidation" before choosing seismic sites [YT:HGAs0lyZ6kw@4:22:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HGAs0lyZ6kw&t=15723s).
- **Whole-district scope, fast timeline, "hard decisions":** "In order to do it equitably, it needs to be a whole district. And I hope that we have the courage to make hard decisions" [YT:qiCQKmMdLr4@3:04:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qiCQKmMdLr4&t=11049s); "a timeline about 7 to 12 months is is considered best practice" [YT:AH3wdS1A3sM@0:59:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH3wdS1A3sM&t=3540s). He sponsored Res. 7296 and voted for it [BB:3998077 p.3].
- **Rationale: fewer students, thinly spread resources:** "We have 6,000 less kids than we did back in 2016, but we have the same number of schools." [YT:jBUIkfQZu-k@1:24:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jBUIkfQZu-k&t=5040s); consolidation so every elementary has a full-time librarian and special-ed teacher [YT:jBUIkfQZu-k@1:24:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jBUIkfQZu-k&t=5064s); "bigger schools don't necessarily mean bigger class sizes" [YT:h_0YMlJnXPk@1:48:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h_0YMlJnXPk&t=6527s).
- **Outcome before number:** "I don't want to start with the number first. I want to start with what it means first, what the outcome is." [YT:5RMfvT-sAPU@2:23:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5RMfvT-sAPU&t=8588s) He prefers one comprehensive change over boundaries-first followed by another change three years later [YT:5RMfvT-sAPU@2:18:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5RMfvT-sAPU&t=8295s).
- **Middle-school consolidation:** cites data "that K8s were not preparing students as well for high school as middle schools" [YT:5RMfvT-sAPU@2:43:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5RMfvT-sAPU&t=9798s) and warns against keeping "too many middle schools open" [YT:zOUH2Jq7Owo@3:21:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zOUH2Jq7Owo&t=12117s).
- **Resists board-driven exceptions and amendments:** voted against the La Forte guardrail amendment [BB:3998077 p.4]; on 10/6 warned that past amendments had "a cascading list of consequences" [YT:zOUH2Jq7Owo@3:18:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zOUH2Jq7Owo&t=11896s); voted no on the Creston split-feeder exception, saying rightsizing is where split feeders get fixed [YT:-qhL23TOZTs@2:27:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-qhL23TOZTs&t=8851s) *(medium)*.
- **Equity guardrails he does endorse:** protection for "school communities that have recently been impacted by recent enrollment and program changes as well as Title One school communities" [YT:AAWpafO4vvQ@1:50:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AAWpafO4vvQ&t=6638s); weight for "communities that have suffered a lot of change" [YT:QIfmGcbkkLE@1:46:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIfmGcbkkLE&t=6405s); avoiding split feeders [YT:SPc05NM0oYs@1:12:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SPc05NM0oYs&t=4322s).

## How his thinking changed over time

**Jan–May 2025: cost hawk on high-school size, then defender of capacity.** In February he argued against building empty capacity: "if we enrolled all our high schools equally right even including all the new high schools we wouldn't even be able to fill up our high schools to maybe about a little less than 1,200 each" [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:37:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zxaXALjzumk&t=9476s) By the May TSCC hearing, with the bond on the ballot, he rejected the overbuilt critique: "when we build our schools to 1700 you do not want to fill up our schools to 1700." [YT:OsyteRB1f9c@0:59:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OsyteRB1f9c&t=3582s) Analysis: the shift tracks the board's adoption of the ~295,000 sq ft framework (Res. 7051) rather than a change in his enrollment view.

**Summer–fall 2025: from "boundaries" to explicit consolidation.** As chair he named boundaries and enrollment "probably the most difficult" priority [YT:BZdKSNeHDaU@0:03:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BZdKSNeHDaU&t=193s). By September he linked seismic choices to future consolidation: "if anyone knows which schools are definitely safe from any consolidation or whatever you would know and also how that overlaps with the ones with the most seismic needs." [YT:HGAs0lyZ6kw@4:22:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HGAs0lyZ6kw&t=15723s) and "consolidating our resources where they're needed and that that's students focused" [YT:dVcyfTroSSI@2:48:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dVcyfTroSSI&t=10120s)

**Jan 2026: willing to change his own Jefferson preference for future flexibility.** "Before, you know, the decision was made, I was actually a big fan of scenario B." [YT:BJMsF-90V6U@1:59:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BJMsF-90V6U&t=7164s) "scenario C allows the flexibility so that our other high schools don't have as much danger of being underenrolled." [YT:BJMsF-90V6U@1:59:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BJMsF-90V6U&t=7180s)

**Feb–Apr 2026: hardening against piecemeal exceptions.** After the MLC 9-12 reversal: "This is a zero sum game which means saving some here is going to take from somewhere else." [YT:3sggkeHNIVk@1:46:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3sggkeHNIVk&t=6411s) In March: "What is would be new is if we have a board that's willing to make hard decisions." [YT:qiCQKmMdLr4@3:01:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qiCQKmMdLr4&t=10909s) In April, on why Res. 7296 needed to bind the board: "those areas got excluded from the process because the board caved." [YT:AH3wdS1A3sM@1:46:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH3wdS1A3sM&t=6382s)

**Late Apr 2026: a stated shift on focus options.** "I have been someone who has been very adamant and vocal about um supporting our neighborhood schools." [YT:j9PzUvPpSVA@4:35:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j9PzUvPpSVA&t=16509s) *(medium-confidence attribution)* He now favors lottery or policy fixes over closure for focus options [YT:j9PzUvPpSVA@4:35:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j9PzUvPpSVA&t=16556s) *(medium)*.

**May–Oct 2026: consolidation as the route to stability; scale left open.** "If we prioritize walkability as number one, then that means keeping the maximum amount of schools open, which means that we don't do anything." [YT:AAWpafO4vvQ@1:51:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AAWpafO4vvQ&t=6684s) "I don't want to start with the number first. I want to start with what it means first, what the outcome is." [YT:5RMfvT-sAPU@2:23:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5RMfvT-sAPU&t=8588s) On the Oct 6 scenarios he defended middle-school consolidation [YT:zOUH2Jq7Owo@3:21:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zOUH2Jq7Owo&t=12117s) and cautioned the board against amendments [YT:zOUH2Jq7Owo@3:18:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zOUH2Jq7Owo&t=11896s).

Analysis: Wang's direction has been stable since mid-2025 (pro-consolidation, districtwide, staff-led). The only notable movement is on focus options (from critic toward "policy solution") and on high-school capacity (Feb 2025).
