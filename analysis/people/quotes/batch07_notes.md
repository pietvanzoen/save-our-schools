## Big picture for this window
- **Late April: engagement wraps; the board starts drafting principles.** The 04-27 Roosevelt session was the 5th in the series.
  - Staff repeated that no list of schools exists [BB:3998089 p.1].
  - Board reflections on 04-28 were the first board-level brainstorm of guiding principles. The draft resolution, with four principles (well-resourced; healthy environments; educational options "with neighborhood schools at the center"; equity lens) plus a workplan, was presented 05-12 [BB:3997246], with a vote set for **May 26**.
- **The superintendent's budget message explicitly ties the deficit to too many buildings:** "We need to continue to plan for consolidation and closure" [YT:j9PzUvPpSVA@1:12:15].
  - Context: a $56M gap closed for 2026-27; $65M+ forecast for 2027-28; -$169M by 2029-30 if nothing changes; the PERS rate jumping from about 4% to about 21%.
- **Five principle fights on 05-12** (not resolved; the vote follows May 26):
  1. "safe" added (consensus);
  2. Splitt's "minimize fragmentation" and "compact boundaries," with a threatened no vote;
  3. Engelsman's timeline and walkability (4th-ranked) asks;
  4. La Forte's TAG in the equity lens, opposed by Chase-Miller;
  5. how to word "neighborhood schools at the center" so it doesn't read as if neighborhood schools close first.
- **Focus options are the live fault line.**
  - Wang says he moved toward valuing focus options.
  - Splitt wants them "more equitable" and says her candor drew "outsized" focus-school turnout.
  - Chase-Miller wants focus options in every cluster.
  - Sullivan, DePass and Splitt like neighborhood-plus-focus hybrids.
  - Armstrong says focus-option resourcing "will be prominent" in rightsizing, and closure, consolidation and program moves remain possible (05-13).
- **ACCESS Academy busing cut** (budget) became a de facto rightsizing issue. La Forte wants it delayed a year and folded into rightsizing; Splitt wants a middle ground. Armstrong cited 300+ families on ACCESS's SW waitlist.

## Per-person observations (this window)

### Eddie Wang (Chair)
- On 04-28 he described himself as a strong neighborhood-school advocate whose view of focus options changed after the engagement sessions. He now prefers lottery or policy fixes over closure to reduce focus-school impacts on neighborhood schools [YT:j9PzUvPpSVA@4:35:09].
  - Medium confidence: the speaker isn't named. The voice and phrasing match Wang, and batch06 shows he attended sessions.
- On 05-12 he backed the draft principles "as is" plus "safe."
  - His framing: principles must not come "from a position of fear"; equity needs "bold moves"; he wants to see scenarios.
  - He resisted Splitt's anti-fragmentation principle: split communities "become new communities" [YT:OLAZDzvg3RM@4:33:45] [YT:OLAZDzvg3RM@5:09:51].
- Budget: he previewed a request for a **three-year staffing plan** to cut administrative layers and add teacher voice ("help needs to feel like help") [YT:j9PzUvPpSVA@2:02:17].
- He opposed the foundation pause ("missed the boat").
- Retreat: the board has historically "resist[ed] progressive change"; act as trustees when voting.

### Michelle DePass (Vice-Chair; Policy Committee chair in 2025-26)
- Consistent vision of fewer but great schools: "great schools throughout the... district, though just not as many" [YT:j9PzUvPpSVA@4:41:16].
  - Design and facility lens: daylight, climate control, renderings, adaptive reuse as a selection criterion.
  - Open to merging neighborhood and focus programs.
- On 05-12 she emphasized not re-harming recently impacted communities, invoking past harm to Portland's Black community. She also wants neighborhood schools centered without being "plucked off", and asked for a transportation (miles-driven) study.
- She linked rightsizing to decarbonization: "exemplary schools that people don't have to crisscross town"
- **Shift:** on 04-27 she called the REE reporting clause "overkill." By 05-18 she endorsed the codified version ("nice to have it codified").
- She opposed the foundation pause and suggested an evaluation date. She has been on the board since 2019 (her own statement on 04-28).

### Christy Splitt
- The most specific author of rightsizing criteria in this window:
  - "minimizing fragmentation of school communities";
  - compact boundaries;
  - "healthy and safe" (seismic);
  - a funding "floor" for all schools;
  - retiring buildings in the worst condition;
  - making focus options more equitable.
- **Conditionality:** "I might vote no on the resolution" without fragmentation and compact-boundary language [YT:OLAZDzvg3RM@4:51:42].
- She insists on representative (non-white, SPED, immigrant) input before adopting principles. She worries K-8 focus enthusiasm is over-represented.
- Says foundations "perpetuate inequity" even though her own school benefited. **Says she is not planning to run again** (04-28) [YT:j9PzUvPpSVA@4:21:19].
- Process reformer: shorter, more frequent meetings.

### Stephanie Engelsman
- Her refrain since 04-27: rightsizing must produce "great schools, not less of what we have now", or families will leave.
- Data requests: lottery applicants who leave PPS; outcomes of past closures (Marshall, Edwards, Bridger).
- On 05-12 she wanted:
  - a timeline in the principles;
  - walkability/proximity (ranked 4th) added;
  - neighborhood-school wording that doesn't signal they are closure targets.
- She requested the added N/NE engagement sessions (her own account on 04-28).
- On the foundation pause she wanted to "give [the Fund] a chance" and revisit if it fails. She is the only member who abstained on 7298A.
- Strong guardrail instincts in policy work: volunteer background checks, REE reporting. She is AI- and screen-skeptical.

### Virginia La Forte
- FIO chair in 2025-26 (she says so on 05-07). On bonds:
  - voted **no on most bond contractor-pool contracts** (7298 A–G, J), objecting that price isn't a factor and task orders skip the board;
  - pushed to reconsider Res. 7291 (contracting rules), saying legal advice was wrong.
- **Rightsizing positions** (medium confidence on 04-27 and 04-28, inferred from content):
  - consolidation can broaden access to arts and supports;
  - facility condition matters;
  - use the "120 million sitting in the bank earmarked for Tubman".
- 05-12/05-13 (medium): strong TAG/ACCESS advocate. She wants TAG in the equity lens, citing the district's "why they stay, why they leave" retention survey, and wants the ACCESS busing cut delayed into rightsizing.
- She is on the Fund for PPS board and opposed the pause.

### Rashelle Chase-Miller (TLE chair in 2025-26)
- Equity operationalizer:
  - won't adopt principles until families of color, SPED and immigrant families are heard;
  - "equity" alone is too vague;
  - plan where staff go to address racism Black students face in majority-white schools;
  - accessibility as a principle (her own child's neighborhood school lacks an elevator).
- Wants focus options in each cluster and a TAG overhaul inside neighborhood schools. She opposes listing TAG in the equity lens.
- Bus stops build community too, so walkability shouldn't dominate.
- She gave the strongest anti-pause speech ("impossible to purchase outcomes").
- Pro-AI framework ("I don't think we should make decisions out of fear"). She is co-drafting a screen-time policy with DePass.

### Patte Sullivan (Audit Committee chair)
- Led the failed effort to pause the 2024 foundation-staffing restriction ("Our students can't wait") and conceded she lacked four votes.
- On rightsizing she likes neighborhood-plus-focus hybrids (Sunnyside model), wants data on why families choose focus schools, and wants popular focus programs replicated. She called the draft principles "perfectly fine the way it is."
- Repeatedly urges tapping the state Education Stability Fund.

### Ian Ritorto (Student Rep, 2025-26)
- He blames state disinvestment (Measure 5/50) and laments cutting focus and early-literacy programs. Strongly opposed to the foundation pause on equity grounds.
- On 05-12 he read a 6th grader's rightsizing questions: Head Start/PreK left out of utilization, lottery drain on Title I schools, and student involvement.

### Kimberlee Armstrong (Superintendent)
- Explicit pro-consolidation framing in her budget message.
- Repeatedly says no decisions have been made ("we have not made any decisions at this stage") and describes the work as listening.
- On focus options (05-13): she frames the review as resourcing, but "uncomfortable closure, consolidation... program moves" conversations will happen.
- She wants a deadline to evaluate the Fund for PPS.
