# Batch 04 notes: Nov 18, 2025 to Jan 23, 2026 (11 meetings)

Quotes are in `analysis/people/quotes/batch04.jsonl` (140 entries). A script checked every quote string against the transcript; bracketed repairs were allowed. Timestamps come from the transcript line where each quote starts. Meeting summaries are in `analysis/meetings/<date>_<videoId>.md`.

Meetings covered: 2025-11-18 work session (CBSE/One North, levy, ERP); 2025-12-02 regular meeting (Procedeo contract, CBSE purchase, field trips); 2025-12-08 Jefferson listening session; 2025-12-09 FIO; 2025-12-11 TLE; 2025-12-15 Policy; 2025-12-16 work session (Jefferson, budget, leadership); 2026-01-08 TLE; 2026-01-13 regular meeting (Jefferson votes, leadership election); 2026-01-20 FIO (BAC charter, seismic); 2026-01-23 Policy.

## How each person's positions evolved in this window

**Eddie Wang (Chair)**
- **CBSE:** strongly supported it. He framed the $60M 2020 bond allocation as legally restricted and said it cannot be redirected to the deficit (11/18).
- **Procedeo:** backed outsourcing bond program management and argued the board should not use contract approval to manage district structure ("If the superintendent wants to make staffing changes, that's her right," 12/2). He credited Procedeo with planning all three high schools at once, saving 3–5 years.
- **Board conduct:** apologized on the board's behalf for the 12/2 meeting (12/8).
- **Jefferson:** changed his mind. He had been "a big fan of scenario B" but voted for C because it "allows the flexibility" for future redistricting and protects other high schools (1/13).
- **Boundary change in general:** drew on his Southeast Guiding Coalition (SEGC) experience to say that in other districts redistricting high school boundaries is routine, and that perceived harm usually exceeds real harm. He also warned that unvetted amendments create unintended consequences (12/16).
- **Analysis:** of the board members, he is the most comfortable with boundary changes, which matters for rightsizing.
- **Other:** opposed the field-trip reporting amendment, partly citing his experience as an Asian American. Re-elected chair 7–0.

**Michelle DePass (Vice-Chair)**
- **Background:** facilities and interior-design experience; Jefferson attendee in the late 1970s; lives in the Jefferson catchment; describes herself as the longest-serving member.
- **CBSE and Procedeo:** supported both and moved Res. 7216.
- **Policy Committee chair:** started a systematic review of board policies, beginning with Section 1.
- **On staff workload:** consistently cautious about adding work during cuts. She resisted expanding the Bond Accountability Committee's duties and meeting frequency because of the "$50 million" in cuts (1/20).
- **Jefferson monitoring:** wary of holding Jefferson to a monitoring standard different from other schools (12/16, medium confidence).
- **Board leadership:** prefers rotating directors through leadership meetings, "thinking as one team." Re-elected vice chair 7–0.
- **Gap to watch:** at the 1/23 Policy Committee she did not take up the public request to revisit the school-closure policy. See Rightsizing below.

**Rashelle Chase-Miller**
- **CBSE:** the most emphatic advocate ("atonement work"). She rejected a dispersed CBSE as echoing the displacement of Black Portlanders.
- **TLE chair:** pressed staff on Access Academy's under-representation of girls and students of color.
- **Jefferson:** a self-described "B person" who struggled with splitting Tubman, but she did not amend and voted for C, calling it the chance to "[right] a historic wrong."
- **Field trips:** deferred to principals' judgment.
- **Class size:** linked classroom technology use to large classes (1/23, medium confidence).
- **Leadership:** withdrew from the vice-chair race; named a School Board Partners fellow.

**Stephanie Engelsman**
- **General approach:** the board's most persistent fiscal and legal questioner.
- **CBSE:** moved from skeptic to yes. On 11/18 she pressed on operating costs, transportation and the atrium. On 12/2 she said staff answers on operating costs and property-tax liability resolved her concerns.
- **Procedeo:** voted no (12/2). She asked whether staff had compared in-house and outsourced management costs, raised staffing and job-loss questions, and said she had been "blocked from getting information."
- **Other priorities:** finishing seismic work (12/2); class-size caps and state funding (12/16); elementary start times (medium confidence).
- **Jefferson:** engaged throughout. She proposed, then withdrew, a course-catalog timeline amendment, and was **the only board member to vote no on Res. 7229 (Scenario C)**. Her statement for the record cites Roosevelt's vulnerability and Grant's programming, saying forecasts are "higher than actual attendance today" [BB:3998053 p.6].
- **Leadership:** withdrew from the vice-chair race, arguing that leadership should rotate.
- **Policy work:** pushed field-trip continuity amendments, which were postponed and then withdrawn.

**Virginia La Forte**
- **FIO chair:** organized monthly closure of outstanding bond-audit items.
- **Bond delivery:** researched other districts' bonds and found most finished faster than PPS.
- **Procedeo:** supported outside program management in principle, but moved to postpone for a cost analysis and moved to strip bonuses and cut the term to one year. Both failed; she voted no (12/2).
- **CBSE:** supported it, drawing on her environmental-health history with Tubman Middle School.
- **TAG and Access Academy:** supportive, as a former TAG advisory council member.
- **Jefferson:** argued for AP parity there, and floated restructuring Benson to bolster neighborhood high schools; the superintendent declined (12/16). She supported Scenario C for Grant's long-term stability, explicitly "looking forward towards right sizing our schools" (1/13).
- **Facilities reporting (FIO, 1/13):** seismic prioritization weights are 90% seismic risk, 5% condition and 5% enrollment, and maintenance staffing is 47% below best practice.
- **BAC charter:** wants it to mirror the ballot's spending-oversight language.

**Christy Splitt**
- **Background:** works for a state agency (Department of Energy, per 1/8); liaison to the Bond Accountability Committee.
- **CBSE:** supported it ("This is Albina... I trust you").
- **Procedeo:** supported it; tried to call the question (12/2), then left that meeting before the later votes.
- **State budget:** warned the legislature may cut K-12 funding pre-emptively (12/16).
- **Jefferson:** framed C as "a compromise." Her parity and monitoring requests (2 FTE per year; annual reports on four high schools with family notice) were written into Res. 7229, and she moved it. She said the process "set the bar" for future tough decisions (1/13).
- **Leadership:** proposed a co-vice-chair mentor model, then withdrew from the vice-chair race.

**Patte Sullivan**
- **CBSE:** voted yes, deferring to the Black community even though she doubted the location (12/2).
- **Procedeo:** supported it out of trust in staff and the superintendent she helped hire.
- **Attendance:** absent 12/8 and 12/16.
- **Jefferson:** supported C after consulting Calvert, hoping future observers will say "that board made the right decision" (1/13).
- **Other:** chairs the Audit Committee; raised concerns about classroom screens and computers.

**Ian Ritorto (Student Rep, Roosevelt senior)**
- **CBSE:** supported it ("not let perfect be the enemy of good").
- **Procedeo:** abstained at first, then voted no over public-engagement concerns.
- **State funding:** a recurring theme (Measures 5 and 50; "choosing between muscles").
- **Rightsizing:** the first person in this batch to ask publicly how rightsizing fits with the Jefferson decision (12/16).
- **Roosevelt:** repeatedly flagged its projected 1,153 enrollment and later relayed interest in extending its boundary across the St. Johns Bridge.
- **Jefferson vote:** voted B (unofficial no on 7229) per the District Student Council (~15–17 for B, ~4 for C).

**Kimberlee Armstrong (Superintendent)**
- **Procedeo:** the decisive advocate. She cited contingency overruns at Jefferson ($55M) and Harrison Park ($25M+), said "OSM has already fallen apart," and accepted being judged by "the amount of incompetence I allow."
- **Jefferson:** recommended Scenario C and insisted that ending dual assignment be voted on first.
- **Rightsizing scope:** described a 12–18 month horizon for school and boundary work, focused on elementary schools, DLI and K-8 vs 6-8 configurations. She ruled out high-school or Benson redesign for now.
- **Budget:** previewed at least 200 FTE in reductions for the $50M gap.

**Margaret Calvert (Asst. Superintendent)**
- Lead on Jefferson.
- **Rightsizing:** confirmed "we are gonna embark on a right siz conversation." It builds on the SEGC's two leftover items (DLI placement and SE middle-school feeders) and aims not to disrupt the same communities repeatedly.
- **Enrollment standards:** described 1,100 students as a floor for comprehensive programming, not an ideal, and committed to monitoring the four high schools through 2030-31.

**Other staff**
- **Jon Franco (Sr. Chief of Operations):** called the Procedeo contract "a bargain." Recommended the "shell" phasing option for Jefferson. Said seismic work and rightsizing run on "parallel" tracks (medium confidence).
- **Michelle Morrison (CFO):** reserves are at the 5% floor; the $50M gap equals about 12.5 elementary school budgets; there are no reserves left for next year.
- **Sarah Norman (OSM/Procedeo):** said past bond management lacked planning and documentation.
- **Janise Hansen (internal auditor):** wants the Bond Accountability Committee to recommend the scope of bond audits.

## Rightsizing signals in this window (for cross-batch synthesis)

- **12/2:** the PAT president warned that rightsizing nationally closes schools in Black and brown neighborhoods.
- **12/8:** a commenter cited a board member's Facebook remark about "flex" in Jefferson-area attendance boundaries "when elementary school consolidation occurs."
- **12/9:** a commenter expected some high-seismic-risk schools to close through consolidation.
- **12/11:** commenters argued Access Academy draws students away from under-enrolled neighborhood schools.
- **12/16:** first explicit board and staff framing of rightsizing. Calvert said it builds on SEGC; Armstrong gave a 12–18 month horizon; staff said engagement could start in February; the minutes record discussion of "alignment with long-term district 'right-sizing' plans."
- **1/13:** Wang and La Forte justified Scenario C partly by future redistricting and rightsizing flexibility. The CFO expressed the deficit as about 12.5 elementary school budgets. Seismic prioritization includes a 5% enrollment weight.
- **1/20:**
  - A commenter asked that seismic prioritization wait for consolidation engagement and that unreinforced-masonry buildings be considered for consolidation.
  - A committee member asked whether the seismic sites had been vetted against consolidation.
  - Staff answered that the two run on parallel tracks.
- **1/23:** a commenter asked the Policy Committee to revisit the 2023 draft amendments to the school-closure policy (Oregon PTA guidance). The committee did not add it to its list.
