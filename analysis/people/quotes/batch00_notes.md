**Context:** there was no formal "rightsizing" item in this window. The relevant threads are:

- the $1.83B 2025 bond and the three high-school modernizations (Cleveland, Ida B. Wells, Jefferson)
- debate over high-school capacity versus enrollment

- the Southeast Guiding Coalition (SEGC) legacy of earlier boundary changes and program closures
- a March 4 directive asking staff for "a path to enrollment balancing across PPS high schools"

## Person-by-person observations

### Eddie Wang (chair)
- Consistently argues the new high schools are oversized relative to likely enrollment.
  - Feb 4: citing a PSU study, even balanced enrollment would put high schools at "a little less than 1,200 each". He would trade empty capacity (about 30 classrooms) for larger rooms and a ~300,000 sq ft cap [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:37:56].
  - In the same breath he is "adamant" that Jefferson become "a full beautiful well- enrolled High School".
- Frames savings as K-8 money: "every 20 $30 million … another Elementary School … HVAC" [YT:XENAU3DUf5g@2:09:04].
- Process-focused:
  - insists on Robert's Rules and equal floor time (Mar 4)
  - opposed Brim-Edwards' late-filed reporting and enrollment-balancing amendment on procedural grounds ("38 minutes before"), voting no [YT:GM0ozDVDOQ4@3:30:58]
- SEGC: sided with the superintendent's reallocation as "a choice between bad and worse", noting that non-SEGC neighbors in SE have high need [YT:XENAU3DUf5g@3:20:23].
- Engagement framing (Jan 7): the phone-policy student survey was only "consult" on the IAP2 spectrum.
- His parent perspective: his child is expected to attend Cleveland.

### Michelle DePass (vice-chair)
- The most consistent Jefferson advocate. Alumna and catchment resident.
  - Feb 4: opposes sizing high schools to current enrollment because "Jefferson suffers"; wants population projections used [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:12:18].
  - Mar 4 (medium): cites new family housing in the Jefferson catchment and a PSU demographer on teen-family growth in the Franklin and Jefferson clusters [YT:GM0ozDVDOQ4@0:53:58].
  - Mar 18: "Jefferson has been disinvested in for decades" [YT:XENAU3DUf5g@1:15:17].
  - Mar 18 (medium): asked how the new 15-student minimum class size hits "under enrolled" schools like Jefferson [YT:XENAU3DUf5g@3:03:35].
  - Apr 8 (medium): permanent home for Indian Education, possibly using Jefferson's early unused space.
- Narrow view of the board's role, reinforced by NSBA/Howard policy training: called the ethics-employment restriction "board policy overreach" (Feb 26) and voted no in committee.
- Jan 7 (medium): once a "very strong advocate" for a standalone Native center but now prefers programming in existing buildings ("the most sustainable option is to use the buildings that we have currently"). She voted against the $40M bond amendment.
- Mar 4: voted no on the reporting and enrollment-balancing amendment (wanted time to review). Called the question on the contracts debate.
- Apr 3 (medium): keep the September 1 kindergarten cutoff on equity grounds.

### Julia Brim-Edwards
- The most active voice on process, bond accountability and enrollment. Leaves the board June 2025.
- **Bond process:**
  - Repeatedly says cost decisions "should have happened before the referral"; the bond "is in Jeopardy … dead in the water" [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:21:02].
  - It had "the least number of meetings … no Community stakeholder group" of the six bonds she has worked on [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:41:11].
- **Author of the Feb 11 cost-reduction framework (Res. 7051):** about 295,000 sq ft, Ed-spec minimums, all-electric, equity contracting kept.
- **Author of the Mar 4 reporting amendment,** including item 5: "a path to enrollment balancing across PPS high schools and a general enrollment strategy". It passed 4–2. This is the first formal enrollment-balancing directive in this window.
- **Capacity:** skeptical of shrinking Wells and Cleveland ("over 1500" for 10 years), citing Madison's growth. Wants each school's enrollment history before deciding.
- **SEGC:** strongest defender of the five-year stabilization promise to schools hit by "boundary changes … program changes en closures".
  - Cites the Lent all-Spanish conversion pushing students, including special education students, to Marysville [YT:XENAU3DUf5g@3:11:19] [YT:XENAU3DUf5g@3:12:04].
  - Clashed openly with the superintendent (Mar 18).
- **Small schools:** blends land on "lower enrolled schools … single strands"; wants transparency (heat map) [YT:XENAU3DUf5g@3:23:26].
- **Enrollment retention:**
  - Kindergarten early entry: worried families sent to private kindergarten never return (Feb 26, Apr 3).
  - Raised inter-district transfers: some schools are majority non-resident (Apr 8).
- **Climate:** non-negotiable on all-electric; open to dropping LEED certification or mass timber.
- **Jan 7:** moved the failed $40M Native center bond amendment (2–5).

### Herman Greene
- **Cost hawk on high schools.**
  - Jan 7: asked for an independent "construction audit", not value engineering [YT:YLB6Aken-Vo@2:19:32].
  - Mar 4: "currently what we're doing doesn't work" (CM/GC) and "no reason why we should be spending half a billion dollars on a single solitary High School".
  - Apr 8: "The math ain't mathing" and "When does the bleeding stop?"
- **Yet opposes shrinking capacity or arts.** Feb 4: building smaller "is looking at right now"; would accept no basement and cuts to some premium spaces [YT:zxaXALjzumk@2:22:48].
  - Analysis: he wants lower unit costs, not fewer seats.
- **Facilities:** urged quick action on the headquarters move (Albina Vision Trust deal), saying "this building will fall down" (Jan 7).
- **Votes:** for the $40M Native center amendment (Jan 7); against the 5–2 phone policy.
- He was absent from some votes (Mar 4 first vote, Mar 18).
