## Big picture for this window

- **3/10: rightsizing goes public.** Armstrong said she would bring "a list in late fall that may contain five to 10 schools listed for closure" [YT:qiCQKmMdLr4@2:27:49]. Headlines followed the same night [NEWS:NEWS-20260310-kptv-1].
  - Every board member endorsed a districtwide, all-at-once process. Most wanted K-8s and focus options in scope.
  - Seismic prioritization excluded schools under 300 students "given ... right sizing" [YT:qiCQKmMdLr4@1:11:20].
- **3/12 and 3/31: criteria take shape.**
  - Splitt's "consolidation over closure".
  - Chase-Miller's "consolidate into the safest seismic buildings" and "blending" of school cultures.
  - Wang's split-feeder concern and his point about focus options "whiter than their neighborhood".
  - Staff's "use all levers" (program moves, grade reconfiguration, mergers, consolidations, closures).
  - The timeline moved from a December to a **November** recommendation [YT:QIfmGcbkkLE@1:53:38].
- **4/11–4/22: three listening sessions.**
  - The Kellogg session was criticized (attendance "97% white" per Wang; "talked at" per FACE). The virtual session was Zoom-bombed.
  - The district added N/NE and other sessions (Benson 4/25, Roosevelt 4/27).
  - Staff repeatedly denied that any closure list exists.
- **4/14: Res. 7296 adopted 5–2** (Engelsman and La Forte no). Recital E: the process "will include school and program closures, consolidations, or boundary changes". The La Forte/Engelsman guardrail amendment failed 3–4.
- **4/22: Armstrong reframes the scale.** "I can't imagine that we'll be higher than 12 ... we can be as low as one, two, or or three". Boundaries might come first. "closing schools is not a cost-saving strategy" [YT:h_0YMlJnXPk@0:59:00] [YT:h_0YMlJnXPk@1:25:55].

## Person-by-person observations

- **Kimberlee Armstrong (Superintendent)**
  - 3/10: blunt. "I want to call it what it is ... a recommendation to close a ... subset of schools". Asked the board for direction on resolution timing, engagement and scope.
  - 3/12: cautions against blanket rules (Skyline K-8) and against "equal" replacing "equity".
  - 3/31: closures as "reinvestments and not just a reduction"; pairs rightsizing with a new vision and strategic plan.
  - 4/14: resisted softening Recital E from "will" to "may" ("result in nothing?").
  - 4/22: much softer on scale (1–12, possibly boundaries first) and on rationale (not cost saving).
  - Analysis: she wants firm board direction and a binding commitment to change, but has tempered public expectations as community anxiety has grown.
- **Eddie Wang (Chair)**
  - Consistently urgent and pro-consolidation: "a board that's willing to make hard decisions" (3/10); 7–12-month best practice; "the board caved" in SEGC (4/14); "6,000 less kids ... same number of schools" (4/20); bigger schools can mean smaller classes (4/22).
  - Wants staff-led and technical processes (seismic is "an engineering problem"; "tail wagging the dog"). Opposed adding the guardrail amendment before staff vetting.
  - Equity framing: split feeders, focus options draining Title I schools, and weighting communities hit by repeated change (3/31).
  - Abstained on adding "boundary changes" to Recital E (4/14). Reason not stated.
- **Michelle DePass (Vice-Chair)**
  - 3/10: objected that seismic site selection came before rightsizing ("order of operations") and asked for a community advisory body. (Both 3/10 attributions are medium; see attribution notes.)
  - 3/31 and 4/14: centers equity outcomes and communities historically harmed by closures ("I have uh personal experience with that"). Supports the staff/union advisory committee.
  - On 4/14 she voted against the guardrail amendment because it should follow broader listening ("I don't like when I hear people say I think we all agree").
  - Self-reports "six years on the board" (3/31); the roster shows her term from 7/2023. To verify.
- **Rashelle Chase-Miller (TLE chair; Zone 4, N/NE)**
  - Enthusiastic about rightsizing ("pull the band-aid off"; "dreaming about what can be possible ... once they are right-sized").
  - Wants K-8s and focus options in scope, consolidation into the safest seismic buildings, and protection of North Portland and underserved communities.
  - 4/14: voted for the original Res. 7296 and against the amendment ("hear from more community"). Later said "we will not arrive at an equitable outcome if we prioritize speed over doing the work well".
  - Analysis: pro-rightsizing, but process-equity first. Not a guardrails-in-writing advocate.
- **Virginia La Forte (FIO chair; Zone 5, NE)**
  - Most quotes in this batch.
  - 3/10–3/12: strongly pro-middle-school conversion ("We need to pick a lane"), "consolidating at scale", fold Tubman money into planning.
  - **Change of position, 3/31:** after survey data showed belonging dropping at grade 6, she said "I've always believed that middle schools were, but the more recent research has actually shown the opposite" and asked for K-8 vs MS breakdowns. That evening's group work still listed "middle school conversion" as "a big one" (medium attribution), so the shift is tentative.
  - 4/22: wants "what's working" (project-based learning, focus-option features) delivered in every neighborhood school.
  - Persistent push on the missing seismic prioritization plan (3/10, 3/17, 3/31). It was not on the 4/21 FIO agenda after all.
  - Fiscal skeptic: asked to use reserves instead of furloughs (3/31); "closing schools doesn't really save any money. I don't know that" (4/14).
  - Author of the A–F guardrail amendment; voted no on 7296 without it.
- **Christy Splitt (Zone 1, SW)**
  - Architect of the resolution sequence (April launch, later criteria). Co-sponsored 7296.
  - Core principles: consolidation over closure; every part of the city and every school type in the calculus, including focus options "for closure as well"; per-student cost and building condition as criteria; transition plans; visit affected schools before announcements.
  - Opposes prioritizing lottery schools for seismic money.
  - Open to "may" on 4/14 (heard parents ask "are we sure we have to close schools?") but voted no on the amendment.
  - 4/22: said "12,000 fewer students", which overstates district data. Likely a misstatement.
- **Stephanie Engelsman (Zone 6, SE)**
  - The most consistent skeptic of consolidation:
    - wants Bridger post-merger data;
    - small schools "are desperately trying to grow their enrollment";
    - lottery losses push families out of PPS;
    - floated expanding high-demand programs;
    - first to warn that a sub-300 school could become a 550-student consolidated school that was never seismically evaluated.
  - Process: wanted the recommendation by October (3/10). Co-authored the guardrail amendment and argued commitments must be written down because future boards (2027 and 2029 elections) will implement them. Voted no on 7296. Criticized how resolutions are drafted under open-meeting law.
  - 4/22 (medium): reported strong support for focus-option K-8s.
- **Patte Sullivan (Zone 3)**
  - Low-key. Supports districtwide scope; asked whether the west side is included; "nobody's looking forward to this to the downsizing".
  - Voted for the guardrail amendment and "may", then for 7296.
  - Reported that Kellogg participants "really wanted to keep their small schools small". Led the push to discuss pausing local-foundation restrictions (4/14).
- **Ian Ritorto (Student Rep)**
  - Ties everything to state funding (Measures 5/50, the Education Stability Fund).
  - Sees closures as provoking outrage "proportionate[ly]" beyond net harm (3/10). By 4/14 he said "I would absolutely like to see a scenario where no schools are cut" and voted no (unofficially).
  - Wants rapid official statements after leaks (lesson from MLC).
- **Margaret Calvert (Asst. Supt.)** is the operational lead and facilitator. Key framings: "describing current state", "pivot point", "use all levers", "not just enrollment". Said she was Jefferson HS principal for about a decade (4/11).
- **Jon Franco (Sr. Chief Ops):** rightsizing "is not about money" (3/10); explained the seismic under-300 exclusion and the flexible A/E pool.
