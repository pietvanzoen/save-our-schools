## Weak points

**1. Equity burden test vs. what the scenarios actually do in her own region.**
- What the PPS documents show:
  - Both scenarios close the same four schools in the Jefferson/Roosevelt region: Beach, James John, Peninsula and Sabin [BB:14505792 p.1].
  - Three of them (Beach, James John, Peninsula) are Title I [BB:3997157 p.1].
  - PPS's own list of closures since 2000 is heavily N/NE: Meek, Applegate, Kenton, Clarendon, Tubman MS, Humboldt [BB:14374370 p.4]. She cited Kenton and Applegate herself [YT:zOUH2Jq7Owo@3:26:10] *(medium)*.
  - On the other hand, this region has the worst status quo: only 16% of its schools are projected above the sustainability threshold. Under A or B that rises to 73%, and students in poverty in sustainable schools go from 32% to 84% [BB:14505792 p.1].
  - Districtwide, the share of K-8 students who move is similar across groups (poverty 27%, historically underserved by race 28%, multilingual 31%) [BB:14511742 p.44].
- Analysis:
  - Her test, "not placing a disproportionate burden", does not say how to weigh the burden of moving against the benefit of ending up in a sustainable school. Both are large in North Portland.
  - Counted by Title I status among K-5/K-8 schools (25 of 50 are Title I [BB:3997157 p.1]): Scenario A closes 6 Title I schools of 13 (46%). Scenario B closes 6 of 10 (60%), because the three schools B spares (Stephenson, Rose City Park, Lewis) are all non-Title I [BB:14509719 p.1].
  - Applied literally, her equity test favors the larger Scenario A over the smaller B. A North Portland director would not intuitively expect that.
  - Res. 7307, which she moved, requires a "standardized equity impact analysis" for each scenario, including Title I and recently impacted communities [BB:3997273 p.2]. The October release contains only summary metrics [BB:14505788 p.1] [BB:14511742 p.44], and she has not yet asked for the full analysis on record.

  - PPS's own "well-resourced" model promises "Libraries open more consistently" [BB:14374370 p.10], not a full-time librarian in every school.
  - Armstrong (medium attribution) said the district is not claiming a "huge dollar amount" in savings from closures [YT:zOUH2Jq7Owo@3:43:36].
  - A $65M gap is projected for next year [NEWS:NEWS-20260708-wweek-1].
- Analysis:
  - Her own test, "a plan that guarantees those things for as many students as possible" [NEWS:NEWS-20260901-katu-2], cannot be met literally.
  - She will have to accept "stabilize, then bridge" in place of guarantees, or press for a written per-school staffing floor. Res. 7307 a.ii already promises "Descriptions of well resourced schools by grade spans" [BB:3997273 p.2], which she could hold staff to.
  - If the 11/17 fiscal statement shows modest savings, her "dreaming about what can be possible again" rationale [YT:jBUIkfQZu-k@0:08:34] gets thinner. Her "sustainable baseline" rationale [YT:AAWpafO4vvQ@3:07:12] *(medium)* survives.
