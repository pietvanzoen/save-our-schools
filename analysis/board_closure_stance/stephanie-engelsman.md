## Bottom line

**Current score (Oct 2026): −2. Range −2 to −1.**

On Oct 6 she called the draft scenarios "mayhem". She has voted against the one resolution that committed PPS to closures (Res. 7296), and she has made lower class sizes a condition of support, which staff say is outside the rightsizing scope. Analysis: a yes vote on Dec 15 is unlikely (roughly 15–20%). The only route her own words leave open is a heavily amended, smaller package. Her Oct 6 remark that she would be "flustered" if the board adopted the plan "without any changes" points the same way.

**Trajectory:**
- Through 2025 she treated closures as a likely fiscal fact (0).
- From Feb to Aug 2026 she was a process-focused skeptic (mostly −1). She engaged on guiding principles and voted for Res. 7307.
- She hardened when the scale jumped to about 20 closures (Sept 1–15) and when the scenarios were released (Oct 6: −2).

## Weak points (checked against PPS data)

**Theme 1, class size.**
- Analysis: her factual reading is correct. Dr. Green's report says the QEM 360-student prototype equals "three classrooms per grade at about 20 students per class" [BB:3997427 p.144](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/3997427?org=915#page=144). PPS's own 10/6 data supports her: Richmond (505 students) averages 28.1 per class and Rosa Parks (161) averages 23.0 [BB:14511742 p.7](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14511742?org=915#page=7). The per-pupil savings of large schools come partly from larger classes.
- The weakness is that her condition is outside what is on the table. PPS answered her directly: "Class size capping is not a part of the rightsizing conversation." [BB:14493920 p.33](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14493920?org=915#page=33) Green calls the QEM an adequacy benchmark that Oregon "has not consistently funded" [BB:3997427 p.136](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/3997427?org=915#page=136).
- Her alternative also costs more. Keeping small schools open means higher per-pupil cost: Rosa Parks $12,870 vs. Richmond $8,222 [BB:14511742 p.7](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14511742?org=915#page=7). That comes on top of a $65M deficit [YT:zOUH2Jq7Owo@3:45:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zOUH2Jq7Owo&t=13542s).
- She has not said how to fund both smaller classes and more buildings. On June 23 she said finding the money is "not my job or our job" [YT:yinJ7KK-Bqc@4:11:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yinJ7KK-Bqc&t=15101s).

**Theme 2, family flight.**
- Analysis: PPS's answer to her own question does not support the strong version of her claim. "while building retention dropped during consolidation, district retention remained stable at over 93%" [BB:14493920 p.22](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14493920?org=915#page=22).

The evidence is mixed, and it does not show large district exits.

Abernethy fell from 520 (2018-19) to 269 (2025-26) without any closure [PPS:pps_net/data-and-reporting/2025-26/10yr_enrollment_history_detail.pdf](https://www.pps.net/fs/resource-manager/view/aefd9869-b3da-4cf6-b9c6-1bec5149eb03).
