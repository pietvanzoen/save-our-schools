## Bottom line

**Current score (Oct 2026): +2 (range +1 to +2).** DePass is the board's most consistent and most expansive supporter of closing schools.
- She voted to make closures a required outcome of the process (Apr 14).
- She asked for a "9.5" out of 10 magnitude (Sep 1).
- She says savings are "not what we're trying to solve for" (Sep 8).
- On Oct 6 (medium attribution) she said "We can't not do something."

Short of a scenario that plainly concentrates harm on the low-income, Black, special-education and multilingual students she calls her "north star", she is very likely to vote to adopt Scenario A or an amended package that still closes schools. She may well move or second it.

**Trajectory:** 2025 Jefferson-capacity defender (−1/0) → Jan–Mar 2026 sequencing critic who assumes consolidation (+1) → April votes making closures mandatory (+2) → Sept–Oct "go big" (+2).

### How to read the scores
- **Score:** what the statement or vote implied at that moment for a Dec 15 vote to adopt a closure package. The scale runs from −2 to +2.
- **Confidence:** both how sure the attribution is and how directly the item bears on a closure vote.
  - Attribution is high unless the item is marked *(attr. medium)*.
  - Medium attributions rest on seating order or context. The evidence is listed in `analysis/people/quotes_all.jsonl` and in the notes below.
  - Low-confidence attributions are excluded from the timeline.
- **Quotes:** all quotes were checked word for word against `sources/youtube/transcripts/`.

## What would move her

Her own words suggest she can be moved only on how the package is built, not on whether to adopt one:
- **Evidence that the burden falls disproportionately on students in poverty, Black students, special-education students or multilingual learners.** This is her stated north star. A finding of that kind in the 11/17 recommendation could lead her to push amendments, such as removing a Title I or recently disrupted school (e.g., Creston, Marysville). She would still be unlikely to vote no.
- **No credible "future state" or receiving-school support plan.** She has asked for both (Oct 6). A thin answer would draw conditions or friendly amendments, not opposition.
- **Transportation data showing large increases in driving or busing.** This conflicts with her climate commitments and could make her prefer certain receiving schools or boundaries.
- **What will not move her:**
  - Cost data: she has said savings are not the point.
  - Crowd size or lobbying: she frames resistance to it as courage.
  - A smaller or phased package: she opposed it on Sep 1. If the final package shrinks toward Scenario B or below, she would probably still vote yes while saying it is too small.
