## Bottom line

**Current (Oct 2026) score: −1 (range −2 to +1).** She says some closures are needed, but she has called the A/B packages "just too much" (14 or 11 closures; 24–27% of K–8 students move). She is most likely to vote yes on a package cut toward Scenario B or below that changes the West-side plan, and most likely to vote no on Scenario A as presented.

**Trajectory:**
- **Mar–May 2026: +1/+2.** She helped design the process and urged a districtwide scope and a quick decision.
- **Sept 1, 2026: −1.** She turned against the scale of the plan.
- **Mid-Sept:** she softened briefly.
- **Oct 6:** she was back at −1, with a new Southwest-specific objection.

**Her voting record runs ahead of her words.** She voted for every rightsizing resolution (7296 on 4/14; 7307 on 5/26), including after she threatened a no vote. She has never asked to defer the work.

## Weak points

1. **The "scalpel" cannot meet the 360 threshold she accepted.**
   - What staff showed on 9/1, for the 45 K-5s:
     - Status quo: eight schools at or above 360 by 2031-32.
     - Five closures: 13.
     - Ten closures: just over half.
     - Fifteen closures: all 30 remaining [YT:5RMfvT-sAPU@1:33:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5RMfvT-sAPU&t=5625s) [YT:5RMfvT-sAPU@1:34:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5RMfvT-sAPU&t=5662s).
   - Analysis: her "5 to 10" baseline would leave roughly half or more of K-5s below a threshold she said the board had "landed". Unless she argues for a lower threshold or a lower share of students in sustainable schools, her scale objection has no stated target.
   - Staff say they modeled 50%, 60% and 70% sustainability targets (meeting summary, `analysis/meetings/2026-10-06_zOUH2Jq7Owo.md`). Analysis: a smaller target is the concrete form a "scalpel" would take, and she has not named one.

Analysis: the slide appears to count every K–8 move, including the boundary changes and K-8 reconfigurations she has supported in principle. It is not only students at closing schools.
