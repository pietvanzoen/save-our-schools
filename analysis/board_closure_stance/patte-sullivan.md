## Bottom line

**Current (Oct 2026) score: +1.5. Range +1 to +2.**

She is very likely to vote to adopt a closure package on Dec 15. She was in favor of every rightsizing step that came to a vote. She hedged only on wording, in April. By October she was telling the crowd that "something needs to be done" [YT:zOUH2Jq7Owo@3:16:09], and had told her own family "we have to do something" [YT:zOUH2Jq7Owo@3:15:02].

She is less firmly committed on **scale** (Scenario A or B) and on **specific features**: K-8 conversions, the loss of the Sunnyside and Buckman focus models, and walkability. She has praised all three, and the scenarios cut against all three. Her likeliest move is to vote yes on an amended package, not to vote for deferral.

**Analysis:** she sits in two different 4–3 coalitions. On bond oversight she votes with Splitt, Engelsman and La Forte [YT:SPwXuNlwP_U@2:43:39]. On rightsizing direction she is with Wang, DePass and Chase-Miller. That is why she is called the swing vote. On closures specifically, though, the record shows a steady pro-action trend, not a swing pattern.

## Weak points

**1. State rescue / ESF.**
- Analysis: the theme mostly *supports* acting, since she does not expect Salem to close the gap. But her picture of the ESF as idle money that could be "sent" to districts is contradicted by what she has been told:
  - CFO Morrison: the fund exists for economic downturns, and one-time dollars are a lesson already "learned" [YT:YfANSrxqAMI@0:37:13].
  - Lobbyist: legislators want to hold it for a downturn [YT:YfANSrxqAMI@0:39:41].
  - On using it for PERS, the IGA presenter said "I think that might be a challenging conversation with the legislature" [YT:U8Cg5voCxXc@0:49:56].
- Her own question about whether one-time money "jeopardize[s] ... getting to stability" undercuts any argument that a state deal should delay closures.
- The risk is narrow: a large ESF or PERS announcement before 12/15 is the one fiscal event that could tempt her toward a smaller package.

**2. "They all look good" and equal schools.**
- Analysis: her anecdotes match PPS data. The 10/6 deck shows small schools can carry large classes (Creston, 198 students, average class 28.3) [BB:14511742 p.7], so a 32-student class at 287-student Maplewood is plausible [BB:3997157 p.1].
- But both scenarios leave many schools below threshold: 33% under A and 43% under B [BB:14505788 p.1].
- In her own west-side region, 30% of Maplewood goes to Rieke. Rieke has 264 students and PPS describes it as "projected below steady enrollment levels" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/rieke.pdf p.3] [BB:3997157 p.1].
- The deck's guardrail promises moves to "higher-quality facilities" [BB:14511742 p.11]. Staff have not shown class sizes for receiving schools; that comes with the 11/17 staffing work [YT:zOUH2Jq7Owo@3:45:39].
- Her case depends on a class-size claim PPS has not yet documented.

**3. Neighborhood plus focus, K-8s and walkability.**
- Analysis: the scenarios run against several preferences she has stated:
  - They end the Sunnyside focus lottery and the Buckman Arts program, the two hybrids she praised [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/sunnyside.pdf p.3] [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/buckman.pdf p.3].
  - They convert seven K-8s [BB:14509719 p.2].
  - They propose "No alternative or focus option school closure", even though she named focus-option draw as a cause of neighborhood-school weakness [BB:14509719 p.2] [YT:SPc05NM0oYs@1:15:43].
