Turning points

  • Mar 10, 2026. The superintendent floats "five to 10 schools". Every director backs a districtwide, all-at-once process.
  • Apr 14. Res 7296 passes 5–2, with La Forte and Engelsman voting no. Their guardrail amendment fails 3–4.
  • May 26. Guiding principles are adopted 7–0. This is the high point of consensus.
  • Sep 1. Staff modeling shows roughly 15–20 closures. Splitt flips to "scalpel", Engelsman hardens, and DePass and Wang push bigger.
  • Oct 6. The scenarios are released (A closes 14, B closes 11). DePass and Sullivan say "do something"; La Forte, Splitt and Engelsman object to specific closures.

Director by director

Shared weak points

  1. No savings number yet. Directors who frame closures as budget relief, and those who demand cost data first (La Forte, Engelsman), are all exposed to the Nov 17 fiscal impact statement. Staff have signaled the savings won't be large.
  2. Equity tests cut in different directions. Six Title I schools close in both scenarios. By Chase-Miller's own share test, A is less concentrated on Title I than B. DePass's "don't re-harm" principle conflicts with closing Marysville and Creston. No per-scenario equity impact analysis (required by Res 7307) has been released.
  3. "Well-resourced" guarantees aren't on offer. Wang, Chase-Miller and Sullivan condition support on librarians, arts and class size. Staff say they can't commit to the same resources, and class-size caps are out of scope.
  4. Thresholds vs. "scalpel". Splitt and La Forte accept the 360/500/1,100 floors but resist the closure counts those floors imply. With 5–10 closures, about half of K-5s stay below 360.
  5. Seismic money at closure schools. Several directors said seismic dollars should go only to schools safe from consolidation. About $18M of completed seismic work sits at closure-list schools, and Rose City Park closes under A.
  6. Home-zone exceptions. Splitt (Southwest) and La Forte (Peninsula, Sabin and Irvington) warned against cherry-picking, then objected to closures in their own areas.

Watch before Dec 15

  • Oct 13. TLE committee (La Forte chairs; Splitt sits on it).
  • Oct 20. Real-estate executive session.
  • Oct 27. Audit committee (Sullivan chairs).
  • Nov 4. Feedback summary. Nov 9/10. Committee sessions.
  • Nov 17. Superintendent recommendation and fiscal impact statement. This is the pivot for La Forte, Splitt and Engelsman. Watch:
    • the closure count;
    • whether Maplewood, Stephenson, Peninsula or Rose City Park come off the list;
    • any added schools;
    • staffing guarantees.
  • Dec 15. Expect school-specific amendments before the main motion. A deferral motion would most likely come from Engelsman.
