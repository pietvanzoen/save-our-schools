## 1. Policy factors (Student Assignment Policy 4.10.045-P)

As presented on March 10 and March 31, 2026, the policy factors are introduced as follows: "The Board recognizes that such factors may conflict with one another, and include, but are not limited to, the following non-prioritized list" [BB:3988980 p.17]. The list reads [BB:3988980 p.17] [BB:3997004 p.19]:
- A. "A feeder pattern that allows as many students as possible to continue together from one school level to the next"
- B. "Student body demographics"
- C. "Compact boundaries that promote safer routes to schools and a sense of community as well as recognize and address natural and human-made barriers"
- D. "Optimal use of existing facilities"
- E. "Program and enrollment stability in the surrounding schools"
- F. "Limiting the impact of boundary changes to the smallest number of students possible"

**April 2026 recasting for public engagement.** The same six factors were relabeled under the heading "Rightsize. Right Conditions." [BB:3997148 p.23]:
- Right Pathways (A);
- Right Mix of Students: "Schools reflect and support the diverse identities of students, including race, language, and background" (B);
- Right Boundaries (C);
- Right Use of Space: "School buildings are used in ways that support strong programs, full classrooms, and access to resources and opportunities for students" (D, reworded from "Optimal use of existing facilities");
- Right Balance (E);
- Right Impact (F).

**Community ranking (Slido).** The poll asked participants to "select the three most important factors to consider during program and enrollment balancing across our system". PPS estimates "up to 1614 community members participated" [BB:3997282 p.4]. The bar chart is image-only. Approximate response counts read from the slide image are:

| Rank | Factor | Approx. responses (read from slide image) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Right Boundaries | ~1,060 |
| 2 | Right Pathways | ~1,020 |
| 3 | Right Balance | ~800 |
| 4 | Right Impact | ~660 |
| 5 | Right Use of Space | ~640 |
| 6 | Right Mix of Students | ~530 |

Source: [BB:3997282 p.4].

**Analysis:** the factor ranked fifth by the public, "Use of Space" (utilization), together with the enrollment thresholds, became the main modeling driver in October. The top-ranked factors were compact boundaries and feeder continuity. In the October deck these appear only indirectly, as "feeder pattern alignment where possible" and the receiving-school cap [BB:14511742 p.11].

## 2. Decision-criteria language before the principles

**March 31 work session.** The board book posed candidate "Decision-Making Criteria" [BB:3988980 p.10] [BB:3988980 p.15]:
- "Data-Driven Thresholds": "Facility utilization", "Proximity to other schools".
- "Contextual Criteria": "Historical investment", "Neighborhood context".
- "Core Values": "Academic continuity", "Equity of access".
- "Policy Requirements".

The peer-district models shown that day were [BB:3988980 p.12] [BB:3988980 p.13] [BB:3988980 p.14]:
- San Antonio: "Drafted Initial Decision Framework: A. Values B. Primary Criteria C. Contextual Criteria".
- Seattle: "Defined “North Star”: What is a Well-Resourced School?" and "Established Guiding Principles and Decision Framework", ending in "Withdrew Recommendation".
- Denver: "Developed Methodology & Applied Criteria".

**Earlier PPS size measures** (March 12 TLE) [BB:3997023 p.12] [BB:3997023 p.13]:
- **DBRAC (2014–17):** "K8 range from 470-800 students", "K5 range from 330-720 students", "Middle Schools - 675 students was the education specification".
- **SEGC (2020–23) "Impact Measures":** "K5 270 + students", "Middle Schools 500 + students", "School utilization > 80%", "Proximity: Students within 1 mile of school", "Number of affected students", "Impact on Black and Native students and multilingual learners".

**La Forte amendment to Res. 7296 (April 14).** The proposed conditions were [BB:3990653 p.1] [BB:3990653 p.2]:
- "A. Decision Criteria and Analysis: The Superintendent shall present to the Board, for approval, clear decision-making criteria prior to developing proposed scenarios."
- "B. Equity Impact: Each scenario shall include a standardized equity impact analysis assessing effects on student access, opportunities, and outcomes, with specific attention to historically underserved students."
- "E. Financial Transparency: all proposals shall include clearly stated assumptions, one-time implementation costs, and ongoing financial impacts, and shall be made public prior to Board action."

The minutes record the motion "to adopt the changes to the proposed amendment" as "passed (4-yes, 3-no, 0-abstain)". The roll call printed beneath it, however, lists three yes votes and four no votes [BB:3997322 p.4]. The same minutes list for future discussion "consideration of the proposed amendment that did not pass" [BB:3997322 p.6]. At the meeting, Chair Wang "asked if the proposed amendment could be moved to be included in the next resolution they adopt on the guiding principles" [BB:3997322 p.3].

**Analysis:** the weight of evidence is that conditions A–F were not adopted in 7296. Of the six:
- **B (equity impact analysis)** was carried into 7307 nearly verbatim (§3).
- **A (Board approval of criteria before scenarios)** was not. On September 15 the decision framework was presented with "No decision is requested today" [BB:14430171 p.3].
- **E (financial transparency)** survives only as a recital about "financial transparency and stewardship" and as an operational-feasibility review that includes "financial impacts" [BB:3997273 p.1] [BB:3997273 p.2].

## 4. Workplan (Res. 7307 Exhibit 1)

**Approach and Analysis** [BB:3997273 p.2]:
- The analysis "will incorporate multiple data sources, including, but not limited to, enrollment trends and projections, program access and parity, student demographics, facility utilization, and financial conditions."
- "PPS will be transparent about the data sources used in its analysis."
- "In parallel, the District will assess the operational feasibility of potential scenarios, including staffing, transportation, facilities, nutrition services, program continuity, and financial impacts, to confirm that proposed strategies are viable and sustainable."
- "Each proposed scenario shall include a standardized equity impact analysis that":
  1. "Assesses the impacts on focal student groups using disaggregated data;"
  2. "Assesses access to specialized programs, including choice schools or programs, and culturally relevant supports, transportation, and proximity"
  3. "Assesses impacts on school communities that have recently been impacted by recent enrollment and program changes as well as Title I school communities."
  4. "Incorporates community input and reflects how feedback informed the recommendation."

**Community Engagement** [BB:3997273 p.2] [BB:3997273 p.3]:
- "PPS will engage the broader community in a structured way to get feedback in multiple phases including guiding principles and scenarios, and the plan for engagement will be shared publicly."
- PPS will engage "historically underserved communities, communities that have recently been impacted by enrollment and program balancing decisions and those who will be most impacted by decisions in this process."

**Implementation Plan** [BB:3997273 p.3]:
- "in a manner that minimizes disruption for students, families, and staff, including an assessment of cohort cohesion". "including an assessment of cohort cohesion" was added after May 12 [BB:3997246 p.3] [BB:3997282 p.7]; the board book calls this "fragmentation language" [BB:3997282 p.7].
- "Implementation will be sequenced based on district priorities, urgency of need, and operational readiness."
- "Staff will present the proposed implementation plan to the Board for final approval."

**Recital C (enrollment forecast).** Res. 7296 said "at least the next five years" [BB:3988127 p.1]. The May 12 draft and Res. 7307 say "at least the next nine years" [BB:3997246 p.1] [BB:3997273 p.1].

## 6. Sustainability thresholds and capacity definitions

### 6.1 The thresholds
- **August 4 memo, first statement:** "We used the threshold of 360 students for elementaries and 500 students for middle schools as guided by the prototype school description in the Quality Education Model. We applied the same number for K-8s and used the 1100 students threshold we developed during the sunsetting of Jefferson dual assignment process." [BB:3997426 p.2]
- **October 6 definition:** "Sustainable Enrollment Range: Minimum enrollment to offer stable programming." "Threshold: minimum enrollment", with "Elementary threshold 360 students", "K8 and Middle School threshold 500 students" and "High School threshold 1100 students" [BB:14511742 p.10].
- **Staff, September Q&A:** "We are defining sustainable schools as an elementary school with stable enrollment of 360 or more." Staff also said the thresholds "do not speak to specific class size" and quoted the audit that they "should be understood as planning floors" [BB:14493920 p.23] [BB:14493920 p.27].
- **What the equity audit recommended.** The Green & Hanson audit proposed higher planning floors that add "a five-year attrition buffer to the QEM adequacy benchmark" [BB:3997427 p.145]:
  - elementary "Approximately 450+";
  - middle "Approximately 600+";
  - K–8 "Approximately 650+", as "a local planning estimate" because "QEM does not provide a separate K–8 prototype" [BB:3997427 p.145] [BB:3997427 p.146].
- **The audit's caveat:** "these numbers should not be used as automatic closure thresholds" [BB:3997427 p.145]. It also warned that "a 650 student K–8 school is not equivalent to a 600-student dedicated middle school" [BB:3997427 p.145].

**Analysis:** PPS kept the unbuffered QEM prototypes (360/500). It applies them to 2031–32 projections, which plays the role of the audit's five-year buffer. It also applied the middle-school number (500) to K-8s, which the audit's K-8 discussion argues against. The audit's own 650 K-8 figure assumes about 72 students per grade. A K-8 at 500 would average about 56 per grade, roughly two sections per grade. That conflicts with the "6-8 sections per grade" middle-grades description on [BB:14374370 p.7]. This is arithmetic from the cited figures, not a PPS statement.

### 6.2 Capacity and utilization definitions
- **Functional capacity:** "A practical estimate of how many students a school site accommodates." [BB:14511742 p.10]
- **Q&A detail.** Gross capacity uses classroom size bands: "Classrooms less than 800 SF have a student capacity of 24; classrooms greater than 800 SF and less than 1,000 SF have a student capacity of 27; classrooms greater than 1,000 SF have a student capacity of 30." [BB:14493920 p.26]
- **Efficient utilization:** "PPS defines Efficient Utilization as a school operating between 70% and 100% of its Functional Capacity." For K-5 and K-8 schools, "utilization rate reductions" apply for Title I, TSI or CSI, neighborhood K-8 and co-located neighborhood/immersion programs [BB:14493920 p.26].
- **How the utilization definition moved:**
  - April 11 deck: "Utilization = enrollment/functional capacity" [BB:3997148 p.17].
  - April 20 deck: "enrollment/capacity" [BB:3997156 p.19]. This is the version recorded in `rightsizing.md` §5.
  - Staff later: "The utilization model is a system-level planning measure and may not reflect how individual rooms are currently being used or current class sizes." [BB:14493920 p.31]
- **Capacity against the thresholds:** "There are no current K-5 buildings with a functional capacity below 360." Below-360 K-8 or other sites are "East Sylvan, Skyline, Winterhaven, and Terwilliger"; "There are no middle schools with a functional capacity under 500. The smallest is Gray with 588 then George at 623." [BB:14493920 p.32] (But see open questions: the April 20 table lists Creston's functional capacity as 357.)

## 7. Decision framework and KPIs (September 15)

Framing: "Show how the Guiding Principles translate into measurable KPIs", and "No decision is requested today." [BB:14430171 p.2] [BB:14430171 p.3] The flow runs "Guiding Principles → Decision Framework → Scenario Analysis → Community Input → Recommendation" [BB:14430171 p.11].

**Questions applied to each scenario:** "What are the impacts?", "Who experiences the benefit?", "Who experiences the burden?", "What trade-offs emerge?", and "Will the changes create stronger and more equitable learning environments?" [BB:14430171 p.12]

**Impact dimensions** [BB:14430171 p.13]:
- "Magnitude": "How many people, schools, and communities are affected".
- "Intensity": "How significant the impact is for an individual or a community".
- "Complexity": "How many intersecting factors make a transition harder to navigate or mitigate".

**KPI families.** The October deck labels these "Physical Environment and Pathway Indicators", "Balance and Impact" and "Opportunity and Stability" [BB:14511742 p.15]. On September 15 they were defined as:

| Family | Indicators (verbatim) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Opportunity and Stability | "1. Percent of schools at a grade level with enrollment above threshold" · "2. Percent of focal students enrolled in schools above the threshold" · "3. Range of enrollment (largest - smallest) by grade level" | [BB:14430171 p.14] |
| Physical Environment and Pathway | "1. Percent of efficiently utilized schools" · "2. Percent of students living within 1.0 and 1.5 miles of school" · "3. Number of schools with split feeders" · "4. Percent of middle school feeders moving to the same high school" | [BB:14430171 p.17] |
| Balance and Impact (for scenarios) | "1. Percent of focal students who will experience the change" · "2. Number of Title I and CSI/TSI schools impacted by the change" · "3. Number of students moving to a new school or site" | [BB:14430171 p.20] |

Focal students for the KPIs: "Focal students include students experiencing poverty, historically underserved by race, students receiving special education services, and multilingual learners." [BB:14493920 p.33] **Analysis:** this list of four groups differs from Res. 7307's five named groups (Native, Black, multilingual, SpEd, poverty). The KPI uses a combined "historically underserved by race" category instead of naming Native and Black students separately.

**Equity checkpoints**, under the heading "Equity is Part of the Analysis - not a Final Check" [BB:14430171 p.7]:
- Workgroups and Criteria → "Disaggregated benefit and burden analysis".
- Scenarios and Clusters → "An equity analysis review attached to every scenario".
- Readiness and Decision → "Required mitigation and an Equity Impact Statement".
- Implementation → "Disaggregated monitoring and course correction".

"Every checkpoint works through the same five sections": "People Opportunity Voice Systems Action" [BB:14430171 p.7]. Earlier, the August 4 deck had promised "Narrative for Each Scenario" and that PPS "Will compare equity impact statements for each scenario." [BB:3997425 p.15]

**Precedent shown, "What We Learned from Jefferson is Rising"** (read from slide image) [BB:14430171 p.21]. The Jefferson KPIs carried explicit targets:
- "% of High Schools in each scenario with enroll above 1100 students (target = 100%)";
- "HS Enrollment Range (target = <350)";
- "% MS feeders moving to the same HS (target = 100%)".

**Analysis:** the September 15 rightsizing KPIs carry no numeric targets.

## 8. Criteria actually applied to build the October scenarios

**Guiding question:** "How can we increase the number of school sites that operate in a sustainable enrollment range, so that students across our district can experience stable and adequately resourced programs while also using our buildings efficiently?" [BB:14511742 p.10]

**Four criteria** [BB:14511742 p.11]:
1. "Enrollment Trends & Capacity: Evaluated projected enrollment patterns and capacity by grade band through 2031-32."
2. "Facility & Capital Viability: Assessed facility condition alongside enrollment to identify viability of site."
3. "Programmatic & Grade-Band Optimization: Examined K-8 to K-5/6-8 conversions and specialized programs during regional analyses to strengthen middle school cohorts and DLI pathways as well as feeder pattern alignment where possible."
4. "Student & Operational Guardrails: Capped receiving schools at no more than two per closure. Ensured students transition to higher-quality facilities."

**The memo's version:** "criteria focused on enrollment sufficient to sustain stable, adequately resourced programming, effective use of buildings, and keeping school communities together through change as much as possible." It adds that "Criteria were weighed against one another rather than applied in a fixed ranking, with the balance differing by regional conditions." [BB:14509719 p.1] It also names "functional capacity, facility conditions and planned improvements, geographic access, and existing feeder patterns" [BB:14509719 p.2].

**Held constant ("What STAYED THE SAME")** [BB:14511742 p.13]:
- "Number of Available Seats in: Kindergarten DLI Programs";
- "ACCESS Alternative Program";
- "Benson and da Vinci focus option schools";
- "All 5 languages sustained in Dual Language Immersion (DLI) Programs";
- "Schools or programs with specific entrance requirements (DART, Pioneer, Alliance)".

The memo adds: "No alternative or focus option school closure is proposed." [BB:14509719 p.2] (But see open questions: Buckman, listed as a focus-option K5 in June [BB:3997345 p.6], closes. Sunnyside's focus option is discontinued, and MLC 9–12 closes.)

**Facility data used:** staff list "building condition and reliability, environmental health, life safety, accessibility, educational suitability, safe access to school, functional capacity, and utilization". They state "we do not have a single current building-condition report that represents the full analysis that is available for public review" and that they are "intentionally looking at these factors together rather than using a single facility score" [BB:14493920 p.16].

**Which KPIs were reported in October.** The scenario release reports [BB:14505788 p.1] [BB:14511742 p.44]:
- Opportunity & Stability indicator 1 (% of schools above threshold);
- indicator 2 (% of focal students in sustainable schools, as percentages);
- Balance & Impact indicators 1 and 3 (K-8 students who move, numbers and percentages).

**Analysis:** the following were not reported:
- the Physical Environment & Pathway indicators (efficient utilization, distance within 1.0/1.5 miles, split feeders, % of middle schools feeding a single high school);
- the "Number of Title I and CSI/TSI schools impacted";
- the 2031–32 enrollment range by grade level.

See `rightsizing_open_questions.md`.
