# Rightsizing: open questions, contradictions and data gaps

As of 2026-10-10. This file covers contradictions between PPS documents, numbers that do not reconcile, questions PPS has not answered, data gaps in the archive, and claims to check against meeting video.

Companion files: `rightsizing.md`, `rightsizing_criteria_and_principles.md`, `../data/rightsizing_scenarios.csv`, `../data/school_metrics.csv`. For facility and seismic overlaps, see `bond_and_seismic.md` and `bond_seismic_open_questions.md`; those are not repeated here except where they bear on the scenarios.

**Analysis:** marks my own arithmetic or inference. All other statements cite the document.

## A. Numbers that do not reconcile

1. **Scenario B "65 Schools/Programs" is probably an error, and the denominators behind the percentages are unclear.**
   - **What the deck says.** The October 6 deck gives Scenario A "Total Schools Remaining Open" as 68 and adds "Scenario B: 65 Schools/Programs. Lewis, Stephenson and Rose City Park remain open." [BB:14511742 p.20] The starting point is "80 schools 2 programs" [BB:14511742 p.19].
   - **Analysis, the 65.** 82 − 11 = 71 for B. The four regional rows for Scenario A (16 + 15 + 13 + 21) sum to 65, so the B figure looks like A's regional subtotal, excluding the three citywide schools.
   - **Analysis, the percentages.** "Schools above the sustainability threshold" is 67% (A) and 57% (B) [BB:14505788 p.1]. Neither can be reproduced exactly with 68 or 71 schools: 45/68 = 66.2%, 46/68 = 67.6%, 40/71 = 56.3%, 41/71 = 57.7%. The status quo 26% does reproduce as 21/82. PPS should publish the count of schools above threshold and the denominator for each scenario. This was also requested by a Board member: "Please provide actual number of school sites, not just percentages" [BB:14493920 p.33].

2. **Number of school sites.** The September 1 deck says sites changed by "+2 to 83" since 2018-19 [BB:14357211 p.9]. The October deck says "80 schools 2 programs" (82) [BB:14511742 p.19]. The August memo says "more than 80 school sites" [BB:3997426 p.2]. The difference of one site is unexplained; candidates include an admin or alternative site, or counting Odyssey or MLC 9–12 separately.

3. **K-8 count.** The median and range table, used March–April, counts "K-8 11" schools [BB:3997004 p.7] [BB:3988980 p.5]. The August projection charts, the September KPI counts (2 Title I + 11 non-Title I) and the October current-state table all use 13 K-8s [BB:3997425 p.16] [BB:14493920 p.33] [BB:14511742 p.19]. **Analysis:** the 11 probably excludes focus-option or program K-8s such as Odyssey, Winterhaven or ACCESS. PPS has not said which.

4. **"69% of K8 ... 100 students or more below"** The August 4 memo says "69% of K8 and 47% of middle schools falling 100 students or more below the enrollment for stable programming by 2031-32" [BB:3997426 p.2]. The same day's chart (read from slide image) shows 2031–32 K-8 enrollment bands of 7 schools "less than 400", 2 at 400–449, 2 at 450–499, 1 at 500–549 and 1 at 550+ [BB:3997425 p.16]. **Analysis:** 100+ below 500 means under 400, which is 7 of 13 (54%). 69% = 9 of 13 corresponds to schools under 450. The middle-school figure of 47% matches the chart (7 of 15 under 400 [BB:3997425 p.17]). The K-8 figure appears overstated, or uses a different cut.

5. **Enrollment decline, stated four ways:**
   - "−12%", 2018–19 to 2025–26 [BB:3988980 p.6]. Several decks print "12%" with no sign [BB:3997004 p.6] [BB:3997148 p.6].
   - "declined 12.2%" "Since 2019-20" [BB:3997181 p.14].
   - "When enrollment declined 13%" [BB:3990695 p.1].
   - "-6086" since 2018-19 [BB:14357211 p.9], against PRC's "just over 6,000 students below the pre-pandemic baseline of 2019-20" [BB:14493920 p.15].

   The base years differ (2018–19 vs 2019–20). The decline also differs by span: the budget deck's "13%" against −12%/−12.2%.

6. **Projected decline to 2031–32.** The September 1 deck: "-3446 Enrollment Decrease Projected in 2031-32" [BB:14357211 p.9]. The forecast deck figure: "−12%" from 2025–26 to 2035–36 [BB:3988980 p.6]. As of 9/11/2026, kindergarten was "running 115 students below the baseline projections" and total K-12 was "about 700 students below last year's official October counts" [BB:14493920 p.32].
   - **Open:** do the October scenarios use the July 24, 2026 PRC forecast [BB:14493920 p.28] or a newer base? The maps carry "Rightsizing Model 2026.09.24" and "Version: 10/5/2026 12:58 PM" [BB:14505796 p.1].
   - **Open:** will the October 2026 count be folded in before the November recommendation?

7. **Functional capacity statements conflict with the April table.**
   - **Creston.** Staff wrote: "There are no current K-5 buildings with a functional capacity below 360." [BB:14493920 p.32] The April 20 table lists Creston E.S. at 213 enrolled, functional capacity 357, 60% [BB:3997157 p.1].
   - **Sunnyside.** Staff said "Sunnyside is just below 500 around 490" [BB:14493920 p.32]. The April table lists Sunnyside Environmental at 485 enrolled against 546 [BB:3997157 p.1].
   - **Why it matters.** Sunnyside becomes a K-5 that receives about 50% of Buckman [BB:14509719 p.3]. Its usable capacity therefore affects receiving-school readiness.

8. **Utilization definition changed between engagement sessions.** April 11: "Utilization = enrollment/functional capacity" [BB:3997148 p.17]. April 20: "Utilization = enrollment/capacity" [BB:3997156 p.19]. September Q&A: functional capacity applies reductions for Title I, TSI/CSI, neighborhood K-8 and co-located programs [BB:14493920 p.26]. A Board member asked how Creston, with every classroom occupied, shows low utilization [BB:14493920 p.31]. **Open:** which capacity figure was used in the October modeling?

9. **Program-move count may double-count Chávez 6–8.**
   - "Chavez 6–8 and Ockley Green MS to George MS" is listed as a Spanish program move, and "Chavez 6-8 to George" is also one of the 8 grade-level changes [BB:14505788 p.2].
   - **Analysis:** the 13 program moves in A count Beach, James John and Sitton separately (three), plus Chávez 6–8 and Ockley Green separately. The headline counts depend on these conventions. A plain-language count would be: 14 closures, 7 K-8 → K-5 conversions, MLC 9-12 closing and MLC becoming K-8, and Odyssey relocating.

10. **Regional membership is inconsistent across documents.**
    - Harrison Park MS appears in the Grant/McDaniel boundary list [BB:14505791 p.2]. Bridger's 6–8 move to Harrison Park is listed under Cleveland/Franklin [BB:14505790 p.2].
    - Mt. Tabor appears in the Cleveland/Franklin boundary list [BB:14505790 p.2]. Laurelhurst's 6–8 move to Mt. Tabor is listed under Grant/McDaniel [BB:14505791 p.2].
    - The memo describes the Clark Chinese immersion move to Woodstock in both the Grant/McDaniel and the Cleveland/Franklin paragraphs [BB:14509719 p.2] [BB:14509719 p.3].
    - **Analysis:** because of this overlap, the regional percentages ([BB:14505790 p.1], etc.) cannot be recomputed without PPS's school-to-region assignment list.

11. **Regional "schools remaining open" ([BB:14511742 p.20]) vs. current state ([BB:14511742 p.19]).**
    - West: 18 → 16 under A (−2, Maplewood and Stephenson).
    - North: 19 → 15 (−4).
    - NE: 15 → 13 (−2).
    - SE: 27 → 21 (−6).
    - **Analysis:** consistent with 14 closures. West does not subtract the MLC 9–12 closure, consistent with "It is not counted as a school closure" [BB:14505788 p.2].

## B. Statements that conflict with each other

12. **Was the La Forte amendment to Res. 7296 adopted?** The April 14 minutes record the motion "to adopt the changes to the proposed amendment" as "passed (4-yes, 3-no, 0-abstain)". The printed roll call is Chase-Miller No, DePass No, Engelsman Yes, La Forte Yes, Splitt No, Sullivan Yes, Wang No, which is 3–4 [BB:3997322 p.4]. Two other passages suggest it failed:
    - Future agenda items include "consideration of the proposed amendment that did not pass" [BB:3997322 p.6].
    - The October deck quotes the 7296 resolve clause without the amendment's "subject to the following" conditions [BB:14511742 p.4].

    The "will" → "may" vote is recorded as "failed (2-yes, 5-no, 1-abstain)", eight votes for seven directors. The roll call shows no abstention [BB:3997322 p.4].
    - **Open:** the final adopted text of Res. 7296 is not in the archive. Check the April 14 video ([MTG:2026-04-14/739410], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH3wdS1A3sM) and the June 9 adopted minutes (Res. 7322).

13. **"No alternative or focus option school closure is proposed" [BB:14509719 p.2].** Against this:
    - Buckman, listed as a "Focus Option" K5 school in June [BB:3997345 p.6] and as "Neighborhood / Focus Option" in the April table [BB:3997157 p.1], closes in both scenarios [BB:14505788 p.2].
    - Sunnyside's environmental focus option "would be discontinued" [BB:14509719 p.2].
    - The MLC 9–12 alternative program closes, "not counted as a school closure" [BB:14505788 p.2].
    - **Analysis:** the memo's statement is defensible only if Buckman is treated as a neighborhood school with a focus strand. Ask PPS to reconcile.

14. **SEGC outcomes are framed differently.**
    - **August memo:** impacted students "had similar academic and disciplinary outcomes to peers, this did not match how families experienced the changes, especially in middle school". It also cites "major staffing disruptions especially for staff of color" [BB:3997426 p.1].
    - **Staff in the Q&A:** the audit "found no significant negative changes in student outcomes, and modest positive gains for several student groups" [BB:14493920 p.29].
    - **The audit itself:** SEGC projections "overestimated enrollment at 12 of 20 schools" [BB:3997427 p.6].
    - **Analysis:** the Q&A summary omits the experiential and staffing harms and the forecasting error. The forecasting error is directly relevant to trusting the 2031–32 projections.

15. **Focal-student definitions differ.**
    - Res. 7307 names "Native students, Black students, multilingual learners, students receiving special education services, and students experiencing poverty" [BB:3997273 p.2].
    - The KPIs use four groups, with "historically underserved by race" as a single category [BB:14493920 p.33] [BB:14505788 p.1].
    - **Open:** no scenario result is reported separately for Native or Black students.

16. **The timeline moved earlier and later.**
    - **Recommendation date:**
      - November 2025: rightsizing "Study + Recommend" in the "2026-27 School Year" [BB:3996734 p.20].
      - March 10, 2026 deck: "Share Rec. November", "Vote December 2026" [BB:3997004 p.20].
      - March 10 minutes: Superintendent "aiming for a final recommendation by October to allow for a December vote" [BB:3990212 p.4]. Verify on video ([MTG:2026-03-10/734436], https://youtu.be/qiCQKmMdLr4).
      - Now: "mid-November" / 11/17 [BB:14509719 p.1] [BB:14511742 p.45].
    - **Scenario engagement:** planned for "Summer 2026" [BB:14357211 p.7]; actually October 9–29 [BB:14511742 p.46].
    - **Analysis:** the public has about 3.5 weeks of scenario engagement before the feedback summary (11/04), then about 4 weeks between recommendation (11/17) and vote (12/15). Implementation begins in SY 2027-28 [BB:14505796 p.1].

## C. Promised items not in the released materials

17. **Cost and savings.**
    - **Required:** La Forte's proposed condition E required "clearly stated assumptions, one-time implementation costs, and ongoing financial impacts ... made public prior to Board action" [BB:3990653 p.2]. Res. 7307 requires operational-feasibility review including "financial impacts" [BB:3997273 p.2].
    - **The Board question.** A director asked "(1) what is the estimated cost savings to closing a school?". The written response answers only the layoffs question and begins "2. The number of potential layoffs can not be determined at this time" [BB:14421589 p.4]. In the October 1 version the "2." is dropped, and there is still no answer to (1) [BB:14493920 p.7].
    - **Another unanswered request:** "how much will we save, per school closed, to close a school? ***** we need to have a number for this, even if it's not a lot." [BB:14493920 p.5]
    - **What exists instead:** the only cost evidence released with the scenarios is per-pupil formula cost and a 10-school "subsidy" sample [BB:14511742 p.6] [BB:14511742 p.7]. The only closure-specific savings figure in the archive is MLC 9–12 ($1.1M projected) [BB:3996961 p.13].
    - **Context:** the 2027–28 budget plan already assumes "($65.2M) Ongoing structural savings" [BB:3997181 p.50].
    - **Open:** how much of that is expected from rightsizing?

18. **Equity impact analysis per scenario.**
    - Res. 7307: "Each proposed scenario shall include a standardized equity impact analysis" covering four elements [BB:3997273 p.2].
    - September 15 promised "An equity analysis review attached to every scenario" [BB:14430171 p.7]. August 4 promised to "compare equity impact statements for each scenario" [BB:3997425 p.15].
    - The October release has only aggregate focal-group percentages and K-8 mover counts [BB:14505788 p.1] [BB:14511742 p.44]. The memo refers to "equity impact findings" to be considered later [BB:14509719 p.4].
    - **Open:** none of the following has been published:
      - the narrative analysis;
      - the access-to-programs assessment;
      - the impact on "recently impacted" (SEGC) and Title I communities;
      - the "Number of Title I and CSI/TSI schools impacted" KPI [BB:14430171 p.20].
    - **Analysis:** 6 of the 14 Scenario A closures are Title I schools in the April table: Beach, Buckman, James John, Marysville, Peninsula and Woodmere [BB:3997157 p.1]. Several affected schools went through SEGC changes and are profiled in the school-by-school analysis: Bridger [BB:3997428 p.11], Clark [BB:3997428 p.23], Lent [BB:3997428 p.34], Marysville [BB:3997428 p.48], Lewis [BB:3997428 p.66], Harrison Park [BB:3997428 p.83] and Kelly [BB:3997428 p.119]. Arleta and Creston are discussed in the audit [BB:3997427 p.82] [BB:3997427 p.87].

19. **Physical-environment KPIs not reported.** These were defined on September 15 [BB:14430171 p.17] but not reported for the scenarios:
    - "Percent of efficiently utilized schools";
    - "Percent of students living within 1.0 and 1.5 miles of school";
    - "Number of schools with split feeders";
    - "Percent of middle school feeders moving to the same high school".

    Staff had also promised "similar visuals showing the density of students ... with circles indicating a one mile (elementary) and 1.5 mile (for middle schools) radius" [BB:14493920 p.25].

20. **Per-school projections.**
    - Staff gave the Board "historical, current, and projected enrollment by school" [BB:14493920 p.33]. It is not in the archive, and the October release has no per-school 2031–32 projection under any scenario.
    - Staff also said "These indicators will be for the scenarios and we will include the number as well as the percentage" [BB:14493920 p.33]. The October focal-group KPIs are percentages only [BB:14505788 p.1].

21. **Transportation.** There are no ride-time, distance or cost estimates. PPS states that transportation "will require further implementation planning" [BB:14509719 p.2]. **Analysis:** some proposals plainly lengthen trips:
    - Skyline's high school assignment changes "from Lincoln to Roosevelt" [BB:14509719 p.2].
    - Spanish DLI students from Beach and James John consolidate at César Chávez [BB:14509719 p.3].
    - The memo concedes that "consolidating an underenrolled immersion program may lengthen some students’ trips" [BB:14509719 p.1].

22. **Building condition and disposition.**
    - Staff: "we do not have a single current building-condition report that represents the full analysis that is available for public review" [BB:14493920 p.16].
    - The equity audit recommends disposition plans "decided before closure votes, not after"; PPS says future use "is still to be determined" [BB:14493920 p.29] [BB:14493920 p.23].

      - Will a repurposing plan for East Sylvan and the closed sites precede the December vote?

In order to offer more, the status quo would not be able to stay the same" [BB:14493920 p.24]. **Open:** no staffing model for the post-consolidation schools has been published, such as sections per grade, FTE, or specialists per school.

## D. Why particular schools are on the list (unexplained selections)

27. **Schools at or above their threshold today that close.** In 2025–26, James John (365), Rose City Park (417) and Sellwood MS (564) are at or above their threshold [BB:3997157 p.1]. The memo gives a reason only for Rose City Park ("facility concerns") [BB:14509719 p.3]. Sellwood is the only middle school closed. The September 1 "magnitude" chart showed only 2 of 15 middle schools at 500+ by 2031–32 even with 0 closures [BB:14357211 p.12]. **Open:** per-school 2031–32 projections for Sellwood, Hosford and Brentwood.

28. **Why Rose City Park, Stephenson and Lewis are the swing schools between A and B.** All three are non-Title I [BB:3997157 p.1]. The A-vs-B difference is 3 closures for +10 points of schools above threshold (67% vs 57%) and +7 points of students in sustainable schools (80% vs 73%) [BB:14505788 p.1]. **Open:** the per-school projections that produce these deltas.

29. **K-8 conversions.**
    - Seven K-8s become K-5, including Faubion (600 enrolled; 84% utilized) and Laurelhurst (610; 93%) [BB:3997157 p.1].
    - The rationale is to "strengthen middle school cohorts" [BB:14511742 p.11]. Board members raised "the elimination of K-8 models" in March [BB:3990212 p.4].
    - The audit warned that K-8s are "not equivalent" to dedicated middle schools and suggested a ~650 K-8 floor [BB:3997427 p.145].
    - **Open:** why Astor and Skyline remain K-8 [BB:14505796 p.1], and what their 2031–32 projections are. Skyline: 207 enrolled, functional capacity 285 [BB:3997157 p.1].
