# Phase I rightsizing engagement (spring 2026): what PPS posted in October

Written 2026-10-22. Companion to `rightsizing.md` §3.4 and `rightsizing_criteria_and_principles.md` §1.
Citation formats follow `CLAUDE.md`. Labels used:
- **[Doc]**: a PPS document says this.
- **[Said]**: a person said this on the record.
- **Analysis:** my own inference or arithmetic.

Most of these PDFs hold their charts only as images. Every number marked "(chart, approx.)" was read from a 100-dpi rendering of the page by measuring bar length against the axis. Treat these numbers as ±3% of the axis range. Members of the public are not named. No child is named in any of the documents.

## 3. Document-by-document

### 3.1 Student Voice Advisory Council, April 9 (Slido, 20 students)
- **What helped students through past changes:** relationships with family, familiar peers and supportive teachers, plus knowing their peers faced the same transition [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-09_svac_engagement.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-09_svac_engagement.pdf).
- **What makes a student successful:** belonging (40%) and strong teacher relationships (30%) rank highest. Classes and programs got 15%, friends 10%, "being close to home" 5% and safety 0% [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-09_svac_engagement.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-09_svac_engagement.pdf).
- **Programs versus friends and distance:** asked to choose, students picked programs and opportunities over staying with friends 62% to 38%. They picked a school with more resources and classes over one closer to home 95% to 5% [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-09_svac_engagement.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-09_svac_engagement.pdf).
- **What adults should never forget:** the daily impact on students, and the need to protect "specialized classes (like dual language immersion and international baccalaureate programming)" and electives [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-09_svac_engagement.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-09_svac_engagement.pdf).
- **What would feel unfair:** splitting up communities through the loss of trusted teachers, friends and staff "who represent diverse backgrounds" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-09_svac_engagement.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-09_svac_engagement.pdf).

### 3.2 Board session at Kellogg Middle School, April 11 (in person, Slido)
- **Who came:** most represented were Creston, Lee and Chief Joseph. More than 40 school communities attended [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-11_board_engagement_kellogg.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-11_board_engagement_kellogg.pdf).
- **Policy factors:** compact boundaries, pathways and balance ranked above impact, demographics and facility use. From the chart (approx.): Boundaries ~87, Pathways ~75, Balance ~59, Impact ~42, Use of Space ~37, Mix ~33 [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-11_board_engagement_kellogg.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-11_board_engagement_kellogg.pdf).
- **Positive school environment:**
  - The text says respondents chose variety of learning opportunities, warm and welcoming, learning needs, and "school diversity over" proximity and walk/bike [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-11_board_engagement_kellogg.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-11_board_engagement_kellogg.pdf).
  - The bar chart counts (approx.) put proximity (~53) and walk/bike (~50) *above* classroom diversity (~46) [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-11_board_engagement_kellogg.pdf p.2](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-11_board_engagement_kellogg.pdf).
  - The 111-response Slido export already in the archive gives weighted ranking scores. There, diversity (1.29) is just above proximity (1.19) and walk/bike (1.03) [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-04_engagement_poll_results.pdf p.32](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-04_engagement_poll_results.pdf). **Analysis:** the text appears to follow the weighted score and the chart the raw counts. The export appears to be from this session (same response count; Creston, Lee and Chief Joseph prominent).
- **Transitions:** what matters is staying with neighborhood friends and peer cohorts. Principal turnover causes stress. Families who changed schools did so for programs (arts, STEM, immersion), special-needs support, safety, or housing moves [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-11_board_engagement_kellogg.pdf p.2](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-11_board_engagement_kellogg.pdf).
- **Equity and closures:**
  - Families made a "strong demand for an equity-centered approach that prioritizes Title 1 schools" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-11_board_engagement_kellogg.pdf p.2](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-11_board_engagement_kellogg.pdf).
  - They worried "consolidation will destroy the social and academic safety nets" and named Creston, Chief Joseph, Lent and Buckman as hubs [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-11_board_engagement_kellogg.pdf p.3](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-11_board_engagement_kellogg.pdf).
- **Who gets heard:** meetings are "dominated by loudest voices (typically white, affluent parents)". Participants asked leaders to go to school buildings instead [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-11_board_engagement_kellogg.pdf p.3](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-11_board_engagement_kellogg.pdf).

### 3.3 Village Table, April 15 (Faubion; 9–15 responses)
- **Policy factors** (votes, read from the chart): Boundaries 11, Mix of Students 8, Pathways 7, Balance 5, Use of Space 4, Impact 1 [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-15_village_table_engagement.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-15_village_table_engagement.pdf). Demographics ranked **second**, the highest of any session.
- **Positive environment:** variety (11), learning needs (10), learning alongside students from different backgrounds (7), then warm and welcoming and walk/bike (5 each). Proximity got about 2 [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-15_village_table_engagement.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-15_village_table_engagement.pdf) [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-15_village_table_engagement.pdf p.2](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-15_village_table_engagement.pdf).
- **Transitions** (eight families): keep classmates and consistent staff, including dedicated special education teachers. Keep siblings and cohorts together [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-15_village_table_engagement.pdf p.2](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-15_village_table_engagement.pdf).
- **What leaders should understand:**
  - Look "beyond budget numbers" to the impact on marginalized families [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-15_village_table_engagement.pdf p.2](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-15_village_table_engagement.pdf).
  - Protect neighborhood programs "already been weakened by previous transitions" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-15_village_table_engagement.pdf p.2](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-15_village_table_engagement.pdf).
  - Explain the reasons for decisions [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-15_village_table_engagement.pdf p.2](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-15_village_table_engagement.pdf).

### 3.4 Virtual Board session, April 20 (Slido, 69–169 responses)
- **Who answered:** most represented were Winterhaven, Odyssey, Creston and Peninsula. 45% reported a TAG-identified student [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-20_virtual_engagement_summary.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-20_virtual_engagement_summary.pdf).
- **Transitions:** keep peer cohorts and stable staff. Families also valued transparency about classroom changes, "walkable neighborhood connections" and welcoming programs [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-20_virtual_engagement_summary.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-20_virtual_engagement_summary.pdf).
- **Reasons for moving:** families moved for academic fit (TAG at ACCESS, STEM at Winterhaven), for unmet learning needs, or to escape classroom safety problems. Some sought "the stability and continuity of K-8 models" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-20_virtual_engagement_summary.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-20_virtual_engagement_summary.pdf).
- **Gap:** "Due to time constraints" the two ranking questions were not asked. That data "will be analyzed at a later date" from the feedback form [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-20_virtual_engagement_summary.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-20_virtual_engagement_summary.pdf). No such analysis was posted.

### 3.5 Board session at Ida B. Wells, April 22 (106–205 responses; the largest in-person session)

## 4. Methods and After-Action Review [Doc]

**What Phase I was for.** Phase I ran "from April to June of 2026" under "Listening and Learning". It was meant to hear concerns and "identify guiding principles to shape the updated Student Assignment Policy and guide the development of rightsizing scenarios" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf).

**What PPS presents as working:**
- The sessions were built "that would systematically collect data from and about engagement participants" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf).
- There were four channels: session notes, the Slido poll (sent afterward to all families, with paper translations on request), the FACE feedback form (227 responses, a "different data source"), and culturally specific outreach [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf p.2](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf).
- The Slido questions were adapted from the 2015 Oregon Kitchen Table survey and the Student Assignment Policy. The twelve questions are reproduced in Appendix A [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf p.2](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf) [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf p.4](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf) [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf p.5](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf).
- **How data informed policy:** summaries were posted after each session and a cross-session memo was written. The information "was used to update the Student Assignment Policy and identify the guiding principles for Phase 2" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf p.3](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf).
- **SEGC equity audit:** PPS partnered with Dr. Terrance Green. The audit drew on more than 1,200 survey responses, 50 focus groups and 10 interviews. Its insights "helped to drive decision-making" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf) [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf p.3](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf). See `segc_equity_audit.md`.

**What did not work (PPS's own account):**
- Respondents were "majority white and parents of elementary-aged students ... disproportionately represented when compared with the district's overall demographics" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf p.3](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf). TAG families were also over-represented [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf p.2](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf).
- "Black and Native voices were underrepresented". In response, PPS planned events for Black families (June 11) and Native families (June 15) [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf p.3](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf).
- Several polls and forms produced data that could not easily be combined [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf p.3](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf).
- The synthesis memo adds that no non-English data had reached the analysts and that per-session participant counts were unavailable [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/slido_and_listening_session_synthesis.pdf p.2](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/slido_and_listening_session_synthesis.pdf).

**Planned changes for Phase II** [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf p.3](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf):
- "identify sampling thresholds for stakeholders" so PPS knows "when we have heard from a representative sample";
- sampling and outreach strategies to reach under-represented voices;
- "protocols to supplement survey data collection with focus groups and other forms of data collection when needed";
- "shared data collection platforms used by all PPS stakeholders to collect standardized data regardless of the session attended."

**Analysis: follow-through so far.**
- The first Phase II recap reports 1,132 survey completions [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-10-05_to_12_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-10-05_to_12_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf). Respondents were 64.01% white and 2.84% Black [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-10-05_to_12_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf p.3](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-10-05_to_12_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf). PPS gives the district as 53% white [BB:14576326 p.7](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14576326?org=915#page=7).
- The recap does not state any sampling threshold, and does not say whether one was met.

## 6. How Phase I input compares with the scenarios

**Analysis throughout this section.** It draws on `BRIEFING.md`, `rightsizing.md` §4 and `rightsizing_criteria_and_principles.md` §§1, 6 and 8.

**1. Questions Phase I never asked.**
- The twelve Slido questions did not ask about any of the following [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf p.4](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf) [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf p.5](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/rightsizing_phase1_methods_and_after_action_review.pdf):
  - school size or minimum enrollment;
  - closing schools;
  - K-8 versus K-5/6-8;
  - closures versus other savings.
- The 360/500/1,100 thresholds were first announced Aug 4 [BB:3997426 p.2](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/3997426?org=915#page=2). They now drive the scenarios' headline KPIs [BB:14505788 p.1](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14505788?org=915#page=1). Phase I therefore cannot be said to have endorsed or rejected them.

**2. Where the scenarios follow Phase I.**
- **Variety of learning opportunities.** This was the #1 "positive environment" factor in every session and in the combined chart. It matches PPS's core "well-resourced schools" rationale ("Arts, language, PE, athletics" [BB:14374370 p.7](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14374370?org=915#page=7)).
- **Students' preferences.** The SVAC findings (programs over friends 62%; resources over proximity 95%) are the strongest Phase I support for consolidating into larger schools, though they come from 20 students.
- **Res. 7307 additions.** Walkability and "neighborhood schools are the foundation" [BB:3997273 p.2](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/3997273?org=915#page=2) track the Boundaries #1 ranking.
- **Kept constant:** all five DLI languages, kindergarten DLI seats and the named focus programs [BB:14511742 p.13](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14511742?org=915#page=13). This answers the "protect DLI/focus" comments.

**3. Where the scenarios diverge from what was ranked highest.**
- **Right Boundaries (#1): compact boundaries and safer routes.**
  - Scenarios A/B carry 38 or 34 boundary changes and close 14 or 11 schools [BB:14505788 p.1](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14505788?org=915#page=1) [BB:14505788 p.2](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14505788?org=915#page=2).
  - Several closing schools are very walkable: Peninsula 95.9% of students within 1 mile, Creston 85.2%, Buckman 82.0% [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/peninsula.pdf p.3](https://drive.google.com/open?id=15xt7PqKoHCdKNXu2tSzPph_CbsN3yJD2&usp=drive_copy) [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/creston.pdf p.3](https://drive.google.com/open?id=17XkDaems0GP2Kkj4Udax81mvsryvOcjC&usp=drive_copy) [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/buckman.pdf p.3](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1luY28d7t3WIQ9yklVt8CPqWrUnvYo1Fq&usp=drive_copy).
  - PPS defined a "within 1.0 and 1.5 miles" KPI [BB:14430171 p.17](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14430171?org=915#page=17), but it was not reported with the scenarios. The effect on the top-ranked factor is therefore unmeasured.
- **Right Pathways (#2) and the K-8 question.**
  - Feeder continuity is a stated criterion ("where possible") [BB:14511742 p.11](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14511742?org=915#page=11), and the split-feeder KPI was not reported (criteria file §8).
  - The seven K-8→K-5 conversions [BB:14509719 p.2](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14509719?org=915#page=2) [BB:14509719 p.3](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14509719?org=915#page=3) remove the K-8 continuity that Wells and virtual-session families described as a reason they stay in PPS [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-22_board_engagement_ida_b_wells.pdf p.3](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-22_board_engagement_ida_b_wells.pdf) [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-20_virtual_engagement_summary.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-20_virtual_engagement_summary.pdf).
  - On the other side, Roosevelt families' complaint about "a lack of elective variety" fits the middle-school consolidation logic.
- **Right Balance (#3) and Right Impact (#4).**
  - About 27% (A) or 24% (B) of K-8 students would change schools [BB:14511742 p.44](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14511742?org=915#page=44).
  - 13 or 12 program moves are proposed, including DLI consolidation [BB:14505788 p.1](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14505788?org=915#page=1).
  - Both run against "stability" and "smallest number of students" as ranked.
- **Right Use of Space (#5) became central.** Utilization and enrollment-versus-capacity is the first scenario criterion [BB:14511742 p.11](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14511742?org=915#page=11). The public ranked it fifth of six, as the criteria file already notes.
- **Right Mix (#6 overall).** The scenarios report focal-student shares in above-threshold schools [BB:14505788 p.1](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14505788?org=915#page=1). The Oct 20 deck now presents demographics as what "the families most affected told us matters most" [BB:14576326 p.7](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14576326?org=915#page=7).
  - Phase I evidence for that is thin: one small session, about 12 voters.
  - Meanwhile the Title I concern raised at Kellogg, the Village Table and Roosevelt sits against six Title I closures in both scenarios [BB:3997157 p.1](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/3997157?org=915#page=1).

**4. Closures versus other savings.**
- Phase I comments leaned against closures:
  - Kellogg: "consolidation will destroy the social and academic safety nets" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-11_board_engagement_kellogg.pdf p.3](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-11_board_engagement_kellogg.pdf);
  - Benson: fear of "any" closure [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-25_board_engagement_benson.pdf p.2](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-04-25_board_engagement_benson.pdf);
  - May poll: cut central office first [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-05-18_virtual_slido_summary.pdf p.2](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/phase1/2026-05-18_virtual_slido_summary.pdf).
- The scenarios answer on program sufficiency rather than savings. Armstrong has said "closing schools is not a cost-saving strategy" [YT:h_0YMlJnXPk@1:26:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h_0YMlJnXPk&t=5164s).
- Creston and Buckman, both named as community hubs at Kellogg, close in both scenarios; Chief Joseph and Lent stay open and receive students or programs (`data/school_impact.csv`, citing the school-impact PDFs). Rieke and Skyline, named at Wells, stay open.

**5. Bottom line.**
- On what families ranked highest (compact, walkable boundaries; feeder continuity; stability), the scenarios mostly carry costs that PPS has not yet measured with its own pathway and distance KPIs.
- On variety of programs, which was ranked highest on the environment question and strongly by students, the scenarios line up with Phase I.
- PPS's October summaries of spring input lean toward the parts that fit the scenarios: "feeder patterns matter more than anything else", and "communities of color" ranking demographics first. The posted documents support neither as stated.
