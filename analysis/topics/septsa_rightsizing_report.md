# SEPTSA report: "PPS Rightsizing: Special Education Impacts and Information Requests" (Oct 20, 2026)

**Source:** PPS Special Education PTSA (SEPTSA). The 36-page report was approved by its members and delivered to the PPS Board, the Rightsizing Committee and the Superintendent on Tuesday, Oct 20, 2026 [COMM:septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf p.2](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf) [COMM:septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report_page.html](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report_page.html). Archived copies are in [`sources/community/septsa/`](../../sources/community/septsa/): the PDF, the published Google Doc, the announcement page and a sample email to the Board, with a [manifest](../../sources/community/septsa/_manifest.json).

This is an **advocacy document**. The figures below are SEPTSA's preliminary estimates from public PPS data, which SEPTSA says PPS has not verified [COMM:septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf p.23](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf). Page numbers are PDF pages; the printed page number is one lower.

## Bottom line
SEPTSA says PPS has not shown that either scenario would preserve "equitable access to appropriate educational settings, services, and supports for students with disabilities" [p.3](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf). It asks that the Superintendent not issue a final recommendation, and the Board not vote, until PPS answers its requests and gives families time to respond [p.20](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf).

## Main findings (SEPTSA estimates)
- **IEP students concentrate in high-poverty schools.** Under Scenario A, the share of elementary students with IEPs attending high-poverty schools would rise from 33% to 38%. High-poverty schools are a third of remaining elementary schools but would absorb 48% of the IEP students added to receiving schools. The largest growth would be at Rosa Parks, Whitman and Sitton [p.22](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf).
- **Small middle-grade settings disappear.**
  - Middle-grade seats in K-8 settings fall about 70%, from 1,926 students in 12 schools to 566 in 5.
  - Choice and alternative K-8 middle-grade seats fall from 694 to 361 [p.24](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf).
  - The focus options losing grades or programs (MLC high school, Bridger middle grades, Sunnyside) serve 22–31% students with IEPs, against a 16% middle-school average [p.25](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf).
  - The attendance gap between students with disabilities and their schoolmates is about 11 points at comprehensive middle schools and 5 points at K-8s [p.26](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf).
- **"Subsidy" framing.** SEPTSA argues that calling higher per-student costs a "subsidy" ignores Oregon's extra funding weight for students on IEPs, and calls that framing "ableist" [p.26](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf). This answers PPS's K-8 cost argument at the Oct 13 TLE committee [BB:14535647 p.5](../../sources/boardbook/meetings/2026-10-13_771664_teaching-learning-and-enrollment-committee-meeting/attachments/14535647_TLE_Presentation_Middle_Grades.pdf).
- **Not enough space.** SEPTSA counted the minimum rooms needed: homerooms at contract class-size maximums, Learning Center rooms, one sensory-motor room and half-size break spaces. By that count, 7 of 11 Scenario A receiving elementary schools would lack enough classrooms for projected 2028-29 students, including all four Inclusive School Communities that absorb another school [p.28](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf).
- **Inclusive School Communities shrink and grow.**
  - Three of the eight model schools close: Marysville, James John and Peninsula. César Chávez leaves the model.
  - The remaining five grow from an average 307 to 470 students, and from 73 to 123 students with IEPs [p.30](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf).
  - The four absorbing schools would average 34% above the 360-student goal, against 20% for other receiving K-5s. Arleta is highest at 49% [p.31](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf).
  - SEPTSA cites PPS's 2025-26 progress report on the model: reading proficiency among autistic students rose from 10.7% to 24.2%, with better attendance [p.30](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf).
  - It also cites the SEGC equity audit's finding that staff tried to run an inclusive model "without enough paraeducator and special education support" [p.32](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf).

## Twelve requested deliverables (A–L)
The requests are [p.19](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf):
- A: a districtwide special education inventory
- B: a disability-specific equity and impact analysis
- C: pathways and continuity
- D: staffing and caseloads
- E: facilities and accessibility
- F: sensory environments and inclusion
- G: school-specific impact plans
- H: a review timeline. SEPTSA asks that materials be published at least 30 days before any vote, plus a dedicated special education session [p.17](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf)
- I: documented family engagement
- J: scenario methodology and alternatives, including net savings after closed-building costs [p.18](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf)
- K: a special education presentation at least 7 days before the vote
- L: a public response tracker with 14-day deadlines

## Southwest / West Side items
- **Maplewood → Markham.** SEPTSA asks how Maplewood's K-2 Intensive Skills Classroom would be housed with Markham's grades 3-5 ISC. It asks for the data behind Maplewood's enrollment figures and "reported discrepancies," and whether ISC students could stay with peers going to Rieke or Hayhurst [p.14](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf).
- **Stephenson → Capitol Hill.** SEPTSA asks whether Stephenson's Communication Behavior classroom moves under both scenarios, and what space Capitol Hill has [p.14](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf).
- **Focus classrooms in the area.** SEPTSA's list includes Hayhurst SES (1-3 and 4-5), Bridlemile CB, Maplewood ISC K-2, Markham ISC 3-5, and SES/ISC at Gray, Jackson and West Sylvan middle schools [p.33](../../sources/community/septsa/2026-10-20_septsa_rightsizing_report.pdf).

## Cross-check against PPS documents (Analysis)
- The program moves SEPTSA lists match PPS's family guides:
  - Maplewood's K-2 ISC moves to Markham, and Stephenson's CB classroom moves to Capitol Hill, both in Scenario A.
  - Marysville, James John and Peninsula close; Rosa Parks takes Peninsula; Arleta takes Marysville ([`data/school_impact.csv`](../data/school_impact.csv)).
- Arleta's 49%: PPS projects Arleta at 536 in 2028-29 under A/B [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/arleta.pdf p.4](../../sources/pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/arleta.pdf), and 536 is 49% above 360. So SEPTSA's "above the goal" figures appear to use the 2028-29 projections.
- PPS's methodology lists no special education inputs among its data sets or criteria [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/methodology/rightsizing_methodology_2026-10.pdf p.2](../../sources/pps_net/rightsizing/methodology/rightsizing_methodology_2026-10.pdf). That supports SEPTSA's point that no disability-specific analysis has been published. The family guides do report a "Special education" percentage per school.
- Several charts in the report are images with no text layer, so their exact values aren't in the `.pdf.txt`. The published Google Doc copy has the same content.
