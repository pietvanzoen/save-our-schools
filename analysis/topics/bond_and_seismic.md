# 2025 General Obligation Bond, Seismic Retrofits and Facilities — context for 2026 rightsizing

Scope: what the May 2025 PPS bond contains, how it was referred and approved, how the bond program (especially the
three high school modernizations) has been managed and re-budgeted since, what the 2025 Holmes seismic risk
assessment found and how the first seismic tranche was chosen, and every documented point where bond, seismic or
facility facts meet the 2026 rightsizing (school closure) process.

Sources: BoardBook attachments and minutes only (no video transcripts, pps.net or news were in the archive for this
pass). Citations follow `CLAUDE.md`: `[BB:<docId> p.<page>]`; meetings `[MTG:<date>/<meetingId>]`.
Companion data: `analysis/data/bond_projects.csv` (44 rows), `analysis/data/seismic_facilities.csv` (96 Holmes
campus rows, joined to seismic program and Oct 2026 rightsizing scenarios). Contradictions and gaps:
`analysis/topics/bond_seismic_open_questions.md`.

Labels: plain statements = what a PPS document says; "Analysis:" = my inference.

## 1. Summary of key points

- **Measure 26-259 (May 20, 2025)**: up to **$1.83 billion** in GO bonds, expected to keep the PPS bond tax rate at
  **$2.50 per $1,000** assessed value [BB:3990414 p.1-2]. Certified result **68,382 yes / 46,546 no** (Multnomah,
  Washington, Clackamas combined) [BB:3996484 p.1]. Analysis: about 59.5% yes.
- **Referral**: Resolution 7038 passed **7-0** on Jan 7, 2025; an amendment to add **$40 million** for a Center for
  Native American Student Excellence failed **2-5** (Brim-Edwards and Greene yes) [BB:3996227 p.5].
- **Contents (Exhibit A, 12/13/2024)**: $1,150,000,000 modernizations (Jefferson, Cleveland, Ida B. Wells, plus
  elementary/middle schools from savings); $190M deferred maintenance & priority scope; $79M athletics; $176M
  technology; $10M PE; $56M curriculum; $83.05M admin; $83.05M contingency; total **$1,827,100,000** [BB:3991930 p.1].
- **High school costs**: the 2025 bond was built on the premise that the three HS projects (then estimated at
  $491M / $469M / $455M) could be cut to targets of $366M / $360M / $360M, freeing up to **$470M** for elementary and
  middle schools [BB:3992439 p.6-7]. Actual revised comprehensive plans (Oct–Nov 2025) came in at **$466,007,500 /
  $472,553,760 / $449,350,000** [BB:3993106 p.3; BB:3993120 p.3; BB:3990445 p.2]. The leftover "Elementary & Middle
  Schools" line is **$171,738,255** and was entirely unspent and unencumbered as of 7/1/2026 [BB:14383796 p.17].
- **Seismic**: Resolution 7109 (May 6, 2025, 7-0) makes retrofitting the **8–10** highest-risk schools the
  "default presumption" for elementary/middle money after a ~$100M emergency reserve [BB:3996420 p.4, p.27]. The
  Holmes assessment (v2.0, Aug 27, 2025) scored building parts at 75 sites and estimated **$902,940,375** (2023 $,
  construction only) to fully retrofit all assessed schools [BB:3991109 p.3, p.14].
- **First seismic tranche** (~$100M, chosen with a 90/5/5 formula): full campus retrofits at **Beverly Cleary–
  Fernwood** and **Rose City Park**; targeted retrofits at Ainsworth, Beach, Capitol Hill, Kelly, Richmond (2025 bond)
  and Vernon, Winterhaven (2020 bond) [BB:3988546 p.2; BB:3990267 p.9]. Schools with projected enrollment under
  **300** were excluded "Given the rightsizing process" [BB:3988546 p.2].
- **Intersections with rightsizing (Oct 2026 scenarios)**: Rose City Park (planned full seismic retrofit) **closes in
  Scenario A**; Beach (planned targeted retrofit), Woodmere (2026 roof+seismic) and Marysville (SRGP full retrofit,
  2020 bond) **close in both scenarios**; Beverly Cleary (full retrofit) becomes a K-5 receiving school; several
  receiving schools have campus risk scores ≥9.0 and no retrofit in this bond (details §7) [BB:14505788 p.2;
  BB:14509719 p.2-3].

## 2. Bond planning and referral (2024 – Jan 2025)

### 2.1 Planning parameters
- The bond target was set by the Board's School Facilities Improvement Oversight Committee (SFIOC) starting Oct 2024:
  "A total bond target between $1.5 and $1.8 billion", an 8-year cycle, retaining the existing tax rate, and a May
  2025 ballot [BB:3992439 p.1]. Earlier in 2024 the scope "approached $3 billion" and planning was paused
  [BB:3992439 p.1].
- Facility need context given to the BAC: deferred maintenance backlog "totaling more than $1 billion"; PPS's
  overall facility condition index "is rated as 'poor'"; ~$700M over 10 years to reach "fair"; APPA capital-renewal
  benchmark ~$171M/year [BB:3992439 p.2].

### 2.4 Pre-election scrutiny (TSCC hearing, May 6, 2025)
- TSCC asked why PPS would build HS capacity "to 15,000" when the PSU forecast projected ~11,000 HS students in ten
  years; PPS answered that the Board directed building "for the next 100 years" and quoted the Superintendent: "there
  is no situation where a newly modernized, community-invested high school will go empty or even be half full"
  [BB:3996394 p.5].
- Jefferson total "up to $466 million": $366M from 2020 and "up to $100 million" from 2025 [BB:3996394 p.6].
- About $700M remained from prior bonds; capital spending capacity $150–250M/yr; all projects in "10-12 year"
  window [BB:3996394 p.6-7]. S&P downgraded PPS from AA to AA-, citing "declining fund balances and declining
  enrollment" [BB:3996394 p.7]. "No plans have been developed to pursue additional debt beyond the 2025 Bond"
  [BB:3996394 p.7].
- CBSE: $60M allocated in 2020 bond, "none of these dollars have been spent to-date" [BB:3996394 p.6].

## 3. High school modernizations: cost reduction and revised plans

### 3.1 Cost study and Board framework (Nov 2024 – Feb 2025)
- Designs of all three HS were paused in Nov 2024 [BB:3996230 p.2] (the Year 6 audit dates the Superintendent's pause
  to Dec 2024 [BB:3996481 p.25]).
- Cornerstone Management Group comparison (Jan 2025): total construction cost (not total project cost) Cleveland
  $383,320,202; Jefferson $417,748,736; Ida B. Wells $377,374,537; Lincoln $198,274,885 (escalated to 2027:
  $292,942,308); Beaverton HS $216,359,367 [BB:3996231 p.3-4]. Staff summary: escalation averaging 8%/yr ("a 47%
  increase" over 5 years), unique spaces and sustainability standards were the biggest drivers [BB:3996230 p.1].
- Feb 11, 2025: Resolution 7051 "Framework to Build High Quality-High Schools in a Cost-Efficient Manner" passed 7-0
  (Brim-Edwards moved, Hollands seconded) [BB:3996300 p.2]. Guidelines: ~295,000 SF per school; meet Ed Spec
  minimums; health center only with provider commitment; teen parent center only on demonstrated need; keep
  all-electric and equity-in-contracting; options on LEED, mass timber, basements; "$10M per project target" in
  additional reductions [BB:3990188 p.1-2]. Minutes note a report error "which resulted in calculation of escalation
  being off by 45 million dollars" and staff estimated savings of "approximately 20-40 million" [BB:3996300 p.2].

### 3.2 Spring 2025 cost-reduction results
- April 7, 2025 memo: combined estimated cost **$1.348B–$1.380B**; $406M funded by 2020 bond; leaving
  **$176M–$208M** of the $1.15B for "additional modernization initiatives" [BB:3996343 p.2]. Per-school ROMs (table
  image): Cleveland $450–462M vs $469M; Wells $440–452M vs $455M; Jefferson $458–466M vs $491M [BB:3996343 p.2].
- Area program at ~295–297k SF for all three; JHS, IBW, CHS each designed against the 1,700-student Ed Spec
  (Lincoln comparator 295,768 SF) [BB:3996363 p.1; BB:3996362 p.2].
- Staff warned that because projects were paused, "More construction escalation is included in the cost reduction
  ranges" [BB:3996362 p.3].

## 5. Seismic risk: assessments, resolution, prioritization

### 5.1 Starting point (spring 2025)
- Staff (May 3, 2025): average PPS school ~76 years old; 9 schools fully modernized, 3 in progress, 6 full seismic
  retrofits, interior seismic upgrades at 12, roof-level bracing "at dozens of sites"; "approximately 75% of PPS
  schools have received some level of seismic upgrade since 2012" [BB:3989487 p.1].
- 2024 Holmes Phase 1 ROM construction costs (as reported May 2025): URM-only $129,251,000; full retrofit at schools
  with some URM $340,050,875; non-URM schools $585,497,250; total **$925,548,125**; total project cost "can exceed
  twice the estimated construction costs" [BB:3989487 p.2].
- Staff named a PPS-only step 3 criterion: "anticipated impacts of potential school consolidation" [BB:3989487 p.3];
  by Aug 2025 the slide wording was "anticipated impacts of potential school right-sizing" [BB:3996555 p.10].

### 5.2 Resolution 7109 (May 6, 2025)
- Passed 7-0 (Greene moved, DePass seconded); introduced by Director Hollands; three public commenters in support
  [BB:3996420 p.4].
- Recitals: ~37% chance of a Cascadia megaquake in 50 years; URM schools "definitely not safe for large earthquakes"
  [BB:3996420 p.25].
- Funding logic: $190M + $176–208M of HS savings = "approximately $366-398 million"; first a ~$100M reserve; then a
  "default presumption" that the first use of elementary/middle funds is "to mitigate the seismic deficiencies at the
  8-10 schools assessed to pose the greatest risk of injury or death"; and "If the District determines that any of the
  8-10 schools ... should be replaced entirely, any plan to build new schools or consolidate existing schools will be
  determined by the District's closure and consolidation policy and process" [BB:3996420 p.26-27].
- Directed a Seismic Risk Reduction Plan "no later than September 1, 2025" with quarterly public progress reports
  [BB:3996420 p.26-27].

### 5.3 Holmes "PPS Seismic & Seismic Risk Assessments" v2.0 (Aug 27, 2025) [BB:3991109]
- Presented Sept 9, 2025 [MTG:2025-09-09/706408]; minutes record Board discussion of "how seismic planning
  intersects with potential school consolidations" and questions on why soil sampling was not used
  [BB:3996620 p.6].
- **Method**: Phase 1 (2023–24) screened 80 sites using FEMA Rapid Visual Screening and ASCE 41 Tier 1 checklists
  (not a full Tier 1) with ROM $/SF pricing; Phase 2 scored building parts at 75 sites [BB:3991109 p.3-5]. Score
  steps: Hazus baseline by structural type and era (1–9) → building-specific adjustments for retrofits and
  deficiencies (0–10) → occupancy adjustment (assembly spaces raise, back-of-house lowers) [BB:3991109 p.16-20]. All
  sites assumed same seismicity; site geology not considered [BB:3991109 p.17]. Highest scores: pre-code URM and
  pre-cast tilt-up [BB:3991109 p.21].
- **Costs (2023 $, construction only)**: URM-only $118,581,000; full retrofit of URM schools $326,594,375; non-URM
  schools $576,346,000; total **$902,940,375** [BB:3991109 p.14]. Pricing excludes soft costs, relocation and
  non-seismic upgrades; "should NOT be used for final budgeting" [BB:3991109 p.11].
- **Recommendation**: use risk scores "as a primary decision-making tool" and integrate them "into any future
  discussions regarding district enrollment changes and facility planning" [BB:3991109 p.7]. Two approaches:
  campus-by-campus (A) vs highest-risk building parts (B) [BB:3991109 p.7]; OSM recommended a hybrid ("Approach C")
  [BB:3990341 p.4; BB:3996609 p.8].
- **Highest campus scores** (max building part; full table in `seismic_facilities.csv`): Fernwood/Beverly Cleary 9.9;
  Jackson 9.8; Rose City Park 9.8; Lee 9.7; Buckman 9.6; Rieke 9.6; Kelly 9.5; Capitol Hill 9.4; Roseway Heights 9.3;
  Ainsworth, Beach, Glencoe, Rigler 9.2 [BB:3991109 p.191, 207, 237, 216, 178, 233, 211, 179, 238, 166, 172, 195, 234].
  Largest ROM full-retrofit costs: Marshall $47,520,000; Jackson $30,170,000; King $21,200,000; Buckman $21,350,000;
  Rose City Park $21,100,000 [BB:3991109 p.223, 207, 213, 178, 237].
- Excluded from scoring: retrofitted (Benson, Creative Science/Clark, Franklin, Grant, Hayhurst, Lent, Lewis,
  McDaniel, Roosevelt), replaced (Faubion, Kellogg, Lincoln, Rosa Parks), in-progress HS (Cleveland, Jefferson, Ida B.
  Wells), Marysville (SRGP in progress), decommissioned (Kenton, Meek, Rice, Smith), leased (Trillium at Edwards)
  [BB:3991109 p.174-248].

### 5.4 Prioritization and first tranche (Nov 2025 – Mar 2026)
- Oct 2025: rubric "incorporating Facility Condition Index (FCI), URM status, occupancy, and equity impacts"
  [BB:3996658 p.2]. Nov 3, 2025 FIOC: same nine schools as the final list already shown [BB:3996723 p.9]; at the
  Nov 4 Board meeting commenters objected to upgrading buildings "not rated the most seismically unsafe"
  [BB:3996763 p.3].
- **Final formula** (Mar 10, 2026 memo): Score = 0.90 × highest campus seismic risk + 0.05 × deferred maintenance
  (incl. CSI/TSI equity adjustment) + 0.05 × enrollment indicator; "Schools not meeting the enrollment threshold of
  300 were not prioritized for this first round" [BB:3988546 p.2].
- Consistently top-ten campuses: "Fernwood (Beverly Cleary), Glencoe, Grout, Jackson, Kelly, Rose City Park, and
  Roseway Heights" [BB:3988546 p.3]. Very costly high-risk campuses deferred for "more detailed investigation and
  scope refinement, along with long-range facilities planning" [BB:3988546 p.3].

## 7. Intersections with rightsizing

### 7.1 Explicit links in PPS documents
1. **Res 7109** routes any decision to replace one of the 8–10 highest-risk schools through "the District's closure
   and consolidation policy and process" [BB:3996420 p.27]; **Res 7110** makes the FIOC the venue for "potential school
   enrollment or consolidations changes related to seismic plans" [BB:3996420 p.28].
2. Staff listed consolidation/right-sizing as a PPS criterion layered on Holmes scores from May 2025 onward
   [BB:3989487 p.3; BB:3996555 p.10; BB:3990341 p.4]; Holmes recommended integrating scores into "enrollment
   changes" [BB:3991109 p.7].
3. **300-student threshold** in the seismic formula, explicitly because of rightsizing [BB:3988546 p.2;
   BB:3990212 p.3]. Analysis: the rightsizing sustainability threshold is higher — 360 for K-5, 500 for K-8/MS,
   1,100 for HS [BB:14505788 p.1].
4. Seismic construction contracts structured as a flexible pool to allow changes after December 2026 rightsizing
   decisions [BB:3990212 p.3].
5. Rightsizing criteria include "Facility & Capital Viability: Assessed facility condition alongside enrollment" and
   a guardrail that students "transition to higher-quality facilities" [BB:14511742 p.11]; scenario memo: analysis
   "considers functional capacity, facility conditions and planned improvements" and Grant/McDaniel changes account
   "for Beverly Cleary seismic work and Rose City Park facility concerns" [BB:14509719 p.2-3].
6. Res 7307 guiding principle (b) commits to "maintain a sustainable portfolio of school facilities"
   [BB:3997273 p.2]; Res 7296 recital: "our facilities are aging and costs have increased" [BB:3990244 p.1].

### 7.5 High school capacity vs. enrollment
- Each of the three modernized HS is designed for 1,700 students [BB:3996231 p.3; BB:3993106 p.3]; rightsizing HS sustainability threshold is
  1,100 [BB:14505788 p.1]. TSCC, the BAC and public commenters questioned building that capacity amid declining
  enrollment [BB:3996394 p.5; BB:3996656 p.5; BB:3996761 p.2].
- Jefferson enrollment ranged 440–670 under dual assignment; the Dec 2025 Scenario C recommendation (from 2027-28)
  projects all four N/NE high schools ≥1,100 with 70–90% utilization in 2030-31 [BB:3988475 p.2, p.4]. Jefferson
  is described as the "only non-modernized high school in North/Northeast Portland" [BB:3988475 p.2].
