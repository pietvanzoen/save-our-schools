# PPS timeline as reported in news coverage (Sept 2024 – Oct 2026)

Derived from `sources/news/index.jsonl` (built 2026-10-10). Each line reports what the cited outlet said, with its date. Dates are event dates where the article gives them, otherwise publication dates. Figures sometimes differ between outlets: for example, the 2026-27 cut was reported as 320, 322 and 336 FTE. Where they conflict, each figure is shown with its own citation. Lines marked **Analysis:** are my inference and are not reported fact. Primary documents (BoardBook, video) should be checked before any of these lines is relied on.

## 2024 background

- **2024-09-12**: Axios reports that PPS enrollment fell 6% from 2019 to 2023. PSU projects 20 of 45 elementary schools below 300 students, and the board had approved cuts to close a $30M deficit. [NEWS:NEWS-20240912-axios-1]
- **2024-10-22**: Cleveland, Jefferson and Ida B. Wells are the three high schools still awaiting modernization. The board is weighing a $1.5–1.8B bond for 2025. [NEWS:NEWS-20241022-koin-1]

## 2025: bond, board turnover, 2025-26 cuts, seismic fight, Jefferson

- **2025-01-13/14**: The board appoints Christy Splitt to the Zone 1 vacancy left by Andrew Scott, 4–2 in the final round. [NEWS:NEWS-20250114-wweek-1]
- **2025-01-22**: First draft of 2025-26 cuts: about $41M and up to 228–230 positions, most of them school-based for the first time. PERS costs and the post-strike PAT contract are cited. [NEWS:NEWS-20250122-wweek-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20250122-opb-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20250122-oregonian-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20250122-kptv-1]
- **2025-01-23**: Preliminary PSU forecast: 43,375 students falling to 37,057 by 2034-35 (−14.5%). [NEWS:NEWS-20250123-wweek-1]
- **2025-02-05**: At a budget forum, Armstrong first says PPS will begin talking about school closures, though not that year. [NEWS:NEWS-20250212-wweek-1]
- **2025-03-11**: Armstrong presses Gov. Kotek to "fully fund classrooms" as 200+ positions are slated for cuts. [NEWS:NEWS-20250311-koin-1]
- **2025-03-19**: PSU projects high school enrollment down about 18% over eight years, which raises questions about the size of the high school rebuilds. [NEWS:NEWS-20250319-wweek-1]
- **2025-03-26**: A district assessment finds 19 high-risk unreinforced masonry (URM) schools. The proposed bond has no dedicated seismic money. URM retrofit is estimated at $109M, later $126M. [NEWS:NEWS-20250326-wweek-1]
- **2025-04-23**: The superintendent's proposed 2025-26 budget cuts 242 positions to close a $40M deficit. [NEWS:NEWS-20250423-wweek-1]
- **2025-05-05**: Parents (Safe Structures PPS) push for more than $100M of the bond to go to seismic work. Axios lists the schools with the most URM, led by Buckman and Rose City Park. [NEWS:NEWS-20250505-axios-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20250505-axios-2]
- **2025-05-06**: The board votes 7–0 for a seismic priority resolution (Res. 7109): $100M for imminent-risk projects and a retrofit schedule due Sept. 1. [NEWS:NEWS-20250506-koin-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20250507-wweek-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20250511-opb-1]
- **2025-05-10**: WW lists the 19 URM schools and their retrofit estimates. **Analysis:** seven of them (Beach, Buckman, Creston, James John, Marysville, Rose City Park, Sabin) appear on the October 2026 closure lists. [NEWS:NEWS-20250510-wweek-1]
- **2025-05-20**: Measure 26-259 ($1.83B, the largest school bond in Oregon history) passes: 54–58% on election night, about 59% by May 22. In the board races, Splitt (Z1), Chase-Miller (Z4, unseating Greene), La Forte (Z5) and Engelsman (Z6) win. [NEWS:NEWS-20250520-wweek-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20250520-opb-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20250521-koin-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20250522-mercury-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20250520-wweek-2]
- **2025-06-10**: The board adopts the ~$2B 2025-26 budget 5–2, with 200+ positions cut. [NEWS:NEWS-20250611-koin-1]
- **2025-08-01**: Bridger Creative Science (an SEGC merger school) loses its promised SEGC staffing; 4th-grade classes are projected at 33–34 students. [NEWS:NEWS-20250801-seexaminer-1]
- **2025-08-07/14**: PSU forecast: about −9% by 2029-30, bottoming in 2034-35. The kindergarten capture rate is about 71%, against 86% in 2013. [NEWS:NEWS-20250807-wweek-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20250814-wweek-1]
- **2025-09-09/10**: Holmes report: URM retrofit $118.6M, all schools $902.9M. Staff say consolidation will be weighed in seismic selection. [NEWS:NEWS-20250910-wweek-1]
- **2025-09-20**: Staff propose phasing out Jefferson's dual assignment from fall 2027. [NEWS:NEWS-20250920-wweek-1]
- **2025-09-22**: Safe Structures PPS says the district missed the Sept. 1 retrofit-schedule deadline. [NEWS:NEWS-20250922-wweek-1]
- **2025-10-08/10**: The draft seismic formula weights risk only 35%, with "right-sizing" at 20%, and advocates object. [NEWS:NEWS-20251010-wweek-1]
- **2025-10-29**: The Oct. 1 count is 42,622 students (−753, −1.7%). Kindergarten rises for the first time since 2012-13. [NEWS:NEWS-20251029-wweek-1]
- **2025-10-30**: Jefferson options are narrowed to three, with a target of 1,700 students. [NEWS:NEWS-20251030-opb-1]
- **2025-11-13**: The revised seismic formula weights risk at 90%. Nine schools are selected, with full retrofits at Beverly Cleary and Rose City Park. [NEWS:NEWS-20251113-wweek-1]
- **2025-12-06 / 12-16**: Armstrong recommends Scenario C for Jefferson (Sabin to Jefferson, Irvington to Grant) and calls it "the first step in a systemwide review". Rightsizing is to follow in 2026. [NEWS:NEWS-20251206-wweek-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20251216-katu-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20251210-wweek-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20251215-wweek-1]

- **2026-04-28/29**: Proposed 2026-27 budget: $2.77B, a $56.3M gap, 322–336 FTE cut. A $65.2M gap is forecast for 2027-28. [NEWS:NEWS-20260428-wweek-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20260429-opb-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20260429-kptv-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20260430-wweek-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20260506-wweek-1]

## 2026 Jul–Oct: escalation to 10–20 closures, scenario release

- **2026-07 (early)**: Wang is re-elected chair and Chase-Miller becomes vice chair. Armstrong says any extra money should reduce the 2027-28 deficit. [NEWS:NEWS-20260708-wweek-1]
- **2026-07-22**: The PPS "Rumors & Facts" page says bond projects do not signal a school's rightsizing status. [NEWS:NEWS-20260722-pps-1]
- **2026-07-31**: The SEGC evaluation finds balanced enrollment and no academic decline, but high staff turnover (55% vs 26% administrator departures). [NEWS:NEWS-20260731-wweek-1]
- **2026-08-18/24**: PSU projects 36,763 students by 2035-36 (−14%). Multnomah County is projected to lose 17,474 school-age children. [NEWS:NEWS-20260818-wweek-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20260824-wweek-1]
- **2026-08-26**: At the back-to-school event, Armstrong raises the closure range to 10–20 and cites up to $65M in further cuts. [NEWS:NEWS-20260826-wweek-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20260826-katu-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20260826-kptv-1]
- **2026-08-28/31**: The staff model shows 15 elementary closures are needed for all remaining schools to reach 360 students. A parent coalition, "Right-Sizing the Right Way", forms. [NEWS:NEWS-20260828-wweek-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20260831-katu-1]
