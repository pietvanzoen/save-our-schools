## What the scenarios do
**Scenarios A and B (same):** Stays open; welcomes the regional Deaf and Hard of Hearing program (with CRIS) from Creston; feeder to Mt. Tabor CRIS programming.

PPS's wording, "What is being proposed?" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/glencoe.pdf p.3]:
> Glencoe would remain open as a neighborhood K-5 school. Glencoe will partner with Columbia Regional Inclusive Services (CRIS) to welcome students in the Deaf and Hard of Hearing (DHH) Program, which serves students across the region, including communities such as Molalla and West Linn- Wilsonville. Glencoe Elementary School serves as a feeder for Mt. Tabor Middle School, ensuring continuity for students who will move on to middle school CRIS programming at Mt. Tabor. To ensure a seamless transition, school and district leaders will conduct site walk-throughs to evaluate programmatic readiness, space, transportation, and ADA accessibility, and special education administrators will work closely with school principals to meet all student IEP requirements.

"Why is PPS considering this?" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/glencoe.pdf p.3]:
> Clear middle school path: Moving Creston's Deaf and Hard of Hearing Program to Glencoe connects elementary students directly with the Mt. Tabor Middle School feeder pathway.

"What else was considered?" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/glencoe.pdf p.3]:
> Route to middle and high school cohesion: Surrounding schools were evaluated, but space numbers and route to high schools made Glencoe the best fit for the DHH program
