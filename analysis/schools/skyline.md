## What the scenarios do
**Scenarios A and B (same):** Stays K-8 (distance from other schools); high school assignment shifts from Lincoln to Roosevelt.

PPS's wording, "What is being proposed?" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/skyline.pdf p.3](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1mJIvkuoYopD36cwEU1Iv0kZNWiPllHT6&usp=drive_copy):
> Skyline would remain open as a neighborhood K-8 school. Its high school building pathway assignment would shift from Lincoln High School to Roosevelt High School.

"Why is PPS considering this?" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/skyline.pdf p.3](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1mJIvkuoYopD36cwEU1Iv0kZNWiPllHT6&usp=drive_copy):
> Shorter travel times: Reassigning Skyline’s high school route from Lincoln to Roosevelt significantly decreases travel for rural western students. Preserving K-8 structure: Skyline remains a neighborhood K-8 school because of its distance from other schools.

"What else was considered?" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/skyline.pdf p.3](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1mJIvkuoYopD36cwEU1Iv0kZNWiPllHT6&usp=drive_copy):
> Distance between schools: Kept as a K-8 school because distance to other middle school buildings is too far to combine with other schools.

**Board-member alternatives and advocacy (Oct 20, 2026):**
- Engelsman proposes dropping the shift of Skyline's students from Lincoln to Roosevelt, citing St. Johns Bridge traffic and earthquake risk [NEWS:NEWS-20261020-oregonian-1](https://www.oregonlive.com/education/2026/10/2-portland-public-schools-board-members-pitch-alternatives-to-closing-14-schools.html).
