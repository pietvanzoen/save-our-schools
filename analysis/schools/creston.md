## What the scenarios do
**Scenarios A and B (same):** K-5 building closes; neighborhood students to Atkinson (then Kellogg MS, Franklin HS); Deaf and Hard of Hearing (DHH, with CRIS) program to Glencoe; Creston Annex preschool stays open as a preschool center.

PPS's wording, "What is being proposed?" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/creston.pdf p.3](https://drive.google.com/open?id=17XkDaems0GP2Kkj4Udax81mvsryvOcjC&usp=drive_copy):
> Creston School would close as a K-5 school building. Neighborhood K-5 students would move to Atkinson K-5. The Deaf and Hard of Hearing (DHH) program would move to Glencoe K-5. Clear middle and high school pathways: All K-5 students in the new neighborhood boundary would go together to Kellogg for middle school and Franklin for high school. Early learning stays: The Creston Annex preschool building would stay open as a preschool center.

"Why is PPS considering this?" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/creston.pdf p.3](https://drive.google.com/open?id=17XkDaems0GP2Kkj4Udax81mvsryvOcjC&usp=drive_copy):
> Enrollment is dropping: Creston is expected to drop to just 187 students over the next few years, making it too small to support full staffing and programs. Nearby schools have plenty of space: Neighboring schools like Atkinson have the room and buildings ready to welcome Creston students. Better pathways for special-focus programs: Moving the Deaf and Hard of Hearing (DHH) program to Glencoe connects students with the Mt. Tabor Middle School pathway.

"What else was considered?" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/creston.pdf p.3](https://drive.google.com/open?id=17XkDaems0GP2Kkj4Udax81mvsryvOcjC&usp=drive_copy):
> Atkinson is easier to reach: Atkinson was chosen over Creston because it has the space needed and is more central to where families live in the new boundary. Peaceful Villa Housing: We took into account the new students who will be attending school from this new housing unit. Creston currently partners with Columbia Regional Inclusive Services (CRIS) to deliver services to students served in the Deaf and Hard of Hearing (DHH) Program. This is a regional program serving students from across the region, extending to communities such as Molalla and West Linn-Wilsonville. The program currently partners with Mt. Tabor Middle School for students continuing from Creston to Middle School programming in CRIS classrooms. When combining school communities with distinct Special Education programming, we are planning and ensuring tailored support through the transition. Students in the Deaf and Hard of Hearing program at Creston will receive their services in a DHH program based out of Glencoe Elementary School, a feeder for Mt. Tabor Middle School.
