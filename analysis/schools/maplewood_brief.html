Maplewood Elementary: why it's on the closure list, and what's been said

Maplewood K-5 closes under both of the district's rightsizing scenarios. This brief collects PPS's stated reasons, the comments made at the board meeting where the scenarios were presented, and the weak points in the district's case.

Updated Oct 11, 2026Superintendent recommendation Nov 17Board vote Dec 15

What PPS proposes (Scenarios A and B, identical for Maplewood): "Maplewood School would close as a school building. 70% of the neighborhood boundary would move to Hayhurst K-5, and 30% would move to Rieke K-5. Middle grade students would move to Robert Gray Middle School instead of Jackson. The K-2 Intensive Skills Classroom would move to Markham K-5 to rejoin the 3-5 classroom."

Why PPS chose Maplewood

These reasons were given in the staff presentation at the Oct 6, 2026 board meeting (presented by Richard Smith, Senior Director of Data & Accountability) and in the written school sheet. Quotes are from YouTube auto-captions; "Ricky" in the captions is corrected to [Rieke].

  1. 1

    All three schools are projected to shrink below the target

    "Maplewood drops to 247 students, [Rieke] to 311 … and Hayhurst to 266 students, leaving all three well under the 360 student sustainability threshold."
    Staff presentation · 2:23:24
  2. 2

    Geography: more students live closer to Hayhurst and Rieke

    "[Rieke] and Hayhurst were selected to remain open over Maplewood based off geographical accessibility and student density across all three attendant zones."
    Staff presentation · 2:23:46
  3. 3

    Who bears the burden of moving

    The written sheet says 70% goes to Hayhurst "because most of the Maplewood area is closer to Hayhurst." It adds: "As we look at who bears the burden of moving, we wanted to reduce the impact on students experiencing poverty and underserved communities and that is a contributing factor for moving Maplewood to Hayhurst."

    PPS school sheet, Maplewood p.3. Maplewood has 19.5% of students in poverty; Hayhurst has 26.2%.
  4. 4

    Buildings were not the reason

    "There are no real facility issues with any of these three schools."
    Staff presentation · 2:24:15. On middle school, staff said moving Maplewood students to Gray instead of Jackson "is solely to align feeder patterns," and acknowledged this "brings enrollment down at Jackson significantly" (2:25:41).

Where the district's case is thin

Analysis

The walkability claim cuts both ways

45% of Maplewood students live within a mile of their school, compared with 31% at Hayhurst. After the merge, Hayhurst drops to 23%. The "students live closer to Hayhurst and Rieke" argument rests on a density calculation across all three zones that PPS hasn't published.

Thirty percent of students move into a higher-risk building

Staff said there were "no real facility issues," but Rieke has an unreinforced-masonry section with a seismic risk score of 9.6. Maplewood's highest score is 7.5, with no unreinforced masonry. PPS has also already spent about $1.5M on roof-level seismic work at Maplewood (KGW, Oct 9).

Maplewood keeps its neighborhood better than the school it would merge into

82% of neighborhood children attend Maplewood, compared with 74% at Hayhurst. A parent at the meeting cited a PPS estimate that about 7% of affected families leave the district. Any such losses would cut into the projected gains at Hayhurst and Rieke.

Hayhurst keeps shrinking after the merge

The merged school is projected at 472 students in 2027-28, falling to 410 by 2031-32. Whether the new housing near Hayhurst adds 12 children or 50 to 70 changes how much margin it has above 360.

Scenario A explains the Rieke choice; Scenario B doesn't

Under Scenario A, Capitol Hill and Markham are already taking Stephenson's students, which likely answers Engelsman's question. Under Scenario B, Stephenson stays open, yet Maplewood students still go to Rieke.

The only reason that singles out Maplewood was never said aloud

Enrollment decline applies to all three schools. What tips the choice toward Maplewood is the stated aim of not moving Hayhurst's higher-poverty population. That reason is in the written sheet but was not raised at the meeting, and it is the one most open to public debate.

Questions to put to PPS

  • Publish the density and travel-distance analysis that ranked Hayhurst and Rieke over Maplewood.
  • Why does Maplewood's 30% go to Rieke rather than Capitol Hill or Markham, especially in Scenario B?
  • What is the net savings from closing Maplewood specifically: operations, staffing and transportation?
  • How was the housing development near Hayhurst counted, and what yield of new students was assumed?
  • How will Rieke's unreinforced-masonry section be addressed before it takes on more students?
  • What is the plan for the Maplewood building after closure?
