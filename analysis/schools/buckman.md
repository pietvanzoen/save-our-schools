## What the scenarios do
**Scenarios A and B (same):** Building closes (urgent structural/seismic repair needs); Arts special-focus choice program closes; neighborhood split: north of Hawthorne & south of Burnside -> Abernethy, north of Burnside -> Sunnyside.

PPS's wording, "What is being proposed?" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/buckman.pdf p.3]:
> Buckman School would close as a school building. The Arts special-focus choice program would close. Neighborhood boundary split: Half of the neighborhood goes to Abernethy K-5: Students living north of Hawthorne and south of Burnside would move to Abernethy. Half of the neighborhood goes to Sunnyside K-5: Students living north of Burnside would move to an updated Sunnyside K-5.

"Why is PPS considering this?" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/buckman.pdf p.3]:
> The Buckman building needs major repairs. The school requires urgent structural fixes and heavy maintenance to make it safe and modern. Enrollment is dropping. Buckman is expected to drop to just 239 students over the next few years, making it difficult to fund full programs and staffing. Nearby schools are in better shape. Both Abernethy and Sunnyside have better buildings and are in good locations to welcome Buckman families.

"What else was considered?" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/buckman.pdf p.3]:
> Building condition over size: Even though Buckman is a larger building than Abernethy, Buckman’s building repair needs are severe. Title I Funding Support: District teams considered Buckman’s Title I status carefully to ensure students moving to receiving schools continue to get strong learning and family support services.
