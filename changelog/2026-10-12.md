#### Dual-language immersion (DLI) deck

"TLE Presentation: DLI Programs", 14 pages: [public viewer](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/FileViewerOrPublic/915?file=14540954) ·
[repo PDF](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/boardbook/meetings/2026-10-13_771664_teaching-learning-and-enrollment-committee-meeting/attachments/14540954_TLE_Presentation_DLI_Programs.pdf) ·
[repo text](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/boardbook/meetings/2026-10-13_771664_teaching-learning-and-enrollment-committee-meeting/attachments/14540954_TLE_Presentation_DLI_Programs.pdf.txt)

- **Problem PPS states:** Spanish DLI is spread across 9 elementary and 6 middle-school campuses, and all but two share a building with an English neighborhood program. PPS lists the challenges as "off-balance enrollment, resource distribution, staffing, educator collaboration & support" [[BB:14540954 p.2]](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/FileViewerOrPublic/915?file=14540954).
- **Direction:** PPS is moving toward whole-school DLI, which it calls "the configuration most likely to reliably deliver these conditions at scale" [[BB:14540954 p.9]](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/FileViewerOrPublic/915?file=14540954). It cites Lent Elementary as its proof point [[BB:14540954 p.11]](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/FileViewerOrPublic/915?file=14540954).
- **César Chávez:**
  - Under both Scenarios A and B it becomes an all-Spanish-immersion K–5.
  - 2026–27 enrollment is 65 English-program and 214 immersion students (K–5). The proposed 2027–28 enrollment is 541 immersion students [[BB:14540954 p.10]](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/FileViewerOrPublic/915?file=14540954).
- **Kindergarten DLI, 2026–27 (enrollment / lottery slots):** Chinese 102/127, Japanese 101/104, Russian 14/25, Spanish 250/278, Vietnamese 38/52 [[BB:14540954 p.4]](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/FileViewerOrPublic/915?file=14540954).
- **Transportation:** the proposed North Portland Spanish DLI model has 3–4 dedicated bus routes, 10–14 stops and a "20 min average per route" [[BB:14540954 p.8]](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/FileViewerOrPublic/915?file=14540954). The current system relies partly on the "hitcher" program, under which students can ask to ride existing bus routes [[BB:14540954 p.7]](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/FileViewerOrPublic/915?file=14540954).
- **Transition timeline:** draft bus routes go to families in spring 2027. Focus groups, planning time and family visits run through spring and fall 2027 [[BB:14540954 p.12]](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/FileViewerOrPublic/915?file=14540954).

**Analysis:** the deck does not say where César Chávez's roughly 260 additional immersion students would come from. Consolidating other Spanish strands is the likely source, but that should be confirmed with PPS.

#### Middle-grades deck

"TLE Presentation: Middle Grades", 14 pages: [public viewer](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/FileViewerOrPublic/915?file=14535647) ·
[repo PDF](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/boardbook/meetings/2026-10-13_771664_teaching-learning-and-enrollment-committee-meeting/attachments/14535647_TLE_Presentation_Middle_Grades.pdf) ·
[repo text](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/boardbook/meetings/2026-10-13_771664_teaching-learning-and-enrollment-committee-meeting/attachments/14535647_TLE_Presentation_Middle_Grades.pdf.txt)

- **Problem PPS states:** "We are subsidizing K-8 middle grades programs with 21 FTE to ensure core programming" [[BB:14535647 p.2]](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/FileViewerOrPublic/915?file=14535647).
- **Subsidy:** the per-school table across 13 K–8s totals 21.1 FTE and $3,376,000. Bridger ($448,000), MLC 6–8 ($416,000) and Laurelhurst ($352,000) are the largest [[BB:14535647 p.5]](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/FileViewerOrPublic/915?file=14535647).
- **Cost per student:** about $8,750 in K–8 middle grades versus about $7,000 in 6–8 schools (general 6–12 instructor FTE only) [[BB:14535647 p.6]](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/FileViewerOrPublic/915?file=14535647).
- **Test outcomes:** state test (OSAS) results "do not show significant differences between 6-8 and K-8 schools" [[BB:14535647 p.7]](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/FileViewerOrPublic/915?file=14535647).
- **Access:** four anonymized case studies compare small and large K–8 and 6–8 schools on electives (9 to 25 options), world languages and intervention courses [[BB:14535647 p.8]](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/FileViewerOrPublic/915?file=14535647). The deck also says about 75% of K–8 case-study teachers teach more than one grade [[BB:14535647 p.9]](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/FileViewerOrPublic/915?file=14535647).
- **Summary claim:** the scenarios "expand access to comprehensive middle school programming, reduce non-formula FTE subsidies, and free up space for well-resourced elementary programs" [[BB:14535647 p.14]](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/FileViewerOrPublic/915?file=14535647).

**Analysis:** PPS's own deck reports no test-score difference between the two models, so its case for converting K–8s to K–5s rests on cost and on access to programs. The stated subsidy, about $3.4M a year, is small next to the roughly $65M 2027–28 shortfall reported in coverage. One example is Seattle Red/KTTH, NEWS-20261008-seattlered-1, in section 3.
