Subject: The proposed closure of Maplewood Elementary

Dear Dr. Armstrong, Portland Public Schools Board Members, and Staff,

Thank you for all your hard work to guide the future of PPS and serve the over 43,000 children in our school system. I know the work you do is tireless and so often thankless. I also recognize that with declining enrollment and birth rates in the metro area, we are at the point of “something must be done,” and I can appreciate the difficulty of the fraught decisions before you. That said, as the State Representative of an impacted school community (and mom of four now-grown PPS students), I would like to add my voice that the proposed closure of 14 schools in PPS is devastating. The more I learn, I am concerned that the plan is based on flawed information, and as presented has a lack of transparency and accountability that my community and I are requesting. I recognize that this is a very long email, but I want to be thorough in sharing my thoughts.

I am, as is every constituent that has reached out to my office, opposed to the closure of Maplewood Elementary. Based on the criteria that PPS itself has put forward, Maplewood should not be on this list. There is deep concern that the information presented to the board failed to accurately state the number of students, as well as the impact of significant incoming housing developments on the area. Maplewood has had recent seismic upgrades, several significant material improvements to the school (including a turf field), and even as I was reading to Ms. Looney’s class there on Wednesday October 7th, it was to the sounds of siding being installed on the main building. I am confounded that a school that has received significant investment in material improvement is now on a closure list. I write this as someone who lives close by Smith Elementary School, another PPS school that was closed in 2006 and now sits literally rotting, vandalized, and infested with black mold instead of serving the community in any way other than as open fields and a playground.

Gathered through emails, phone calls, neighborhood meetings, and interactions in person in the last 5 days, I also respectfully submit that the following list of questions and concerns be taken into consideration. In my 6 years of elected office as a State Representative, this issue has generated more community passion and input than any other– and certainly the first time that input has been unanimous: don’t close our school.
Please consider deferring any vote until the revised PSU enrollment and housing numbers are published and independently verified. For example: Maplewood currently has 310 students enrolled, not the 288 used in the presentation. Rejected transfers and new housing were also not counted in that figure.


We ask that you publish the methodology of the study as well as the full equity impact analysis before the vote, and that this include a service continuity plan for the ISC and special education students.


What is the cumulative impact on SW given the 2006 Smith School closure, especially as it relates to transit and the movement of students? SW Portland West of Hwy 26 and South Beaverton Hillsdale Highway has extremely limited bike lanes and sidewalks: less than 30% of our roads have sidewalks, which is the lowest in the city (the same goes for bike lanes). Neighborhood clusters like Maplewood are some of the most effective ways to keep students and families safe in reaching schools.


Raleigh Crest (the Alpenrose development) is adding 263 single-family and duplex homes nearby; we dispute the staff presentation assertion that this will result in a net gain of 12 students to PPS. Additionally, 246 housing units have been permitted within a mile of Maplewood since 2022.


Maplewood has already undergone $1.5 million in seismic retrofitting. According to PPS’s own September report, major structural seismic improvements are complete. The remaining tasks involving the gym and cafeteria physical surfaces is the second-lowest outstanding retrofit cost of 48 PPS elementary and K-8 schools.


Has there been a study or projection modeling how many students and families may choose to leave the district entirely if Maplewood is closed?


There are other questions that I have received via email. Parents have asked for PPS to publish school-by-school projections with ranges, and show results if enrollment runs 5%, 10% or 15% above forecast, and to show the effect of each proposed closure on its own and not only as part of the package. Will PPS make available to the public the enrollment cutoffs, capacity figures, and housing assumptions behind the listed scenarios?



K-12 schools are the highest funded line item in the state budget, and every year I have served in office we have approved historic levels of investment that puts us into a higher bracket of states for spending per student– but as you all know too well this also doesn’t tell the full story, as much of that is reflected in carrying PERS and other debt, unlike other states. I also recognize that of that spending, in PPS the majority of the money comes from local revenue, which was massively constrained by measures 5 and 50 in the 90’s and continues to hurt our schools. I have always vocally supported increased funding for our state school budget and will continue to do so if it comes before me in the 2027 session, but I hope we all will take a deeper look at the decisions being made in and about our school districts. I look forward to the intensive work some of my colleagues are doing on the distribution formula, the QEM, and ways to address the increasing PERS debt.



To summarize: as the elected State Representative for HD 28, I stand with parents, teachers, and community members who are protesting and voicing their opposition to the proposed closure of Maplewood. And I join those calling for a delay to ensure that while we know change must happen, that it is done with accurate, accountable, and clearly transparent information.  Most importantly, as I have repeatedly heard from community parents– this isn't about one school versus another school. All PPS children are our children, so to speak, and we must make decisions centering all of them.



Thank you for taking the time to consider my thoughts as I speak up on behalf of our community. Again– I want to reiterate that I absolutely see the looming crisis before us in both the declining enrollment and birth rate. I know that bold action is needed, and I implore that action to be centered and based on vetted and accurate data, and taking all possible alternatives into consideration if they haven’t already been.

Please don’t hesitate to reach out with your concerns, questions, or feedback.

Sincerely,
Dacia
