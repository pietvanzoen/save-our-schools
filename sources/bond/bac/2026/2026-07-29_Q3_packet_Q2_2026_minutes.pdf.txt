Seismic Update:

Jennifer Eggers, Principal, Holmes Engineering & Sarah Norman, Executive Director RISE
Discussion:
   ●​ PPS has completed seismic work at 75 campuses since 2012, including partial and full
       retrofits
   ●​ Title I schools were noted and included; 21 of 34 Title I schools have received seismic
       improvements
   ●​ The district developed a seismic prioritization formula made up of the following:
           o​ 90% risk assessment based on Homes Engineering Report
           o​ 5% based on deferred maintenance, which includes scoring based on CSI/TSI
                designation which is a part of the Facility Condition Index (FCI)
           o​ 5% based on enrollment. Given we will be engaging in a rightsizing conversation
                we did not include schools with an enrollment of less than300 students.
   ●​ Committee members engaged in a discussion regarding the "Total" vs. "Targeted"
       seismic approach and how these decisions affect long-term building safety
   ●​ Several projects are applying for Seismic Rehabilitation Grant Program (SRGP) funding
   ●​ Members inquired about the contracting methods, specifically the use of hard-bid (lump
       sum) for roofing/targeted projects and CM/GC for total campus retrofits.

Consolidated Project Updates Project Highlights:

Joanna Evenson, Senior Director, RISE, PPS
Discussion:
   ●​ Staff presented the scope of the Summer 2026 construction cycle, which involves a $50
       million investment across more than 50 campuses.
   ●​ The committee reviewed progress on several athletic field and track renovations
       scheduled for completion before the fall season.
   ●​ Committee members asked questions regarding the logistics of managing a high volume
       of concurrent projects during the summer window.
   ●​ The district is executing a major "Device Refresh" for student and staff hardware.
   ●​ The discussion centered on the district’s ability to manage a high volume of concurrent
       "targeted" projects (roofing, seismic, and facility improvements). Staff noted that
       grouping these systemic upgrades allows for better budget control and reduces the
       long-term maintenance burden on the district's general fund.
