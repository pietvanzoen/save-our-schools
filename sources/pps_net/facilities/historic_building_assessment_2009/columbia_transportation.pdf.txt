Summary Description

Columbia Elementary School is situated in north Portland at 716 NE Marine Drive. The school campus consists of a primary U-shaped classroom and
administrative building (353A). A detached L-shaped building, originally utilized for automotive classes, is situated at the east side of the parcel.
This one story building features a gable roof and horizontal board siding. A concrete block bus garage (353B), utilized by Portland Public School’s
(PPS) transportation department, occupies the southwest portion of the campus. The wood frame building that rests on a poured concrete
foundation. The exterior is clad in horizontal board siding. Gable roofs, covered in asphalt shingles, cover the various wings that comprise the
building. The building features a very modest Colonial Revival detailing including a boxed cornice and a slender-columned portico that projects from
the east elevation. The fenestration consists of grouped metal frame windows.

Architectural Description

Columbia Elementary School is situated in north Portland at 716 NE Marine Drive. Located adjacent to the Columbia River, the school is surrounded
by a mixture of marinas, light industrial buildings, and recent multi-family residential developments. With its location adjacent to the Columbia River
on a 9.69-acre parcel lined with trees, Columbia Elementary School retains a park-like setting. The school campus consists of a primary U-shaped
classroom and administrative building. A detached L-shaped building, originally utilized for automotive classes, is situated at the east side of the
parcel. This building features a gable roof and horizontal board siding. A concrete block bus garage, utilized by Portland Public School’s (PPS)
transportation department, occupies the southwest portion of the campus. Athletic fields occupy the western edge of the campus.

The primary school building is a wood frame structure that rests on a poured concrete foundation. The exterior is clad in horizontal board siding.
Gable roofs, covered in asphalt shingles, cover the various wings that comprise the building. The building features Classical Revival style details
including a boxed cornice and columned portico that projects from the east elevation. The fenestration is comprised primarily of grouped metal
frame windows.

Interior plan

The primary entry to the building is from the east into a lobby, flanked by office and classroom spaces. Double loaded corridors extend north-south
and east-west to provide access to the classroom and community spaces. The primary classroom and administrative spaces occupy the north and
central portion of the U-shaped building. This portion of the school is single story. A two story wing, located at the south portion of the building,
houses the gymnasium. PPS transportation department utilizes the one story wing located in the southwest section of the U-shaped building. The
floors of the corridors are covered with 6” x 6” tile. Fluorescent light fixtures, suspended from the acoustic tile ceiling, illuminate the corridors.

The primary community spaces for the school are provided in a multi-purpose room and gymnasium. The multi-purpose room is located at the
northwest end of the building. This space features glulaminated arches. The gymnasium, located in the two story portion of the school, features a
double height space lined with vertical tongue-and-groove paneling. A narrow balcony wraps around the room.

The classrooms are primarily rectangular with built-in cabinetry lining an interior wall. Many classrooms also feature wood chair, picture, and base
moldings. Ventilation for the classrooms is provided through either an operable awning panel or a louvered ventilation panel.

Oil fueled boilers, located within the basement, provide heat for the school. Heat is provided to the classrooms through registers set within a soffit
beneath the window. Additional registers, enclosed in metal surrounds, line the corridors.

Alterations

There have been extensive alterations to the orginal campus and single story school building. The only remaining facility from the original campus is
the garage and shop that were built in 1912. An addition to the original 1937 building was added in 1946 (PPS Architectural Plans 1946). A covered
walkway on the north elevation of the school was removed at an unknown date (OHS Photo Columbia School 12-27-1942). In response to the need
for classroom space, additions were built in 1950 and 1956. In 1959 a new cafeteria and locker room facility were constructed for the school.
Although the double loaded corridors retain their original height and width, many spaces including the multi-purpose room and classrooms have
been subdivided and/or expanded. The fenestration and interior finishes throughout the building have been altered (Portland Facility Profile).

Statement of Significance

In 1907 School District 33, established a new school adjacent to the Columbia River on Marine Drive. The existing garage facility was added in
1912. The older school building soon became obsoletet; a new school building, including the present two story gymnasium, was constructed in
1937 as Portland was attempting to emerge from the Great Depression. In response to the need for classroom space, additions were built in 1950
and 1956. In 1959 architect Donald Edmundson designed a new cafeteria and locker room facility for the school. Since the establishment of the
school in 1907, ownership has been transferred multiple times before its current annexation by Portland Public Schools in 1964 (Portland
Chronology Binder). The Transportation building was added in 1993.

Although archival information does not indicate the architect of the original school building and the early addition, the architect of the 1959 wing was
well versed in the design of public buildings. A native of Oregon, Donald Edmundson graduated from Salem High School before attending North
Pacific Evangelistic School and classes at the University of Oregon Extension School. In the mid-1920s Edmundson gained experience in the office of
Houghtaling & Dougan, the architects of Washington High School. During World War II he worked for the Vancouver Housing Authority. From 1943-
1953 Edmundson worked independently until he formed a partnership with Neil R. Kochendoerfer. The firm added Evan Kennedy, as engineer to
the partnership Edmundson, Kochendoerfer & Kennedy in 1962. Edmundson is listed as the sole architect of Clinton Kelly School and an addition to
Cleveland High School. With Neil Kochendoerfer he designed Wilson High School. During Kennedy’s tenure, the firm designed the gymnasium
addition for Cleveland High School. Other important projects designed by the firm were Morgan’s Alley and O’Bryant Park in Portland and hospitals in
the Dalles, Hood River, and Pendleton, Oregon (Ritz 2003: 122).

As a result of the extensive additions to the school and campus Columbia School retains little integrity. The various wings of the school are designed
in distinctively different manners and feature incompatible fenestration and building materials. Due to the additions and interior alterations and
resulting lack of coherent building vocabulary, Columbia School is not eligible for the NRHP under Criterion A, B, or C.
