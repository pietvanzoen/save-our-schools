ARCHITECTURAL / PROPERTY DESCRIPTION
(Include expanded description of the building/property, setting, significant landscape features, outbuildings, and alterations)
Summary Description

The Green Thumb Facility is situated in the Brentwood Darlington neighborhood of southeast Portland at 6801 SE 60th Avenue. The 13.05 acre-
campus consists of a residential style office/caretaker building, an L-shaped classroom building, a rectangular portable, three greenhouses, a
rectangular workshop building, a vehicle storage structure, and several small sheds. The collection of buildings is surrounded by large fields, several
smaller planting areas, and multiple clusters of trees.

Architectural Description

The Green Thumb Facility is situated in the Brentwood Darlington neighborhood of southeast Portland at 7200 SE 60th Avenue. Development in the
neighborhood consists of primarily single family residences constructed between 1925 and 1950 (Sanborn Maps updated to 1950). The 13.05-acre
campus retains a pastoral quality due to its location immediately to the west of Lane Middle School and Brentwood Park. Fields, planting areas, and
fruit trees occupy the south and north portions of the campus. The primary entrance to the facility is from SE 60th Avenue.

The principal structures on the campus are three greenhouses. The largest, a 22,019 square foot rectangular complex, is comprised of five attached
rectangular structures located in the center of the parcel. The double height space is covered by a gable roof supported by steel trusses. Cladding
for the exterior consists of corrugated composition panels. The middle panels are operable in the manner of a hopper window. Two additional
greenhouses are located in the northwest corner of the campus. Cladding for the wood frame structures consists of a corrugated acrylic material.
An arched metal frame supports the roofs.

The primary conditioned spaces are located in the northeast corner of the campus. The largest building is an L-shaped classroom building. The
wood frame structure is clad in plywood siding. Fenestration consists of grouped metal frame windows. Asphalt shingles clad the cross gable roof.
A portable classroom building, covered by a gable roof, provides space for two classrooms. Cladding for the building consists of plywood panels
sandwiched between the expressed steel frame. A two story residential style building serves as the office and caretaker’s space. This building is
clad in horizontal board siding. A broad roof overhang covers a 2nd story deck and a ground floor patio.

Other structures on the campus include a rectangular workshop, a vehicle storage building, and two sheds. Corrugated metal panels provide the
cladding for the walls and roof of the Industrial style workshop. The gable roof is supported by metal arches. The vehicle storage building is covered
by a shed roof. Cladding consists of metal panels on the north, west, and south elevations and operable chain link gates on the east elevation. The
utilitarian sheds are covered with T-1-11 siding.

HISTORY
(Chronological, descriptive history of the property from its construction through at least the historic period [preferably to the present])
Statement of Significance

In 1970, Portland Public Schools established a career development program in which students developed new skills in the area of construction and
landscape maintenance. The program initially operated in rented space at the Jeibmann Greenhouses. In 1974 the district devoted a portion of the
Lane High School site for the program. The initial structures on the site included a portable, a utility building, a storage shed, and the principal
green house. In 1976 students from Marshall High School constructed the caretaker’s house/office building. The three-room classroom building was
added to the campus in 1986 (Portland Chronology Binder). The district currently leases a large portion of the greenhouse facility to Portland State
University and the Chinese Gardens.

Although representative of an innovative program designed to assist students in developing job skills, the Green Thumb Facility does not appear to
be eligible for the NRHP under Criterion A for an association with the development of Portland Public Schools planning, curriculum, or other events.
The Green Thumb facility is not eligible for the NRHP under Criterion C as archival research does not indicate it is associated with the work of a
significant builder or architect. Additionally, the eclectic range of buildings and structures do not embody the distinctive characteristics of a type,
period, or method of construction.
