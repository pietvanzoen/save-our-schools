        Portland Public Schools Board Approves One-Year Agreement with Portland Association of
                                             Teachers
    Agreement provides near-term stability while positioning district to address long-term financial
                                            challenges

PORTLAND, Ore. — The Portland Public Schools Board of Education tonight approved a one-year
collective bargaining agreement with the Portland Association of Teachers (PAT), following ratification by
union membership.

The agreement provides short-term stability for educators, students, and families while allowing the
district to continue navigating significant financial challenges facing public education locally and
nationwide.

Portland Public Schools entered bargaining with a responsibility to balance support for employees with
stewardship of public resources during a period of declining enrollment, rising operational costs, and a
substantial structural budget deficit.

Dr. Kimberlee Armstrong, Superintendent: “In this critical moment, I appreciate the Portland Association
of Teachers’ recognition of the financial realities facing our district and the difficult decisions required
ahead. We are committed to doing the work necessary to protect student learning, preserve meaningful
school experiences, and steward public resources with discipline and urgency. Strong financial
stewardship cannot be a side conversation; it must be central to how we lead, and we are moving
forward with that focus."
Because this is a one-year agreement, it creates an opportunity for both parties to revisit compensation
conversations with greater clarity around future budget conditions.

PPS remains focused on protecting classroom learning, maintaining safe and supportive school
environments, and making responsible decisions that support the long-term health of the district.

The district recognizes the complexity of this moment for school systems across the country and remains
committed to continued partnership with educators in service of students.
For more information about Portland Public Schools budget planning and district priorities, visit pps.net.

About Portland Public Schools
Portland Public Schools, founded in 1851, is Oregon’s largest PK-12 school district, preparing students to “lead change, and improve the world.” With more than
47,000 students in 81 schools, PPS strives to ensure academic excellence and personal success for every student, especially our Black, Native American, students of
color and/or with disabilities. Central to this goal is affirming and operationalizing our deeply held community value of racial equity, social justice and inclusion. Learn
more at pps.net.
