 Portland Public Schools Provides Board of Education Rightsizing Scenarios
            Scenarios A and B propose closing 14 and 11 schools, along with boundary,​
                                grade-level and program changes

Oct. 5, 2026 (PORTLAND, ORE.) —Portland Public Schools today sent Rightsizing proposals to the Board of
Education to address decades of declining and increasingly uneven enrollment. Scenario A proposes
closing 14 schools and Scenario B proposes closing 11, along with boundary, grade-level and program
changes. A third option would defer the decision and maintain the current school configuration for now,
without addressing the district’s declining and uneven enrollment.
The Board asked the superintendent to provide data and possible scenarios to review. PPS will continue to
gather feedback and engage with students, parents and the community through November. The
superintendent will consider the feedback received and deliver a final recommendation to the Board,
which will vote in December. Changes approved by the Board will begin taking effect in fall 2027.
“We know how deeply connected students, families, educators and communities are to their schools, and
considering the closure of any school is extraordinarily difficult,” said Superintendent Dr. Kimberlee
Armstrong. “But we also have a responsibility to look at Portland Public Schools as a whole and consider
changes that give every student access to strong programs, stable staffing and the opportunities and
support they deserve.”

Deferring the Decision
Deferring the decision would maintain the district’s current school configuration for now, with no
immediate school closures or consolidations, boundary changes, grade-level reconfigurations or program
relocations. As enrollment continues to decline and become increasingly uneven, staffing and resources
would remain stretched, potentially limiting programs and opportunities for students. The Board would
need to revisit school closures and other changes in a future year.

Other Changes Included in Scenarios A and B
Both scenarios would relocate Odyssey from the East Sylvan school building to Metropolitan Learning
Center. MLC would remain open as a K–8 school, but its grades 9–12 program would close and students
would be moved to an appropriate educational option. The move would leave East Sylvan vacant and
available for consideration through the district’s separate building-repurposing process. No alternative or
focus-option school is proposed for closure.

Seven K–8 schools would become K–5 schools. Beverly Cleary’s grades 6–8 would move to Beaumont;
Laurelhurst’s to Mt. Tabor; Sunnyside’s to Hosford; Bridger Creative Science’s to designated neighborhood
middle schools; César Chávez’s to George; Faubion’s to Ockley Green; and Vernon’s to Harriet Tubman.

Dual Language Immersion programs would be consolidated to provide more consistent pathways across
grade levels, but no DLI program would be eliminated. Scott Spanish immersion would move to Rigler;
Beaumont Spanish immersion for grades 6–8 would move to Roseway Heights; Atkinson Spanish
immersion would move to Lent; Spanish immersion from Beach, James John and Sitton would move to
César Chávez; and Ockley Green Spanish immersion for grades 6–8 would move to George. Clark
Mandarin immersion would move to Woodstock, and Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Mandarin immersion
would move to Boise-Eliot/Humboldt. Under Scenario A, Rose City Park Vietnamese immersion would
move to Vestal; under Scenario B, it would remain at Rose City Park.

Both scenarios would also discontinue the environmental focus option at Sunnyside Environmental School
and transition the school into a neighborhood K–5 school. Sunnyside would no longer accept new lottery
transfers, while currently enrolled lottery students could remain through fifth grade.

Complete details for each scenario, including proposed changes by school along with boundary changes,
grade-level reconfigurations and program moves are available on the PPS website.

The scenarios reflect months of analysis and community engagement to better align schools, programs
and resources with current enrollment and build a stronger, more stable school system.
“These scenarios mark the next phase of an important community conversation,” Armstrong said. “We
need to hear from students, families, educators and communities about how these proposals would affect
them and what the district must consider before the Board makes a decision in December.”

Student enrollment in PPS has declined while the district has continued to operate essentially the same
number of schools. Enrollment has also become increasingly uneven: some schools remain large, while
others serve significantly fewer students, making it more difficult to provide consistent staffing, programs
and support across the district.

Detailed school by school information and a complete description of each scenario and all proposed
changes are available at pps.net.

About Portland Public Schools
Portland Public Schools, founded in 1851, is Oregon’s largest PK–12 school district, with more than 43,000
students in 81 schools. PPS aspires to graduate compassionate, critical thinkers who are able to
collaborate to solve problems and lead a more socially just world. Central to this goal is affirming and
operationalizing our deeply held community value of racial equity, social justice and inclusion. Learn more
at pps.net.
