Portland School Board Votes to Change High School Attendance Boundaries
   Vote establishes Jefferson as a comprehensive high school and initiates
                           phased implementation
​
Jan. 13, 2026 (PORTLAND, Ore.) – Tonight, the Portland Public Schools Board of Education approved two
resolutions that together mark a turning point for Jefferson High School and for students across North
and Northeast Portland.

The Board first voted to sunset Jefferson’s dual assignment policy and affirm the school as a
comprehensive high school, restoring clarity of purpose and long-term commitment to its community.
The Board then approved new attendance boundaries and feeder patterns, along with a phased
implementation plan with follow-up actions to support students, families, and schools as the changes
take effect.

“This vote affirms a long-term commitment to Jefferson as a comprehensive high school and to the
students it serves.” said Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong. “Our responsibility now is to deliver on
that commitment through strong programs, sustained investment, and continued partnership with the
community.”

The approved boundaries will end Jefferson’s dual assignment policy and establish standard
neighborhood attendance boundaries for Jefferson and nearby high schools, beginning with incoming
ninth graders in the 2027–28 school year, with full phase-in by 2030–31. Jefferson High School will serve
students from the Beach, Boise-Eliot/Humboldt, Chief Joseph, Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., Faubion, Sabin,
Vernon, and Woodlawn school communities. Irvington will feed to Grant High School, while Peninsula
and the Beach Spanish Immersion program will feed to Roosevelt High School. The Dr. Martin Luther King
Jr. Chinese Immersion program will continue to feed to Jefferson.

The Board’s action follows months of engagement and analysis, including six community listening
sessions with hundreds of participants, a School Board listening session, and multilingual surveys.
Community feedback emphasized the need for clarity, stable enrollment, equitable access to academic
and extracurricular programming, and transparency about next steps.

“The Board’s vote reflects both community input and our responsibility to act,” Board Chair Eddie
Wang said. “This decision provides direction, accountability, and a clear framework for
strengthening Jefferson while maintaining stability across neighboring high schools.”

Jefferson High School is currently in the planning phase of a $465 million modernization, with
construction beginning in spring 2026 and new facilities opening in fall 2029. In response to community
concerns about timing, construction impacts, and access to programming during the transition, the
district is committing to sustained engagement and clear communication as implementation moves
forward.

Dr. Jon Franco, Senior Chief of Operations, emphasized that implementation will be deliberate and well
resourced. “With the Board’s action tonight, we can move forward with coordinated planning that aligns
enrollment, staffing, facilities, and modernization,” Franco said. “This clarity allows us to focus on
execution and support schools through a smooth, well-managed transition.”

What Comes Next

Following tonight’s vote, PPS is committing to:

    ●​ Phased implementation beginning in 2027–28 to ensure continuity for current students and
    ●​ families
    ●​ Targeted investment in staffing and academic programming at Jefferson, including expanded
       course offerings, electives, and extracurriculars
    ●​ Ongoing engagement with feeder schools and communities, including bringing Jefferson feeder
       schools together later this spring to build shared identity and community
    ●​ Regular monitoring and public reporting on enrollment, programming access, and student
       outcomes as the plan is implemented

Vice Chair Michelle DePass underscored the importance of follow-through. “This action acknowledges
that past policies produced unequal outcomes,” DePass said. “Our charge now is to ensure Jefferson’s
future is shaped by equity, access, and opportunity, not uncertainty.”

“This is generational work,” Armstrong added. “Our responsibility now is to deliver through action,
partnership, and sustained investment on the promise of Jefferson Rising.”

About Portland Public Schools
Portland Public Schools, founded in 1851, is Oregon’s largest PK-12 school district, with more than 43,000
students in 81 schools. PPS aspires to graduate compassionate, critical thinkers who are able to
collaborate to solve problems, and lead a more socially-just world. Central to this goal is affirming and
operationalizing our deeply held community value of racial equity, social justice and inclusion. Learn more
at pps.net.
