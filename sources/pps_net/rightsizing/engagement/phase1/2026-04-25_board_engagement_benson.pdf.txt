                          Board Engagement Summary at Benson High School
                                          April 25, 2026

Between 8-15 respondents answered each Slido question during the engagement session held at Benson
High School on April 25. While over 10 school communities were represented, Winterhaven K-8 and
Alameda Elementary School were the most widely represented schools at this engagement session, and
approximately 86% of participants were connected to grades K-5. Approximately 80% of attendees
identified as white. Due to the small number of participants, other demographic information is
suppressed. Only 13% of families responding had experienced a boundary change in the last five years;
an additional 13% experienced their school’s grade bands changing. Nearly half (44%) of respondents
enrolled their students in focus option programs.

Of the six factors in the PPS student assignment policy, respondents prioritized boundaries that promote
safer routes to schools and a sense of community as well as recognize and address natural and
human-made barriers, feeder patterns that allows as many students as possible to continue together
from one school level to the next, and program and enrollment stability in the surrounding schools over
other factors. See Chart 1 for a breakdown of responses to this question.

When asked to rank four factors that make a positive school environment, respondents identified a wide
variety of learning opportunities, access to services that meet every student’s learning needs, learning
alongside children from different backgrounds, ability of children who live close together to attend the
same school, and access to services that meet every student’s learning needs (including special
education as the highest priorities. See Chart 2 for a breakdown of responses to this question.

Chart 2:
Responses to the question, “select the top four factors that you think make a positive school

Nine (9) respondents answered the question, “What has helped you/your student feel connected as they
move from one school to the next?” Families shared that welcoming teachers and proactive outreach
from counselors and other school staff helped students acclimate to new learning environments. Beyond
staff support, the greatest sense of security comes from consistency and attending school with
established friends and neighbors.

Overall, feedback from this listening session emphasized that neighborhood schools act as community
hubs and expressed fear that the closure of any neighborhood schools would erode trust and lower
property values. When thinking about remaking school boundaries, participants encouraged prioritizing
underprivileged families and neighborhood proximity. Prioritizing stable, desirable local options is the
only way to stop the loss of enrollment to the suburbs or private institutions.
