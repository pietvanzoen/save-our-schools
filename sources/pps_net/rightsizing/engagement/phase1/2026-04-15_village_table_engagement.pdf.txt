                                     Board Engagement Summary:
                                             Village Table
                                            April 15, 2026

Between 9-15 responses were collected per question at the Village Table engagement session on April
15, 2026. An array of school communities and grade bands were represented, though 87% of
respondents were associated with K-5 schools. 27% of participants identified Black/African American, 7%
as American Indian/Alaska Native, 20% as Asian, 7% as Hispanic/Latinx, 7% as Pacific Islander, 67% as
White, and 7% as Multiracial. A third of respondents have experienced a boundary change in the last 5
years.

Of the six factors in the PPS student assignment policy, 12 members of the Village Table prioritized
boundaries that promote safer routes to schools and a sense of community as well as recognize and
address natural and human-made barriers, student body demographics, and feeder patterns that allows
as many students as possible to continue together from one school level to the next. Chart 1 shows the
number of votes each factor received.

When asked to rank four factors that make a positive school environment, 12 respondents’ top choices
were a wide variety of learning opportunities (11), learning alongside students from different
backgrounds (7), and access to services that meet every students’ learning needs (10). A warm and
welcoming school environment (5) and the ability of children to walk or bike safely to school (5) were

When asked, “what has helped you/your student feel connected as they move from one school to the
next?”, eight families responded, reporting that maintaining connections with familiar classmates and
consistent staff is the most vital factor in feeling supported during transitions caused by boundary shifts,
grade-level changes, or building modernizations. Successful transitions were often facilitated by
dedicated special education teachers, parent-teacher engagement, and administrative efforts to keep
siblings or peer cohorts together.

Finally, Village Table members were asked, “What should leaders understand about your community
before making decisions like this?” In response, the VIllage table advocated that the district must look
beyond budget numbers to understand the community impact of school changes, particularly for
marginalized families whose voices remain underrepresented in the current process. They emphasize
that true equity requires protecting the stability of neighborhood programs that have already been
weakened by previous transitions. Ultimately, parents are calling for a transparent process that explains
the reason behind these decisions.
