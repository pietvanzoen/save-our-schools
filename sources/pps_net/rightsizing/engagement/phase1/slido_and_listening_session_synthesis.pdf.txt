                             Right Size. Right Supports. Right for Students.
                                     Synthesis of Engagement Data
                                                April 2026

Executive Summary
Throughout April 2026, the Family and Community Engagement (FACE) team, the Office of School
Planning Modernization, and the Office of Strategic Project Management hosted a series of engagement
sessions to gather feedback on the school rightsizing process. This effort included six community
sessions, a dedicated student advisory session, and meetings with school staff. While the engagement
reached a broad cross-section of the district, the resulting data is disproportionately representative of
white families (76-80% across most sessions) and families of students identified as Gifted and Talented
(TAG). The majority of participants were connected to K-5 school communities, and nearly a third had
students enrolled in focus option programs.

When asked to rank the six factors of the PPS Student Assignment Policy, a clear system-wide preference
emerged for compact boundaries, "right pathways" (ensuring predictable transitions from elementary to
middle and high school), and program balance. However, individual sessions revealed localized
deviations from this trend. For instance, families at the Village Table session—representing more
marginalized communities—placed a much higher priority on student body demographics and safer
routes to school. In contrast, families at Ida B. Wells and Benson prioritized community and enrollment
stability, viewing the preservation of established neighborhood hubs as essential to preventing
enrollment loss to private institutions.

Participants identified several key factors as essential for a positive school environment, led by a wide
variety of learning opportunities and access to specialized services for all student needs. While a warm
and welcoming atmosphere was a universal priority, the value placed on diversity varied by session.
Participants at Village Table, Kellogg, and Roosevelt explicitly prioritized "learning alongside students
from different backgrounds," whereas other sessions focused more on geographic proximity and the
ability of neighborhood children to attend the same school. This highlights a tension between the desire
for integrated, diverse learning environments and the practical convenience of neighborhood-based
schooling.

                                             Synthesis of Slido Data
During the month of April, Portland Public School’s Family and Community Engagement (FACE) team, the
Office of School Planning Modernization, and the Office of Strategic Project Management worked
together to host community engagement sessions regarding the school rightsizing process. A total of six
engagement sessions with parents and community members were held, as well as one session with
students, and two sessions with elementary school front office staff. During each session, participants
were given the opportunity to use a personal electronic device to answer a series of questions using
Slido, a digital polling tool. After each engagement session, data were shared with members of the
Research, Assessment, and Accountability team for synthesis. This memo synthesizes the feedback
received from parents and other community stakeholders across sessions using Slido.

The Slido was available in English, with paper copies available in Chinese, Somali, Spanish, Russian, and
Vietnamese by request. As of April 28th, no data in languages other than English was shared with the
Research, Assessment, and Accountability team. While the exact number of participants in each session
is not available, we are able to report the number of responses for each Slido item. Please note: all
demographic questions encouraged participants to select all that apply, and total counts might be higher
than the overall number of participants. For that reason, we report counts rather than percentages.

Identifying Key Factors in Student Assignment Policy
The Portland Public Schools Student Assignment Policy asks stakeholders to consider the six factors,
including:
    ●​ Right Pathways: A feeder pattern that allows as many students as possible to continue together
        from one school level to the next
    ●​ Right Mix of Students: Student body demographics
    ●​ Right Boundaries: Compact boundaries that promote safer routes to schools and a sense of
        community as well as recognize and address natural and human-made barriers
    ●​ Right use of Space: Optimal use of existing facilities
    ●​ Right Balance: Program and enrollment stability in the surrounding schools
    ●​ Right Impact: Limiting the impact of boundary changes to the smallest number of students
        possible

Defining a Positive School Environment
In 2015, a survey was deployed, asking families what they hoped PPS would be like in 2025. The factors
that were included as options were:
    ●​ Learning alongside children from different backgrounds
    ●​ Opportunities for parent involvement
    ●​ Access to dual language immersion
    ●​ A warm and welcoming school environment
    ●​ Wide variety of learning opportunities including access to music, art, library, and physical
        education
    ●​ Access to after-school programs
    ●​ Ability of children who live close together to attend the same school
    ●​ Access to learning in the student’s preferred language
    ●​ Ability of children to walk or bike safely to school
    ●​ Access to services that meet every student’s learning needs (including special education, English
        as a second language, talented and gifted program)

                                     Discussion and Differentiation
Respondent groups in individual listening sessions had significant differences in how they prioritized the
six factors of the Student Assignment Policy. While this overall synthesis highlights compact boundaries,
right pathways, and program balance as the top three factors system-wide, the individual showed
different values.
                                                                                                           8

Geographic proximity was the dominant priority for families in Southwest and Southeast Portland, as
well as for many focus-option families, while feeder patterns and compact boundaries were prioritized
by respondents who attended sessions at Wells, Benson, and Roosevelt. Additionally, while student body
demographics was ranked lower in the overall synthesis (often sitting in the bottom three), it was a
top-tier priority for members of the Village Table and respondents at Roosevelt.

When asked about the factors that make a positive school environment, participants across different
sessions showed distinct differences in what they prioritized for a positive school environment. While a
wide variety of learning opportunities and access to services for all students were universal top
concerns, groups differed on the importance of geographic proximity versus diversity and welcoming
environments. Members of the Village Table, as well as respondents who participated in sessions at
Kellogg, Roosevelt, and Benson ranked the opportunity to learn alongside children from different
backgrounds higher than families that attended the Southwest Portland session at Ida B. Wells. However,
there was greater turnout at Wells than at other sessions.

In sessions with high concentrations of Talented and Gifted (TAG) families and those that chose focus
option programs (e.g., the virtual session and the session at Ida B. Wells), participants expressed
different priorities. For example, many families that attended the Ida B. Wells session stated that
specialized focus programs or K-8 models keep families opting into enrollment in Portland Public Schools
rather than opting for private education. Similarly, families of Gifted and Talented students at the Virtual
Session reported moving schools specifically to escape unaddressed learning needs in their assigned
neighborhood schools, prioritizing academic rigor over geographic proximity.

                                        Conclusions and Next Steps
After a series of community engagement sessions, the Family and Community Engagement (FACE) team,
the Office of School Planning Modernization, and the Office of Strategic Project Management have
collected community feedback about priorities for the Student Assignment Policy and information on
what makes a positive school environment across Portland Public Schools. This information can be used
to inform the priorities in future conversations about school rightsizing.

It is worth considering the limitations of the community voice synthesized across these sessions. White
families and families with students labeled as Gifted and Talented (TAG) are disproportionately
represented in this dataset, and only English-language responses were collected and synthesized.
