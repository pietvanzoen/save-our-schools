                                        Rightsizing Phase 1:
                                   Methods and After-Action Review

Phase 1 of Portland Public Schools’ Rightsizing efforts took place from April to June of 2026. This period
of planning and engagement centered on “Listening and Learning.” The purpose of engagement and data
collection in Phase 1 was to generally hear feedback and concerns from the community, as well as
identify guiding principles to shape the updated Student Assignment Policy and guide the development
of rightsizing scenarios. To do this, the Office of School Modernization, the Office of Family and
Community Engagement, and the Research, Assessment, and Accountability department worked
together to build community engagement events that would systematically collect data from and about
engagement participants.

Engagement and Data Collection
Throughout April 2026, the Family and Community Engagement (FACE) department, the Office of School
Planning Modernization, and the Office of Strategic Project Management hosted a series of engagement
sessions to gather feedback on the school rightsizing process. This effort included six community
sessions, a dedicated student advisory session, and meetings with school staff. Additionally, two focus
groups were held to conduct an equity audit of the Southeast Guiding Coalition, a rightsizing effort that
took place from 2020 to 2024. Insights from this equity audit helped to drive decision-making in the
current iteration of rightsizing. Table 1 lists the engagement sessions that took place during Phase 1.

Slido Polls
During engagement sessions, participants utilized the digital polling tool Slido to submit responses.
While the poll itself was in English, there were paper copies of the questions available in Simplified
Chinese, Somali, Spanish, Russian, and Vietnamese upon request. After the conclusion of the Board
listening sessions, the same Slido poll was sent out to all district families regardless of whether they
attended a listening session. Altogether, approximately 900 stakeholders responded to this poll. The
questions included in the Slido were adapted from the 2015 Oregon Kitchen Table survey, as well as the
Student Assignment Policy. See Appendix A to view the questions asked in the Slido poll.

While the engagement reached a broad cross-section of the district, the responses were
disproportionately representative of white families (76-80% across most sessions) and families of
students identified as Talented and Gifted (TAG). The majority of participants were connected to K-5
school communities, and nearly a third had students enrolled in focus option programs. See the Slido
and Listening Session Synthesis Memo for more information.

When asked to rank the six factors of the PPS Student Assignment Policy, a clear system-wide preference
emerged for compact boundaries, "right pathways" (ensuring predictable transitions from elementary to
middle and high school), and program balance. However, individual sessions revealed localized
deviations from this trend. For instance, families at the Village Table session—representing more
marginalized communities—placed a much higher priority on student body demographics and safer
routes to school. In contrast, families at Ida B. Wells and Benson prioritized community and enrollment
stability, viewing the preservation of established neighborhood hubs as essential to preventing
enrollment loss to private institutions.

Participants also identified several key factors as essential for a positive school environment, led by a
wide variety of learning opportunities and access to specialized services for all student needs. While a
warm and welcoming atmosphere was a universal priority, the value placed on diversity varied by
session. Participants at Village Table, Kellogg, and Roosevelt explicitly prioritized "learning alongside
students from different backgrounds," whereas other sessions focused more on geographic proximity
and the ability of neighborhood children to attend the same school. This highlights a tension between
the desire for integrated, diverse learning environments and the practical convenience of
neighborhood-based schooling.

FACE Department Feedback Form
In addition to the Slido poll, the FACE Department internally developed a feedback form and used it to
develop an informational flier summarizing what had been heard. A total of 227 people responded to
this form. This drew from a different data source than the Slido or Slido Summary Memos.
Culturally Specific Outreach
At the conclusion of Phase 1, the demographic data from the Slido polls showed that Black and Native
voices were underrepresented in the feedback PPS has received. In response, the FACE Department
planned two culturally specific engagement events: one for Native families (June 15) and one for Black
families (June 11).

SEGC Equity Audit. Portland Public Schools implemented the Enrollment and Program Balancing
initiative, often called the Southeast Guiding Coalition or SEGC, as part of a broader district effort to
address enrollment imbalances, program access, and related equity concerns across Southeast Portland
schools between 2020 and 2024. In 2026, PPS partnered with Dr. Terrance Green to evaluate the equity
impact of SEGC to better understand how SEGC-related changes were experienced by students, families,
principals, educators, and staff, in schools that experienced boundary changes, program relocations,
enrollment shifts, and other related transitions. Additionally, Dr. Green’s team analyzed patterns in
student outcomes in these impacted schools before and after SEGC implementation, while recognizing
that these outcomes are shaped by multiple factors and should not be interpreted as solely caused by
SEGC. To that end, this team implemented surveys that received over 1200 responses, as well as
conducted 50 focus groups and 10 interviews with families and staff impacted by SEGC.

How Data Informed Policy
Feedback from the Slido polls was synthesized after each engagement session and summaries were
posted on the Rightsizing website. A summary memo considering feedback from across sessions was also
written and posted. Additionally, this information was used to update the Student Assignment Policy and
identify the guiding principles for Phase 2 of Rightsizing.

Lessons Learned and Next Steps for Phase 2
Through the engagement and data collection process in Phase 1, we learned several lessons that will
improve data collection and representation for Phase 2 of the Rightsizing process.

Attendees at the engagement sessions (and subsequently, respondents to the Slido informational poll)
were majority white and parents of elementary-aged students. These voices were disproportionately
represented when compared with the district’s overall demographics. To address this, we will identify
sampling thresholds for stakeholders in Phase 2 which will help us to know when we have heard from a
representative sample of district stakeholders. To accompany these goals, we will also identify sampling
and outreach strategies to increase underrepresented voices, as well as establish protocols to
supplement survey data collection with focus groups and other forms of data collection when needed.

Additionally, we intend to align data sources more closely in Phase 2. Instead of operating with multiple
polls and forms, we hope to establish shared data collection platforms used by all PPS stakeholders to
collect standardized data regardless of the session attended. This way, we can more easily synthesize
feedback we hear from various community stakeholders.
