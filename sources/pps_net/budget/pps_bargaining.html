Portland Public Schools
Collective Bargaining

Overview

Portland Public Schools engages in collective bargaining with its labor partners as part of our commitment to supporting students, staff, and school communities. Bargaining is a structured, legally defined process where the district and employee groups meet to discuss working conditions, compensation, and other employment-related topics. These conversations take place over time and involve multiple steps, including proposal development, discussion, and review. This page serves as a central source of verified, high-level information about the bargaining process and will be updated as key milestones are reached. While not every detail can be shared during negotiations, Portland Public Schools remains committed to clear communication, transparency, and continuity for students and families throughout the process.

How Bargaining Works at Portland Public Schools

  • 1. Notice to Bargain
  • 2. Proposal Exchange
  • 3. Ongoing Negotiations
  • 4. Mediation (If Needed)
  • 5. Ratification
  • 6. Implementation

1. Notice to Bargain

When a contract is nearing expiration, either the district or the labor organization may initiate bargaining by providing formal notice. This signals the start of negotiations for a successor agreement.

2. Proposal Exchange

Both parties develop and present proposals related to contract terms. These proposals are discussed at scheduled bargaining sessions. Discussions may involve clarification, revisions, and counterproposals.

3. Ongoing Negotiations

Bargaining sessions occur over time. Some topics may reach tentative agreement quickly, while others require additional discussion. Tentative agreements are documented but are not final until the full contract is ratified.

4. Mediation (If Needed)

If the parties are unable to reach agreement on certain issues, a neutral state-appointed mediator may assist in facilitating further discussion. Mediation is a common and structured part of public-sector bargaining in Oregon.

5. Ratification

Once a full tentative agreement is reached, both parties complete their respective approval processes. For labor organizations, this typically includes a member vote. For the district, the agreement is presented to the School Board for approval at a public meeting.

6. Implementation

After ratification and Board approval, the new contract goes into effect according to the agreed-upon timeline.

What the Public Can Expect

Because collective bargaining is an active negotiation process, not all details can be shared in real time. PPS provides high-level updates when milestones are reached and will communicate confirmed information that impacts students, families, and staff.

While negotiations focus on employment matters, the district’s priority remains continuity of instruction, student support, and stable school operations.

Oregon-Specific Context

In Oregon public education, bargaining is governed by state law and overseen by the Employment Relations Board (ERB). The process is designed to support good-faith negotiations between public employers and labor organizations while maintaining essential public services.

PPS–PAT Substitute Bargaining Update for May 7, 2026

Dear PPS Staff, Families, and Community,

On Wednesday, May 7, 2026, Portland Public Schools (PPS) and Portland Association of Teachers (PAT) Substitutes Unit held their second bargaining session, continuing contract negotiations for a one-year successor agreement. 

PPS presented several proposals that included the following articles:

  • Article 2 (Association Rights). PAT proposed expanding time without loss of compensation from five to 10 Substitute Educators, which is a change from the current language of five Substitute Educators, and requested updated language changing “times” to “days” regarding their payment allocation.
  • Article 6 (Discipline Procedure). PAT proposed reducing the disciplinary document retention period from six years to three years; the district counterproposed four years to align with the core PAT collective bargaining agreement. 
  • On paid administrative leave, PAT proposed paying substitutes at the daily rate for up to two weeks. The district counterproposed a minimum days-worked threshold with a cap of ten (10) paid days, after which substitutes on paid administrative leave would be unpaid. 
  • Article 7 (Substitute Rights). PAT proposed a substantial rewrite of this article, including overload pay for substitutes assigned to classrooms where the main unit teacher is receiving overload pay. The district is reviewing the scope of proposed changes in the context of a one-year agreement.
  • Article 8 (Work Hours & Work Days). PAT proposed revisions to the late cancellation policy and the removal of minimum work day requirements. The district proposed  adding clarifying language for student contact days, specifying that two half-day assignments would constitute one full student contact day.
  • Article 9 (Sick Leave).
