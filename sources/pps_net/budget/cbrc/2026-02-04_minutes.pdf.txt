Meeting Called to Order
The meeting was called to order at 5:33 p.m. by Chair Adriel Person, who welcomed members and
invited Nichole Watson, Senior Director of Family & Community Engagement, to facilitate the
meeting and guide the Committee through the agenda.

Welcome and Introductions
Nichole welcomed Committee Members and staff, noting that this was the first CBRC meeting of
the year and the first meeting for several new members. Committee members (and staff)
introduced themselves sharing their names, preferred pronouns and new or returning member
status, as well as their connection to Portland Public Schools and an area of interest for this year.

Nichole acknowledged the scope and significance of the Committee’s charge and reaffirmed the
shared commitment to centering student experience while addressing fiscal constraints.
Housekeeping
   Meeting Logistics
   Meeting logistics and participation expectations were reviewed, including public meeting
   requirements and recognition that meetings are publicly noticed and recorded; procedures for
   public discussion; and methods for requesting staff follow-up outside of meetings.

   Staff confirmed that all meeting materials will be distributed by email and posted publicly on the
   CBRC webpage. A structured process will be implemented to track Committee questions and
   staff responses. Cheryl Pitman was identified as a primary point of contact.

   Meeting Schedule and Timeline
   PPS Budget Manager Alexandra Martin reviewed the CBRC meeting schedule and major
   milestones, including:
   • Monthly CBRC meetings through the spring
   • A joint work session with the Board of Education
   • Presentation of the Superintendent’s Proposed Budget (anticipated April 28)
   • CBRC work sessions to develop and finalize recommendations
   • Presentation of the CBRC report to the Board of Education in May


Committee members were advised to review the schedule and plan for meetings where quorum
and formal actions may be required.

CBRC Mission and Agreements
Nichole reviewed Committee operating expectations, including transparency, avoidance of conflicts
of interest, respectful engagement across differing viewpoints, balancing individual perspectives
with a system-wide lens, and supporting clear communication.

The CBRC’s mission to review, evaluate and make recommendations to the Board of Education was
discussed, as well as the importance of centering student voice and maintaining consistency with
prior CBRC recommendations; it was also clarified that student members participate in an advisory
capacity.

   District Context and Alignment
   Nichole shared an overview of the district’s guiding framework, including Board of Education
   goals, the PPS ReImagined Framework, and the District Continuous Improvement Plan. The
   Committee’s advisory role in supporting alignment between budget decisions and district
   priorities was reaffirmed.

CBRC Charter and Structure
Nichole reviewed the CBRC Charter, outlining:
   • CBRC’s advisory role to the Board of Education
   • Membership structure and terms
Staff clarified that a quorum requires a majority of adult Committee members and that motions
require a majority vote of the full Committee membership. In future, either the meeting facilitator or
the Committee Chair will ask at the beginning of each meeting whether a quorum (one more than
half) is present.

Reviewed CBRC leadership guidance and role of staff.Student members participate in an advisory
capacity; consistent with Board practice, their votes are recorded but do not count toward official
vote totals, which raised questions. It was noted that any changes would require Board action; staff
will look into this and report back to the Committee.

Committee operating agreements were reviewed, including:
  ● Discretion and transparency
  ● Avoiding conflicts of interest or the appearance of such
  ● Balancing personal experiences with a system-wide perspective
  ● Engaging respectfully across differing viewpoints

Members emphasized the importance of student voice in Committee deliberations. Staff
committed to returning with proposed approaches to further center student participation.

2026–27 Budget Context and Preliminary Information
Chief Financial Officer Michelle Morrison provided high-level background and context regarding
financial pressures contibuting to the current shortfall, reduction targets and the budget
development timeline.

Chief Morrison also presented preliminary budget assumptions and staffing recommendations,
noting that the information being shared is preliminary and remains subject to refinement.

Richard Smith, Senior DIrector Research Assessment and Accountability and Tami Booth, Budget
Director provided more detailed discussion of changes by grade level, clarifying and distinguishing
school-based impacts from central office reductions including:
    • Clear articulation of impacts on student experience
    • Interpretation of staffing ratios, class size overages, and FTE calculations
    • Anticipated use of attrition versus layoffs


Staff highlighted the fact that the recommendations are preliminary and that additional data,
clarification, and noted that updated materials will be provided at future meetings.

Chief Morrison presented additional slides outlining preliminary central office services and support
reductions, including both personnel and non-personnel impacts. She noted that while these
reductions are to the central office, they may have downstream impacts on schools, and
emphasized that the guiding objective is to preserve student experience. It was further clarified that
reported FTE reductions reflect standardized staffing calculations and do not directly equate to the
number of individual employees affected, as impacts may include changes to schedules,
assignments, or hours consistent with established human resources processes.
