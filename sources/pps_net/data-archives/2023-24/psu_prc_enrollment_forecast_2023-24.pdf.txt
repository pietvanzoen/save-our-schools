Abstract
This report summarizes the methodology and results of a demographic study
conducted by the Portland State University Population Research Center (PRC) for

Portland Public Schools (PPS). The study includes an analysis of population
dynamics, housing, and enrollment trends affecting PPS, and detailed annual

enrollment forecasts for the District overall, for students in each of its high school
clusters (HSCLs), and for students enrolled at each school according to grade, as

of October 1 of each school year.



Demographic Trends
Between 2000 and 2010, the PPS area grew at an annualized rate of 0.8%, from
426,110 persons to 460,694. In the most recent decade, growth accelerated:

between the 2010 and 2020 decennial censuses, the population grew at 1.2% per
year (to 519,048).

   The share of Portland’s overall city population residing within the PPS boundary
increased from 77 percent in 2010 to 80 percent in 2020. The PPS area is younger

on average than the rest of the state, with a much higher proportion of persons
age 20-39 (36%, compared to 27% statewide), and a lower median age (38.6
compared to 40.1 statewide).

   The birthrate in Portland has declined significantly, and is much lower than the
rest of the state. Approximately 2.5% of women 15-50 reported a birth in the past

year, compared to 4.6% statewide. The Total Fertility Rate in the city-- an
approximation of expected total lifetime births-- is fewer than one child per woman

(approximately 0.8), a significant decline from 1.6 in 2000.
   The Census Bureau counted 253,369 housing units in the PPS zone in 2020, up

from 219,365 in 2010 (an annual rate of growth of 1.4%). Multi-family units have
accounted for about 85 percent of new homes. Correspondingly, the average

household size has been trending downward for both rented and owner-occupied
units for at least the past 5 years (2.6 to 2.47 for owner occupied housing, 2.12 to

1.86 for renter occupied). The most rapidly growing segment of households since
2015 is those with just 1-2 people, and households with 3+ persons did not

increase.

   Household income in the PPS zone ($82,700) is higher than Multnomah overall
($78,300) and the statewide average ($71,500). New affordable housing projects

within PPS scheduled for occupancy from the 2022-23 school year onward and
2024 include about 1,367 family-size units of two or more bedrooms. These units

are expected to contribute approximately 689 more students across PPS schools
than would otherwise have been the case if only market-rate housing was built.



Enrollment Trends
In fall 2022, Portland Public Schools (PPS) enrolled 44,799 students in grades K-12,

a decrease of 206 students from fall 2021. This marked a significant recovery from
the preceding two years, which saw average declines of 1,500 students year-over-

year.

   Most of the 7.5 percent decline over the two-year period between fall 2019 and

fall 2021 was attributable to choices that families made during the COVID-19
pandemic, either by moving out of the District or by choosing other school options

including private schools, online charter schools, or homeschooling.

   The greatest declines during the COVID-19 pandemic were observed in
elementary grades, for example much smaller entering kindergarten (K) cohorts
than expected in 2020-21 and 2021-22, as the share of the population age 4-5 that

is enrolled in a PPS school (“K capture rate”) declined from approximately 80% in
2019 to 70% in 2020, and stabilized at 71% at the start of the 2021 and 2022 school

years.

   District-wide enrollment in secondary grades had experienced mostly steady
growth between 2010-2019. The pandemic caused a reversal of the trend for

middle grades; losses in enrollment in 6th-8th grades averaged 400 students per
year between 2019 and 2021. These were only partly due the pandemic: declines

in elementary enrollment driven by low birthrates and a declining K capture rate
began to appear as early as 2015.

   High school enrollment continued to increase throughout the pandemic, with

9th-12th grades adding 155 students per year on average. Final enrollment in fall
2022 of 14,389 students marks the highest enrollment since 2005-06.

   Since the pandemic, the experiences have diverged for individual schools in the
PPS system. Some have seen a return to pre-pandemic enrollment patterns, and

others have seen a persistently low rate of enrollment from their neighborhoods.
The district overall capture rate has grown from a nadir of 77.9% in fall 2021 to

79.3% in 2022, but is still far from its recent high of 83% in the 2018-19 school year.



Enrollment Forecast: Methodology
The enrollment forecasts are made within the context of an overarching
demographic forecast of the district, and based on expected shares of the general

population that enroll in a PPS school at a critical juncture such as kindergarten,
first grade, sixth grade, or ninth grade. Capture rates of PPS residents at these
grades are predictive of these cohorts’ future attachment to PPS schools. The
enlargement or attrition of currently enrolled cohorts is determined by application

of grade progression ratios (GPR).

   A similar forecast is produced for each high school cluster (HSCL) by similar
methods, except that the incoming cohorts are modeled not by capture rate of the

general population of the district, but of the PPS forecast student body at each
grade level (therefore, capture rates for high school clusters sums to 100%).

   Each individual school has an independent forecast as well, where GPRs are

similarly applied and intake of new students is formulated as a share of the total
forecast high school cluster enrollment for the cluster to which each school

belongs (or, in the case of special programs that are not part of a HSCL, the share
of the total districtwide enrollment).

   An additional adjustment is made for the number of additional units in large
affordable housing developments with 2 or more bedrooms, which have higher

than average children per unit. Because they are not entirely market-driven
developments, these developments are treated as an exogenous source of

additional students in the schools that are served by the neighborhoods, where the
number of additional students is estimated based on citywide survey data.

   The forecasts are then compared for internal consistency. Discrepancies

between the districtwide forecast by grade and the forecast for HSCL by grade are
resolved by adjusting the HSCL forecast, either through adjustments to the GPRs

or to the specific enrollments at each HSCL. In a similar way, the individual school
forecasts are made consistent with the higher-level forecasts.

   The 2023-24 school year sees a number of significant changes, including
program moves, attendance boundary changes, facility openings and closures, and
other changes, including implementation of Southeast Enrollment and Program
Balancing. These were incorporated into the enrollment forecast based on data

provided by PPS on their timing, geographic scope, and affected populations.



Enrollment Forecast: Summary of Findings
The K-12 enrollment of 44,799 students in fall 2023 is 206 students (0.5 percent)
lower than the fall 2022 total, and nearly 4,000 students below the pre-pandemic

level in 2019-20.

   Enrollment is projected to continue to decline, at a decelerating rate, until
reaching low point of 39,129 in 2032-33. Thereafter, it begins to recover as the

historically small entering classes from the COVID period age out, and higher
capture rates in earlier grades provide larger incoming classes. Under this baseline

scenario, the enrollment could recover to 40,020 at the end of a 15-year period, in
the 2037-38 school year.

   The kindergarten/first grade capture are predictive of an age cohort’s future

attachment to PPS. To maintain enrollment, PPS will need to achieve a higher share

of age-eligible population enrolling in a PPS kindergarten or first grade classroom.

   The K/1 capture rate is currently low (approximately 71% of K and 76% of 1st
population, down from a high of 86-88% for K and 88-90% for 1st during 2007-
2013). The K/1 capture rate is expected to recover to 80%/83%, respectively, by
2030, their last pre-COVID values.

   Although K/1 capture rates are up slightly since the COVID years, the size of

entering K cohorts has not yet recovered: the underlying population age 4-5 that
forms the pool eligible for enrolling in K has also been in decline. This pressure

toward declining enrollment is due to declining birth rates since 2010, and is not
expected to reverse in the time horizon of the enrollment forecast. All future
growth in kindergarten/first enrollment therefore must come from a larger share

of the age eligible population entering PPS in the future.

   In total, PPS kindergartens are expected to enroll 3,113 students in the 2023-
24 school year, a 39-student decline from 2022-23. Elementary schools overall

decline from 20,192 to 19,901 students. The decline is projected to continue until
the 2029 school year, when the enrollment will begin to pick up from its low of

18,400.

   Middle schools similarly see declines next year, from 10,218 to 9,971 (-2%). As
smaller elementary cohorts progress through the middle school grades, enrollment

will continue to decline to a low of just 8,288 students in 2033, before resuming
growth.

   High schools were resilient during the pandemic and saw increases, even as
elementary and middle schools saw large declines. High school enrollment is

projected to grow again next year, from 14,389 to 14,416 (+27 or 0.2%). Eventually,
smaller classes in elementary and middle schools will exert strong downward

pressure on HS class sizes. While some years are likely to see fluctuations higher
or lower than projected, the overall trend is towards declining HS enrollment

through the 2032 school year, when total HS enrollment will fall to 39,129, before
beginning a recovery.
