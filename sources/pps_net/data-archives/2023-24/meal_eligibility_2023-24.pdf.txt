This report shows how many students are eligible for free meals. The enrollment data is as of October 2nd, whereas the counts and percentages of students eligible for free
meals reflect updates through November.


The federal government reimburses the District at a set rate for breakfasts and lunches through the National School Breakfast Program and the National School Lunch Program,
respectively. Breakfasts and lunches are available to all students at no cost at a site based on two reasons (See Note column):
 1. Schools identified as Community Eligibility Provisioning (CEP) schools. Through CEP, the federal government provides meals at no charge to all students, including students
who would not normally qualify for free or reduced meals.  CEP was developed to reduce paperwork for families and decrease barriers to accessing school meals by using
available data to determine the school community's eligibility for this provision; therefore paper applications are not collected or processed for CEP schools.
 2. Head Start is only available to students from low-income, qualifying families, and all students are eligible for free meals.
 3. Provision 2 (Prov 2) schools offer meals to all participating students at no charge for a period up to 4 years. During the first year, or base year, the school makes eligibility
determinations, including paper applications, and takes meal counts by eligibility. If the District can financially support the continuation of Provision 2 after fully assessing the
base year, meals may continue for up to 3 additional years pending no change in the student population (by boundary or grade change). During the next 3 years, the school
makes no new eligibility determinations and counts only the total number of reimbursable meals served each day. Reimbursement during these years by applying the
percentage of free, reduced, and paid meals served during the corresponding month of the base year to the total meal count of the claiming month.


Free Meal by Direct Certification % is the percent of total students who receive free meals through Direct Certification. Direct Certification status is determined by state
agencies and shared electronically with school districts. Students or families qualify for Direct Certification through a number of avenues such as eligibility for SNAP or TANF,
Migrant Ed, McKinney Vento, or foster care.

Paper applications, supplied voluntarily by families, are used to determine students who can receive free or reduced priced meals based on the family's income. The federal
government provides free meals to families whose income is at or below 130% of the federal poverty level and reduced priced meals to families whose income is at or below
185% of the poverty level. Additionally, starting in 2009, the state of Oregon provided additional funding so all students living at 185% of the poverty level can eat at no charge.
The USDA provides funds for the reduced priced meal and the state of Oregon covers the co-pay that would normally be charged to families.

Starting in 2023, Medicaid was added as another way for families to qualify for free or reduced priced meals in Oregon. Students who qualify for free meals via Medicaid are
shown as Free Meal by Direct Certification and students who qualify for reduced priced meals via Medicaid will be shown as Reduced (185% Poverty Guidelines).
