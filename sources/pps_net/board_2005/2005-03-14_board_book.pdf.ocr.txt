Portland Public Schools budget outlook for
2005-07 includes further shortfalls, reductions

Governor’s budget and local option expiration reduce revenues As the School Board and Superintendent begin
public discussions about the budget outlook for Portland Public Schools, once more, the future includes budget cuts.
Under projections provided to the Board in a public work session on February 24, the school district faces a General
Fund budget shortfall of up to $51 million in 2005-06, if the local option property tax levy is not renewed.

The local option property tax levy, worth $33.5 million, expires at the end of this school year. The School Board is
weighing the option of putting the local option on the May ballot. However, under the “double majority”
constitutional amendment Oregon voters passed, to win the levy would require not only majority support, but also a
voter turnout of more than 50 percent. Given the historic voter registration levels from the November presidential
race, the turnout requirement is a significant obstacle.

The Governor’s recommended budget - which statewide falls far short of maintaining K-12 education - contributes
$17.5 million to the Portland Public Schools shortfall next year, bringing the projected shortfall to $51 million, The
following year, the temporary local income tax expires, and the shortfall could grow to a total of $98 million.

Superintendent to focus on student achievement despite budget shortfalls On March 14, Superintendent Phillips

will make a budget proposal for next year thar outlines her plan to manage the Portland Public Schools budget
through those shortfalls. (Even if the local option levy were on the May ballot, the budget proposal could not count
on that revenue.) First, PPS will save $7.9 million in each of the next two years by buying down PERS rates, under
the PERS bond refinancing the district carried out in 2004. Second, the district will push for the Legislature to
increase state support over the Governot’s recommended level. An additional $200 million statewide would bring

PPS about $7.6 million a year.

Finally, the Superintendent plans to propose $35.4 million in budget reductions. This will be a real challenge, but
she promises to focus every dollar possible on PPS’s core educational mission. The district will continue to look for
ways to work more efficiently and recoup savings, and will weigh programs based on their effectiveness at reaching
the critical educational goals: to raise the bar for all and to eliminate the achievement gap. These will be difficult
cuts, but the Superintendent will try to make them in ways that preserve the best education possible for students.

The superintendent does not plan to balance the 05-06 budget with one-time measures, such as shortening the
school year and spending down reserves, That preserves those options for the following year, when the local income
tax expires and the projected shortfall increases by another $47 million.

Effective education and increasing efficiency build base for long-term solution PPS has already weathered many
lean years, and has taken action to cut costs in many ways already, such as containing district health care spending,
bringing more special education services in-house, refinancing PERS, cutting facilities costs, outsourcing custodial
setvices, reducing central administrative staff. These measures are saving tens of millions of dollars each year.

Together, the school district and the Portland schools community must work through another year or two of
challenge, always working to achieve the ultimate goal: an excellent system of school supported by a stable, strong
and predictable long-term funding source.

For more information, contact Sarah Carlin Ames, PPS Communications, 503-916-3212

TO: The Board of Education

FR: Bobbie Regan, Acting Chair
Board’s Educational Options and Professional Development Committee

RE: Recommendation for Portland Public International Charter Middle
and High School Charter Application Appeal

In accordance with the district charter application process, an application was
submitted by Ms. Laura Larson on November 1, 2004 for the Portland
International Middle and High School (PIMHS), now known as the Portland
Public International Charter Middle and High Schoo! (PPIC), to the Portland
Public School District to begin operation in September, 2005. A staff review was
conducted, presented to the applicant and the Board’s Educational Options and
Professional Development committee, and a hearing was conducted with the
applicant on January 11, 2005. At that hearing, the applicant submitted a revised
application. During and following the hearing, the applicant was asked to provide
supplemental materials and to respond to questions from the committee.

The Educational Options and Professional Development committee met on
January 18, 2005 and voted unanimously to recommend to the full Board that the
application be denied. The Board approved the resolution to deny the charter
with a 5-1 vote at its regular meeting on January 24, 2005. In accordance with
Chapter 338 of the Oregon Revised Statutes, the applicant was provided written
suggestions for how the application could be revised to meet the Portland Public
Schools Application Review Criteria. The applicant submitted responses to the
remediation suggestions on February 22, 2005.

Staff reviewed the revised application as submitted January 11, 2005 and
supplemental materials as submitted on February 22, 2005. In addition, the staff
review team asked the applicant for further information, submitted on March 1,
2005. Each member of the staff review team was selected based on expertise
required to evaluate the application. The reviewers used an evaluation
document that is consistent with Oregon statutes and rules regarding the
provision of charter schools and school! district policy.

The staff review team considered the application in light of all supplemental
materials and saw considerable progress in addressing the criteria required by
the application and in addressing the expressed concerns of the review team and
the Board’s Educational Options and Professional Development Committee.
However, doubts remained regarding the proposed governance and financial
viability. The staff reviewers rated the application a “Does Not Meet’ in the area
of Financial, Business and Organizational plan, and recommended denial of the
application. A public hearing for the revised PPIC application was held on March
8, 2005. After careful consideration of the staff review and all documents, and
the applicant’s testimony, the Board’s Educational Options and Professional
Development committee recommends that the application be denied. The
funding model proposed by the applicant presents a great enough risk,
particularly during a significant ebb in district funding, to outweigh the advantages
of adding such a program to next year’s district options. The committee notes
that overall, the proposal does not meet the minimum requirements in the area of
Financial, Business and Organizational Plan as indicated below:

(i) The fundraising expectation for the school ranges between
approximately $250,000 in the first year of operation to over $500,000
in the third year of operation. This amounts to more than 30 percent of
the school's operating budget annually, making the program's survival
highly dependent on funding sources that are notoriously unstable and
difficult to sustain; (ii) The budget as presented is contingent on below-
market costs for facilities, as well as capacity or near-capacity
enrollment. Any deviations from these expectations will increase the
dependency on unstable revenue streams placing further stress on a

fragile system.

However, there is agreement among the appointed members of this committee
that the potential of this proposal should not go unrecognized. In this proposal
we see the opportunity to work with the applicant in the potential joint
development of a school that would 1) meet the educational goals of the district;
2) aggressively recruit students from within marginalized and minority
communities; and 3) do so while leveraging substantial private and corporate
dollars that are currently untapped by the district. Therefore it is the
recommendation of the Board’s Educational Options and Professional
Development Committee that the district engage in additional conversations with
the applicants to explore the possibility of pursuing this model as a future district

option.

Discontinue Preparation of Debt Service General Obligation Bond Levy

WHEREAS, On February 14, 2005 the Board of Directors of Portland Public
Schools approved Board Action 3216 directing the administration to take the necessary
steps to prepare for a Debt Service General Obligation Bond Levy; and

WHEREAS, The levy would have been slated for the May 2005 election; and

WHEREAS, Any vote taken outside of an even-numbered November election
would require a “double majority” as stated in the Oregon Constitution; and

WHEREAS, Portland Public Schools voters have strongly supported local
funding measures, as evidenced by voter support approaching or exceeding 60 percent,
most recently in November 2004; and

WHEREAS, Voter registration for the November 2004 election increased by 20
percent due to strong voter registration drives related to ballot measure campaigns and
the presidential election; and

WHEREAS, The greatly increased voter registration makes the likelihood of
sufficient voter turnout to achieve the “double majority” unlikely in May of 2005, when few
contested elections or other matters of wide public interest are before the voters; and

WHEREAS, The lack of a Debt Service General Obligation Bond Levy and
exhaustion of the Portland Public Schools Capital Fund wil! require the General Fund to
pay for critical capital renovation and replacement projects; therefore be it

RESOLVED, That the Board of Directors of Portland Public Schools will pursue
an active strategy to achieve greater state funding for our schools, and increased options
for local communities to support their schoo! districts; and be it further

RESOLVED, That the Board of Directors of Portland Public Schools hereby
directs the administration to discontinue all necessary steps to prepare for a Debt Service
General Obligation Bond Levy; and be it further

RESOLVED, That the Board of Directors of Portland Public Schools will not
pursue a Debt Service General Obligation Bond Levy in the May 2005 election.

(H. Franklin)

Ash Street Boundary Alignment

3250 WHEREAS, The boundary between the Franklin and Grant clusters runs down
the middie of Ash Street between 39" and 44" Streets affecting approximately 15 school-
aged students who are not in the same feeder pattern as their neighbors across the
street; and

WHEREAS, This boundary was established in 1981 when Washington-Monroe
High School was closed, resulting in approximately 33 sections of the Franklin boundary
that are less than 2 blocks long and run down the middle of the street; and

WHEREAS, Neighbors raised their concerns about the inflexibility of the
boundary in combination with the new School District policy on feeder patterns; and

WHEREAS, The School Board understands that having students across the
street from each other attending the same school engenders closer student, community
and neighborhood relations; therefore be it

the Ash Street Boundary between 39" and 44" Streets to be included as part of the
Grant cluster; and be it further

RESOLVED, That the Board of Education directs the Superintendent to identify
similarly situated properties in other areas of the School District and to develop a
consistent policy concerning the borders of school boundaries to spell out a remedy for
similar situations as they are identified. (B. Farver/J. Patterson)

recommendations to stabilize school enrollments to ensure a rich curriculum offering and

Oregon and with the pending expiration of two local supplemental funding measures, the

yet provide greater flexibility in each of these areas; and

WHEREAS, Depending upon the level of funding received from the State of
Oregon and with the pending expiration of two local supplemental funding measures, the
District may have to reduce ongeing expenses by as much as $35 million for 2005-2006
and $47 million for 2006-2007; and

WHEREAS, While the specific savings from elementary school closures vary
based upon the cost of operating the buildings and the alternative uses to which they are
put, it can be stated that:

*¢ The expected annual savings from standard support in closing Smith is
$160,000 and the annual savings from operating costs is approximately
$148,000;

* There are small savings in nutrition services and small increases in
transportation costs;

* The estimated annual rental value of the property is $300,000, bringing the
overall potential financial benefit to closing the school to approximately
$600,000 each year and recognizing that those savings will be somewhat
offset during the first year with relocation costs; therefore be it

RESOLVED, That the Board of Education directs the Superintendent to close the
Smith Elementary School building and to realign the boundaries of Capitol Hill
Elementary, Maplewood Elementary, and Markham Elementary Schools to include the
Smith neighborhood students for the 2005-2006 school year in the following way:

* Students north of Garden Home Road will move into the Maplewood
attendance area;

* Students living east of S.W. 45'748"™ Avenue will move into the Capitol Hill
attendance area; and

* Students living south of Garden Home Road and west of S.W. 45/48"
Avenue will move into the Markham attendance area; and be it further

RESOLVED, That until the extent and pattern of future projected enrollment
increases in the Wilson cluster become more clear, the District will maintain the Smith
building and grounds in its inventory; and be it further

RESOLVED, That the Board of Education, agrees with the Superintendent on the
importance of an effective implementation strategy for these changes and directs the
Superintendent to establish an implementation plan in line with the District Procedures for
the Implementation of Major Schoo! Changes document. (8. Farver/J. Patterson)
