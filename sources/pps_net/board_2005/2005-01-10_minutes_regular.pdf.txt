1.     ANNOUNCEMENTS/CELEBRATIONS

       Co-Chair Brim-Edwards explained the agenda for the evening and
       announced that the January 24, 2005 School Board meeting will be held
       at Hosford Middle School. She thanked Principal Melissa Sandven and
       staff for hosting the Board that evening.

       Aid to Tsunami Victims

       Director Wynde discussed the devastating disaster that occurred over the
       Winter Break. He noted schools were individually raising money for relief
       efforts for victims of this disaster through a variety of fund-raising efforts.
       He stated there would be a concert at Grant High School provided by
       Grant alumni Tom Lauderdale of Pink Martini. Director Wynde thanked
       the students for the tremendous outpouring of efforts. Mercy Corps
       International and Northwest Medical Teams are both from the Portland
       area and are leaders in this international effort.

       Celebrate! Portland Public Schools

       Director Jackson noted the Board Book cover for the evening featured the
       flyer for the Celebrate! Portland Public Schools event to take place on
       Tuesday, January 18, from 5:30-8:30 PM at the Memorial Coliseum. He
       said this would be an opportunity for families and students to visit every
       school. There will also be performances by PPS students, and families
       will learn what to do for kindergarten enrollment and much more.
       Childcare and language translation services will be available, and there
       will also be free transportation from six area high schools.

       Recognition of Margaret Kisser

       Darin Matthews, Director of Procurement, announced that Margaret
       Kisser, PPS Procurement Specialist, was recently recognized as a
       “Partners in Public Procurement Purchasing Hero” by the Oregon
       Department of Administrative Services. Mr. Matthews said this is their
       highest award, and Ms. Kisser was the recipient because she secured
       donated and discounted school supplies for PPS children in need.

       The Board Co-Chairs and Superintendent Phillips presented Ms. Kisser
       with the plaque from the Oregon Department of Administrative Services.

       students – Wild Oats Natural Marketplace. They made a generous

       Williams thanked Wild Oats for their generosity on behalf of our students.

3.     ANNENBERG REPORT

       Superintendent Phillips introduced Marla Ucelli and Greg Hodge from the
       Annenberg Institute and Cynthia Guyer from the Portland Schools
       Foundation. The presence in the audience of various members of the
       Foundation’s Board of Directors was also noted.

       Marla Ucelli referenced the “Findings and Recommendations from the
       Central Office Review for Results & Equity” (a copy of this report is
       attached to the minutes or may be obtained in the Board Support Office).

       Greg Hodge said there was a need for the central office to support school
       reform so it would sustain. He said once choice is created in a public
       school system, it then must work and parents want to know that they can
       be a part of any process. He listed the five recommendations of the
       Central Office Review for Results and Equity (CORRE )report as: 1)
       Develop and communicate a central office service orientation; 2) Build (or
       re-build) centralized guidance and support for instruction; 3) Make
       collecting, organizing, analyzing, and acting on data on student
       achievement and staff and program efficacy a priority across the district;
       4) Provide supports for schools and their students as determined by a
       deeper understanding of their needs and assets; and 5) Directly address
       unhealthy relationships and ineffective structures across central office and
       with schools.

5.     ENROLLMENT: DECISION-MAKING FRAMEWORK

       Superintendent Phillips said review of enrollment data would lead to
       decision-making. She referenced the items in the Board Book pertaining
       to the enrollment discussion: She said trends in enrollment, building uses,
       educational programs and implications of any decisions such as boundary
       changes and school closures would be further explored. She said the end
       result would be a proposal for consideration by the Board and for
       community input and discussion. She said a decision by the Board would
       culminate at the late February meeting, but in the future the process would
       begin in October.

       Co-Chair Brim-Edwards said she been through a process in which there
       was analysis completed that was communicated to the school
       communities and individual schools regarding what the process would
       consist of so all could participate in that process. She emphasized that it
       was now jammed into three weeks of discussion in February.

8.     OPENING DAY OF LEGISLATURE

       Director Regan said she, along with Co-Chair Brim-Edwards, Director
       Wynde and Superintendent Phillips traveled to Salem for the opening of
       the 73rd session of the Oregon Legislature. She said that was the
       beginning of many, many trips they would take to Salem. Director Regan
       stated the vast majority of funding for public education comes from the
       state, and that had steadily decreased over the past year. She said PPS
       would have a strong presence in Salem this session to keep the issue of
       funding for schools a top priority. She encouraged parents and staff to
       visit or write their state legislators and encourage them to develop short-
       and long-term funding solutions.

       Co-Chair Brim-Edwards said there were many positive stories to share
       with legislators, including the work of Annenberg. She said the Board
       wants to be good stewards of funds and student achievement is a priority
       issue.

10.    OTHER BUSINESS

       Director Wynde said the enrollment data review primarily focused on
       elementary schools, but the impact might be beyond the elementary level.
       He acknowledged the commentary in the newspaper recently with regard
       to Whitaker Middle School and its temporary location. He said pending a
       final decision regarding renewal of the capital bond, there needs to be
       serious thought given to what happens to that school site prior to the next
       school year. Director Wynde emphasized that the site on Columbia
       Boulevard was intended to be a temporary location, and it has been used
       far too long. He indicated he would like to see some alternatives for the
       location of that school.

       Superintendent Phillips responded should would bring alternatives to the
       Board.

       Co-Chair Brim-Edwards said Whitaker Middle School was losing the
       capture rate, had shrinking enrollment and a majority of the problems was
       tied to the facility where the students are located and parents making
       decisions regarding what is best for their children. She suggested that
       perhaps the Finance, Audit and Operations Committee could work out a
       timeframe with staff from Facilities.
