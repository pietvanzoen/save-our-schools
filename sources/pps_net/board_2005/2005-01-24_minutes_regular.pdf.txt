4.     CHARTER APPLICATION FOR VICTORY CHARTER SCHOOL

       Director Williams thanked staff for their work on the charter applications,
       and specifically thanked Susan Kosmala, PPS Charter Schools Manager,
       for her diligence and meticulous work on the charter applications. She
       further thanked Dr. Maxine Kilcrease and all PPS staff involved in
       reviewing the applications. She noted to date, the Portland School Board
       has approved eight charters and denied three charters. She referenced
       her memorandum which outlines the reasons why the Education Options
       and Professional Development Committee is recommending denial of the
       Victory Charter School application (a copy of the memorandum is attached
       to these minutes or may be obtained from the Board Support Office).

       Director Williams stated the committee’s reasons for denying the charter
       application for Victory Charter School as overall the proposal did not meet
       the minimum requirements in the areas of Educational Program, Support
       for Learning, and Financial, Business and Organizational Plan.

       Applicant’s Comments

       Mr. Cottrell White, Principal of Victory Charter School and representing the
       applicant, stated his surprise that the committee voted unanimously to
       recommend denial of the application to the full Board. He said Victory
       would not give up hope, and they would apply again to the state to
       continue their charter.

       Viesa Loving – Ms. Loving, grandparent of a Victory student, said

5.     CHARTER APPLICATION FOR PORTLAND INTERNATIONAL
       CHARTER MIDDLE AND HIGH SCHOOL

       Director Williams referenced her memorandum which outlines the reasons
       why the Education Options and Professional Development Committee is
       recommending denial of the charter application for Portland International
       Charter Middle and High School (a copy of the memorandum is attached
       to these minutes or may be obtained from the Board Support Office).

       Director Williams said the applicant submitted a revised application at the
       charter hearing on January 11th, but at that time it was considered an
       additional document and not a formal new application. She outlined the
       committee’s reasons for recommending denial of the application to the full
       Board as: the proposal did not meet the minimum requirements in the
       areas of Educational Program, Support for Learning, and Financial,
       Business and Organizational Plan.

       Applicant’s Comments

       Jeffrey Boly, representing the applicant, referenced his Memorandum to
       the Board of Education dated January 24, 2005 (a copy of this document
       is available in the Board Support Office) and said the applicant was trying
       to accomplish an ideal school with state-of-the-art best practices. Mr. Boly
       said the applicant tried to enlist the involvement of good educational
       professionals, and noted the initial lead administrator for the proposed
       charter school would be a retired principal from Gresham High School who
       during his tenure there began the International Baccalaureate program at
       that school. Mr. Boly said the person they proposed to lead fundraising
       efforts, Mr. Bob Scanlon, is extremely good at fundraising and has proven
       results. He noted there is a disconnect between state standards and PPS
       standards. He said the state gave the charter applicant a $350,000 start-
       up grant and rated their application a 90.7. Mr. Boly said his biggest
       concern is that, as he interprets the law, it is not enough to tell a charter
       applicant that there are problems with the application, but the state
       requires the district to tell the applicant what they need to do in the form of
       guidance. In closing, Mr. Boly said the applicant hopes to work with PPS
       in a collaborative, cooperative way and find out what more is needed to
       accomplish goals that all agree are optimal.

       Board Discussion

       Director Poe said he was struck by Mr. Boly’s comment regarding state
       versus PPS standards.

6.     SUPERINTENDENT’S PROPOSAL BASED ON ENROLLMENT DATA
       ANALYSIS – FRAMEWORK FOR DECISION-MAKING

       Superintendent Phillips referenced the school profiles of all PPS
       elementary schools as contained in the Board Book. She said discussions
       will continue over the next few weeks with District staff, and her goal is to
       bring proposals, not recommendations, to the February 14th School Board
       meeting. The Superintendent indicated that there would be more
       conversations with the community and data would continue to be made
       available. She said this year the framework for decision-making is being
       accomplished, so next year the entire process can start earlier in the
       school year, most likely in October, and the Board would vote on
       recommendations in January. (A copy of Superintendent Phillips’ Power
       Point presentation is attached to these minutes or may be obtained from
       the Board Support Office.)

       Superintendent Phillips laid out the three stages in the framework for
       decision making as: 1) initial data analysis and implications, 2) building
       level and 3) educational analysis, and implementation strategy. She said
       possible decisions could be made regarding: boundary alignments;
       program initiation; expansion, or closure; school initiation or closure;
       and/or restructuring the delivery of effective instruction. Superintendent
       Phillips discussed what would happen in each of the three stages of the
       framework. In closing, she discussed the timeline which will culminate
       with her recommendations and Board approval, tentatively scheduled for
       the February 28, 2005 School Board meeting.

       Board Discussion

       Director Williams asked if staff were looking at enrollment trends/drop-out
       rates for ELL (English Language Learners) and TAG (Talented and Gifted)
       students.

       Superintendent Phillips replied staff would look at that when they look at
       the middle and high school data. She said they currently were only
       looking at the elementary level.

       Director Poe asked the Superintendent if any “surprises” were noted as
       the information was put together.
