OFFICE OF THE SUPERINTENDENT )

MEMORANDUM

Date: February 11, 2005
To: Portland School Board
From: Vicki Phillips

Re: Proposals from the Enrollment Data Review

Accompanying this memorandum are the proposals resulting from our Enrollment Data Analysis.
These are proposals for consideration by the Board and the community with recommendations to
follow on February 28". These proposals follow an intensive process that included the
development of school profiles on all elementary schools and extensive discussions with schools
and area directors about questions presented by the data. This process has been discussed
previously at Board meetings, and a copy of our framework for decision-making and the profiles
were included in your January 24 Board book.

Three of our proposals for the 2005-06 school year involve major changes in schools. One
proposal involves an entire cluster with implementation occurring during 2005-06 and 2006-07.
Two other proposals address smaller boundary issues. While some parts of the proposals require
further development before their formal recommendation on February 28, I believe the thought
and substance of the proposals are sufficient to allow for neighborhood-based hearings and
broader community discussions.

You will be asked to approve our final recommendations in mid-March.

I want you to know how proud I am of the central staff and school administrators who stepped
courageously into the discussions and who worked tirelessly to meet our test of data-based

decision-making.

Section 3: Description of Proposal

Close Smith Elementary School and realign neighboring school boundaries to include
Smith neighborhood students. Redesign instructional programs at Markham,
Maplewood, and Capitol Hill to meet the needs of students blending from neighborhoods

in Southwest Portland.

Section 4: Rationale based on data and research supporting change.

Background information:

Wilson Cluster has seven elementary schools, two middle schools and one high school.
The elementary enrollment of cluster schools ranges from 353 students (Hayhurst) to 219
students (Smith). With the exception of Hayhurst School, all cluster elementary schools
have experienced declining enrollment for the past five years. The Odyssey Program, a
Focus Option, moved from Chapman School to Hayhurst last year, and increased the
enrollments by 112 students (From 212 to 353 students in grades K — 8). Four of the
other cluster elementary schools have enrollment under 300 students in grades
kindergarten through fifth grade.

Historically, Wilson Cluster has opened and closed elementary schools as the population
has changed and the need for elementary schools increased or decreased. In the early
1980’s, Rieke was closed due to low enrollment. Markham was reopened as a K-5
program to relieve over-crowding at Stephenson and Smith in 1989. Rieke was reopened
in the early 1990’s due to an increase of families in the Hayhurst and Capitol Hill
neighborhoods.

In 2003, the District convened a Westside Boundary Task Force to review options for
boundary changes and school mergers. They received input from school staff, parents
and community who indicated that they supported small, high performing schools in the
Cluster.

The goal:

The District’s goal is to build high-quality accessible schools in all neighborhoods with
focus options available to families with interests that can best be met with a theme-based
program. The Wilson Cluster Schools’ goal is to implement an arts focus throughout the
cluster in grades kindergarten through 12. In order to meet these goals, the schools need
to have adequate staff and support. The realignment of boundaries from seven to six
neighborhoods will increase school enrollment and redistribute available support to
programs based on current staffing formulas and student-based allocations, e.g. Special
Ed., ELL, TAG.

The following factors were considered in the recommendation to close Smith School:

1. Academic Achievement:

All Wilson Cluster schools are high performing. Smith received an Exceptional
rating this year.

2. Neighborhood:

All Wilson Cluster elementary schools have a high capture rate of neighborhood
students. The range is from 91% for Stephenson School to 67% for Hayhurst.
Smith’s capture rate is 73%, Out of the 79 students living in the Smith neighborhood
attending other Portland Public Schools, 54% are attending a focus option including
17 who transferred to Hayhurst for the Odyssey program for the 2004-05 school year.
Thirty-nine percent attend other Cluster elementary schools including 8 at Capitol
Hill, 4 at Maplewood and 10 at Markham.

3. Enrollment:

Wilson Cluster elementary schools enrollment ranges between 353 and 219 students.
Smith School has 219 students enrolled this year. They are one of nine elementary
schools in the District with an enrollment under 249 students. This is a decline of 19
students from the 2003-04 school year. The school’s grade level enrollment is from
33 in kindergarten to 42 in fifth grade. Smith has three grade levels with enrollment
under thirty-five students.

4. Facilities/Density:

In general, Wilson Cluster elementary schools were constructed to house K-5
programs and do not have additional room to accommodate focus option programs
that attract students district-wide and increase enrollment in schools. The exception
is Hayhurst School that was originally a K-8 school and is a large building with
twenty-two classrooms. Due to low neighborhood enrollment, Hayhurst had capacity
this year to house the Odyssey Program as a program-of-the-school with shared
resources.

Smith’s facility and physical location is not conducive to bringing in other options.
Smith School has 16 classrooms. Nine are used for classroom instruction, one each
kindergarten, music, art, ESL/Learning Center, computer lab, and small group
instruction. There is insufficient space to add a focus option similar to Odyssey that
could increase their enrollment.

5. Budget/Staffing:

Wilson Cluster has four schools with enrollment under 300 (Capitol Hill,
Maplewood, Rieke, Smith). Schools receive funding for staff and materials based on
Average Daily Membership (ADM) for the past two semesters. Two current General
Fund staffing allocations are student to teacher ratio (30:1) or Earned FTE, and
Standard Support Tables. These four schools are averaging 7 FTE (Full Time
Equivalent employee) from the ratio and 3.5 FTE from Standard Support Tables. The
schools also receive FTE for Kindergarten, Local Option Levy Measure (Expires at
the end of 2004/05 school year), Special Education, ESL, and other support such as
Foundation Grants. Small schools find it challenging to fund staffing positions for
Child Development Specialists, PE, Music and Art Instructors because they use their
staffing allocations to balance out classrooms among the grade levels.

Smith School has 6.97 FTE (30:1 ratio) and 3.0 FTE Standard Support. They
currently have nine classroom teachers for grades 1 through 5 and one % day
kindergarten classroom instructor and one full day fee based kindergarten. Their
current funding also supports a principal, secretary, part-time PE and Music
instruction. They have no Child Development Specialist.

Realigning the neighborhoods would increase the number of staff assigned to a school
in both Earned FTE (student to teacher ratio) and Standard Support (Standard Support
would increase by 1.0 FTE only if their enrollment increased to over 300 students).
Increase in ADM gives principals more flexibility in staffing classrooms and offering
additional staff for Music, PE, Art, Music and counseling.
Section 5: Educational Impact

1. How will the proposal impact student achievement?

All Wilson Cluster Schools are academically successful. The Cluster Elementary
Schools have a long history of working together to implement similar curriculum and
teaching strategies to facilitate smooth articulation to Cluster middle schools and high

school.

With the additional resources resulting from the increase in student population in the
receiving schools, principals and staff will have a higher concentration of resources
(including District and community) to provide enrichment classes during and after
school. The Arts focus will be strengthened at Markham and Capitol Hill by an
increase in enrollment and staffing. The staffing increase will come from additional
ADM and/or standard support. This should allow them to keep the arts staff in spite
of other possible cuts (Local Option Sunset).

2. How will the proposal meet the needs of low achieving, special needs, ELL, and
TAG students?

Currently eight percent of Smith Students are receiving ELL services. By realigning
the boundaries, the percentage of ELL students will increase at neighboring schools
and the amount of time an itinerant teacher spends at the school delivering services
will increase proportionally. The same will be true for Special Education and TAG
students. Increased numbers of ELL students would mean at least half time services
in place of itinerant. Additional FTE for increased numbers of Special Education

students would also occur.

3. Based on projected enrollment, how does the proposal meet the needs of the
students in that school community?

The data shows a continued trend of enrollment decline in most of the Cluster
Schools. Lower enrollment will mean less staffing which will increase the pressure to
eliminate all supports (music, extended day, art, etc.) beyond the basic classroom.

For the Cluster to support an effective and efficient Arts strand, we need to
concentrate resources in fewer schools with higher enrollment and the capacity to
offer support beyond the basic classroom.

Section 6: Cluster Analysis

1. How will the proposed change affect equity of access to schools offering high
quality educational choices?

Equity of access to extended day and community supports will be enhanced with a
larger concentration of efforts at fewer schools. Students who are struggling will get
additional support. Smith students will continue to have opportunities in the Arts, PE,
and counseling despite looming cuts based on budget loses to the district in the 2005-
2006 school year. Smith students will have enhanced opportunities for extended day
services as Markham and Capitol Hill have a plethora of such programs.

The majority of elementary students in Wilson Cluster are transported to their schools
by bus or personal car due to neighborhood boundaries consisting of both sides of I-5,
Barbur Blvd., Beaverton-Hillsdale Highway, Capitol Hwy, Taylors Ferry, Macadam
Blvd, all of which create unsafe or hazardous walking conditions. Students will
continue to receive transportation to and from school with a minimal amount of

additional time on a bus.

2. How will the proposed change affect other schools within the cluster, both in the
quality of their educational programs and in the number of students attending

them including feeder patterns.

The educational programs will be enhanced by having additional FTE to staff
positions beyond the classroom. The boundaries should be redrawn to send the bulk
of the students to Markham and Capitol Hill since Maplewood is close to capacity.

Students attending Capitol Hill will have access to 2 full day kindergartens at
Markham. Additional students may allow enhanced full day kindergarten
opportunities.

The articulation pattern to Jackson Middle School will continue to remain the same
for students who will be attending Markham or Capitol Hill. Students who become
part of the Maplewood neighborhood will have their pattern changed from Jackson to
Gray. Gray and Jackson offer similar programs including music, arts, and PE, All
current Smith 5th graders would be able to articulate to Jackson. New articulation
patterns would go into effect for the 2006-07 school year, with continued option to
apply for transfers when appropriate or desired.

3. How will the proposed change affect schools outside the cluster both in quality of
their education programs and the number of students attending them?

There should be no impact outside the cluster.

The proposal is to merge Edwards and Abernethy Elementary School into a single year-
round school. The merger would occur at the end of the 2004-05 school year. The
program would be located at the Abernethy site and the Edwards building closed. This
will provide continuity to the year-round program and stability for two small schools.

The Edwards neighborhood boundary would need to be redrawn so that all Edwards
neighborhood students would be included in the Abernethy boundary. The new
Abernethy proposed boundary would extend east on Hawthorne to 39", South on 39" to
Division, west on Division to 34% south on 34" to Woodward and west on Woodward.

The new feeder pattern would be Abernethy/ Hosford/Cleveland.

Section 4: Rationale based on data and research supporting change.

Edwards is a small year-round school that splits between the Franklin and Cleveland

clusters. There are 199 students enrolled at Edwards which only has 9 classrooms. There

neighborhood and 28 of those students attend a different PPS school.

Abernethy is currently a small traditional elementary school that feeds Hosford Middle

67% of its neighborhood students. The neighborhood population is 218 students; 145 of
those students attend Abernethy.

Schools with less than 250 students are limited in their curricular offerings based on
staffing. Edwards has the least number of FTE for any school in the district at 9.56.
Since the initial recommendation for closure, Edwards has been in a position of
instability. It is unclear whether this or other reasons are responsible for the school’s

Finally, there is compelling national research that demonstrates that reducing the number
of transitions for students during the early adolescent years yields positive benefits in
terms of higher retention, student self worth, and academic performance. Both the K-6/
7-12 and the K-8/ 9-12 models reduce these transitions.

Research is very clear that knowing students and learners as individuals is key to making
students successful. With longer spans of time with students, schools have a better
chance of knowing students well and designing their supports as well as designing

options that fit students’ interests and goals. Extended contact between students and
adults is important in developing good relationships and has positive impact on student
achievement (Anderson, 1985; Corcoran & Wilson, 1985). Multiple year interactions
(such as K-6, K-8, 7-12 models) provide consistency for students and a feeling that
someone knows them and their history (McLaughlin, 1994; McQuillan, 1997).

Section 3: Description of Proposal

the data and educational implications, it is recommended that the boundary stay as it is
currently configured for the 2005-06 schools year.

However, it is also recommended that the boundary change be reconsidered in 2006-07
after the District has provided sufficient training and support for Rieke teachers in
addressing the needs of students such as English Language Learners. The numbers of
ELL students would increase significantly with a boundary change and Rieke’s
preparation, programs and support would need to increase commensurately.

Section 4: Rationale based on data and research supporting change.

The data and evidence does not indicate that a change in boundary at this time would
result in improved education opportunities for students — there would be no specific
benefit to either community.

The boundary change under consideration included the small area east of 30". There are
35 students within that area. The students within this area have been assigned to

Hayhurst for more than 12 years. There are two housing centers within this boundary.
One is Turning Point that houses families escaping violence and the other is the Hillsdale
Terrace that is a federal housing project. Turning Point has high turnover and the Terrace
has large numbers of ELL students. Hayhurst has developed a strong system of support
and welcome for studerits from this area. Rieke has limited experience serving students
with diverse needs such as ELL students and students from impoverished environments.

The following issues were considered and formed the basis for the current
recommendation:

e The ELL services would be diluted for Hayhurst and both schools would have only
itinerant services. .

e Students currently ride a bus to Hayhurst and would need to continue to ride a bus
due to the extreme safety issue faced in crossing from the Terrace to Rieke.

Title I services would be diluted due to the smal! numbers in each school.
There is strong family support with a full time Child Development Specialist (CDS)
at Hayhurst and no CDS support at Rieke.

e Training to support students of poverty and students with language needs has been
undertaken at Hayhurst for several years, and we will need to support professional
development in these areas for teachers at Rieke if students are to make a smooth and
successful transition.

e With the addition of Odyssey to Hayhurst, we now have a neighborhood program of
150. A reduction of 35 students could have a significant negative impact on
programming as well as funding for the neighborhood school.

e Adding 35 students to Rieke would be difficult as they are nearly at capacity.

FEASIBILITY STUDY FOR THE RELOCATION OF CENTRAL SUPPORT SERVICES
Final Report

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

_ Shiels Obletz Johnsen led a Project Team that included GBD Architects and The Kalberer
Company to conduct a Feasibility Study for the Relocation of the Central Support Services
(Feasibility Study).

Assignment. The original purpose of the Feasibility Study was to examine if it is a feasible and
financially sound option for the District to relocate the central support services located in the
Blanchard Education Service Center (BESC). With agreement from the District, the initial scope
of work was expanded to include looking at the possibility of redeveloping the existing BESC
site with the principal administrative functions remaining at the location, but with the central
kitchen and food services, the warehouse and distribution functions and maintenance services
being relocated elsewhere.

Key Conclusions. The Project Team’s key conclusions for the four key central service functions
of the BESC — central administration, central kitchen and food services, warehouse and
distribution services, and maintenance services are as follows:

e The BESC facility contains 365,435 gross square feet and encompasses more space than
the District needs for its central services.

e The BESC layout does not lend itself to partitioning and subleasing beyond the current
subleasing arrangements.
The functions in the BESC do not need to be co-located to meet District needs.
The District should not sell the BESC; it should engage the Portland Schools Real Estate
Trust to develop a strategy for redevelopment of the site and relocation of some current
central services.

Specific Findings. The following are our specific findings:
Central Administration:

e The District’s central administrative services should be “unbundled” from the other
central services currently located within the BESC.

e Decentralization of administrative functions and employees into other PPS-owned
facilities is not financially feasible.

e Thecurrent BESC configuration is not particularly efficient for administrative office use.

Central Kitchen and Food Services:

e The central kitchen and food services functions do not need to be part of a central
administrative services facility.

e Moving the existing full production kitchen and related functions, as they are currently
configured, from the BESC to a new location is too expensive and cannot be funded by
current foods services operations. We believe relocating to a reconfigured and
downsized facility may be a viable option.

e Moving from one central kitchen to three regional kitchens using the existing format of
full production facilities is even less cost effective than one central kitchen.

e While labor costs are consistent with private food service management companies’ costs,
the District’s benefit costs are significantly greater.

Warehouse and Distribution Services:

e The warehouse and distribution services functions does not need to be part of a central
administrative services facility; they should be co-located as part of a new central kitchen
facility.

e The size of a centralized warehouse facility could be significantly reduced in size. A new
facility utilizing high cube technology and other state of the art technological advances
should greatly reduce the required square footage and improve warehousing efficiency.

e The District should pursue a new inventory management system and just-in-time supply
contracts. Non-essential equipment and supplies could be stored off-site in a non-owned
facility.

Maintenance Services:

e The maintenance services functions do not need to be part of a central administrative
services facility.

¢ The District should pursue maintenance service partnerships with the City of Portland,
Multnomah County and/or other public partners.

Genera! Findings.

e At this time, the District cannot sell the BESC for enough money to fund the relocation of
the current services to another site.

¢ The most cost effective short- to intermediate-term solution is for the District to remain in
the BESC for the next two to four years while it pursues the recommendations set forth in
this Feasibility Study.
