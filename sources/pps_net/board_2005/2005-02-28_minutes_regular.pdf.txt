1.     ANNOUNCEMENTS/CELEBRATIONS

       Co-Chair Wynde reviewed the agenda for the evening.

       National Black History Month

       Director Brim-Edwards announced that February was National Black
       History Month. She said the celebration dated back to 1926 and was
       credited to a Harvard scholar named Carter G. Woodson. Mr. Woodson
       chose the second week of February in honor of the birthdays of pivotal
       Black supporters – Frederick Douglass and Abraham Lincoln. In closing,
       Director Brim-Edwards offered a quote: “Success is to be measured not
       so much by the position that one has reached in life as by the obstacles
       which were overcome while trying to succeed.”

       Read Across America

       Student Representative Cortez said Read Across America was initially
       created as a one-day event to celebrate reading on Dr. Seuss’ birthday,
       March 2nd. She said the National Education Association’s Read Across
       America has grown into a nationwide initiative that promotes reading every
       day. Portland Public Schools has opportunities at most schools for
       readers. She specifically noted the need for readers at Tubman Middle
       School. If you are interested in a particular school, please contact the
       principal.

       Legislative Update

       Co-Chair Regan thanked organizers of the rally to Salem that was held on
       Monday, February 21st. She specifically thanked Stand for Children and
       the many students, staff and parents who joined Board members to rally at
       the state capitol in Salem.

       Co-Chair Regan explained that both the Local Option Levy and the
       General Obligation bond expire in June 2005 and the temporary county
       income tax will expire in June 2006. She said if the levy and bond were
       put on the May 2005 ballot, both would require a double majority to
       succeed. Board members continue to meet with legislators from the
       Portland area and continue to push for stable funding for K-12 education.

4.     FINANCIAL ACCOUNTABILITY

       Update on Board Consideration of Local Option Levy and General
       Obligation Bond

       Co-Chair Wynde said five years ago voters approved a property tax levy,
       the Local Option, which provided incremental funding for Portland Public
       Schools. He noted the levy was intended to be an enhancement for basic
       school funding. He said the General Obligation bond is for improvements
       to buildings owned by Portland Public Schools. He noted both the levy
       and bond will expire in June 2005. Co-Chair Wynde said last week the
       Board had a work session on the subject of the feasibility of going to the
       voters in May to renew both the levy and bond.

       Superintendent Phillips said for the 2005-2006 school year, without the
       Local Option and if the legislature approvers the governor’s budget of $5.2
       billion, Portland Public Schools will have a $59 million shortfall. She noted
       the following school year, when the temporary county income tax runs out,
       that shortfall is predicted to be $98 million. The Superintendent said the
       community has allowed PPS to maintain stability, but the governor’s
       budget would have PPS facing a large deficit.

       Mark Wiener, community activist, said he is a Portland Public Schools
       parent who has worked on a number of local funding measures, including
       the original Local Option levy campaign, the Ballot Measure 26-48
       campaign and also worked to defeat the repeal measure. He said
       because of the double majority rule, which states 50% of the registered
       voters must vote in order for a measure to pass, it was his opinion that it
       will be impossible to pass a measure any time in 2005. He said more
       citizens registered to vote due to the presidential election in 2004 and that
       made the double majority rule harder to clear. Mr. Wiener added newly
       registered voters were motivated by the presidential election or single
       ballot issues.

       Cynthia Guyer, Executive Director of the Portland Schools Foundation,
       urged everyone who is concerned about the funding gap in K-12
       education to put all their energy together and join with the state PTA,
       Stand for Children and other education advocates to get the governor’s
       proposal up to $5.4 billion. She said the Foundation would support the
       Superintendent and Board in creating more efficiencies within the budget
       to get a higher performing district.

       Shannon Campion, Portland Metro Director of Stand for Children, said the
       biggest challenge faced when looking at renewal of the Local Option levy
       and General Obligation bond is the double majority. She said it was her

       Second Reading of Policies

       Administrative Employees’ Terms of Employment (5.60.010-P):

       Director Brim-Edwards said the adoption of this policy would clean up the
       senior administrators’ contract process. She noted there were a handful
       of contracts that contained special perks – payments for cars, golden
       parachutes – and not all were performance-based contracts. She said
       she personally felt that was outrageous and noted there were times when
       the School Board did not see contracts prior to voting on them. She said
       the new policy provides a great level of accountability and transparency.

5.     ACADEMIC ACCOUNTABILITY

       Superintendent’s Proposal for New Graduation Requirements

       Superintendent Phillips noted the graduation requirements proposal will be
       brought before the Board at their meeting of March 28, 2005 with a First
       Reading of the Graduation Requirements Policy.

       Steve Olczak, Director of Secondary Education, provided a Power Point
       presentation (a copy of this document is available in the Board Support
       Office). The proposal would include the following credit requirements and
       would begin with the Class of 2009:

           4     English
           3     Social Studies (Global/American Studies and
                 Government/Economics)
           3     Mathematics (including Algebra and Geometry applications)

           3     Science (Physical Science and Biology, plus a Science credit that
                 supports the student’s education plan)
           2     Wellness

           1     Senior Pathway Portfolio/Project
           5     Elective credits

       Director Morgan asked that three assumptions by addressed be the
       Student Achievement Committee and brought back to the Board: 1) the
       target date for getting the plan up and running for the class of 2009; 2) the
       percentage of students in the School District expected to meet CIM/CAM
       requirements; and 3) what type of professional development will be
       needed.

       Director Brim-Edwards asked if the new graduation requirements would
       align fully with the Oregon University system, with the exception of two
       years of foreign language, and noted the importance that every student
       graduating from PPS should be able to attend a public university.

       Mr. Olczak responded 51% of 2004 graduates took two or more years of a
       foreign language. He said it would be helpful if PPS could build capacity
       at the middle school level for a second language.

       Director Brim-Edwards asked who would grade the efficiency of the work
       samples.

       Mr. Olczak responded teachers and the School District would have a
       major commitment over the next four years to have the bulk of the
       teachers trained.

       Superintendent Phillips added that the School District would have to
       address the issue of teacher work loads and an array of other issues in
       order to implement the new graduation requirements.

       Superintendent Discusses Proposed School Closures and Changes

       Superintendent Phillips said she would put forth her final
       recommendations on school closures and changes next week, following
       the conclusion of the hearings. She said the resolution that will be
       prepared regarding the Rieke-Hayhurst boundary alignment would be
       clear that the change would take place the following year. She said
       regarding the possible closure of Smith Elementary, the resolution would
       state that PPS would keep the property. Superintendent Phillips stated
       she was not recommending any changes to the Ash Street or Richmond
       proposals. Regarding the proposals for the Jefferson cluster, the
       Superintendent said she would spell out the safety measures for seventh
       and eighth graders that would be put in place for the 7-12 structure at the
       hearing at Jefferson next week. Superintendent Phillips said once
       decisions were made by the Board, detailed implementation plans would
       be announced.
