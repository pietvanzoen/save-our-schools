2.     NAME CHANGE FOR VOCATIONAL VILLAGE HIGH SCHOOL

       Co-Chair Brim-Edwards noted that last year the Board decided the
       Glenhaven building was no longer a safe and adequate facility to serve as
       the home of Vocational Village High School. She added the land and
       building were sold to Banfield Pet Hospital, and they are now building a
       new headquarters on the site, which has had a positive impact on the
       neighborhood.

       Director Morgan, a member of the Community and Staff Relations
       Committee, reviewed Resolution #3176, which recommended the name of
       Vocational Village High School be changed to Joseph L. Meek
       Professional Technical High School. He stated the committee examined
       the request and unanimously recommended the name change via
       resolution to the full Board.

       A.J. Morrison, Principal of Vocational Village High School, discussed the
       process that led to the proposed name change. She noted Joseph L.
       Meek was an outstanding Oregon pioneer who brought the Northwest
       Territories into the United States and helped to develop the Oregon
       constitution.

       Co-Chair Brim-Edwards invited Dennis and Virginia Howarth, Co-Chairs of
       the Concordia Neighborhood Association, to come forward to address the
       Board.

       Mr. Howarth stated they, along with Queenie Samuel of the Concordia
       Neighborhood Association, were delighted that the school would retain the
       name of Joseph L. Meek.

       Co-Chair Brim-Edwards thanked the Howarths for their support of their
       neighborhood school.

       Co-Chair Poe asked if Joseph Meek was a slave-holder.

       Ms. Morrison responded not to her knowledge. She said a teacher
       researched that issue recently and said Mr. Meek was one of very few
       white Oregonians who had the full trust of the local Native Americans.

       The Board voted unanimously to approve Resolution #3176 (vote: 5-0;
       Directors Regan and Williams absent; Student Representative Cortez
       voting yes, unofficial).

3.     CLASSIFIED EMPLOYEE RECOGNITION

       Director Morgan stated November 15-19 was Classified Employees Week.
       He said Board members that week visited Columbia High School, Foster
       Middle School, Youngson and the Community Transition Center. He said
       those sites depend on classified employees for many duties. He noted the
       burden on classified employees has increased, and added they are the
       eyes and ears for principals and teachers in our schools.

       Frank Klejmont, Director of Security, explained the role of campus
       monitors in our schools. They stop problems before they happen and go
       above and beyond the scope of their assigned duties. He said all campus
       monitors are certified by the state as unarmed security and are trained by
       the Portland Police Special Reaction Team. He noted further training is
       conducted each year in areas such as gang recognition, bomb
       identification and narcotics recognition. Campus monitors from various
       sites were in attendance, and Mr. Klejmont asked them to introduce
       themselves.

       A.J. Morrison highlighted the outstanding work of four classified
       employees at Joseph L. Meek Professional Technical High School --
       Kathryn Starlyn, Data Clerk; Mary Frances Hill, Educational Assistant;
       Tammie Gill, Principal’s Secretary; and Diane Namba-Welsick,
       Educational Assistant. She noted the many hours classified staff put in to
       facilitate the move from the Glenhaven site to the Meek site.

4.     FINANCIAL ACCOUNTABILITY

       Presentation of Annual Audit

       Co-Chair Brim-Edwards announced the School District receives a
       comprehensive financial audit each year. She noted the Board would vote
       on Resolution 3175, “Acceptance and Approval of the Comprehensive
       Annual Financial Report, Report to Management, and Reports on
       Requirements of the Single Audit Act and OMB Circular A-133” as part of
       the Business Agenda.

       Director Wynde, Chair of the Board’s Finance, Audit and Operations
       Committee, introduced Heidi Franklin, Interim Chief Financial Officer, and
       Parry Ankerson, Pauly, Rogers and Co., P.C.

       Ms. Heidi Franklin referenced two reports: “Comprehensive Annual
       Financial Report for the Year Ended June 30, 2004” and “Report on
       Requirements of the Single Audit Act and OMB Circular A-133 for the
       Year Ended June 30, 2004.” She noted both reports have been reviewed
       in great detail by the Finance, Audit and Operations Committee. She
       noted several items within the District-wide financial statements. She
       commended the Board for having a reserve at 3%, and suggested she
       may recommend that the reserve be set at 5%.

       Mr. Parry Ankerson referenced the letter to the Board of Education, dated
       November 19, 2004, from Pauly, Rogers as contained in the Board Book
       (a copy of this document may be obtained in the Board Support Office).
       He stated it was his firm’s responsibility to audit the financial statements of
       the School District. Mr. Ankerson said PPS received prestigious awards
       from several organizations for excellent reporting on the financial
       statements. He particularly noted Joanne Ossanna, Interim Controller, for
       her work on the financial statements. On an ongoing basis, Mr. Ankerson
       said Pauly, Rogers offers the School District advice in dealing with
       financial issues and noted Portland Public Schools was the largest and
       most complicated school district in the state.

       Director Jackson said he was still interested in having a monthly variance
       report prepared. He said a report of that type would allow one to look at
       the need to readjust mid-stream in order to stay within the constraints of
       the budget.

       First Reading of Policies

       1.40.080-P, Reimbursement of Expenses – Board of Directors: Director
       Wynde, Chair of the Board’s Finance, Audit and Operations Committee,
       said Board members do not receive money for their work and the
       proposed policy connects the Board Office budget to the appropriate
       policies to ensure a high level of accountability. He thanked Heidi
       Franklin, Interim Chief Financial Officer, for her work with Board
       leadership in developing the policy and for her work in providing training
       sessions for Board members on the proposed policy. Director Wynde
       stated it was the unanimous recommendation of the Community and Staff
       Relations Committee to move the policy forward for a First Reading.

       Co-Chair Brim-Edwards stated the policy establishes conditions and
       parameters of reimbursement for necessary and reasonable expenses
       incurred by the Portland Public Schools Board of Directors in the conduct
       of Board business. She said the draft policy may be viewed at
       http://www.pps.k12.or.us/.docs/pg/5899. The policy contact is Heidi
       Franklin, P.O. Box 3107, Portland, Oregon 97208-3107. Ms. Franklin’s
       telephone number is 503-916-2000, and her email address is
       hfranklin@pps.k12.or.us. She noted the last date for comment is January
       13, 2005.

       Director Morgan noted that the policy would be setting policy for Board
       members that was in line with policies currently in place for senior
       management.

       5.60.010-P, Administrative Employees’ Terms of Employment: Co-Chair
       Brim Edwards said that over the last three years as a Board member, she
       has worked to develop terms of employment for senior staff that would
       prohibit excessive buy-out clauses, use of automobiles, supplemental
       health insurance policies, etc. She said previously there was not enough
       support to move a terms of employment contract for senior staff forward.
       Co-Chair Brim-Edwards said the Board has increased its oversight, and
       there would no longer be contracts for employment that had the types of
       provisions that were contained in contracts prior to 2001. She added the
       efforts to develop new contract language have been enthusiastically
       supported by Superintendent Phillips, and the Superintendent spent a
       significant amount of time aligning the draft policy to areas where the
       District is moving forward. Co-Chair Brim-Edwards emphasized that the
       draft policy provides uniformity and fairness, and is a good base for future
       contracts. She further stated the draft policy would provide greater
       accountability to taxpayers and the broader community.

9.     OTHER BUSINESS

       Co-Chair Poe stated he was informed that Portland Public Schools did not
       have an affirmative action plan in place. He noted that Director Jackson
       informed him there was an affirmative action plan in place. He asked the
       Superintendent for clarification, and added that the issue has been a topic
       of ongoing discussion within the community.

       Superintendent Phillips responded that the information she obtained from
       the Human Resources Department indicated there was an affirmative
       action plan in place up until the mid ’80’s. She said PPS did not currently
       have a formal plan in place, but did have a set of principles, and there are
       discussions taking place on how to put those principles into a very
       aggressive recruitment plan.

       Director Jackson said the information he shared with the Board was
       regarding an ORS that was passed when Neil Goldschmidt was governor,
       and it required all school districts in Oregon to employ administrators and
       educators in a proportion equal to the population they served. He added
       the state had until 1997 to come into compliance. He said PPS did not
       have an affirmative action plan per se, and the District needed to become
       more aware of the law and find ways to come into compliance.

       Co-Chair Brim-Edwards asked General Counsel to provide the Board with
       the statute referenced by Director Jackson and further provide the Board
       with her interpretation of the statute and have an overlay from Human
       Resources on how PPS is doing in terms of compliance.
