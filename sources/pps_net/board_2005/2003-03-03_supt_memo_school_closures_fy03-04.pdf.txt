RE:      School Closures FY 03-04

On February 3rd I received recommendations from the Space Allocation Committee on
school closures and other ways to make more efficient use of our school property.
The closure recommendations have generated considerable reaction. Many board
members and I have attended community meetings and have met with concerned
groups of parents, staff, and community members. All neighborhoods in which
school closures are being considered have voiced strong support for their school and
for the neighborhood school concept.

I had originally intended to make my closure recommendations as part of my budget
recommendations. However, to incorporate the tentative settlement with the
Portland Association of Teachers and the accompanying efforts to provide local
funding, we have moved our formal budget process back to April. However, I must
make recommendations on school closures now to give parents, students, and staff
time to plan for next year.

Therefore, we will use the following process:

      March 3      Superintendent Recommendations

      March 6      Hearing at BESC on Proposed Closures at 6:30 pm – public
                   comment welcome

      March 10     (Regular Board Meeting) Decisions on proposed closures at
                   6:30 pm. BESC – no public comment

      March 31     Student transfer submission deadline at Enrollment and
                   Transfer Center
Later this spring, I will recommend a school size and closure policy to the Board and
ask that each of the following categories be addressed as part of any closure
recommendation:

      •     Data reviewed and space needs based on projected student population

      •     Education evaluation and special considerations

      •     Economic efficiency, including projected reuse of building


Today I am attaching the analysis using the categories for the two proposed closures.


Closures Are Still Necessary

The events of the past few days and the prospect of local bridge funding give us hope
that we can operate full school years at a level that moves us forward educationally
until the state finds a school funding solution. Nonetheless, we must still consider
school closures and consolidations because:
• Even with bridge funding of $48 million per year, about $9 million in budget
savings are needed to maintain a 30:1 staffing ratio next year.
•   Our enrollment continues to decline, especially in elementary schools.
• We are moving forward with efforts to market our administrative buildings. I will
have a recommendation on declaring the CSC surplus in June. Recent events
indicate interest in the BESC could be picking up soon. Closing these buildings will
require us to find space to relocate staff housed in them.


Public Participation Has Been Extraordinary


As a superintendent who has served during some very tumultuous times, I must
recognize the extraordinarily helpful and considerate participation by the parents,
staff, and community members of affected schools since the Space Committee’s
recommendations were made. People recognize the difficult situation we are in and
are willing to consider many alternatives to closure and, if closure must occur, are
willing to make the transition as supportive as possible for children.


Effect of Costs on Decisions


One of the most frequent questions I heard during my review was the effect of cost
savings on the decision. For example, it was often pointed out that small schools in
small buildings had lower operating costs than similarly small schools in larger
buildings. This is not a surprising result, but it is not enough cost information to
base a decision on.
Building decisions have long-term effects. In the long run, it makes the most
economic sense to right-size our buildings to the desired size for instruction and
locate them to best serve the geographic and social diversity of our students.


Thus in some cases it may be more desirable to consolidate two small schools into
the larger building if the result is a school within the desired size range for
instruction and is well-located for the students it serves. On the other hand, it may
be more desirable to improve an existing too-small school if the alternatives are not
well-located or are in poor condition.



Recommendations Concerning Specific Schools


I believe the following recommendations of the Space Allocation Committee make
sense educationally, and I am recommending them to the Board.

          •   Closing Brooklyn School and consolidating with Grout School and
              expanding Winterhaven

          •   Closing Meek School and consolidating with Vernon School

These schools are under 200 in K-5 enrollment and are expected to continue to
decline in enrollment. (In fact, they are closer to 110 in projected enrollment with
students from the neighborhood boundary only.) They can be combined with an
adjoining school and maintain an acceptable limit of enrollment. There are
educational advantages to both recommendations.

Brooklyn

I recommend that we proceed with the closure of Brooklyn and consolidate the
school with Grout Elementary as recommended by the Committee for the following
reasons:

      •   The Brooklyn neighborhood program currently enrolls 120 students. This
          is well below the draft school size policy and the enrollment of the two
          schools we closed last year. The area has experienced and is expected to
          continue an overall enrollment decline.

      •   There is adequate space at Grout to house the new students, while
          remaining within the draft guidelines.

      •   The consolidation will expand the program opportunities available for
          existing Brooklyn students. (e.g. grade level classes at all levels; Reading
          Recovery; Portland Impact social services: Arts for Learning; own
          playground ) (A complete report on educational impact is detailed in the
          appendix.)
       •   For students who may wish transfer to Winterhaven, five slots per grade
           level will be given priority status for students currently enrolled in Brooklyn
           for the FY03-04 school year only.

Meek

Similarly, I recommend that we proceed with the closure of Meek School and
consolidate the school with Vernon Elementary as recommended by the Committee
for the following reasons:

       •   The Meek neighborhood program currently enrolls under 110 students.
           (The balance of approximately 70 students are transfers). The total of
           approximately 180 is still well below the draft school size policy and
           comparable to the enrollment of the two schools we closed last year. The
           area has experienced and is expected to continue an overall enrollment
           decline.

       •   There is adequate space at Vernon to house the new students. (Depending
           on the number of non-neighborhood transfers who go to Vernon, the
           numbers may slightly exceed the draft guidelines.)
       •   The consolidation will expand the program opportunities available for
           existing Meek students. (e.g. Pre K and full day kindergarten; Success for
           All; computer labs; OASIS tutoring assistance; RIF program; Chess Club) (A
           complete report on educational impact is detailed in the appendix.)


Edwards

I recommend we not proceed with the Space Allocation Committee’s recommendation
to close Edwards next year and move its program to Richmond School. Instead, I
recommend we, by November, complete a facilitated analysis involving affected
members of the Franklin and Cleveland communities with a goal of closing at least
one facility in the area while preserving education options available to students
within the size policy adopted by the Board. This analysis would be conducted like
the analysis described in more detail for Rieke School below. Based on this analysis,
I will bring a recommendation to the Board in December.

I make this recommendation for the following reasons:

       •   Edwards’ current enrollment is about 200 and is relatively stable. This is
           slightly larger than the schools recommended for closure this year and last
           year.
       •   The Space Allocation Committee’s recommendation warned that it had not
           resolved whether Edward’s year-round curriculum could survive at
           Richmond. This issue is still unresolved. In fact, subsequent analysis
           indicates a number of problems with transferring the program to Richmond,
           including adjustments to the feeder patterns, building issues, and the
           migration of students to other schools.
        •   If a successful transfer could be made, the resulting enrollment at
            Richmond would exceed 600, which is substantially larger than the size
            policy currently being considered by the Board.
        •   Enrollment at the neighborhood schools at Sunnyside and Richmond both
            are less than 120. A more comprehensive analysis may produce better
            choices than we are currently aware of.
        •   In the long run, survival of Edwards’ year-round curriculum requires a
            building that will allow Edwards to grow so that it can operate within the
            size policy adopted by the Board. Edwards has operated for many years
            under the threat of closure.
        •   The site council at Edwards has actively taken on the issue of finding
            alternatives that would ensure the long-run survival of the year-round
            program. I believe that with further analysis and clarification of our size
            policy, the site council will work with those of other schools, parent and
            community groups, and our central staff to come up with a better solution
            that meets our mutual needs. In the meantime, the site council has made a
            number of recommendations to reduce costs next year. These will be
            considered in my budget recommendations in April.

Rieke

I recommend we not proceed with the Space Allocation Committee’s recommendation
to close Rieke next year and move its students to Hayhurst and Bridlemile. Instead,
I recommend that closure or realignment of the boundaries of Rieke be considered as
part of the comprehensive realignment of boundaries and feeder patterns across the
entire West Side that was also recommended by the Space Allocation Committee.

Specifically, I recommend the following process:

        •   Facilities and Asset Management will develop a facilitated process with the
            Lincoln and Wilson school and parent communities to provide
            recommendations to the Space Allocation Committee.       The process should
            allow adequate input from stakeholders including Directors of Student
            Achievement, principals, parents and the community.

        •   This process will:

               a. Realign feeder patterns to alleviate crowding at West Sylvan Middle
                  School and Lincoln High School.

               b. Recommend a more efficient use of our facilities consistent with the
                  final School Size and Closure Policy. Based on the current draft, my
                  goal is to close at least one facility in the area while preserving
                  education options available to students within the size policy adopted
                  by the Board.
              c. Complete recommendations to the Space Allocation Committee by
                 November 1, 2003. I would like to bring closure recommendations to
                 the Board next year in December.

I make this recommendation for the following reasons:
         • Rieke’s current enrollment is 266, which is substantially larger than other schools we
         have closed and could be within a size policy that would be recommended to the Board.
          • The Space Allocation Committee’s recommendation was based largely on
          the limitations of the building and its location for alternative uses. Of the
          four schools nearby with enrollments below 300, Rieke has the smallest
          structure and is the most conveniently located for alternative uses.
          Although these are important criteria, the recommendation does not
          consider the potential long-term goals for most efficiently serving the
          neighborhood population.
          • Due to past controversial boundary decisions, some nearby children are
          not within Rieke’s boundaries. This means that, although the building is
          limited, the immediate surrounding area probably could support a larger
          enrollment.
          • The past controversy involved the effect of alternative boundaries on the
          economic and cultural diversity of the schools in the area. It appears the
          proposed new boundaries (dividing the existing Rieke area between
          Hayhurst and Bridlemile) would not improve this situation, and could make
          it worse.


Standard Support Formula

One alternative to closing small schools is to reduce the amount of standard support
given to each school. As part of my budget recommendations for next year which will
need to include funding cuts, I will recommend a new standard school support
formula.

The standard support formula provides campus monitors, counselors, child
development specialists, bookkeepers, data clerks, and additional secretarial
support. Music, physical education, and library staff also come from basic support
in the elementary schools, as specific allocations for these activities have been cut in
past budgets.

Under the proposed new formula, schools under 300 enrollment will receive
disproportionately less standard support than in past years. Specifically, here is the
section from the draft report developed by a team of principals concerning standard
support. The full formula will be considered by the Board as part of the
Superintendent’s budget in April.

ISSUES FOR TRANSITION

The two schools have different literacy programs. The highly structured Success For All program at
Vernon has resulted in significant staff development over the past six years

Meek has retained a music program.

Meek and Vernon staff would need to develop a common daily schedule.

Vernon staff participate in NSF math while Meek staff participate in NSF science.

school improvement plan would need to be developed).

Both staffs would need to develop a common school improvement plan and determine the reading
program to be used to meet the academic needs of students.

More students at Vernon will increase traffic congestion in the vicinity immediately around the school

require busing which would be a financial increase to the consolidated budget

Since some students could return to Rigler and Faubion the Meek staff may need to be split among three
schools. Consideration needs to be given to staff placement choices.

Planning will need to be completed and assistance will need to be provided to administration to assure a
positive combining of the staffs and the development of a cohesive team.

                 selections by letter on May 1, 2003.
