       Recognition of the Grant High School Royal Blues

       Congratulations to Grant High School’s Constitutional Law Team

       Director Williams congratulated the Grant High School Constitutional Law
       Team for winning the state “We the People: The Citizen and the
       Constitution” competition sponsored by the Classroom Law Project.
       Lincoln High School placed second in state competition this year – both
       Grant and Lincoln won their respective regional meets. The Grant team
       will travel to Washington, DC this spring for national finals.

       Congratulations to Vernon Elementary

       Student Representative Suleima Cortez said State Schools
       Superintendent Susan Castillo recently announced that five schools in
       Oregon were chosen for the 2005 Celebrating School Success Awards,
       and Vernon Elementary was one of those five. She indicated Vernon
       would receive $2,000 to spend as they pleased and would be recognized
       at the Celebrating Student Success Award Banquet to be held on April
       29th. Each winner is a school with a high minority population that has
       demonstrated excellence in student achievement, and earned a “strong”
       or “exceptional” rating on the Oregon school report cards for the past two
       years. They also must meet achievement targets under No Child Left
       Behind. Further, each winning school had to demonstrate improvement in
       math and English/language arts over a period of three years. It was noted
       that Astor Elementary was a finalist for this award.

       The Board Co-Chairs and Superintendent Phillips presented Vernon
       Principal Joan Miller and members of her staff with a certificate.

       Director Brim-Edwards discussed the steps taken by the Board to use
       PPS facilities more effectively. She said the Best Use of Facilities Task
       Force focused on non-school buildings to increase revenue and came up
       with a series of recommendations, which the Board accepted. She noted

3.     FINANCIAL ACCOUNTABILITY

       Local Option Levy and General Obligation Bond

       Director Morgan, Chair of the Board’s Finance, Audit and Operations
       Committee, noted on February 14, 2005 the Board passed Board Actions
       3216 and 3217 which directed administration to take the necessary steps
       to look at the feasibility of renewing the Local Option levy and General
       Obligation bond on the May ballot. He explained Board Actions 3243 and
       3244 recommended not going for renewal on either the Local Option or
       General Obligation bond on the May 2005 ballot. He said the Finance,
       Audit and Operations Committee looked at the issue of putting both issues
       before the voters in May and listened to PPS partners, who were
       unanimous in agreeing that it was not feasible to be on the May ballot.
       Director Morgan indicated the central reason was the problem with the
       double majority rule. In the last presidential election, the number of
       registered voters increased by 20%, which would make it even more
       difficult to get a double majority. He emphasized that the state has an
       obligation to fund K-12 education, and it needs to meet that obligation.

       Director Williams asked when the district would again look at the
       possibility of putting one or both issues before the voters.

       Co-Chair Wynde responded it was safe to say that the Board is in
       continuing discussions with other local elected officials and PPS partners
       and they are also talking to legislators. He said the strategy was to
       evaluate the options for going on the ballot either in November of 2005 or
       May of 2006.

       Co-Chair Regan noted the last General Obligation bond paid for the new
       Forest Heights school, seismic upgrades to schools, updated computers
       boiler upgrades and a host of other capital needs. She also noted that
       due to the double majority, the chance of winning an election in May to

       Superintendent Outlines Options for Consideration for 2005-2006
       Budget

       Superintendent Phillips shared a Power Point presentation on the options
       for consideration for the 2005-2006 budget. The Superintendent noted
       that the budget for K-12 education in Oregon, as proposed by Governor
       Kulongoski, the loss of the Local Option levy ($33.5 million), all contribute
       to a shortfall of $51 million in the 2005-2006 budget. She noted in 2006-
       2007, when the temporary local income tax (Multnomah County) expires,
       the shortfall could be as high as $98 million. Despite the budget shortfalls,
       the Superintendent emphasized the focus will continue to be on student
       achievement. She said PPS will save $7.9 million in each of the next two
       years by buying down the PERS rates. Superintendent Phillips further
       indicated the district will push for the legislature to increase state support
       over the Governor’s recommended level. She said she will be proposing
       plans for $35.4 million in budget reductions, with every dollar focused on
       the core educational mission.

       Co-Chair Regan noted the Superintendent has proposed using a new
       staffing allocation formula. She also stated when the district closed
       Youngson several years ago, there was a $1.5 million savings made by
       moving special education students back into the district and placing them
       at the Youngson site.

       The Superintendent responded that a few years ago the district had a high
       percentage of out-of-district special education placements, and many of
       those have been brought back to the district at a reduced cost with the
       same quality of services.

4.     CHARTER APPLICATION FOR PORTLAND PUBLIC INTERNATIONAL
       CHARTER MIDDLE AND HIGH SCHOOL

       Co-Chair Regan said Portland Public International Charter Middle and
       High School (PPIC) submitted a charter application to PPS on November
       1, 2004. The Board’s Educational Options and Professional Development
       Committee met in January of 2005 and voted unanimously to recommend
       to the full Board that the application be denied. She indicated the full
       Board approved the resolution to deny the application at its meeting on
       January 24, 2005.

       Co-Chair Regan stated the applicant, per statute, submitted a revised
       application for consideration of an appeal of the Board’s decision. She
       said a public hearing on the new application was held of March 8, 2005.
       The committee did not feel the modified proposal met the minimum
       requirements in the area of financial, business and organizational plan.
       Co-Chair Regan said the committee was in agreement that the potential of
       the proposal should not go unrecognized and, in fact, the committee
       believes that the concept of the school, a language immersion middle and
       high school program with an International Baccalaureate component, is a
       desirable option for PPS to pursue.

5.     LEGISLATIVE UPDATE

       Co-Chair Regan said she, Superintendent Phillips and Director Wynde
       met with Portland-area legislators recently and shared the school district’s
       current budget situation with them. She said they wanted legislators to
       understand the reality of a shortfall in funding from the state, plus the loss
       of the Local Option levy and the General Obligation bond at the end of this
       school year. She noted that with the assistance of Justin Martin, PPS
       Government Relations Specialist, she and Superintendent presented to
       legislators in Salem the previous week.

       Co-Chair Regan said she also has met with representatives from Stand for
       Children and the Portland Schools Foundations to discuss PPS’ funding
       situation. She said Superintendent Phillips, Co-Chair Wynde and she also
       met with Mayor Potter’s staff and would soon meet with other city
       commissioners.

       Co-Chair Regan noted a tracking system was in place to ensure that any
       legislation in Salem that could impact public education and PPS was
       monitored. She added that currently they are tracking more than 110
       pieces of legislation. She suggested interested parties log onto the
       Oregon School Board Association’s website at www.OSBA.org and click
       on “Bill Tracking.”
