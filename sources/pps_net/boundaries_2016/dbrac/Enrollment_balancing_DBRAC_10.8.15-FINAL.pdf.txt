  current policy

        KPI Development
• Staff developed and then prioritized a
  subset of items to further refine into KPIs
• Also, differentiated between “global KPI”
  and “situational” metric
  – Global KPI = measured for every scenario.
  – Situational metric = reported on a case-by-
    case basis for affected schools and/or
    scenarios.
                   Global KPIs
1. All neighborhood schools operate with at least 2 sections per grade
   level
2. Reduction in number of schools that are over capacity
3. Fewer middle schools and high schools have split feeder patterns
4. Awareness of racial, ethnic and socio-economic distributions at
   every school
5. Awareness of number of schools that are deemed high, medium
   and low poverty
6. More students attend schools within closer walking distance to their
   home
7. Fewer students are reassigned to new schools
8. Fewer students who were directly affected by recent enrollment
   balancing changes are subject to new changes

          Situational Metrics
• More schools operate within preferred facility capacity utilization
  ranges
• Neighborhood schools operate within preferred enrollment ranges
  given their configuration
• Fewer students need to be bused
• Minimize expenses related to modification of facilities
• Enrollment ranges within similar configurations are minimized
• Awareness when special education focus classrooms are re-located
• More students have safe routes to school as determined by
  Transportation Department.
• More neighborhood association boundaries are aligned with school
  boundaries.
SAMPLE SCENARIO
    Scenario: Sample Test
• This scenario is for training purposes only,
  and does not include several types of
  changes that are expected in actual
  scenarios:
  – Maintains HS feeder patterns
  – Maintains current school configurations
  – Maintains district-wide program locations
  – Does not open new schools
    Scenario: Sample Test
• Additional constraints:
  – Contiguous boundaries
  – K-5 can only feed one 6-8
  – A K-5 cannot feed a K-8
• Uses current students (2014-2015), not
  projections
    Scenario: Sample Test
• Within those constraints, this scenario
  shows boundary changes designed to
  accomplish the following:
  – Decrease number of schools that have less
    than two sections per grade level
  – Decrease number of schools operating at or
    above 105% of utilization
  – Limit the number of students impacted by
    boundary change
  Challenges with Scenario
• DOES NOT lead to significantly more
  right-sized schools (the highest desired
  outcome in the Enrollment Balancing
  Values Framework).
• No significant reduction in the number of
  under-enrolled schools, particularly K-8
  schools
• Half of currently over-utilized schools do
  not see improvement in this scenario
  Challenges with Scenario
• Currently, 31% of K-8 students in historically
  underserved racial groups attend schools with
  fewer than 2 sections per grade level.
• If the sample scenario were adopted PPS
  estimates that rate would reduce to 28%, which
  is not a significant improvement.
• Schools with fewer than two sections per grade
  level have extra challenges in providing core
  programs to all students.

Appendix

DETAILED ASSUMPTIONS
             Timeframe
• Scenarios will show what enrollment at
  schools could be in 2015 if the proposed
  changes were fully in effect today
• Scenarios will show potential enrollment at
  schools in 2016, and 2020
  – 1 & 5 years from now
  – Allows for some level of phased in
    implementation (discussed later)
             Forecasts
• Enrollment forecasts developed in 2014
  based on 2013 data are the baseline for
  scenario modeling for future years
           Capture rates
• The 2014 capture rates by neighborhood
  are the basis for scenario modeling.
• When shifting boundaries, the current
  capture rate will be assumed to continue
  at the new school
• It may be necessary to modify capture
  rates if shifting from a K-8 to a MS, or if a
  trend in capture rate change is visible from
  enrollment/transfer data
           Net transfers
• For modeling purposes, every school will
  have a net transfer rate:
  – Transfers out of a school’s catchment area –
    transfers in to a school from other
    neighborhoods
• For new schools or new configurations,
  the net transfer rate will be based on rates
  at schools with same structures and
  similar demographics/proximity to choice
  programs
