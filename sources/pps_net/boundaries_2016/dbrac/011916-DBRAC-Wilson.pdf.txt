EXECUTIVE SUMMARY FROM 01/19/16 WILSON HS MEETING
Key takeaways from the public comment received by PPS at the 1/19/15 meeting:
   •   Spanish Immersion at Atkinson. Testimony both for and against relocating the Spanish
       Immersion Program was received from the public comment, although more testimony
       was received in support of keeping Atkinson’s SI in its current location. The majority of
       parents expressed opposition, stating the importance that the facility was kept in its
       current facility for the purposes of the importance of socioeconomic and cultural
       diversity, the logistical importance of the programming’s proximity to lower
       socioeconomic communities, and the desire to limit disruptions to the programs successes
       by moving it to East Sylvan. Testimony in support of moving the Spanish Immersion
       program was also received, which acknowledged the difficulties of housing two programs
       in one building and the opportunities for the Spanish Immersion program to successfully
       thrive in its own facility. Parents also pointed out that moving the program would have
       implications for existing students in SI programming at Grey and Lincoln, and that the
       matriculation patterns of SI students need to be studied.
   •   Transportation: Testimony representing virtually every school on the Westside
       expressed significant concern that the DBRAC proposals create significant difficulties for
       families. Many parents noted that they cherished their children’s’ ability to walk or bike
       to school, and that the new boundaries would create significant automobile traffic, create
       logistical hazards for families, provide difficult TriMet routes to new facilities, and force
       parents and students to spend long hours in gridlock on Southwest Portland’s winding
       streets getting to their new facilities.
   •   Solutions for overcrowding. Many parents expressed a desire to see PPS expedite
       options to address overcrowding, ranging from support to a new school in Northwest
       Portland, moving Chapman students across the river to schools on the Eastside, opening
       Smith, utilizing East Sylvan for the Spanish Immersion program currently at Ainsworth,
       or moving MLC (which had substantial representation as well, opposed to any change to
       their current facility).
   •   Approximately 490 parents showed up at Wilson High School to share their concerns
       with the current plans; the majority of comments expressed frustration and discontent
       with the January 9th proposal, the short amount of time for public feedback, and a desire
       to see the boundaries kept where they were previously to preserve intact neighborhoods.
PRESENTATION SUMMARY
Mr. Jon Isaacs welcomed the large audience to the high school and introduced Wilson HS
Principal Brian Chatard, Hayhurst Principal Deanne Froehlich, DBRAC members in attendance,
PPS Board Members, and PPS Superintendent Carole Smith. PPS Staff showed the “Growing
Greater Schools” video that explained why PPS was conducting this enrollment balancing
process. Mr. Isaacs explained the current stage of the District’s redistricting process, noting that
proposed changes to the Westside schools stemmed from PPS’ attempts to address overcrowding
at Chapman, Hayhurst/Odyssey, and Lincoln. He introduced PPS staffer Ms. Sarah Singer, who
explained how the district used the feedback received from the community at 17 public hearings
in regards to Scenarios I and II this past fall to draw these new boundaries to better reflect the
needs and desires of the PPS community. She also noted that in December, PPS received new
data that included 2015 enrollment numbers, which forecast even more crowding at Westside
schools, including Chapman and Capitol Hill.
Ms. Judy Brennan, PPS Director of Enrollment and Transfers, acknowledged that community
feedback in opposition to the split-feeder pattern proposed at Bridlemile helped inform PPS’
current proposal. The proposal involves shifting boundaries to Chapman, Ainsworth, Rieke,
Hayhurst, Maplewood, Jackson, Capitol Hill and Stevenson to address overcrowding and
underenrollment concerns. She noted that while these changes don’t fully address all of the
problems, the forthcoming PPS Bond Measure represents an opportunity to build additional
capacity at Lincoln. She continued that DBRAC asked to study ways to implement their adopted
values into these new proposals, which included moving the Spanish Immersion program at
Atkinson to East Sylvan and shifting Bridlemile to feed into Gray and Wilson, as well as
potentially relocated MLC. She concluded by acknowledging that PPS Policy states that
boundary changes would be implemented to incoming students only and that existing students
would be “grandfathered” into continuing into their existing feeder patterns, although the PPS
School Board has the legal authority to change that policy if it deems it necessary to expedite the
balancing process.
Ms. Brennan noted that information about the DBRAC process and documents provided at
meetings are available on PPS’ website (http://www.pps.k12.or.us/departments/enrollment-
transfer/9522.htm). After their presentation, Mr. Isaacs explained the format for the public
comment section.
