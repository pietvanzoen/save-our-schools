             Scenarios for balancing enrollment among schools
Enrollment at Portland Public Schools is steadily growing. This has
created an opportunity to create strong schools in every neighborhood by
balancing enrollment among schools so that no schools are over-crowded
or under-enrolled and so that the academic program offered in each
school fits well in the school building.
On Oct. 29, PPS released two possible scenarios for balancing enrollment.
The scenarios are a starting point for community discussion about
how best to “right size” schools. A school board decision is expected
in January/February and any changes would start in Fall 2016 and be
phased in over time.
Scenario #1 Most K-8s become K-5/middle schools: Most overcrowded/under-enrolled K-8 schools right-sized
Scenario #2 Some K-8s become K-5/middle schools: Most overcrowded/under-enrolled K-8 schools right-sized
Goal for Scenarios 1 & 2: Create a better mix of K-8 and K-5/middle schools so that more schools are offering the
academic program in buildings of the appropriate size, supported by a sufficient number and distribution of students.

Building Scenario #1
Right size schools and provide greater and more equitable access to programs by:
•   Converting 22 schools from K-8s into K-5s and middle schools, Kellogg and Tubman would re-open as middle
    schools
•   Boundary changes between some schools to right size enrollments and align feeder patterns, including at the high
    school level
•   Consolidate several immersion and international baccalaureate programs at middle schools
•   Relocate Vietnamese Immersion K-5 to Vestal and Odyssey focus option to East Sylvan

Building Scenario #2
Right size schools and provide greater and more equitable access to programs by:
•   Converting 16 schools from K-8s into K-5s and middle schools
•   Boundary changes between some schools to right size enrollments and align feeder patterns, including at the high
    school level
•   Move Vietnamese Immersion to Vestal, ACCESS Academy and Creative Science School to Kellogg, and Odyssey to
    East Sylvan
•   Re-open Kellogg with focus option programs, re-establish Rose City and Clark as neighborhood K-5 schools

Impact of Scenario 1 & 2
•   Vast majority of under-enrollment issues solved in grades K-8
•   Increase in number of schools within preferred enrollment ranges (i.e. 3 sections per grade) in grades K-8
•   Vast majority of over-crowding issues solved
•   Split feeder patterns:
     oo Scenario 1 creates one new feeder pattern (schools feeding to two or more schools at the next transition)

     oo Scenario 2 creates a net increase of two split feeder patterns.

Understanding the scenarios for balancing enrollment among schools
How did PPS determine possible boundary changes?
PPS created possible boundary changes using an analysis of enrollment and building capacity in combination with such
values as keeping the distance from home to school as short as possible. Learn how PPS developed the scenarios at
www.pps.net “Growing Great Schools.”
If my school boundary is changed, will my student or younger siblings have to go to a new school?
Current school board policy allows students already attending a school and their younger, incoming siblings to remain at
that school following boundary change in most cases. Exceptions could be recommended as part of this current process
in order to balance enrollment more rapidly. However, any such changes would be discussed during the upcoming
community input period and become part of the final plan for consideration by the school board.
When would any changes to high school boundaries take effect?
The phase-in process of any new high school boundaries will be determined after the community input process as part
of the Superintendent’s recommendation to the school board in January and would need to be voted on as part of the
board’s decision-making process in January/February.
Under current school board policy, existing high school students can continue to attend the same high school and so
can their younger siblings. Incoming 9th graders and other new students who live in the boundary change area would
enroll in the new high school in the fall following a boundary change. Each year another grade of students is added.
Why is PPS proposing to make many of the K-8 schools into K-5/middle school combinations?
It has proven challenging over time to offer the robust academic program in K-8 schools that middle grade students
need. Many of the K-8 buildings were elementary schools and are too small. K-8’s with low middle grade enrollment are
challenged to support a full staff to offer all the classes students need.
Among the more than 4,000 parents, students, staff and community members who responded the PPS 2025 survey in
spring 2015:
•   71% agreed that students should have the opportunity to attend a middle school (grades 6-8) offering a variety of
    classes even if that means more transitions between schools.
•   The middle school preference persisted racial groups (71-77% with the slight exception of Latino respondents - 59%)
    and across high school clusters, and students, parents, staff and community members.

The draft scenarios for balancing enrollment are expected to change in response to community input. PPS encourages

the Superintendent in December. The Superintendent will then make a recommendation to the school board for
consideration and an anticipated vote in late January or February.
