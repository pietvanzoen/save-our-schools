                    ​About the Office of Internal Performance Audit​

​Purpose and Mission​

​ tate​ ​and​ ​local​ ​governments​ ​can​ ​enhance​ ​credibility​ ​with​ ​the​ ​taxpayers​ ​by​ ​implementing​
S
​performance​​audits​​to​​support​​accountability​​and​​ensure​​that​​tax​​dollars​​are​​spent​​as​​effectively​
 ​as possible.​

​ ortland​ ​Public​ ​Schools​ ​(PPS)​ ​performance​ ​auditing,​ ​conducted​ ​by​ ​the​ ​Office​ ​of​ ​Internal​
P
​Performance​​Audit​​(OIPA),​​is​​an​​objective​​and​​systematic​​examination​​of​​evidence​​to​​provide​​an​
 ​independent​​assessment​​of​​a​​government​​organization,​​program,​​activity,​​or​​function.​ ​The​​goal​
  ​of​ ​these​ ​audits​ ​is​ ​to​ ​improve​ ​program​ ​effectiveness;​ ​improve​ ​the​ ​equity​ ​of​ ​service​ ​delivery;​
   ​provide​ ​useful,​ ​objective,​ ​and​ ​timely​ ​information;​ ​strengthen​ ​administrative​ ​and​ ​management​
    ​systems​ ​and​ ​controls;​​facilitate​​decision-making​​by​​parties​​with​​responsibility​​for​​overseeing​​or​
     ​initiating​ ​corrective​ ​action;​ ​and​ ​improve​ ​student​ ​achievement​ ​District-wide.​ ​The​ ​objectives​ ​of​
      ​performance​ ​audits​ ​vary,​ ​but​ ​generally​ ​address​ ​whether​ ​PPS​ ​is​ ​operating​ ​economically​ ​and​
       ​efficiently, and/or whether it is achieving desired results.​

​ he​​purpose​​of​​the​​internal​​performance​​audit​​function​​is​​to​​help​​improve​​the​​accountability​​and​
T
​performance of PPS through independent auditing and reporting.​

​Independence and Objectivity​

​ he​​OIPA​​Senior​​Internal​​Performance​​Auditor​​is​​hired,​​evaluated,​​and​​may​​only​​be​​removed​​by​
T
​the​ ​PPS​ ​Board​ ​of​ ​Education.​ ​Reporting​ ​directly​ ​to​ ​the​ ​PPS​ ​Board​ ​of​ ​Education​ ​is​ ​meant​ ​to​
 ​ensure​ ​the​ ​work​​of​​the​​OIPA​​is​​independent,​​impartial,​​and​​objective​​so​​stakeholders​​can​​trust​
  ​the audit results and recommendations.​

​Standards​

​ he​ ​OIPA​ ​adheres​ ​to​ ​very​ ​rigorous​ ​and​ ​demanding​ ​professional​ ​auditing​ ​requirements​
T
​described​ ​in​ ​Generally​ ​Accepted​ ​Government​ ​Auditing​​Standards,​​or​​more​​commonly​​referred​
 ​to as GAGAS or the Yellow Book.​

​Authority and Responsibilities​

​ he​ ​authority​ ​and​ ​responsibilities​ ​of​ ​the​ ​OIPA​​are​​defined​​in​​its​​charter​​and​​PPS​​Board​​Policy​
T
​1.60.040-P, both approved by the PPS Board of Education.​
