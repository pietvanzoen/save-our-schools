Superintendent Armstrong expressed appreciation for the district’s labor partners, and noted that the
implementation of furloughs and school calendar changes are a measure of last resort. Staff detailed the
revised schedule. Board members provided comments, including appreciation for the collective problem-
solving efforts of staff and labor partners, and the value of recovering instructional time during the
converted early release days.

Board Members provided comments and asked questions about the proposed furloughs, including
discussion on the possibility of utilizing reserve funds to avoid these cuts. Staff advised against spending
any reserve funds, noting that reserves are already at the policy-mandated minimum of 5%, and current
reserves represent less than one month of payroll. Dropping below the 5% threshold would compromise
the district's financial resiliency and ability to meet payroll in the event of mid-year state funding shortfalls.
Board Members noted that the current plan provides a necessary balance between reducing workdays
and avoiding additional layoffs.

Staff: Michelle Morrison – Chief Financial Officer

Community Budget Review Committee Members: Angela Bonilla, Anne Cherry, Shain Correy, Jen Gray-
O’Connor, Laura Hernandez, Karanja Crews, Julia Plowman, Eli Posada, and Shannon Staley

Staff provided a budget update, including confirming that the current mid-year shortfall has been closed
through spending controls and the implementation of furloughs, with preliminary third-quarter data
remaining on target. Staff reiterated that there is a projected $50 million deficit for the 2026-27 school
year due to declining levy revenue and rising expenses. The presentation outlined the core principles
guiding the process for drafting the 2026-27 budget.

Staff provided an overview of the Community Budget Review Committee (CBRC). Representatives from
the CBRC shared recommendations for the upcoming budget, emphasizing the need for transparency,
systemic stability, and the protection of student-facing roles. Their priorities included evaluating the

Growing Great Schools-Rightsizing

Time: 7:36 pm

Staff: Margaret Calvert – Assistant Superintendent, School Planning and Modernization; and Dr. Jon
Franco – Senior Chief of Operations

An overview of the right-sizing process was provided, including enrollment trends, school building
capacity data, and the current proposed timeline. Information was shared on best practices and
rightsizing approaches used by similarly sized districts, highlighting variations in how right-sizing
processes have been implemented. Superintendent Armstrong indicated that by defining what should be
true at all schools across the district a district wide vision and strategic plan also be developed.

Board members engaged in small group discussions to identify key considerations from the example
materials, including actions to incorporate, non-negotiables, and areas to approach with caution.
Feedback emphasized the importance of clearly defining what constitutes a well-resourced school,
ensuring equitable access to programs across all schools, preserving neighborhood school access, and
minimizing negative impacts on historically underserved communities. Additional considerations included
transportation impacts, building utilization, and avoiding approaches that could create division among
communities.

Staff indicated that the Board’s feedback would inform community engagement efforts, with plans to
share draft values and gather input through upcoming listening sessions and surveys. It was noted that
the timeline is being refined with the goal of bringing forward recommendations in the fall, and that
multiple strategies, including program adjustments and potential consolidations, will be considered. Board
members participated in values prioritization activity.

Data Dive Winter 2026: 2025-26 Successful Schools Survey Results

Time: 8:37 pm

Staff: Dr. Renard Adams – Chief Accountability and Equity Officer

A summary of the 2025–2026 Successful Schools Survey results was provided, including context,
methodology, and participation details. It was noted that the survey supports the district’s continuous
improvement efforts and was administered during a timeframe aligned with conferences to increase
participation. Results indicated overall improvement across respondent groups, with positive trends in
attendance, school climate, and social safety. Additional trends were shared by grade level, area, and
focal student groups, along with family and staff perceptions. It was noted that families indicated an
increase in school safety and climate, and that staff responses indicated that social emotional learning
practices in schools are improving, while improvement in professional learning and district leadership was
still needed.

Board members asked questions and shared feedback regarding survey timing, participation levels, and
data reliability, and expressed concern about the experiences of multilingual learners and students with
disabilities, particularly related to sense of belonging.

                                          RESOLUTION No. 7283

                             2026-27 Standard Inter-District Student Transfers

                                                 RECITALS

A.   State law requires district school boards to decide each year whether to participate in the standard
     inter-district transfer process, including:

            1. The maximum number of resident students, if any, who will be released to schools in
               other district,
            2. The maximum number of non-resident students, if any, who will be accepted for
               enrollment in district schools,
            3. The priorities that will apply in a random lottery, in the event that there are more requests
               than maximum number of slots for releases or approvals, and
            4. The length of time that agreements will be in effect for non-resident students who transfer
               into district school.

B.   More than 1,400 PPS students are residents of other districts, comprising 3.3% of district enrollment.
     Approximately 250 current PPS students who live in other districts will need standard inter-district
     transfers in order to remain in PPS schools next year.

C.   For the 2026-27 school year, Superintendent Armstrong recommends the PPS Board of Directors
     approve the following plan for accepting residents of other districts into PPS through the standard
     inter-district transfer process, so long as they have received permission from their home districts:

            1. An unlimited number of students will be allowed to transfer into PPS if they apply by
               September 15, 2026 and meet at least one of the following priorities

                    a. Students who had a legal change of residence out of the PPS boundary during
                       the past year will be allowed to remain enrolled at their current PPS schools.
                    b. Students who have siblings already enrolled in PPS will be accepted, so long as
                       space is available at the requested schools.
                    c. Students who have reached the highest grade of their current PPS schools and
                       wish to continue at the next school level (such as elementary to middle school or
                       middle to high school) will be allowed, so long as space is available at the
                       requested schools.

            2. Additionally, up to 100 students who do not qualify for any of the above priorities will be
               admitted to PPS, so long as space is available at the requested schools.

                    a. If there are more applicants than slots a random number will be used as a tie-
                       breaker.

            3. New transfers will remain in effect through the highest grade of the approved school,
               subject to conditions described in PPS 4.10.090-AD.

D.   Superintendent Armstrong recommends the PPS Board of Directors approve the following plan for
     releasing PPS resident students to schools in other district through the standard inter-district transfer
     process:

            1. An unlimited number of students will be released out of PPS if they apply by September
               15, 2026 and meet at least one of the following priorities:

                    a. Students who had a legal change of residence into the PPS boundary during the
                       past year will be released from PPS in order to remain enrolled in their current
                       districts.
                    b. Students not yet enrolled in a different district will be released from PPS if they
                       have siblings who attended the requested districts during the 2025-26 school
                       year and will remain enrolled there during 2026-27.

                                MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING


This Memorandum of Understanding is between Portland Public School District 1J, Multnomah County,
Oregon, Portland Public Schools (“PPS”) and the Service Employees International Union (“SEIU”), Local
503.

Recitals:

    ●   PPS is facing an extraordinary and immediate budget shortfall.
    ●   Both Parties want to work together to meet the financial needs and address the budget shortfall.

The Parties therefore agree to the following:

    1. SEIU has agreed that bargaining unit members will take one (1) furlough day prior to the end of the
       2025-26 school year.

    2. Furlough days are unpaid time and cannot be supplemented by paid time off.

    3. Employees on furlough may not perform any work-related tasks, including checking email.

    4. The designated furlough day is May 1, 2026 and will be reflected on the May paycheck.

    5. Employees can voluntarily take more than the designated one (1) furlough day, with advance
       approval by Human Resources. Voluntary furlough days will be reflected on the paycheck
       associated with the date the furlough day was taken.

    6. Employees who elect to retire at the end of the 2025-26 school year and officially submit their
       retirement declaration by April 3, 2026, will be exempt from taking furlough days. If furlough days
       occur on a PPS-wide closure day, these employees will be expected to work and perform duties as
       assigned by their supervisor. If PPS is open, these employees will be expected to work and
       perform their normal duties.

    7. Human Resources will provide a list of employees who are exempt from furlough days as a result of
       their retirement declaration no later than April 10, 2026.

    8. Employees may file for unemployment insurance, however, PPS does not determine eligibility.
       Unemployment claims are processed through the State of Oregon Employment Department.

    9. Employees will not suffer any loss of health insurance benefits as a result of furlough days.

    10. The Parties acknowledge that this Memorandum of Understanding is subject to PPS Board
        approval.
