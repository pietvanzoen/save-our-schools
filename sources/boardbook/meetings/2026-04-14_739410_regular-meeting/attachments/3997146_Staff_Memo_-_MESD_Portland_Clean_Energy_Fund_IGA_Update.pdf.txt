Introduction
The goal of this memo is to provide an overview of funding awarded by the City of Portland’s
Portland Clean Energy Fund (PCEF), particularly the new grant award from PCEF’s
Collaborating for Climate Action and the Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) from Multnomah
Education Service District (MESD) serving as fiscal agent.

Background

PCEF’s Strategic Program 16- Climate Friendly Public Schools
Portland Public Schools (PPS) was previously awarded $19,930,833 Portland Clean Energy
Fund (PCEF) under their Strategic Program 16: Climate Friendly Public Schools. PPS signed
that Intergovernmental Agreement on April 24, 2024, and the award runs through June 30,
2029. There are two separate components:
    1.​ $3,000,000 for Student-Led Climate Initiatives, Grades 6-12. $600,000/year for five
        years, with $15,000 per school: $10,000 for student-led climate initiative plus $5,000 for
        school/district staffing costs.
    2.​ $16,930,833 for Physical Improvements for projects that reduce greenhouse gas (GHG)
        emissions and improve climate resiliency. An agreement with PAT commits $10,000,000
        of this funding to thermal comfort projects.

PCEF’s Collaborating for Climate Action (CCA)
Multnomah Education Service District (MESD) serves as the fiscal agent for PCEF Collaborating
for Climate Action (CCA) grant award for energy efficiency upgrades and green schoolyard
projects in Portland schools. MESD was awarded $70,377,287 for six school districts plus
Coalition for Communities of Color. PPS was awarded $25,568,240.

MESD signed the Grant Agreement with PCEF. In addition, all partners have signed the
Collaborating for Climate Action Partnership Agreement, a Community Benefits Agreement with
expectations for each school district to sub-contract with the Coalition of Communities of Color
for community engagement with a focus on priority populations in impacted schools.

PCEF defines eligible schools as schools located in Portland with 50% or greater students
qualified for free or reduced meals by direct certification, based on the most recent published
data by Oregon Department of Education. The amount of our award from PCEF is based on the
number of PPS schools eligible for infrastructure upgrades, which is currently thirty-four schools:
Arleta, Beach, Boise-Eliot/Humboldt, César Chávez, Chapman, Clark, Creston, Faubion,
George, Harriet Tubman, Harrison Park, James John, Jefferson, Kellogg, Kelly, Lane, Lee, Lent,
Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., Marysville, Ockley Green, Peninsula, Pioneer, Rigler, Roosevelt,
Rosa Parks, Roseway Heights, Scott, Sitton, Vestal, Whitman, Woodlawn, Woodmere.

PPS identified schools for the proposed energy efficiency upgrades and green schoolyards
based on current school conditions, heat index, community demographics and equity
considerations.

PPS’s grant proposal for the CCA includes the following infrastructure improvements:
  ●​ Upgrade HVAC, Water Heaters, and Windows at Woodmere Elementary.
  ●​ Upgrade Water Heaters and Windows at César Chávez K-8.
  ●​ Green Schoolyard Improvements at: César Chávez, Kelly, Lee, Lent, Sitton, Woodmere.

Timeline for Implementation
The grant period is January 1, 2026 through December 31, 2030.
