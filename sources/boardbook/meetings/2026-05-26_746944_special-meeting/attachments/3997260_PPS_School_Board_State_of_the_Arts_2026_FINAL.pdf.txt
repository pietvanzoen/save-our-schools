Subject:​      PPS 2026 State of the Arts Report



Background

Thirteen years into the City of Portland’s Arts Access Fund (Arts Tax) commitment, the students
in Portland Public Schools’ current graduating class were kindergarteners when arts education
was first restored to their schools. Since that time, PPS has worked not only to preserve access
to arts education across the district but also to build a more coherent and student-centered arts
education system over time.

The development and implementation of the PPS Master Arts Education Plan (MAEP)
established a districtwide strategic framework intended to guide this work. Through the MAEP,
PPS defined key conditions necessary for equitable, comprehensive, high-quality arts education,
including access and time, meaningful learning experiences, student performance and voice,
authentic learning spaces, and instructional materials and resources.

At the same time, the City of Portland has continued developing the Portland Arts Education
Framework in collaboration with the six Arts Access Fund school districts and researchers from
Portland State University. The framework represents an increasing emphasis on consistency,
visibility, and accountability around arts education conditions across schools and districts. The
current year represents a pilot year for portions of this work and includes annual school-level
data collection processes aligned to the City’s framework, alongside development of a future
public-facing dashboard intended to provide greater visibility into arts education conditions over
time.


Strategic Alignment & Implementation
PPS is well-positioned for this work because many of the conditions now being measured
through the City’s evolving framework are already reflected within the district’s existing strategic
planning and implementation efforts. Since the launch of the MAEP, PPS has continued
expanding and refining arts education systems across multiple areas, including K–12 pathway
development, curriculum alignment, district-wide and site-based capstone performance and
exhibition, stakeholder engagement structures, and systems for monitoring implementation
over time.

One example highlighted in the accompanying presentation is the Roosevelt cluster, where
targeted pathway investments beginning in 2016 expanded music programming from two
schools to all schools across the cluster. This cluster-based implementation approach later
informed broader district efforts to strengthen pathway continuity and reduce inequities in arts
access across PPS school communities. Today, all clusters in PPS offer K-12 pathways of study
in both visual and performing arts.

As annual reporting structures and public-facing data systems continue to evolve, PPS will have
increasing opportunities to understand better how students experience arts education across
schools and over time. The current pilot year provides the district an opportunity to internally
review emerging data, identify areas requiring further alignment and support, and continue
refining systems prior to broader public-facing implementation.

PPS has also continued investing in structures intended to support stakeholder engagement
and continuous improvement, including ongoing collaboration with the PPS Arts Education
Advisory Council and the launch of the Student Voice Committee as part of MAEP
implementation efforts.


Connection to Board Goals
This work aligns directly with PPS Board goals related to student engagement, belonging,
well-rounded educational experiences, robust secondary pathways, and college and career
readiness. Recent internal analysis conducted by PPS’s Research, Assessment, and
Accountability department found that students enrolled in visual and performing arts courses
outpaced district averages in both attendance and chronic absenteeism measures between
2023 and 2025, suggesting that sustained participation in arts learning may play an important
role in student connection and engagement with school communities.

Ultimately, this work is about more than access alone. It is about ensuring that students across
every school community experience arts learning environments that support creativity, critical
thinking, cultural literacy, personal voice, belonging, and meaningful participation in school life.
As PPS continues implementing the MAEP alongside the evolving City framework, the district
remains committed to strengthening the conditions that enable all students to engage in
high-quality arts learning experiences over time.
