Portland Public Schools
Community Budget Review Committee
July 9, 2026


Local Option Levy Review
Overview
The Community Budget Review Committee (CBRC) conducted its annual review of Portland
Public Schools’ (PPS) expenditures of Local Option Levy (Levy) funds using data provided to the
CBRC by PPS. Renewed in May 2024 through Measure 26-246, the Levy requires independent
community oversight to ensure that funds are used for their intended purposes.

This review includes actual revenue and expenditures from 2024–25 and projected figures for
2025–26, along with trend data through 2026–27.

The Local Option Levy is a vital funding source for PPS, projected to account for 12.1 percent of
the General Fund’s resources in the 2026–27 school year. As a property tax based on assessed
value, Levy revenue is shaped by real market conditions within the PPS taxing district and
constrained by Measure 5 tax limits, also known as compression. While revenue from the Levy
has generally increased from $97.4 million in 2019–20 to a projected $103.9 million in 2026–27,
the CBRC understands that rising personnel costs have significantly reduced the number of
teachers that can be funded with this revenue.

The CBRC examined how Levy funds support the three voter-approved goals:

    ●​ Maintain class sizes and prevent significant increases across the district.
    ●​ Provide a well-rounded education, including career and technical programs and
       enrichments and electives, and classroom support.
    ●​ Fund approximately 660 teachers and classroom support staff annually.

The Levy is also designed to help support teaching positions and classroom support at every
school in the district. Levy funds intend to play a critical role in PPS’s ability to provide stable
staffing and academic offerings. They seek to support:

    ●​   Smaller class sizes than would be possible through state funding alone.
    ●​   Career and technical education (CTE), STEM, and computer science instruction.
    ●​   Enrichments and electives that contribute to a well-rounded education.
    ●​   Academic supports such as reading specialists and intervention staff.
    ●​   Staffing allocations across every PPS school, maintaining equitable access.

Findings

Based on the data and information provided by PPS, and without independent verification by the
CBRC of the source materials, the CBRC makes the following findings:

First, the CRBC finds that in the prior year 2024-25, and the current year 2025-26, all Levy funds
were and are being spent on teaching positions as approved by voters.

Second, with respect to the use of Levy funds for maintaining teaching positions, as of May
2026, PPS is projecting $104.4 million in revenue from Multnomah, Washington, and Clackamas
Counties for current year 2025-26. Based on PPS’s historical projections, 2025-26 receipts, and
an average teacher cost of $152,000 (inclusive of salary, taxes, and benefits), Levy funds are
expected to support approximately 686 teaching positions for 2025-26. Last year, the Levy
funded 741 teachers.

Third, with the help of Levy funding, PPS has been able to maintain smaller class sizes than
would be the case with state funding alone.

Fourth, effective with the 2013-14 fiscal year, PPS established a procedure adopted for federal
stimulus funds and which was acceptable to the federal government whereby Levy funds are
placed in a “sub-account” within the General Fund and can only be used to pay teachers’
salaries and benefits. This provision was written into Measure 26-207’s Explanatory Statement
and is part of the current Levy language. The CBRC finds that the Levy funds for 2025-26 have
been deposited into this sub-account.

Finally, the Levy language continues to require independent community oversight to ensure
that tax dollars are used as approved by local voters. The CBRC finds that the PPS Board of
Education properly appointed the CBRC as the community oversight body to perform this
function.

Local Option Levy Trends
The CBRC notes that due to rising personnel expenses and decreased revenue, the number of
teachers supported by Levy funding is expected to decline, perhaps by as much as 25% in the
next five years.

Additional Remarks
 The CBRC would like to take this opportunity to thank the voters for approving this vital
funding source for Portland Public Schools, which helps ensure that our students continue to
receive benefits from additional teachers, lower class sizes, and enriched classroom offerings
afforded as a result of the Local Option Levy. The Local Option Levy helps mitigate the state’s
inability to adequately fund K-12 education.
