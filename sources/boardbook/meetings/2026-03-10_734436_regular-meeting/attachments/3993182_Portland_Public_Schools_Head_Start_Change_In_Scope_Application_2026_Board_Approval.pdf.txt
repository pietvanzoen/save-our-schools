                         PPS Head Start Change in Scope Executive Summary

●​ What is changing: Portland Public Schools Head Start plans to reduce the number of federally funded Head Start
   slots by 90, from 297 to 207, beginning in September 2026 for the 2026–27 school year.

    The program proposes 90 slots through the following calculation:

        ○​ 65 slots currently filled by over income families;

        ○​ 20 unfilled slots;




    T
        ○​ 5+ for waitlist variance.

●​ Why this change is needed: The adjustment reflects higher staffing costs, changes in community need, and the
   amount of funding available through the federal Head Start grant.

●​ Community trends: Fewer families now qualify for Head Start based on income, and more publicly funded early
  AF
   learning options are available across the community. Population shifts, including lower birth rates and rising
   housing costs, have also reduced demand.

●​ Enrollment efforts: PPS Head Start has successfully met enrollment requirements; however, waitlists have
   remained small and, at times in recent years, insufficient to remain fully enrolled every month.

●​ Financial sustainability: Rising personnel costs with the current level of funding cannot sustainably support the
   existing number of slots. A Change in Scope is a means to increase program funding allocations per slot (revenue
   increase).

●​ How this change helps: Reducing the number of slots allows the program to better match funding with actual
 R
   enrollment, operating costs and personnel costs to ensure long-term program stability.

●​ Impact on families: No currently enrolled children or families will be affected. The change will take place for
   the 2026–27 school year.

●​ Program quality: Classroom models, schedules, and services will remain the same. PPS Head Start will continue
D

   providing high-quality, comprehensive services to children and families.

●​ Looking ahead: This change supports staff stability and ensures PPS Head Start can continue serving families
   effectively throughout the remainder of the grant period.

Cost Allocation Methodology
    ●​ Classroom personnel and classroom-specific costs are allocated based on proportional allocation, reflecting the
       direct benefit provided to each program. The OPK funding requirements are patterned after the federal
                                                                                                                          8
       allowable costs, so expenses are split between the two funding sources based on the percentage of funding from
       each source. Our direct classroom and site support staff positions are allocated based on total slot enrollment at
       each site. For example, our braided Federal Head Start and State OPK classrooms are predominantly 14/5 (75% &
       25% split).

    ●​ All other expenses are split between the federal and non-federal match (OPK) funds based on the percentages
       listed above. Federal Head Start and OPK funds are tracked under separate grant numbers in the PPS financial
       system, and journal entries are made as needed to ensure that spending follows the individual budgets.

                                  Summary of Change In Scope Proposal


Portland Public Schools (PPS) Head Start requests approval for a Change in Scope to reduce federally funded Head
Start enrollment by 90 slots, from 297 to 207, effective September 2026 at the start of the 2026–27 program year.
This adjustment is necessary to align funded enrollment with demonstrated community demand, rising personnel
costs driven by collective bargaining agreements, and the fiscal capacity of the current federal award.

Despite sustained implementation of a Full Enrollment Initiative, PPS Head Start has faced persistent challenges




    T
meeting the 97 percent enrollment requirement. Community Needs Assessment data show a declining pool of
income-eligible families, increasing saturation of publicly funded early learning options in the service area, and
population shifts resulting from declining birth rates and rising housing costs. While enrollment has improved over
time, waitlists increasingly consist of families who are over income or whose preferences cannot be met, limiting the
  AF
program’s ability to sustain full enrollment under the current slot configuration.

Personnel costs now exceed the funding capacity of the existing federal award. Without a reduction in slots or
revised cost allocation, the program is projected to remain structurally underfunded in FY27. The proposed Change
in Scope increases per-slot federal funding by distributing the existing award across fewer children, improving
alignment between expenditures and available federal resources while maintaining compliance with wage
comparability and cost allocation requirements.
 R
Implementation of the Change in Scope will involve the closure of five chronically under-enrolled classrooms. No
currently enrolled families will be displaced, as changes will take effect prior to the opening of enrollment for the
2026–27 program year. Service delivery models, schedules, and administrative structures will remain unchanged.
This request supports fiscal sustainability, staff retention, and continued delivery of high-quality comprehensive
services through the remainder of the grant period.
