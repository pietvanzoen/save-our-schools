             Report to The Portland Public Schools Board of Directors

From: Bond Accountability Committee

Subject: BAC Report #50

Committee Charge

The Portland Public Schools Board of Education (Board) has appointed a citizen Bond
Accountability Committee (“Committee”) whose charge is to monitor the planning and
progress of the bond programs relative to voter-approved work scope, schedule, and
budget objectives.

The duties of the Committee are (i) to receive reports on the status and planned
implementation of the bond program; (ii) to determine whether such status and
implementation are consistent with the program approved by the voters; and (iii) to
report their findings to the Board.

Background:

In November 2012, voters approved a $482M capital improvement bond measure for
Portland Public Schools. In May 2017, voters approved a $790M capital improvement
bond measure. In November 2020, voters approved a $1.206B capital improvement
bond measure and in May 2025, voters approved a $1.8B capital improvement bond
measure.

All regular BAC meetings are publicly noticed and open to the public. Staff presentation
materials, along with BAC reports are regularly posted on the District website. Meetings
are also video recorded, which allows BAC members unable to attend scheduled
meetings to watch the proceedings and review the materials at their convenience.
The BAC meeting was held on January 28, 2026, at Jefferson High School. The BAC
has a practice of alternating its meetings between in person and Zoom. In person
meetings have rotated among the various schools that have seen modernization or will
see modernization. Six members participated in the meeting, Greg DiLoreto, Travis
George, Jonathan Truit, Norm Dowty, Ryan Kinsella, Chris Dunning and our BOE
liaison Director Splitt. The meeting focused on three main areas: overall financial
position; update on school modernization efforts at Jefferson High School,
Cleveland High School and Ida B. Wells High School; and a presentation on
possible changes to the BAC Charter.

The BAC received public comment from four individuals. One concerned the
renaming of the Center for Black Student Excellence; two comments concerning
the need for the BAC to be involved in the decision of where to spend funds for
seismic improvement and one concerning the recent restructuring in the Office of
School Modernization and the effect on the workload since several people were
let go from the program.

Report
1. Whether the school district bond revenues are expended only for the purposes for
which the bonds were approved, and that bond revenues are not used for any purpose
prohibited by law.

   ● The BAC reviewed the Bond Program Administration report and additional
     reports specific to bond revenues and expenditures. A bond sale is planned for
     February 10th, with debt service not to exceed ballot authorization. Of note is
     that Moody’s bond rating agency has downgraded the District’s credit
     rating from Aa2 to Aa3. The District maintained its AA- rating from
     Standard & Poor’s. The downgrading from Moody’s reflects the Districts
     low operational fund balance at only 5% of total balances, which compares
     to over 14% in 2022 and 2023. Also influencing the bond rating is the local
     economy and student enrollment, which is decreasing. This downgrade
     could have some effect, perhaps small, on the interest rate the District will
     pay for its cost of borrowing.
   ● Staff has nearly completed reconciling accounts and based on these reports
     bond funds from the 2012, 2017 and 2020 bond program accurately funded all
     items identified within those programs. Based on these reports the BAC
     believes the programs to have been properly funded; it appears that the
     school district is spending revenues only for the purposes for which the
     bonds were approved. For the 2025 bond measure, almost no funds to
     date have been expended.
   2. Whether the bond budget is sufficient to complete the scope of work as outlined in
   the voter-approved bonds.

       •   As presented to the BAC there are sufficient funds to complete the
           scope of work as outlined in the voter approved bonds.
       •   A total of $4.6 billion in bonds have been approved by the voters in
           2012, 2017, 2020 and 2025 combined. To date $2.0 billion have been
           spent, outstanding encumbrances are $167.7 million and $2.4 billion in
           bond funds remain, the majority of which is in the 2025 bond. All that
           remains from the 2012 bond issue is $403,500 out of the $547.8 million.
           Nearly $60 million remain from the 2017 bond measure out of the total
       •   $888.5 million, and $551.7 million remain from the $1.3 billion in the 2020
           bond. As noted previously almost no funds have been spent from the 2025
           bond.
       •   Cleveland high school is currently budgeted at $449 million, up from the
           original budget of $442 million. However, the value engineering process is
           underway to reduce costs below the budget. Jefferson high school, funded
           from both the 2020 bond and 2025 bond measure is currently budgeted at
           $466 million and Wells High School is currently budgeted at $432 million and
           at the 50% design drawings was on track with the current budget. The BAC
           did not learn of anything in the staff report that suggests these budgets are
           not sufficient. A risk to these budgets could come from permit delays,
           which would impact start of construction, however the City of Portland
           has committed to a 16-week review/approval process. An additional risk
           is the availability of mass timber. To mitigate this risk, supply has been
           diversified since all projects are on the same timelines so the District is
           not completing with itself for production.

3. Whether the projects planned, in progress, and completed will meet the scope of
work specified in the voter-approved bonds.

   •   The projects discussed at the BAC meeting will meet the scope of the work
       specified in the voter-approved bonds. Cleveland, Jefferson and Wells will
       be completely new high schools meeting the District’s education design
       standard.
4. Whether the projects are being delivered on schedule relative to the voter-approved
bonds.

   •   Cleveland and Wells high schools are shown to being delivered on
       schedule relative to the voter-approved bonds. Jefferson is behind the
       original schedule shown in the 2020 bonds requiring additional funds in the
       2025 bond measure. Jefferson is now shown to be tracking correctly with
       the revised schedule.
   •   Cleveland high school is at 70% design plans. Construction activities will start in
       late June 2026. The project is currently under design and securing permit
       approval from the City of Portland. The schedule shows move into the new
       building prior to the start of school 2029.
   •   Jefferson high school is also at 70% design plans. Groundbreaking is tentatively
       scheduled for May 29, 2026. As with Cleveland high school construction will
       begin this summer, first with the south fields construction. New building
       construction begins in 2027. Move in will be prior to the start of school 2029.
       Currently the project is in the design, land use and permitting phase.
   •   Wells high school is slightly behind Cleveland and Jefferson high schools.
       Construction document phase will begin in March with construction slated to
       begin in December 2026. However, move in is scheduled prior to the start of
       school 2029.
   •   Center for Educational Excellence, renamed from the Center for Black Student
       Excellence to reflect its larger mission is moving forward with the purchase of the
       property at 1 North. Staff is working with PPS procurement in solicitation of 3rd
       party services such as geotechnical civil and structural engineering. A complete
       schedule was not provided to the BAC.

5. The Committee will receive and review copies of annual performance audit reports
and financial audit reports to ensure Bond revenues are expended in compliance with
state law and the ballot measure language.

   ● District staff is diligent in ensuring all bond revenues be in compliance with
     state law and the ballot measure language and the BAC has seen no
     evidence of operating outside of this compliance.

General Comments

In conclusion, we commend PPS with what they have accomplished as the number of
projects is numerous and complex. Projects are generally coming in on time and within
budget and scope. Procedeo, the newly retained project management firm, has revised
their reports to the BAC to reflect our comments from the previous meeting and these
reports meet the requirements the BAC needs to report to the BOE. We continue to
appreciate the quality and professionalism of OSM staff, particularly as they work
through the change in leadership. We thank the Board for this opportunity to serve and
play a small part in your bond programs.
