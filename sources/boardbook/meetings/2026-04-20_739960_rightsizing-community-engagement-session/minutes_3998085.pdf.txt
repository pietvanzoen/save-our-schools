                   Community Engagement Session: School Rightsizing - Virtual

In accordance with ORS 192.650, the District’s official School Board Meeting Minutes are maintained via
video recording and may be viewed at: https://www.youtube.com/@ppsboardofeducation

Pursuant to notice made by posting to the Board’s public notices webpage and emailed to persons on the
mailing list, a Portland Public Schools Board of Education Community Engagement Session was held
virtually and streamed live at: https://www.youtube.com/@ppsboardofeducation/live

Attendance

Board Members Present: Christy Splitt, Michelle DePass, Patte Sullivan, Rashelle Chase-Miller, Virginia
La Forte, Stephanie Engelsman, Eddie Wang, Student Representative Ian Ritorto

Staff: Kimberlee Armstrong - Superintendent; Jon Franco - Senior Chief of Operations; Margaret Calvert -
Assistant Superintendent; Nichole Watson - Senior Director of Family and Community Engagement;
Sharon Toncray - Chief Legal Counsel; Kristina Howard - Senior Chief of Academics.


Call to Order and Introductions

The meeting was called to order at 6:06 pm.

Welcome + Grounding + Framing​
Superintendent Armstrong opened the meeting. Board Members, including the Student Representative,
introduced themselves and shared what they hoped to learn. Context was provided for the need to
consolidate schools.

Interactive Polling​
Participants were asked to provide information about who they were and what their experiences may be
with past school changes.

Small + Whole Group Discussion​
Assistant Superintendent Margaret Calvert explained the factors driving decisions — enrollment trends,
facility utilization, boundaries, program placement, grade configurations, and possible consolidations —
emphasizing multiple factors would be considered together, not just enrollment alone.

Neighborhood Gallery Walk​
Participants were provided an opportunity to engage with a shared interactive map showing school
locations, capacity, enrollment, and utilization rates.

Ranking the Factors​
Participants were invited to identify their top 2–3 priorities from the six factors named in the Student
Assignment to Neighborhood Schools Policy.
