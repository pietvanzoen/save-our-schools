Metropolitan Learning Center Programmatic Restructuring

Time: 6:10 pm

Superintendent Armstrong opened by providing some comments about the decision to no longer offer a
high school option at the Metropolitan Learning Center. She acknowledged the feedback that has been
received on engagement and decision making. She also shared that the legal team has confirmed that
the District is operating within Board Policy.

Kristina Howard, Renard Adams, and Isaac Cardona provided a presentation on the restructuring of
Metropolitan Learning Center (MLC) to discontinue grades 9-11. Conversations started with principals
when looking at providing quality and stable instruction with equity of access for next year and beyond.
They shared the enrollment process and enrollment trend at MLC., as well as enrollment demographics,
program staffing comparisons, projected savings by the change, and what alternative options are
available to students.

Student Representative Ritorto expressed empathy and encouraged people to turn to the State
Legislature. Director Sullivan advocated for the state school stability fund to be released and expressed
sadness as a former teacher who taught for 20 years at MLC.

Director Chase-Miller stated she understands the need for an alternative school in the District and asked
questions about other options for students that benefit from an environment like MLC, for example
students who are neuro-divergent. There were questions about engagement, alternative options, process,
and the timeline that led up to the decision to no longer offer high school grades. Director La Forte
expressed her opinion that she thinks the Policy was not followed and requested a pause and that staff
bring forward a fiscal impact statement. Chair Wang acknowledged the decision is hard and that ignoring
the issue creates downstream effects.

Graduation data for SY 2024-2026; Post Secondary Readiness Indicators

Time: 8:03 pm

Kristina Howard, Renard Adams, and Isaac Cardona provided a presentation on Graduation and
Completion rates. There was an overview of the graduation data showing there has been a two year trend
of lower graduation rates. Staff acknowledged that racial disparities, language barriers, and needing
special education services predict lower graduation rates. The District has initiated “Project Graduation”
as a short term focus on support for high schools by engaging with principals to understand what targeted
support is needed. Staff also walked through the continuum for postsecondary readiness, from transition
into high school to advanced credit options.

Board members had questions about the data presented and wondered if there was data that correlated
participation in Career Technical Education and graduation rates. There were also questions about the
correlation between English Language Learners participating in Dual Language Immersion programs and
graduation rates.

Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) Update

Time: 8:47 pm

Russel Adamson provided an update on the Enterprise Resource Planning project which is fully bond
funded. This will replace a 25 year old system and will impact all systems across the organization, with
the exception of the student information system. He shared a timeline for selection of a vendor. There
were questions about the location and makeup of some of the vendors who responded to the request for
proposal.

Finance Focus

Time: 8:58 pm

Michelle Morrison shared an update about the current year budget assumption and variances since
adoption, including several emergency declarations pertaining to the health and safety at schools that
needed to be addressed this year. She shared that the District no longer has the flexibility in its budget to
absorb these things as they come up. She provided an overview of mitigation plans in place including
spending controls.

Dr. Armstrong shared there is no more fund balance that we can pull to address real needs in schools and
that she just learned of this shortfall. This financial hardship is real and any action taken or not taken will
have impacts next year.

There were questions about furlough days and what savings it would provide and whether unfunded
mandates have contributed to this shortfall. Superintendent Armstrong responded that Oregon Family
Leave Act is an example of a requirement that has a big impact on the budget and if furloughs don’t
happen, then there will be mid-year layoffs.

Other Business and Future Agenda Items

Time: 9:39 pm

None at this time. Director Splitt thanked the Board and staff for being willing to hear from the
Metropolitan Learning Center community.

Adjourn

The meeting was adjourned at 9:42 pm by Board Chair Wang.

                                     RESOLUTION No. 7254

                     Students Requesting Exemption from PE State Requirement

                                              RECITALS

A. In accordance with OAR 581-022-1910, Portland Public Schools may excuse students from a
   state required program or learning activity, where necessary, to accommodate students’
   disabilities or religious beliefs:

B. Approval of the exemption shall be based upon and shall include:

    1) A written request from the student’s parent or guardian or the student, if that student is 18
       years of age or older or a legally emancipated minor, listing the reasons for the request and a
       proposed alternative for an individualized learning activity which substitutes for the period of
       time exempt from the program and meets the goals of the learning activity or course being
       exempt;

    2) An evaluation of the request and approval by appropriate school personnel (the alternative
       should be consistent with the student’s educational progress and career goals as described in
       OARs 581-022-1670 and 581-022-1510).

C. Following approval by Portland Public Schools Board of Education, and upon completion of the
   alternative, credit shall be granted to the student.

                                          RESOLUTION

1. Be it resolved that the Board of Education approves six Portland Public Schools high school
   students to be granted exemption from the state PE requirement.

2. In accordance with OAR 581-022-1910, all students have a physician statement that documents
   their specific physical limitation as it relates to this requirement, as well as a written state of
   agreement from their parent/guardian. All students will replace the PE credit requirement with
   alternative coursework as required .

                                         Resolution No. 7256

     Resolution to Appoint Student Representatives to Portland Public Schools Board of Education
                                            Committees

                                              RECITALS

A. The Portland Public Schools Board of Education Policy 1.20.014-P Board Committees authorizes
   Board committees to appoint, subject to Board approval, advisory members from the student body or
   community in order to obtain internal and external stakeholder input; and to determine the length of
   term for such advisory members.

B. Policy 1.20.014-P also states that advisory members may not be counted toward quorum
   requirements and may not vote on recommendations made to the Board.

C. Additionally, 1.20.012-P Student Representative and District Student Council Policy states that the
   Student Representative shall present opportunities to members of the District Student Council (DSC)
   to advise Board and District committees and task forces on issues of significance to the DSC and to
   Portland Public Schools students.

D. The Portland Public Schools Board of Education values student voice and seeks to strengthen
   student voice and engagement in the work of Board committees by appointing Student
   Representative members from the District Student Council.

                                             RESOLVED

The Portland Public Schools Board of Education hereby appoints Eve Thompson and Mel Schuller to
serve as student advisory members on the Teaching, Learning, and Enrollment Committee, and Nora
Isles and Eesa Taylor to serve as student advisory members on the Policy Committee, through June 30,
2026.
