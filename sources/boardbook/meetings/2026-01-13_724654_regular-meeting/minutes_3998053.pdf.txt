Submitted by Director Stephanie Engelsman:

I voted no on Resolution 7229 as I would have preferred an alternative proposal that strengthened all of
our high schools while also providing Jefferson with the enrollment to function and thrive as a
comprehensive high school. I remain concerned about unanswered questions regarding Roosevelt and
how these changes will affect that traditionally underserved school and its students. Roosevelt had very
low enrollment prior to its modernization and since that time, has seen enrollment growth that has
enabled it to offer a full array of courses as well as extracurricular activities that are available at other
schools. Reducing enrollment at Roosevelt might be a significant step back for that school. Lastly, I am
worried about Grant's ability to continue to offer a diverse range of courses and extracurriculars. The
forecasting for future attendees in the scenarios is higher than actual attendance today. For these
reasons, I voted no on Resolution 7229.

                                        RESOLUTION No. 7228

                Resolution to End Jefferson Dual Assignment and Designate Jefferson
                                  as a Comprehensive High School

                                              RECITALS

A. In December 2022, The PPS Board of Education adopted Resolution 6627, which stated that “By the
   start of the 2026-27 school year, the Superintendent will initiate a process to develop a plan to
   increase student enrollment at Jefferson High School.”

B. Jefferson enrollment has been impacted for decades by transfer options, including voluntary busing
   to other schools in the 1980s and 1990s and No Child Left Behind in the 2000s. The largest driver
   of enrollment at Jefferson High School for the past fifteen years has been the dual assignment
   choice, endorsed by the PPS Board of Directors in resolutions 4236 in September 2010, 4358 in
   October 2010 and 4394 in January 2011. The assignment plan is unique to the Jefferson region and
   allows high school students a choice between Jefferson and either Grant, McDaniel or Roosevelt
   depending on their home address.

C. Resolution 4358 also designated Jefferson High School as a Focus High School. The initial plan
   allowed students within the historical Jefferson boundary to maintain guaranteed assignment at
   Jefferson while also providing dual assignment to one of the neighboring comprehensive high
   schools.

D. Since the implementation of dual assignment Jefferson enrollment has ranged from 391-670
   students. Over that same period, the comprehensive high schools that receive dual assignment
   students have seen significant enrollment growth, spurred, in part, by building modernizations
   completed at Roosevelt (2017), Grant (2019) and McDaniel (2021). Dual assignment has
   contributed to enrollment instability and decline at Jefferson, impacting program opportunities for
   Jefferson students.

E. In 2020 and 2025 Portland voters approved funding for full modernization of Jefferson High School.
   Groundbreaking is scheduled for May 2026, with the new building slated for completion in Fall 2029.

F. In Fall 2025, community members were invited to provide feedback on options for sunsetting Dual
   Assignment and returning to a standard school boundary assignment system. Feedback was
   received from more than 400 stakeholders.

G. To stabilize enrollment and bring equitable, comprehensive, comparable programming to the
   revitalized campus, the Jefferson Dual Assignment choice will end with the 2026-27 school year.
   Beginning in the 2027-28 school year, students will be assigned to Jefferson and surrounding
   schools through standard neighborhood boundaries, and Jefferson will operate as a comprehensive
   high school.

H. The Superintendent has developed a plan to convert dual assignment zones to standard school
   boundaries in time for implementation in the 2027-28 school year [see Resolution 7229].

                                             RESOLUTION

1. The Board of Education adopts the Superintendent’s recommendation to end dual assignment and
   designate Jefferson as a comprehensive high school beginning in the 2027-28 school year.
                                          RESOLUTION No. 7229

                Resolution to Designate Attendance Boundaries for Jefferson High School

                                                 RECITALS

A.​   In December 2022, The PPS Board of Education adopted resolution 6627, which stated that “By the
      start of the 2026-27 school year, the Superintendent will initiate a process to develop a plan to
      increase student enrollment at Jefferson High School.”

B.​   The largest driver of enrollment at Jefferson High School for the past fifteen years has been the dual
      assignment choice, endorsed by the PPS Board of Directors in resolutions 4358 in October 2010
      and 4394 in January 2011. The assignment plan is unique to the Jefferson region and allows high
      school students a choice between Jefferson and either Grant, McDaniel or Roosevelt depending on
      their home address.

C.​ The initial dual assignment area included Beach, Boise-Eliot/Humboldt, Chief Joseph, Dr. Martin
    Luther King Jr, Faubion, Vernon and Woodlawn elementary attendance areas. Peninsula was added
    in 2016 as part of the Ockley Green Middle School feeder pattern and Irvington and Sabin were
    added in 2018 as part of the Harriet Tubman Middle School feeder pattern.

D.​ In 2020 and 2025, Portland voters approved funding for full modernization of Jefferson High School.
    Groundbreaking is scheduled for May 2026, with the new building slated for completion in Fall 2029.

E.​   In January 2026, the Board of Education adopted the Superintendent’s recommendation to end dual
      assignment and designate Jefferson as a comprehensive high school beginning in the 2027-28
      school year [see Resolution 7228].

F.​   To stabilize enrollment and bring equitable, comprehensive programming to Jefferson and
      neighboring high schools, PPS staff developed five options for standard high school boundaries.

         a.​ Boundary scenarios were developed and analyzed utilizing the factors that contribute to
             optimal school boundaries described in PPS 4.10.045-P and PPS 4.10.049-AD, along with
             the PPS racial equity lens.

         b.​ Six community engagement opportunities were held in October-November 2025 to gather
             input on the proposals, drawing feedback from more than 400 community members.

G.​ Based on the engagement work with the community and the analysis from PPS staff, the
    Superintendent recommends that, beginning in 2027, the following high school boundaries, based
    on elementary school attendance areas:

         a.​ High school students who reside in the Beach, Boise-Eliot/Humboldt, Chief Joseph,Dr. Martin
             Luther King Jr., Faubion, Sabin, Vernon and Woodlawn attendance areas will be assigned to
             Jefferson High School.

         b.​ High school students who reside in the Irvington attendance area will be assigned to Grant
             High School.

         c.​ High school students who reside in the Peninsula attendance area will be assigned to
             Roosevelt High School.

H.​ If adopted, the boundary changes will begin with new students, as described in PPS policy
    4.10.045-P(V). Additional student assignment procedures would continue without change:

         a.​ The Beach Spanish Immersion program will continue to feed to Roosevelt High School.

         b.​ The Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Chinese Immersion program will continue to feed to Jefferson
             High School.

         c.​ Students who live in other areas will continue to have access to Jefferson High School
             through the annual transfer process, including Right to Return transfers for students who live
             in other districts.
I.​   Effective implementation of this transition would include the following:

         a.​ Continued engagement with students, families, community partners and school staff at high
             schools and feeder schools to build knowledge and encourage participation in shaping
             courses, course progressions, electives, sports, clubs and other features at Jefferson as the
             school transitions to a comprehensive high school.

         b.​ Engage current community, academic, and industry partners to help design and support
             implementation of academic pathways.

         c.​ A commitment to fund up to two additional full-time positions at Jefferson each year through
             2030-31 to support program growth.

         d.​ Annual progress updates to the Board for Jefferson, Grant, McDaniel, and Roosevelt High
             School including, at a minimum, a report on changes in staffing levels, descriptions of staff
             recruiting efforts, how stakeholders were involved in identifying and designing course
             offerings, impacts on athletics, Visual and Performing Arts, extracurricular activities, and other
             pertinent information. Prior to presenting this information to the Board, families in the
             affected schools will be notified that this information will be discussed at a board meeting, and
             this report will be posted on the Board webpage in accordance with its Board protocols.

         e.​ Throughout this transition, District staff will be monitoring enrollment levels of all impacted
             high schools.

                                                RESOLUTION

1.​   The Board of Education adopts the Superintendent’s recommendation to implement the following
      high school assignments, based on elementary attendance areas:

         a.​ High school students who reside in the Beach, Boise-Eliot/Humboldt, Chief Joseph, Dr. Martin
             Luther King Jr., Faubion, Sabin, Vernon and Woodlawn attendance areas will be assigned to
             Jefferson High School.

         b.​ High school students who reside in the Irvington attendance area will be assigned to Grant
             High School.

         c.​ High school students who reside in the Peninsula attendance area will be assigned to
             Roosevelt High School.

2.​   All changes will be implemented at the start of the 2027-28 school year.

         a.​ Boundary changes will begin with new students, as described in PPS policy 4.10.045-P(V).

3.​   The Board directs the Superintendent to initiate staffing and program changes and other operational
      adjustments to support the effective implementation of this resolution, and to report the impacts to
      the Board at least annually through 2030-31.

                     Amendment of Community Budget Review Committee Charter

                                               RECITALS

A.   The mission of the Community Budget Review Committee (CBRC) is to review, evaluate, and make
     recommendations to the Board of Education (Board) regarding the Superintendent’s Proposed
     Budget and other budgetary issues identified by the CBRC or the Board. The CBRC receives its
     charge from the Board.

B.   In May 2024 through Measure 26-246, the voters of the Portland Public School (PPS) District
     passed a renewal Local Option Levy, which mandated independent community oversight to ensure
     tax dollars are used for purposes approved by local voters, and the CBRC serves that function for
     PPS.

C.   The CBRC is composed of eight to twelve volunteer members. The Board appoints adult members
     to three-year terms with student members appointed to one-year terms.

D.   The CBRC Charter was last updated in 2019 and outlined the mission, structure, charge, and
     protocol for selecting new members.

E.   A proposal to expand committee membership to up to thirteen members was developed.

F.   A proposal to include additional student members was developed.

G.   The charter revision adjusts the language relevant to student members to allow for additional student
     members and increases the adult membership to up to thirteen. There are no further revisions to the
     charter.

                                             RESOLUTION

1.   The Board amends the CBRC charter to allow for up to thirteen adult members and additional
     student representatives.

                                          RESOLUTION NO. 7237

                       Resolution to Name the Buildings at the One North Property

                                                 RECITALS

A.   The Portland Public Schools (PPS) Board of Education recognizes that the names of school and
     district buildings carry profound meaning and play an important role in fostering student belonging,
     identity, pride, and a sense of possibility.

B.   On December 2, 2025, the PPS Board of Education approved the purchase of the One North
     property, a dual-building campus located in Portland’s historic Albina neighborhood, advancing a
     long-standing, community-driven vision.

C.   The acquisition of the One North property represents a historic investment by PPS in honoring
     student brilliance, repairing past harm, and creating a space rooted in joy, identity, creativity, and
     academic excellence, shaped by years of advocacy from students, families, elders, and community
     partners.

D.   The One North campus consists of two distinct but complementary buildings, each designed to serve
     a unique purpose in advancing the district’s “Birth to Brilliance” framework.

E.   The West Building is envisioned as a center for academic excellence, imagination, and innovation—
     serving as a creative studio where students engage in learning through technology, design,
     storytelling, STEM, and entrepreneurship.

F.   The East Building is envisioned as a welcoming, community-centered gathering space—serving as a
     place where students, families, elders, educators, and partners come together for cultural
     programming, services, and collaborative learning grounded in belonging.

G.   Dr. Harriet Adair devoted 47 years of service to Portland Public Schools as a classroom teacher,
     principal, and district leader, including service as an Assistant Superintendent, and is widely
     recognized for her unwavering commitment to scholarship, leadership, equity, and the belief in the
     boundless potential of every student.

H.   Naming the West Building the Dr. Harriet Adair Center honors her legacy of academic excellence,
     visionary leadership, and lifelong dedication to cultivating brilliance, curiosity, and opportunity for
     generations of PPS students.

I.   Michael “Chappie” Grice dedicated decades of service to Portland Public Schools, serving as a
     trusted leader, mentor, and administrator whose work strengthened schools, supported educators,
     and uplifted students and families across the district.

J.   Naming the East Building the Michael “Chappie” Grice Center honors his enduring legacy of
     compassion, integrity, mentorship, and community-centered leadership, and reflects the building’s
     role as the living room for student-centered excellence.

K.   PPS has shared the proposed names in various public forums and has received considerable
     feedback from members of the community in support of these names.

                                                  RESOLVED

1.   The Portland Public Schools Board of Education hereby names the West Building located on the
     One North property the Dr. Harriet Adair Center (West).
2.   The Portland Public Schools Board of Education hereby names the East Building located on the One
     North property the Michael “Chappie” Grice Center (East).

3.   The Board finds that the process to date related to these names has met the intent of Policy
     2.20.010-P, and to the extent any formalities have not been met, the Board hereby waives 2.20.010-
     P’s application to this naming decision.

4.   The Portland Public Schools Board of Education expresses its gratitude to the community members,
     students, families, elders, and staff whose advocacy, vision, and leadership made this historic
     milestone possible.
