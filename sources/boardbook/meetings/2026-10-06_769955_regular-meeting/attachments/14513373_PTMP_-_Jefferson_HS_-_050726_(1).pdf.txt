​3.​ ​Project Overview​

   ​a.​ ​Overview of the bond language re: project​

        I​ n the July 28, 2020 referral to voters approved by PPS Board of Education, proposed health and​
   ​safety investments include “2020 Modernizations - transforming the historic Jefferson… into [a]​
    ​modernized 21st century [center] of learning.”​

   ​b.​ ​Description of the project​

            ​The current Comprehensive Plan includes a compact, four-story building accommodating 1,700​
   ​ tudents, on the existing track and football field at the North side of the site. In the first phase of​
   s
   ​construction, students will remain on site in the existing school buildings during the construction of​
    ​the new school. Two new multipurpose baseball and softball fields located to the south of the existing​
     ​school will be permitted and constructed during the first phase to avoid the complete loss of athletic​
      ​fields during construction.​

         ​Upon completion of the new school, students will occupy the new facility and the old school​
   ​ uildings will be demolished. Following demolition of the old school, a second phase of construction​
   b
   ​will complete the site work for the modernization project. This work will be scheduled for completion​
    ​before demolition of the existing track and field begins.​

​4.​ ​Project Delivery Model​

         ​The Construction Manager / General Contractor (“CM/GC”) method was determined to be the​
   ​ ost appropriate for this project. In the Fall of 2022, the Board of Education granted an exemption​
   m
   ​from the traditional Design-Bid-Build low-bid procurement process and approved the use of an​
    ​Alternative Contracting Method for the construction of Jefferson High School Modernization.​

   ​ M/GC is the commonly used alternative contracting method by local governments for large,​
   C
   ​complex projects such as major modernizations involving existing buildings and sites like the​
    ​Jefferson High School Modernization Project.​

   ​Under the CM/GC contracting method:​

   ​●​ T   ​ he contractor is solicited early in the design phase pursuant to a competitive Request for​
           ​Proposals (RFP) process wherein selection is based upon evaluation of factors relating to the​
            ​experience, expertise, and project approach of the contractor rather than lowest bid.​
    ​●​ ​The contractor works with the owner and architect to develop the final design with the goals of​
             ​improved constructability and value engineering, which results in fewer change orders and the​
              ​ability to expedite the construction schedule. Under the standard design/bid/build (low​
               ​bid)method, the design is completed before the project is bid and the contractor brought on​
                ​board.​
     ​●​ ​During the design phase, the owner and contractor negotiate and agree on a guaranteed​
                 ​maximum price ("GMP") and the construction schedule for the construction phase of the project.​
      ​●​ ​Unlike traditional design-bid-build procurement, the CM/GC project delivery method allows for​
                  ​construction to begin before design is complete (via Early Work Amendments), reducing the​
                   ​overall project schedule.​

​5.​ ​Schedule​

         ​Building design based on the revised comprehensive plan began in May, 2025. The design will​
   ​ eed to navigate a multi-step land use approval process, and multiple building permits; construction​
   n
   ​is planned to begin in the summer of 2026.​

        ​Students and staff will remain on site during construction, and will be kept separate and safe from​
   ​the impact of that construction.​

           ​An Early Field Phase of construction is planned to start in the summer of 2026, with Site​
   ​ reparation to begin in the Fall of 2026 and Building Construction to begin in the Winter of 2026/27.​
   P
   ​Construction is planned to be complete by the summer of 2029, with move-in of students & staff into​
    ​the new building in the Fall of 2029. Abatement & Demolition of the existing building will start in the​
     ​Fall of 2029, followed by Site Construction of the track & field which will be completed by the fall of​
      ​2030.​

​8.​ ​Governance/Reporting​

            ​Project decisions that are within the authority of the project team are made at the discretion of​
   t​ he lead project manager in consultation with members of the project team, other RISE members,​
    ​Facilities Management, school personnel, and third party design consultants as needed. For decisions​
     ​beyond the authority of the project team, decisions are forwarded to the appropriate party with any​
      ​background information needed to assist in making the decision. Project decisions and actions will be​
       ​reported out to the appropriate parties as discussed in the project communication plan, through​
        ​meetings, emails, or e-Builder correspondence.​

​10.​​Certified Business Recruitment Plan​

             ​PPS Purchasing and Contracting has an aspirational goal of 18% or more of project contracts by​
   ​ ost be awarded to certified firms. To achieve or surpass this goal on the Jefferson High School​
   c
   ​Modernization project, the project team is utilizing multiple strategies to attract, contract, and​
    ​execute work with certified firms. For the RFP for architectural services the project team included​
     ​categories to both proposal and interview panels requesting information and providing scoring on​
      ​potential firms certified business strategies and internal workforce diversity. On the CM/GC RFP the​
       ​project team included evaluation criteria in the RFP to help ensure the selection of a prime contractor​
        ​with a strong commitment and demonstrated success in using Certified minority firms as well as a​
         ​substantive plan of outreach to, partnership with, and inclusion of Certified subcontractors.​

          ​On smaller procurements for goods, services, and other professional services, the project team is​
   ​ oliciting proposals from multiple companies, especially recruiting from certified firms whenever​
   s
   ​possible. The project team is also attending certified business trade gatherings such as those put on​
    ​by OAME wherever possible to recruit potential certified firms who have not previously worked with​
     ​the District.​

​11.​​Challenges/Opportunities​

       ​The challenges of the project include a tight, phased design & construction schedule, a​
   ​constrained site, and occupancy of an active construction site by students & teachers.​

         ​Each of these challenges offer opportunities, including careful coordination among the​
   ​ wner-architect-contractor team & stakeholders in order to meet the ultimate completion dates,​
   o
   ​careful planning from a facility and programmatic standpoint in order to minimize impact on students​
    ​& teachers, and a strategic approach to pre-construction planning & investigations.​
