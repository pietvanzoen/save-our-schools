Committee Charge

The Portland Public Schools Board of Education (Board) has appointed a community
Bond Accountability Committee (“Committee” or “BAC”) whose charge is to monitor the
planning and progress of the bond programs relative to voter-approved work scope,
schedule, and budget objectives.

The duties of the Committee are (i) to receive and review reports and other information
on the current status and planned implementation of the bond programs, including bond
project and program status reports, critical path schedules, budgets and expenditures,
project prioritization within bond programs, identification of risks, bond-project
management key performance indicators, and annual bond performance audits; (ii) to
determine whether such status and implementation are consistent with the programs
approved by the voters; and (iii) to report its findings and recommendations to the
Board.

Background:

In November 2012, voters approved a $482M capital improvement bond measure for
Portland Public Schools. In May 2017, voters approved a $790M capital improvement
bond measure. In November 2020, voters approved a $1.206B capital improvement
bond measure, and in May 2025, voters approved a $1.8B capital improvement bond
measure.

All BAC meetings are publicly noticed and open to the public. Staff presentation
materials, along with BAC reports, are posted on the District’s website. Meetings are
also video recorded, which allows BAC members and the public unable to attend
scheduled meetings to watch the proceedings and review the materials at their
convenience.

This BAC meeting was held at the Prophet Education Center on July 29, 2026. The
BAC has a practice of alternating its meetings between in person and Zoom. Seven
members participated in this meeting: Greg DiLoreto, Kelly Wingard, Brian Harper,
Jonathan Trutt, Norm Dowty, Chris Dunning, Ryan Kinsella and our BOE liaison Director
Splitt. The meeting focused on four main areas: A presentation on the
collaboration between RISE and the District’s Finance Dept.; Updated financials
for the bond program; Modernization project update; and a presentation on
PROCEDEO program management plan and project team management plan for
each of the three high schools and Grice-Adair Center for Educational Excellence,
undergoing modernization. We did in-depth introductions of the committee members
and staff, giving the Committee a better understanding of committee member
experience. Additionally, according to our revised charter, which calls for new officers at
our first meeting after July 1st, we elected Greg DiLoreto as chair and Chris Dunning as
vice chair.

Report
1. Whether the school district bond revenues are expended only for the purposes for
which the bonds were approved, and that bond revenues are not used for any purpose
prohibited by law.

   ●​ The BAC reviewed the Bond Program Administration report for Cleveland,
      Jefferson and Wells high schools and the Grice-Adair Center.
   ●​ An in-depth presentation was provided regarding how the District determines that
      projects meet the test of bond funding eligibility. The definition is that the work
      must add value, results in betterment of the facility, extends the property’s life, or
      adapts it for new use. Capitalization threshold is $25K. Project are placed into
      one of four buckets: Construction and Renovation; Systems and Infrastructure;
      Sites and Grounds; and Fixtures and Equipment. All projects are reviewed by a
      designated PPS staff team to ensure that they meet the test of usability of bond
      funds.
   ●​ Based on these reports the BAC believes the programs to have been
      properly funded; it appears that the school district is spending revenues
      only for the purposes for which the bonds were approved.

   2. Whether the bond budget is sufficient to complete the scope of work as outlined in
   the voter-approved bonds.

      ●​ As presented to the BAC there are sufficient funds to complete the
         scope of work as outlined in the voter-approved bonds.
      ●​ Bond funds from the 2012 bond are nearly 100% spent, the last,
         approximately, $306,000 available. Bond funds from the 2017 bonds are
         95% spent of which $45.4 million is still available. Encumbrances are up
         $10 million from our previous report. Bond funds from the 2020 bonds
         are 64% spent with $480 million available with early work at Jefferson,
         accounting for the increase in expended funds. Thirteen percent of the
         2025 bond funds have been spent, most on modernization work.
      ●​ There is an ongoing claim dispute between the District and contractor
         for Benson high school. At this time the District and contractor
         continue to explore resolution of the various claims that have been
         made by both parties.
      ●​ A total of $4.6 billion in bonds have been approved by the voters in
         2012, 2017, 2020 and 2025 combined. To date 54% have been spent, up
         4% from our previous report. Outstanding encumbrances are $318.6
         million. A 57% increase, and $2.1 billion in bond funds remain, the
         majority of which is in the 2025 bond.

3. Whether the projects planned, in progress, and completed will meet the scope of
work specified in the voter-approved bonds.

      ●​ The BAC received a presentation on the status of the three high school
         modernization projects and the Grice-Adair Center. Status at Jefferson HS
         shows the design procurement is 100% complete. Staff received the 50%
         construction drawings on July 17th and review is underway, with 75% of the
         design completed. The contractor has mobilized, and fencing is up on the
         site.
      ●​ Status at Cleveland HS shows the design procurement is 100% complete.
         Staff has completed their review of the 50% construction drawings, with 80%
         of the design work completed. Demolition work on the old high school began
         on July 6th. Construction work is at 2%.
      ●​ Wells HS design procurement is 100% complete. Design is 75% complete.
         Staff expects to receive the 50% construction drawings on August 19th with
         100% of construction drawings due by the end of the year.
      ●​ Status at Grice-Adair Center shows that design procurement is 100%
         complete. No design or construction work has begun.
      ●​ Based on these reports the BAC believes the projects planned, in
         progress, and completed will meet the scope of work specified in the
         voter-approved bonds.

4. Whether the projects are being delivered on schedule relative to the voter-approved
bonds.

      ●​ A project schedule for the modernization projects was presented. All projects
         show a school opening of fall 2029.
      ●​ Design phase for the 3 high schools shows completion by the end of 2026.
         Design for Grice-Adair Center shows completion in the 3rd quarter of 2027.
         Land use for Wells HS is taking considerably longer than for either Jefferson
         or Cleveland. Staff is working with the City of Portland’s major project groups
         and is confident that everything is still on schedule. The schedule notes that
         there will still be construction occurring after the opening of Jefferson and
         Wells high schools, and this will be the demolition of the old schools.
      ●​ Risks to the projects include costs, scheduling, design scope creep,
         permitting and site security. Staff is working to mitigate these risks as follows.
         o​ Costs – continued value engineering at every submission to reconcile
            costs and value engineering as needed. The contractor is, and will,
            release bid packages early to gain best pricing ahead of escalating market
            pricing. Procurement early of steel and lock in of diesel price to avoid large
            price increases. Tariff impacts may affect costs. All budgets include an
            allowance for cost escalations to mitigate unforeseen cost increases.
         o​ Scheduling – staff and contractor proactively identify potential schedule
            risks during their regular design and construction meetings. Working
            closely with the contractor to develop recovery schedules when needed.
         o​ Design (scope creep) – consistent effort from the design team at every
            submission to reconcile costs and value engineering to maintain budgets.
            Staff weigh the cost of must haves versus nice to have design elements.
         o​ Permitting – Staff is working with the City of Portland major projects group
            in collaborative design review meetings to expedite review and permitting
            processes. There is also increased communication from multiple points of
            contacts from the professional partners reaching out to the permitting
            agency for status updates.
         o​ Site Security – there are enhanced security measures at the project sites
            which include increased patrols, and continuity in district property
            insurance prior to the contractor turning the completed projects over to the
            District.
      ●​ Based on the information presented, the projects will be delivered on
         schedule.

5. The Committee will receive and review copies of annual performance audit reports
and financial audit reports to ensure Bond revenues are expended in compliance with
state law and the ballot measure language.

   ●​ As noted in previous BAC reports, District staff is diligent in ensuring all
      bond revenues comply with state law and the ballot measure language, and
      the BAC has seen no evidence of operating outside of this compliance.
      Portland Public Schools RISE and Finance Leadership staff review the proposed
      projects to verify that they fall within the requirements for capital projects for
      which bond funds may be used.
