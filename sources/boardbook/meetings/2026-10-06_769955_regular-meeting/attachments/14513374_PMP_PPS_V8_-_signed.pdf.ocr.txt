1. PROGRAM MANAGEMENT APPROACH AND ORGANIZATION

As the Program Manager (PgM), PROCEDEO is
responsible for the day-to-day management of
all bond program activity, which includes the
completion of the projects within the approved
bond program budget.

PgM approach is to assist PPS in making
decisions for the best interests of the students
and community, and support Board of Education
(BOE), Bond Accountability Committee (BAC)
and Community expectations.

Throughout the Bond Program, there are many
opportunities for BOE, BAC, FIOC, and community
input. Per established PPS procedures, the BOE
is presented with project information for new
schools, additions, and major renovations.

In addition to the project information; program
status will be presented to the BOE as needed.
This information includes overall status of the
projects, program expenditures, and a report for
all changes monthly or at a cadence determined
by the BOE.

Consistent with the development of PPS's
Strategic Plan, PPS has formed a Bond
Accountability Committee (BAC). The primary
function of the BAC is to meet with the PgM
quarterly to review the status of the Bond to
ensure that the implementation is:

Consistent with the promises made to the
community.

Managed effectively within the approved
scope, budget, and schedule.

Consistent with the needs of PPS and in
support of PPS's Strategic Plan, and being
effectively communicated to the Community.

The BAC will present to the BOE and the
Superintendent regularly to advise them on
the implementation of the Bond Program.
Community members can also provide input to
the Board and Superintendent at the monthly
BOE meetings, specifically those meetings when
planning and design milestone information is
being presented.

To facilitate effective management of the program
and ensure that the program is delivered on
time and within budget, PgM includes over 20
team members. Design Managers who oversee
the planning and design phase of the projects,
Construction Managers who oversee the
construction phase of the projects and assigned
Project Managers that serve as a single point of
contact for each project. The PgM also includes
a project controls team that manages and
administers all document and cost procedures.

3. PROGRAM MANAGEMENT CONTROLS

There are many best practices for implementing
a successful bond program. These best practices
can be grouped into three categories: the right
people, the right tools, and the right processes.

To ensure that the Bond Program is delivered per
PPS and taxpayer expectations, the PgM has set
up a structure of controls to track, process, and
report progress and risks. In addition to reporting
to PPS, the PgM is expected to meet on a regular
basis with PPS to discuss: Budget, Finance and
Accounting, and Bond Program Communication
Services.

Monitoring the scope of work and the budget is
one of the major tasks for the PgM. Consequently,
the budgets for the individual projects, which
collectively add up to the total of $4.31B, must
be set up to reflect a multi-level account code
structure that includes: the bond year, the bid
package sequence number, the bond tranche,
the department identification, standard account
codes, and category type. PROCEDEO Is working
with PPS's current accounting structure to further
explore the tools needed for efficient monitoring.
Exhibit 3 is the current project budget model
PPS is utilizing.

On a monthly basis, actual expenditure costs
are being reconciled to ensure that all bond
expenditures are allowable and acceptable.

Owner representation services provide structured
support throughout all stages of design and
construction. Early engagement is used to
understand project requirements and determine
the most effective approach for meeting program
objectives. With the owner’s interests as the
guiding priority, the PROCEDEO team manages
each phase of project delivery by coordinating
with all participants to ensure safe execution,
schedule adherence, budget discipline, and
consistent quality.

helps with:

creates accountability throughout a project by
establishing projection worksheets and other
cost-control measures to monitor and maintain
the budget.

To ensure that all bond accounting line items
are accomplished consistently with the intent of
scope descriptions and resources provided under
the approved bond program, certain guidelines
have been established for, architects/engineers,
and the PgM to follow:

No project at any site will be added or
deleted without prior written approval by
the PPS Superintendent (or Senior Chief or
Operations), and PPS Board of Education,
when applicable.

Scope of work for any line item approved
by the PPS Superintendent (or Senior Chief
of Operations) will not be significantly
reduced or expanded without prior written
justification to and written approval by
the PPS Superintendent (or Senior Chief of
Operations).

Line-item budget adjustments within a
bid package at an individual facility, e.g.,
from “Non- Construction Contingency”
to “Furnishings & Equipment” must be
requested from and authorized by the PgM
and the PPS Superintendent (or Senior Chief
of Operations).

Costs to abate hazardous materials in the
path of new construction will be paid from a
separate budget established under the bond
program and shall not decrease the budgets
for authorized projects at any facility unless
previously approved, prior to PROCEDEO
contract.

Costs for Seismic upgrades will be paid from a
separate budget established under the bond
program and shall not decrease the budgets
for authorized projects at any facility.

Projected cost variances for an individual
line item is being managed within a specific
package at each facility, balancing overruns
and underruns in the construction budget,
up to the time bids are received and/or
a guaranteed maximum price (GMP) Is
established.

Cost overruns shall be the responsibility of the
project architect or engineer, working with
the PgM to reconcile pricing with budget
allowances by one of the following means:

1. Identifying unnecessary costly
requirements of the bid documents
and negotiating price reductions with
the apparent low-bidder or CM/GC as
allowable.

2. Undertaking redesign of the work as
necessary, without additional design
fees and without serious impact to the
original scope of work.

3. Submitting to the PgM a written request
for supplemental funding (from within
available bond program resources) to
cover overruns which are the result of
inadequate line-item budgets or other
unforeseeable factors).

Initial construction contract commitments
are preserving a contingency within the total
budget to cover conflicts, changed conditions,
and design revisions during construction.

Uncommitted funds are being retained
at each facility until work under a given
package is substantially complete and
final costs are identified, at which time any
savings are reverted toa central PPS program
contingency fund.

Contingency funds, whether created with
the bond program budget or accumulated
through project savings or increased bond
interest earnings, shall be allocated first
as necessary to complete authorized bond

8. DEVELOPMENT OF CONSTRUCTION PROCESS GUIDELINES

The commissioning process comprises the
integrated application of a set of engineering
techniques and procedures to check, inspect and
test every operational component of a project.
Commissioning activities are applicable to all
phases of the project, from the basic and detailed
design, procurement, construction and assembly,
until the final handover of the building to the
owner.

CM/GC is a collaborative project delivery method
authorized for public agencies in Oregon (under
ORS 279C). A CM/GC is selected during the early
stages of design based on their qualifications,
which include their demonstrated ability to
partner with designers and add their “hands-
on” perspectives on existing conditions and
construction techniques to design development,
construction documentation, and cost control. As
CDs are finalized, the CM/GC obtains competitive
proposals from pre-qualified subcontractors and
works with the PgM to fix a Guaranteed Maximum
Price (GMP) representing the maximum amount
PPS will have to pay for the project as defined in
the CDs. This process has an added benefit: if the
schedule compels it, the CM/GC can begin some
critical work before documents are complete.

As with any delivery method, incomplete and/or
inaccurate construction drawings will result in
change orders. The misunderstanding associated
with the GMP is that this maximum price will
not be exceeded in any case. It is important to
understand that the GMP is based on the plans
and specifications at the time of the GMP with
some reasonable assumptions made and a
reasonable contingency included.

PROCEDEO | ROGRAM MANAGEMENT PLAN

Major changes in scope will result in a change
order, which increases the GMP contract. In
addition, any Owner changes to the project and
scope require a change order. By requiring the
documents by 100% complete prior to receiving
the GMP, the risk of unanticipated change orders
is minimized.

The GC or CM/GC furnishes all labor, equipment,
and materials necessary to complete the
specified work within the prescribed time for
the amount stated in the bid or GMP. Prior to
construction, the architect and the Construction
Manager (CM) and Program Manager (PM)
review the contractor’s detailed work schedule to
confirm completeness and constructability, and
to ensure compliance with PPS requirements for
coordinating work with the building staff and the
academic calendar. The principal and select PPS
staff continue to meet regularly with the CM, PM,
architect, and contractor during construction to
stay informed and help resolve any conflicts or
unexpected problems which may arise.

The project architect observes construction
to ensure that all materials and workmanship
comply with the contract documents, and the
project manager monitors the project overall
to verify that quality, costs, and progress are
maintained, with the least impact to education
and administrative operations. Once the work
completed has been deemed to be used for it’s
intended purposes, ‘Substantial Completion’ has
been reached. This will vary by project and its
projected scope of work.

9. PROJECT ACCEPTANCE AND TURN-OVER GUIDELINES

While wrapping up the construction process, the
project architect and appointed PM is not only
ensuring that all materials and workmanship
comply with the contract documents, but
will create a list of items that do not comply in
order to verify that quality, costs, and progress
are maintained. These Punchlist items are
to be completed at subsidiary cost and can
only be approved by the owner or their hired
representative. Once the project is complete and
requires no additional work, ‘Final Completion’
has been reached.

The PM and architect monitor progress to ensure
the project is completed on time. The principal
works with the architect, contractor, and PPS staff
to coordinate the ordering, delivery, and setup of
furnishings, movable equipment, and supplies
in time for classes to begin. The GC or CM/GC
will be responsible for what is referred to as a
construction clean. Once the construction clean
is completed, PPS staff will complete the final
clean, which includes activities such as stripping
and waxing of floors.

Close-out is the final phase: the contractor
has completed the work and corrected any
deficiencies; the architect certifies that all work
has been completed satisfactorily; the PM verifies
that all contract requirements and documents
are complete; and PPS makes final payment to
the contractor and closes all accounts on the
project.

During the first year after a project is completed,
if items are found to be deficient or incorrect, the
GC or CM/GC is called back to the site to repair
the item with the architect, as verification of
proper completion of warranty items. The site-
based maintenance representative and the PM
complete this coordination.
