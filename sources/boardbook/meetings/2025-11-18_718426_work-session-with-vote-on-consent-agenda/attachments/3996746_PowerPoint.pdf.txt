A Living Expression of
Beloved Community
In our developing framework, Birth to Brilliance, the Center for
Black Student Excellence is anchored in Beloved Community.
The Center embodies one of the core principles of what we call
thrivability, where power, partnership, and belonging take
physical form.

Love is our infrastructure.

The CBSE Vision is values, and outcomes-based, it deﬁnes what
this space must accomplish. The Feasibility Study translates
those aspirations into spatial and functional possibilities.

Each program area transforms values into place. Each hub
becomes a manifestation of Beloved Community,
connection, creativity, and collective care in action.

Michael Chappie
Grice Center (East)
The East building (Chappie East) is envisioned as a
convergence point for student-focused gatherings,
community-oriented organizations, and
workspaces.

Chappie East is envisioned as the living room of
the Center for Black Student Excellence, the place
where students, families, elders, and community
partners converge.

It will be an open invitation: a space where learning
is surrounded by belonging.

Design Intent: The East Building should feel like a
village living room, vibrant, caring, and full of
cultural connection.
Dr. Harriet Adair
Center (West)
The West Building (Adair West) is dedicated to the
pursuit of academic brilliance, imagination, and
innovation. It embodies the spirit of Dr. Harriet Adair: A
legacy of scholarship, leadership, and unwavering belief
in the potential of every student.

Adair West stands as the studio of brilliance. A space
where curiosity is cultivated, ideas are born, and
learning becomes a creative act. Here, students engage
in design, technology, and artistry that reﬂect both
intellect and imagination.

Design Intent: Adair West should feel like a creative
engine, dynamic, forward-looking, and ﬁlled with the
energy of students shaping the future they deserve.

   Building Assessment
● The buildings are currently used as commercial oﬃce space.
   PPS use requires we change the occupancy to an “E” occupancy
  (educational).


● This change will necessitate additional life safety measures
  (including seismic and ﬁre suppression). Due to the height of the
  buildings, the City may limit our classroom use on the upper ﬂoors,
  potentially impacting as much as 15,000sf.
    Building Condition & Operations
●   Building Condition: The buildings are built of mass timber and are
    approximately ten years old. They are in good overall condition.
     ●   HVAC system: uses a refrigerant that is being phased out and
         remaining useful life estimated to be ﬁve years or less. Given the
         invasive nature of HVAC replacement, this work should be done in
         conjunction with the program and occupancy improvements.

●   Building Operations: The buildings are currently professionally managed. We have reviewed the
    operating expenses for the buildings, and they are consistent with PPS experience. PPS will
    continue to engage the property management company through a transition period to be determined
    post closing.
     ●   Commercial tenants pay pro rata share of maintenance and property taxes (PPS is exempt from
         paying property taxes on the portion of the property it uses).

●   Parking: Parking is limited at the site, and PPS will acquire a portion of a lease for additional parking
    from the neighboring Life Change Church, which expires in 2027. PPS will work with the Church to
    extend the term post closing.

    Cost & Timing
● The cost for the program and occupancy-related improvements is estimated at $21M-$25M,
  the cost for the HVAC replacement is estimated at $5.5M, and the purchase price is $16M for
  a total of $37M-$41M, well within the $60M allocation in the 2020 bond.

● The time for completion of student-facing improvement work is 24-30 months from Board
  approval.
