Subject:          High School Modernization Cost Analysis


In April and May 2024, the Board of Education (Board) approved comprehensive plans for
Cleveland High School (CHS) 1 and Ida B. Wells High School (IBW) 2 and authorized an update to
the comprehensive plan for Jefferson High School (JHS). 3

In November 2024, concerns over the total project costs led to the suspension of the design
phases of all three modernization projects. At that time, the Board requested a comparison of
project costs, including a detailed cost comparison report. 4

On February 11, the Board provided Staff with guidance in the form of a cost reduction
“framework.” The framework outlines general directions for reducing project costs.5

On March 4 the Board approved Resolution No. 7067 6 requiring that Staff return to the Board
with progress updates.

Staff have presented progress updates at the March 18, 2025 and April 8, 2025 Board meetings
including presenting updated designs, projected cost reductions and how the designs compare
to PPS’s High School Education Specifications and previous projects.

The next key step is for each team to finalize their design concept revisions and submit updated
plans to third-party cost estimators. Refined cost estimates - along with the updated schematic
designs and project schedules - will be presented to the Board when the updated information is
ready, approximately June/July, 2025.

The combined projects continue to forecast total savings ranging from $67 million to $33
million.7 This range is largely based on savings from the recent design changes. The teams have
prioritized attention on cost savings measures that impact the project design in an effort to
expedite any design decision therefore allowing the projects to proceed as quickly as possible
thereby limiting design, permit and construction delays. The teams continue to actively pursue
additional cost-saving opportunities. They are evaluating the insurance requirements in our
CM/GC contract to reduce insurance costs. Evaluations of the costs and benefits of structural
and mechanical system alternatives will continue into the new schematic design phase. The
teams are also exploring ways to strengthen our CM/GC contract requirements to improve
auditability of costs and clarify the documentation required to show that the contractor’s labor
rates include only the overhead costs allowed in the contract.

It’s also worth noting that although the projects have realized cost reductions due to design
changes, some costs have increased including design fees to make changes to design
documents that reflect cost savings strategies. Because the projects have been paused,
construction start dates will be later than the assumptions in their comprehensive plans’
estimates. More construction escalation is included in the cost reduction ranges than was
included in the Comprehensive Plans presented a year ago.

Staff will provide a holistic, and updated, project cost with the updated Comprehensive Plans.
