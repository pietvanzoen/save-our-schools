Pursuant to notice made by posting to the Board’s public notices webpage and emailed to persons on the

Prophet Education Center - Board Auditorium, 501 N. Dixon St, Portland, OR 97227 and streamed live at:
https://www.youtube.com/@ppsboardofeducation/live

Attendance

Director Julia Brim-Edwards: Present, Director Michelle DePass: Present, Director Herman Greene:
Present, Director Gary Hollands: Present, Director Christy Splitt: Present, Director Patte Sullivan: Present,
Director Edward Wang: Present; Student Representative JJ Kunsevi: Absent

Call to Order / Opening

The meeting was called to order at 6:12 pm by Board Chair Eddie Wang.

Superintendent's Report

Time: 6:13 pm

Superintendent Armstrong provided her report, with topics including Arab American Heritage Month, the
HeART of Portland K-12 Arts Showcase and highlighted the 2025 Rose Festival Court. She also
highlighted the “Lift Literacy” Campaign which included a video of personal stories related to reading.

        Agenda.
    •   Boards Governance and Strategic Leadership Academy: Vice-Chair Michelle DePass provided a

Presentation: 9th Grade Success, Advanced Placement, and Career and Technical Education
(CTE)

Time: 6:57 pm

Staff: Dr. Kristina Howard - Chief Academic Officer; Dr. Filip Hristic - Senior Director, 9-12 Core
Academics; Yeng Dhabolt - Director, College & Career Readiness; Chris Brida - Director CTE, AP, & IB

The Board received a presentation on the programs that support post-secondary readiness, including 9th
Grade Success, Advanced Placement and Career and Technical Education. There were questions about
access to college credit programs, including which schools house each program including advocating for
these options for students that attend Alliance and Metropolitan Learning Center. There were questions
about how the students' Advanced Placement passing rates compare nationally and a request to get data
on students connecting to licensed apprenticeship programs post-graduation. There were questions about
the correlation between Oregon Statewide Assessment System test scores and post-secondary
readiness. Board members expressed interest in making sure that the districts helps students that are
falling behind before entering night grade. There was a request to receive the information as
disaggregated data and an interest in bolstering attendance rates.

Integrated Grant Guidance (IGG) Presentation

Time: 7:44 pm

Staff: Jey Buno -Chief of Student Services; Michelle Morrison - Chief Financial Officer; Dr. Camedra
Jefferson - Interim Senior Director of Funded Programs

Staff presented the Integrated Grant Application for 2025-27. There were questions about the alignment
to the Board Goals regarding 3rd Grade and 5th Grade proficiency and how the District is tracking
progress. There were some questions about whether the Integrated Grant Application process resulted in
a more streamlined process for school districts. The Board will vote on the application at the April 22nd
meeting.

        Public Comment
        None provided

Recommendation for Distribution for Staff from the Parent Advisory Committee

Time: 8:07 pm

Staff: Robyn Farone, Director of Strategic Partnerships, Trina Huff, Committee Chair, Annika Houghton,
Student Committee Co-Chair - Daniel Levine, Committee Member

The Parent Advisory Council provided their recommendation for distribution of funds to schools which
included high impact tutoring, support for food pantries, IXL Digital Platform and seed money to support
fundraising goals. There were questions about how the funds for these services would be distributed to
schools.

Update: Draft Modernization Designs and Cost Reductions

Time: 8:25 pm

Staff: Stormy Shanks - Senior Director, Office of School Modernization

Staff presented an update on the Jefferson, Cleveland, and Ida B. Wells Modernization plans and
potential cost savings. There were questions about whether the plans for each of the high schools were
above educational specifications or below them. There was board discussion about the location of the
Indian Education program and whether or not to include the program at Jefferson on a temporary basis
until the school reaches capacity. There were questions about the timeline for securing a contractor for
Jefferson High School and the impact to the overall timeline. There were also questions about what was
being done to prevent cost overruns.

There was discussion about the additional cost for architecture fees for the design of Ida B. Wells as well
as a question about the construction timelines and a request for an itemization of additional costs.
Concerns were raised about insufficient cost reductions in the project, including the difference in cost

                                     RESOLUTION No. 7090

                        2025-26 Standard Inter-District Student Transfers

                                            RECITALS

A. State law requires district school boards to decide each year whether to participate in the
   standard inter-district transfer process, including:

        1. The maximum number of resident students, if any, who will be released to schools in
           other district,

        2. The maximum number of non-resident students, if any, who will be accepted for
           enrollment in district schools,

C. For the 2025-26 school year, Superintendent Armstrong recommends the PPS Board of Directors
   approve the following plan for accepting residents of other districts into PPS through the standard
   inter-district transfer process, so long as they have received permission from their home districts:

        1. An unlimited number of students will be allowed to transfer into PPS if they apply by
           September 15, 2025 and meet at least one of the following priorities

                  a. Students who had a legal change of residence out of the PPS boundary during
                     the past year will be allowed to remain enrolled at their current PPS schools.

                  b. Students who have siblings already enrolled in PPS will be accepted, so long
                     as space is available at the requested schools.

                  c.   Students who have reached the highest grade of their current PPS schools and
                       wish to continue at the next school level (such as elementary to middle school
                       or middle to high school) will be allowed, so long as space is available at the
                       requested schools.

        2. Additionally, up to 100 students who do not qualify for any of the above priorities will be
           admitted to PPS, so long as space is available at the requested schools.

                  a. If there are more applicants than slots a random number will be used as a tie-
                     breaker.

        3. New transfers will remain in effect through the highest grade of the approved school,
           subject to conditions described in PPS 4.10.090-AD.

                  a. Students who had a legal change of residence into the PPS boundary during
