Share the importance of this Tribal Consultation for this specific application(s):
This Tribal Consultation is an important component of the PPS 2025–27 Integrated Plan and Application for ODE’s
Integrated Guidance, which represents $125 million in combined state and federal funding. The funding priorities were
informed by a comprehensive district-wide needs assessment and a budget engagement process that included input
from the Title VI Indian Education Parent Committee. Through this consultation, we invite your input to help strengthen
how these investments are implemented and to enhance the overall supports provided to American Indian and Alaska
Native students.

How does this specific application impact AI/AN and all tribally affiliated students directly and indirectly?
This application directly impacts American Indian, Alaska Native, and all tribally affiliated students by allocating over
$200,000 a year ($400,000 total) specifically for supports tailored to Native students. It also includes additional funding
for school-based Restorative Justice efforts that engage both Migrant and Indian Education families, helping to create
more inclusive and responsive school environments.

As a whole, the application supports Native students through equity-informed investments across key priority areas,
including Early Literacy Achievement, K–12 Academic Success, Attendance and Engagement, Graduation and
Postsecondary Readiness, Safe and Supportive Schools, and fostering a Culture of Racial Equity and Care. These broader
investments aim to improve outcomes for all students while addressing the unique needs of historically underserved
communities, including AI/AN students.

Articulate clarity on consultation goals:
What does a successful consultation look like for this application?
A successful consultation for this application means that Siletz Tribal leaders have access to key information about the
targeted investments included in the PPS 2025–27 Integrated Plan. It also means they are given the time and space to
meaningfully reflect, ask thoughtful questions, and offer insights that help shape how these investments are
implemented to better support American Indian and Alaska Native students.

What is the district’s or the tribe’s commitment to this specific consultation process?

PPS:Portland Public Schools (PPS) is deeply committed to honoring and upholding the tribal consultation process as a
vital component of our responsibility to serve Native American and Alaska Native students and families. We recognize
the sovereign status of tribal nations and the importance of fostering meaningful, government-to-government
relationships built on trust, transparency, and mutual respect.
In alignment with federal and state requirements, PPS actively engages in ongoing consultation with tribal leaders and
representatives to inform decisions related to programs, policies, funding, and services that impact Native students. This
includes thoughtful collaboration in areas such as curriculum (e.g., Tribal History/Shared History), culturally responsive
practices, academic achievement, student support services, and Title VI programs.

We are committed to ensuring that tribal voices are not only heard but integrated into our district’s strategic planning
and continuous improvement efforts. Through open dialogue, listening, and shared decision-making, PPS aims to create
educational environments where Native students are affirmed, supported, and empowered to thrive.


What concerns do you have about this process?

PPS: While Portland Public Schools (PPS) is committed to strengthening its government-to-government relationships with
tribal nations, we also recognize that there are challenges and areas for growth in our current Tribal Consultation
process. Some of our key concerns include:​
​
PPS acknowledges that meaningful consultation depends on strong, sustained relationships with tribal governments.
Establishing and maintaining these relationships requires time, consistency, and trust—something that cannot be rushed
or limited to annual meetings. We are mindful that tribal partners often have multiple demands on their time, which
makes consistent engagement challenging for both parties. We remain committed to meeting quarterly instead of
annually to engage in meaningful tribal consultation.

We want to ensure we are consulting with the appropriate representatives from each tribal nation and that all
voices—especially urban Native families and those not connected to a recognized tribe—are included in the broader
conversation. Internally, the district is working to ensure that staff are trained and prepared to engage respectfully and
effectively in these government-to-government processes.​
​
 PPS is working to strengthen how tribal feedback is documented, shared, and integrated into district
decisions—especially related to budget planning, curriculum, and student support services. We recognize the importance
of demonstrating how consultation informs our actions and priorities.


What data is pertinent to review and share out for this application? What input from the tribe is critical for this
application?
There are two key parts to the information we aim to review and share for this application. First, it’s important to provide
an overview of the broader PPS 2025–27 Integrated Plan, which is outlined in these slides. This includes the overall goals,
priorities, and strategies guiding the district’s investments.

Second, we want to focus on a more in-depth discussion of the targeted investments that directly impact American
Indian and Alaska Native students, such as the dedicated funding for Native student supports and school-based
Restorative Justice efforts.

Input from the Tribe is critical in helping us understand how these investments can be implemented most effectively to
support Native students. Tribal leaders' insights and questions are essential for ensuring that the strategies are culturally
responsive, community-informed, and aligned with the needs of the students and families we serve.
