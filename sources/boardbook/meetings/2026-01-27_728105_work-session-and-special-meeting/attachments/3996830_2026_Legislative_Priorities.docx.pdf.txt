BACKGROUND

The Legislature will convene for the 2026 Short Session from February 2nd to March 8th. The
short session’s compressed timeline means limited runway for policy development, rapid
committee deadlines, and clear prioritization. The legislature will primarily focus on the budget
deficit, protecting immigrants, and raising revenue.

2026 Legislative Session Preview

Top of mind for everyone will be how the state wrestles with the existing budget deficit during
the current biennium. Oregon faces an estimated $600 million budget gap—driven largely by the
impacts of HR 1—and the outlook for the 2027–29 and 2029–31 biennial grows more
challenging as those impacts compound. In the September 2025 revenue forecast the State
Economist projected a $373 million budget hole for the 2025-27 biennium. In response,
Governor Kotek asked state agencies to prepare budget reduction options in 2.5% and 5%
increments. An updated revenue forecast in November found that the deficit shrunk from $373
million to roughly $63.1 million.

The Legislature will receive an updated Revenue Forecast on February 4th. This forecast will
include updated unemployment data unavailable last fall because of the government shutdown.

Heading into the 2026 legislative session, we recommend that the team focus on key three
priorities: preventing cuts to education, raising additional revenue to narrow the budget deficit
and protect our community members who are being impacted by ICE.


Key Bills to be tracked

During the Short Session, each legislator is allowed to introduce no more than 2 bills, and each
committee can introduce up to three bills. Below is a list of bills we will be tracking:
   ●​ A bill that would make class size, student and teacher safety, and discipline mandatory
       subjects of bargaining. This bill is Sponsored by the House Committee on Labor and
       Workplace Development
   ●​ A bill sponsored by Senator Neron Misslin (D-Wilsonville) which would require that every
       school offer free meals to every student. This program would be funded by an existing
       program in the federal government.
   ●​ There are two bills that would require that schools and universities to notify parents if
       federal immigration agents appear on campus. One version of the bill is sponsored by
       Senator Lew Fredrick (D-Portland), the other is sponsored by Representative Sara
       Finger-McDonald (D-Corvallis).
   ●​ A bill to codify the federal McKinney-Vento Act which would guarantee students
       experiencing homelessness the right to enrollment, transportation and access to
       learning. This bill is sponsored by Representative Emerson Levy (D-Bend)
   ●​ A bill that would retroactively fund outdoor school in Oregon. This bill is sponsored by
       Representative Hudson (D-Troutdale).
   ●​ Representative Lamar Wise (D-SE Portland) has a bill that would require quarterly
       reporting on attendance data to the Department of Education.
   ●​ Education Committee bills
