                             Be brief; please limit your responses to 50 words per question.

1. Describe in your own words the mission and goals of OSBA.
   OSBA's primary objective is to support School Board Directors. That support shows itself in providing ongoing training options; governance
   support around policy implementation; expanded lobbying partnerships in State & Federal legislation; overall leadership education. These
   areas focus on improving education outcomes for the students in the state of Oregon.




2. What do you want to accomplish by serving on the OSBA board of directors?
   Education is in constant flux. The methods change, the content changes, and the necessary outcomes change. We build on past success,
   and look to the next challenge. We support our community, and are the conduit for accurate information about student education. OSBA
   gives board members the tools to succeed; that means focusing on the student needs, and having timely and accurate info . We support
   each other, so everyone can succeed.



3. What leadership skills do you believe you bring to the board of directors? Give an example of a situation in which
   you demonstrated these skills.
   I think I bring a calm, thoughtful voice to the conversation. I have experienced through my local board service and OSBA board service,
   budget crises, sudden leadership changes, and external pressure by people who don't always have a student focus. We have had
   challenging bargaining scenarios, and commuity concerns about safety in our schools. Having accurate information and sharing that
   information is crucial. One of my proudest moments was successfully leading OSBA in strongly supporting Measure 98.

4. What do you see as the two most challenging issues faced by OSBA?
   Maintaining affordable services for districts who are facing budgetary impacts.
   Offering training in a variety of formats to meet the needs of citizen elected board members.




5. What do you see as the two most challenging issues faced by your region?
   Pockets of declining enrollment
   Economic stresses on families




6. What is your plan for communicating with boards in your region?
   Use of expanded distribution tools, e.g. Email list serve for OSBA updates. Attending regional and statewide training sessions with fellow
   board members. Using existing technology to attend local board meetings, and provide updates via public comment. Always availble via
   email, phone or text for questions or concerns.
