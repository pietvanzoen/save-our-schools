Pool RFP Process

This item was added to agenda following Board approval of several "not-to-exceed" contracts utilizing Pool
Requests for Proposals (Pool RFPs).

During previous Board discussions, RISE indicated that Pool RFPs had been used previously within PPS. The
District was unable to locate similar examples following the meeting, prompting this discussion.

Director of Purchasing & Contracting Paul Williams provided an excellent overview of the procurement process
and acknowledged that while Pool RFPs are not new, "pools of this size and this quantity are different for us,"
adding that in terms of "size and scale-wise,” this approach is new to PPS.

Discussion

The presentation highlighted operational efficiencies associated with Pool RFPs. But, it also raised governance and
oversight questions that remain unresolved.

During both the April 14 Regular Board meeting and subsequent discussions, vendors responding to the Pool RFPs
submitted questions about the expanded scope of these procurements and how they differed from previous district
practices.

My primary concern is governance and transparency.

Because the Board approves the overall pool rather than individual task orders, contracts that would otherwise
require Board approval in response to the District's $150,000 threshold, will instead be awarded administratively
without subsequent Board review. While this may improve administrative efficiency, it also reduces Board
visibility into significant expenditures.

It’s my opinion that the Board should consider an approved reporting process that provides regular visibility into:

    ●​ Task orders exceeding the Board approval threshold of $150,000;;
    ●​ Remaining contract capacity.


Follow-up: Staff has added a new pricing bid information sheet for each vendor to relevant Pool RFPs that had not
been included in prior Board materials, improving transparency during the approval process. Would recommend
Board training on Pool RFPs and a system reporting process for contracts/task orders that exceed $150,000

Bond Audit Update

Year 6 Recommendation #13 (Mislabeled in FIO Presentation)

Recommendation #13 calls for comparing staffing capacity with projected bond workload to identify staffing gaps
and determine whether resources are under- or over-utilized. The recommendation was originally identified for
closure in January 2026 by RISE.

The Bond Performance Auditor has reopened it with the following comment:

Our Bond Auditor's current comment states:

"No comparison of capacity to workload with gap identification was provided. Recommendation remains in
progress."

In practical terms, this means the District has not yet demonstrated that it has compared expected bond work to
available staffing resources or identified potential capacity gaps or excesses.

During the meeting, staff indicated it could potentially be closed with comments in April; however, it remains
open.

Follow-up: Assume this will be addressed in pending Bond Audit.


BAC Charter


Greg DiLoreto, the Bond Accountability Committee (BAC) Chair, commented that the BAC had reviewed their
proposed charter revisions developed with BAC members, FIO input, and input from PPS legal team. The BAC
recommended it be sent to the Facilities Improvement and Oversight Committee for review.


Discussion included whether the BAC should receive copies of program-level and project-specific Program
Management Plans containing key performance indicators. Staff indicated they would confirm that BAC members
have access to those materials. There were questions regarding KPIs referenced in the Committee Charge section,
and whether bond expenditure testing should be included in the district's financial audit process.


Director La Forte stated the BAC Charter would go to the Board for approval—via the BAC, not FIO—at the June 9th
meeting.


Follow-up: The BAC Charter was adopted by the Board with a 7-0 vote.
