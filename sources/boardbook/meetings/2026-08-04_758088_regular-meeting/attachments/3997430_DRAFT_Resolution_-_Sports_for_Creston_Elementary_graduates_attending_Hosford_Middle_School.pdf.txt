                                     RESOLUTION No. XXXX


            Sports for Creston Elementary graduates attending Hosford Middle School


                                             RECITALS

A.​ WHEREAS, Portland Public Schools (PPS) is committed to student success and equitable
    access to ensure every student can reach their full potential; and

B.​ WHEREAS, Sports in middle school are crucial for physical health, social development,
    academic performance, and personal growth; and

C.​ WHEREAS, Creston Elementary School (“Creston”) 5th grade graduates do not all go to the
    same PPS middle school. Specifically, a small number of Creston 5th grade graduates are split
    from their elementary school friends and relationships of six years and tracked to attend Hosford
    Middle School (“Hosford”) (which is a Cleveland-cluster middle school). Those students spend
    three years together making new friends and relationships, to then split again from their Hosford
    friends who are Cleveland High School (“CHS”) bound. The Creston students then attend
    Franklin High School (“Franklin”); and

D.​ WHEREAS, in the 2025-2026 year, there were Creston graduates who wanted to stay after
    school at Hosford to participate in sports practice at Hosford with their Hosford friends, but were
    denied that chance. Those sports include, but are not limited to, cross-country, volleyball,
    basketball, track and field.

E.​ WHEREAS, many of those Creston students are documented to have opted OUT of sports
    because they are not permitted to play sports at Hosford with their new middle school friends, the
    same students they see in school the day before and after sports practice. Instead, they have to
    play at a different middle school (one which is a Franklin cluster middle school) with students they
    do not know and do not get to see the next day at middle school; and

F.​ WHEREAS, those Creston students cannot simply stay after school at Hosford, but instead must
    get to a different middle school in order to attend practice. This causes transportation issues and
    cost problems for families and PPS; and

G.​ WHEREAS, middle school sports was discussed during the SouthEast Guiding Coalition
    (“SEGC”) process and families were promised that the boundaries would be resolved before their
    students got to high school; and

H.​ WHEREAS, Participation in sports ensures that middle school students engage in regular
    physical activity, which is essential for overall health and well-being. Sports help improve
    cardiovascular fitness, strength, coordination, and flexibility, while also promoting long-term
                                             Draft resolution proposed by Director Engelsman


    healthy habits. Establishing these habits early can reduce the risk of obesity and other
    lifestyle-related health issues.

I.​ WHEREAS, Research indicates a positive link between sports participation and academic
    performance. Middle school athletes often achieve higher GPAs and have a greater likelihood of
    graduating high school, tying directly into one of the PPS Board of Education goals. Physical
    activity may enhance brain function through increased blood flow, which supports concentration,
    memory, and learning. Additionally, sports cultivate skills such as goal-setting, discipline, and
    perseverance, which translate into classroom success, directly tying to PPS Board of Education
    goals.

J.​ WHEREAS, Sports provide a structured environment for students to develop social skills,
    teamwork, and communication. Working together toward a common goal teaches collaboration,
    conflict resolution, and empathy. Being part of a team also fosters self-esteem, confidence, and a
    sense of belonging, helping students navigate the social challenges of adolescence. Inclusive
    sports programs allow students of all skill levels to participate, promoting mutual respect and
    good sportspersonship.

K.​ WHEREAS, Middle school sports offer opportunities for leadership development. Students can
    serve as team captains, mentor peers, and demonstrate accountability, which builds
    self-confidence and responsibility. Time management and decision-making skills are also
    strengthened, as students balance practices, games, and academic responsibilities. These skills
    are valuable for future academic, professional, and personal endeavors.

L.​ WHEREAS, Engaging in sports supports mental health by reducing stress, improving mood, and
    fostering resilience. Students who participate in athletics are less likely to engage in risky
    behaviors and more likely to adopt a healthy lifestyle. The sense of achievement and
    camaraderie gained from sports contributes to emotional stability and overall well-being.

M.​ WHEREAS, Research from the Women’s Sports Foundation shows that girls who play sports are
    more likely to hold leadership roles later in life. Skills like teamwork (73%), learning from mistakes
    (53%), handling pressure (51%), and pushing physical boundaries (46%) are directly transferable
    to careers and civic life. Even without professional play, sport participation builds a “leadership
    pipeline” that benefits society and the economy.

N.​ WHEREAS, Sport participation helps girls build stronger bones, reducing th​
    risk of osteoporosis later in life. Weight-bearing activities can increase bone mineral density by up
    to 10%, which significantly lowers fracture risk.It also supports cardiovascular health, weight
    management, and overall fitness.

O.​ WHEREAS, Girls who play sports report lower rates of depression and anxiety, improved
    self-esteem, and better body image. The structure, social support, and sense of achievement in
    sports provide protective factors during adolescence and into adulthood.​
    WHEREAS, Sports foster belonging, confidence, and positive social connections. They also help

       teams—qualities essential in any profession.

   P.​ WHEREAS, Despite these benefits, girls drop out of sports at twice the rate of boys by age 14,

                                              RESOLUTION

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, the Board of Education directs the Superintendent to:

   1.​ For the 2026-2027 school year, allow Creston Elementary School graduates who attend Hosford
       Middle School but are in the Franklin High School cluster to choose whether they would like to
       play sports for Cleveland High School or Franklin High School.
