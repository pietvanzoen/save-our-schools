Background on CBRC

Community Budget Review Committee (CBRC) reviews, evaluates
and makes recommendations to the board regarding the
Superintendent's Proposed Budget and conﬁrms that the Local
Option Levy funds are being spent according to the voters’
wishes. It provides a vital connection between the district and
school students and families.

CBRC Core Messages for 2026-27

➔ Mitigating the direct impact of reductions on students and classroom
  staff is a top priority for the communities CBRC represents.
➔ Without a structural plan that spans several years, PPS risks drifting
  further into austerity while losing sight of its goals for equity, excellence,
  and innovation.
➔ Transparency is key to public trust, and the CBRC has identiﬁed multiple
  recommendations to improve the public's ability to understand where PPS
  resources are allocated and how they are reaching students.

Budget Accessibility and Transparency
In the budget, costs like construction and debt payments are mixed in with student
spending in ways that are diﬃcult to untangle, and there is no accounting of
student-facing vs. non-student facing spend.

CBRC recommends:
  ●   Develop a publicly-available category definition of student-facing and non-student
      facing roles
  ●   Create and present a comprehensive per-pupil analysis of the operating budget
      (without capital construction or debt service)
  ●   Provide function and object line-item budgets within each A) major function, B)
      major object, and C) major fund (already there)
                                                                                           6
Levy
In the budget, costs like construction and debt payments are mixed in with student
spending in ways that are diﬃcult to untangle. One consequence of this is that the
budget does not speciﬁcally account for where the local option levy money is spent.

CBRC recommends:

  ●   Provide function and object line-item budgets within each A) major function,
      B) major object, and C) major fund (already there)
  ●   Release cross-walked function and object data, to enable budget reviewers to
      answer questions such as, “What proportion of Elementary K-5 instruction is
      spent on classiﬁed salaries?” that are not possible to address in the current
      structure
                                                                                      7
Benchmarking
The CBRC has noted that instruction comprises a smaller part of PPS’s budget
compared to support services than national averages. CBRC recommends:
  ●   Develop a policy to proactively conduct and publish benchmarking analyses comparing PPS's
      instructional spending proportion and per-pupil instructional absolute expenditure to national
      averages and peer urban districts, on a regular 3-year cadence to align with and inform the district’s
      3-year planning cycle

  ●   Use benchmarking to support operational eﬃciency: The 2022 recommendation Follow-up Report by
      the Oregon Secretary of State coupled with a recommendation for PPS to “conduct regular and public
      peer benchmarking"

  ●   Assess whether any gap in instructional or operational resources falls disproportionately on
      historically underserved students, and report ﬁndings to the board and community with speciﬁc
      recommendations if such a gap is identiﬁed

Risks in the Budget
CBRC is concerned that budget relies on overly optimistic
assumptions.
 ● Labor - 1% COLA is signiﬁcantly lower than historical trends and
   current labor proposals.
 ● Revenue - CBRC questions if property tax projections are
   conservative enough to avoid a repeat of the 2025-26 mid-year
   deﬁcit.
While we appreciate the inclusion of expense buffers and the focus on
student stability, the district must use more conservative projections to
prevent another mid-year shortfall and maintain public trust.

Proportionate Reduction Framework
To address the budget gap while prioritizing equity and stability, CBRC
recommends a proportionate reduction framework focused on these priorities:
 ●   Protect Vulnerable Students - Preserve ELD services and middle school
     mental health supports.
 ●   Maintain Equitable Access - Retain ACCESS transportation and inclusive
     programming.
 ●   Weighted Reductions - Avoid uniform staﬃng cuts; apply reductions in a
     manner that protects high-need schools.
 ●   Transparency - Provide program-speciﬁc accounting of staﬃng changes
     for ELD, counseling, and special education.
 ●   Strategic Retention - Balance core services with high-value arts, athletics,
     and advanced learning to stabilize enrollment.

Advocacy
The CBRC appreciates the district’s transparency regarding state funding impacts. To
address current deﬁcits and ensure long-term stability, we urge the district to expand
advocacy for the following reforms:

 ●   PERS Rates - Form a statewide coalition to separate PERS liabilities from the State
     School Fund. This ensures the state fulﬁlls pension obligations directly and provides
     transparency for instructional spending as rates rise from 4% to 23%.
 ●   Special Education Funding - Advocate to raise the 11% funding cap to better support
     the 17% of PPS students receiving services, ensuring all new mandates are fully
     funded.
 ●   Student Investment Account (SIA) - Ensure SIA funds provide additive support for
     mental health and student services rather than backﬁlling State School Fund gaps.
 ●   Education Stability Fund - Secure one-time dollars from this fund to minimize service
     disruptions during structural budget adjustments.                                       11
Blended Classrooms
The committee remains concerned about potential student tracking inequities,
teacher stress, and the lack of stakeholder input. CBRC recommends:
 ●   The district evaluate the long-term eﬃcacy and equity of this model
 ●   The district explicitly identify multi-grade enrollment in data systems for
     outcome tracking
 ●   Future reports include interviews with families and educators and specify
     where licensed staff are utilized for math instruction
 These measures are necessary to conﬁrm if blended models maintain
 educational standards while reducing costs.
 Reference: Multigrade Teaching and Learning in Portland Public Schools

Contracted Services
 The CBRC is observes a 62% increase in Purchased Services, which
 rises from $270 million to $437 million. CBRC recommends:
 ● The Board direct the district to reduce third-party spending by
   renegotiating contracts or seeking lower-cost alternatives.
 ● To ensure future ﬁscal responsibility, the district must provide a
   transparent breakdown of all professional service contracts and
   evaluate these expenditures based on effectiveness and equity
   impact.

Alternative Revenue
 To ensure operational sustainability and reduce structural risk, CBRC recommends
 formalizing a Community Investment Strategy focused on equitable, durable funding
 through the following initiatives:
  ●   Community Beneﬁt Agreements (CBAs): Partner with major local institutions to
      generate recurring revenue for district-wide athletics, arts, and extracurriculars.
  ●   Scaled In-Kind Partnerships: Establish a centralized oﬃce to expand models
      like the Lincoln/MAC and McDaniel/People’s Courts agreements, scaling
      low-cost enrichment opportunities equitably.
  ●   Strategic Asset Use: Prioritize public-public partnerships over property sales.
      This includes converting underutilized buildings into affordable housing to
      stabilize enrollment and leveraging assets like One North for ongoing revenue.
  ●   Modernized Fundraising: Replace school-speciﬁc fundraising with
      district-supported, equity-based models to eliminate neighborhood wealth
      disparities.

Long-Term Outlook
 ●   The CBRC commends the Board and staff for efforts to implement a
     long-term strategy and community engagement process in this year’s
     budget, as recommended in the previous report.
 ●   However, we re-emphasize the need for continued structural
     reductions and a clear ﬁscal strategy. With PSU’s Population and
     Research Center predicting enrollment declines through 2029-30 and a
     budget gap projected for 2027-28, this year’s changes are only a ﬁrst
     step.
 ●   We urge the Board to continue implementing long-term
     strategies—including program or location reductions if necessary—to
     rebuild reserves. We also request increased candor regarding goals
     and timelines, ensuring ongoing opportunities for meaningful
     community input.
