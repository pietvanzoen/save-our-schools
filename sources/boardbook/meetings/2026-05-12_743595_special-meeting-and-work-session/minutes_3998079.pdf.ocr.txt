Community Budget Review Committee presents the 2026-27 Proposed Budget Report to the
Board

Staff: Michelle Morrison - Chief Financial Officer

Committee Members - Adriel Person, Shannon Stanley, Minyana Bishop, Shain Corey, Eli Posada, and
Aaron Cronan.

The Community Budget Review Committee (CBRC) presented their annual report to the Board, noting
the challenge of a shortened timeline. Key recommendations included: improving public accessibility of
budget information and differentiation of functions; addressing the lack of detailed Levy information;
conducting per-pupil spending analysis and peer district benchmarking; implementing a proportionate
reduction framework that protects ACCESS transportation and underserved students; advocating for
SPED, SIA, and Education Stability funding; evaluating blended classrooms; reducing third-party
contracting; exploring alternative revenue strategies; and formalizing a Community Investment Strategy
with long-term planning. The Board expressed appreciation for the report and the Committee's work.
Board members expressed interest in exploring free state-provided assessments, peer district
benchmarking, earlier initiation of the CBRC process, and reconsidering third-party contracts. The Board
also requested a feedback mechanism to track which recommendations are implemented, and
recognized the value of input received from teachers and families.

Climate Crisis Annual Report
Staff: Deborah Kafoury - Chief of Staff, Kat Davis - Climate Justice Advisor
Committee Members - Alyssa Koomas, Murtaza Balta

Deborah Kafoury introduced Kat Davis and the Committee members, who presented their annual report.
Highlights included progress toward carbon neutrality in 2023 and growth of the electric school bus fleet
to 27 buses, along with successes from the Climate Friendly Public Schools Program. The Committee
noted challenges including the loss of the Climate Policy Public Dashboard, lagging policy
implementation, and reduced staff capacity. Recommendations included recreating and regularly updating
the dashboard, and ensuring clear ownership of policy objectives. The Board expressed appreciation for
Kat Davis and the Committee's work, and raised questions about how the District tracks school-level
climate actions, the intentionality of climate justice in the work, student engagement and surveying, and
whether decarbonization efforts are being considered in rightsizing decisions.

Discussion: Resolution Adopting Guiding Principles and Workplan in the Rightsizing Process for
Portland Public Schools

Staff: Deborah Kafoury - Chief of Staff
The Board discussed community engagement feedback received to inform the draft guiding principles and
resolution for Rightsizing Portland Public Schools. Board members were generally supportive and
discussed adding language around healthy and safe learning environments; clarifying neighborhood
school language and considering a definitions section; emphasizing compact boundaries that minimize
fragmentation and promote safer routes to school; ensuring the guiding principles reflect the full K-12
system. Members also raised questions about consistency in the use of "choice programs," ensuring data
is gathered from underrepresented communities, and using all four community-ranked priorities rather
than three. The Board expressed interest in continuing to collaborate on language refinements, with
flexibility and community-centered values guiding the work.

RESOLUTION No. 7299

Resolution to Recognize Teacher and Administrator Appreciation Week

RECITALS

Over 3,900 Portland Public Schools administrators, teachers and professional educators, through
their expertise and passion, prepare over 40,000 students in Portland Public Schools to succeed in
college and career and to become responsible members of our community.

The Board of Education recognizes and appreciates the ongoing dedication of our administrators
and educators, whose commitment to excellence ensures a high-quality education for all students
within Portland Public Schools.

Each day, Portland Public Schools administrators and educators inspire students by delivering
rigorous, authentic curriculum and instruction, along with personalized learning experiences that are
relevant to students’ lives, ignite their creativity, and empower them to reach their fullest potential.

On a daily basis, administrators and educators build strong relationships with students and families,
fostering teamwork and collaboration that supports students as active, engaged learners both in
school and at home.

Administrators and educators consistently work together to strengthen their teaching practices,
ensuring that they meet each student’s unique learning needs and styles.

Administrators and educators extend their efforts beyond the classroom by developing meaningful
partnerships with community organizations to create dynamic and enriching learning environments.

Our educators and administrators go beyond academic instruction, providing essential social and
emotional learning, mental health support, and holistic care to nurture the whole child.

Daily, educators and administrators support students in embodying the attributes outlined in the
Graduate Portrait, while modeling the skills, knowledge, and mindset outlined in our Educator
Essentials.

RESOLUTION

The Portland Public Schools Board of Education acknowledges the week of May 4, 2026, was
Educator and Administrator Appreciation Week in recognition and appreciation of their dedicated
efforts to ensure the success of students in Portland Public Schools.

Be it further resolved, that the Board encourages the Portland Public Schools community to join in
honoring Professional Educators and Administrators for their positive impact on our students and our
community each and every day.

RESOLUTION No. 7300

R lution to R nize M Asian American Native Hawaiian and Pacific Islander Heri Month
RECITALS

A. Asian American and Pacific Islander Heritage in the United States was celebrated beginning in
1978 and was made into a month-long event in 1992, as a way to honor and recognize the
contributions of residents from Asia, India and the Pacific Islands.

B. During Asian American, Native Hawaiian, and Pacific Islander Heritage Month, we recognize the
innumerable contributions, vibrant cultures, and rich heritage of Asian Americans, Native
Hawaiians, and Pacific Islanders (AA and NHPIs), who have lived and worked in Oregon for more
than 200 years contributing in to Oregon’s and the United States’ economy, culture, education,
politics, arts, literature, science and technological developments despite institutional and systemic
injustices designed to prevent and limit these achievements and contributions.

C. — Asian American, Native Hawaiian, and Pacific Islanders are among the fastest growing
communities in Oregon and Multnomah County. This migration has been both voluntary and forced
due to war and environmental degradation in their native countries.

D. During Asian American, Native Hawaiian, and Pacific Islander Heritage Month we acknowledge the
additional determination, hard work, and perseverance. Asian Americans, Native Hawaiians, and
Pacific Islanders must put forth to be heard and seen.

E. Our schools honor and preserve the linguistic and cultural assets of students through student clubs
like Asian Student Union, Asian Pacific Islander Club, Asian American Association and Vietnamese
Club. Enrichment programs such as our Dual Language Immersion and the option to obtain a Seal
of Biliteracy upon graduation, honor and enrich the diverse backgrounds of our heritage and native
Chinese, Japanese and Vietnamese speakers, while exposing non-native speakers to diverse
multilingual and multicultural perspectives.

RESOLVED

The Portland Public Schools Board of Education recognizes May as Asian American Native Hawaiian and
Pacific Islander Heritage Month and strongly encourages our staff and community to observe, recognize,
and celebrate the culture, heritage, and contributions of Asian American, Native Hawaiian and Pacific
Islanders to our country, our state, our cities, and our schools.

RESOLUTION No. 7301

Resolution to A M for Metropolitan Learnin nter
RECITALS

A. Portland Public Schools acknowledges that mascots have a significant impact on the overall
educational environment and can foster positive student development and sense of belonging.

B.  Itis the desire of the Metropolitan Learning Center program, to adopt an official school mascot, as a
way to unify Metropolitan Learning Center’s program, build identity, and enhance school spirit. A
mascot would enhance representation, build tradition, and create fun, shared experiences.

C. The Metropolitan Learning Center community commenced an engagement process led by a
committee of students, staff, and community members to determine a school mascot.

D. The community engagement process solicited feedback from the community on a mascot and a final
recommendation from the committee to the Superintendent for her review and final recommendation
to the Board of Education.

E. The committee recommended the “Monarchs” as their choice of mascot to the Superintendent.

F. The Superintendent, having reviewed the process and work of the committee, recommends the
Metropolitan Learning Center adopt the Monarch Butterfly as their mascot.

RESOLVED

The Board thanks the committee for its thoughtful work in developing and proposing a mascot for
Metropolitan Learning Center. The Metropolitan Learning Center mascot will now be known as the
Monarch Butterfly.

RESOLUTION No. 7302

Resolution han he Name of n Lee Elementar hool
RECITALS

A. Portland Public Schools acknowledges that the name of a school building has a significant impact
on the overall educational environment and can foster positive student development and sense of
belonging.

B. In May 2023, a group of parents initiated a request to change the name of Jason Lee Elementary,
gathering over 70 signatures from students, staff, and community members in support of
renaming the school.

C. In March 2024,the Board approved the request from the Jason Lee community, and directed the
Superintendent to begin a renaming process that was grounded in community engagement,
student voice, and alignment to Board policy.

D. A Renaming Committee was formed, composed of students, staff, parents, and community
members. The committee met regularly over the course of two years to design and implement a
process that centered on inclusivity, transparency, and meaningful engagement across the school
community.

E. Ultimately, Sunrise Elementary School received the highest level of support from the community.
The name “Sunrise” reflects themes of renewal, hope, and new beginnings, and was identified by
the community as a symbol that represents both the current identity of the school and its
aspirations for the future.

F. The Superintendent, having reviewed the process and work of the committee, recommends the
Board of Education renames Jason Lee Elementary School to Sunrise Elementary School.

RESOLVED
NOW, THEREFORE, the Portland Public Schools Board of Education approves the Superintendent and

school community’s final recommendation of Sunrise Elementary School, and Jason Lee Elementary
School will be known as Sunrise Elementary School.
