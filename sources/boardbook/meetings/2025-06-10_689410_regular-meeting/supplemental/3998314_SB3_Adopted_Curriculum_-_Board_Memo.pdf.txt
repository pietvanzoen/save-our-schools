Summary
The SB 3 (Senate Bill 3) workgroup began meeting monthly in October 2025 to review new
course standards and evaluate and develop curriculum for the two new high school graduation
requirements: .5 credit Higher Education and Career Path Skills and .5 credit Personal Financial
Education.

The workgroup included counselors, administrators, central office staff, and teachers
(instructional specialists, AVID, Advanced Math, CTE Business, Science, Social Studies, Special
Education). Based upon the recommended criteria for the review and selection of instructional
materials for Higher Education and Career Path Skills and Personal Financial Education, the
workgroup agreed upon a customized curriculum for the Higher Ed & Career Success (HECS)
course and adopted Next Gen Personal Finance for the Personal Finance course. Initial
professional development will be offered to teachers on Wednesday, May 14, 2025.

Higher Education and Career Path Skills: Higher Ed & Career Success
Oregon’s High School Higher Education and Career Path Skills standards include four domains:
   1.​ Seeking Assistance and Self-Advocacy
   2.​ Career Exploration and Preparation
   3.​ Postsecondary Readiness
   4.​ Workforce Readiness

Each of these domains includes three to four standards which encompass the knowledge and
skills that students should know and be able to do to be prepared for postsecondary opportunities
based on their own goals and aspirations for their future. The standards also include Key
Concepts that are relevant to each domain and standard.
To fully address all of the standards and key concepts, the HECS curriculum team included
lessons from the College and Career Exploration course for 9th grade students, the Career
College Collaborative (C3), AVID, MaiaLearning, Get Schooled, and the two adopted
Transformative Social Emotional Learning (TSEL) curriculum: Wayfinder and We Do It 4 the
Culture.

Assessments in the course include self-reflections and evidence, a resume, and My Plan Project.
Both the Resume and My Plan Project fulfill the Oregon diploma Personalized Learning
Requirements.

Personal Financial Education: Personal Finance
Oregon’s High School Personal Financial Education standards include five domains:
   1.​ Credit and Debt
   2.​ Banking, Investing, Saving and Repayment
   3.​ Budgeting, Spending and Money Management
   4.​ Personal Income, Taxes and Services
   5.​ Maintaining Financial Well-being.

Each of these domains includes three to four standards which encompass the knowledge and
skills that students should know and be able to do to be financially literate when they leave high
school. The standards also include Key Concepts that are relevant to each domain and standard.

Teachers on the Personal Finance curriculum team adopted the Next Gen Personal Finance
curriculum, a free curriculum that is highly recommended by current CTE teachers and utilized
by many school districts. The team co-developed project-based assessments for students at the
end of each unit. These assessments focus on demonstrating knowledge acquired throughout the
unit and ensuring that students are completing a project that will have an immediate impact on
their personal lives. Teachers will be given the flexibility to adjust these projects based on the
needs of their students, and the workgroup led with voice and choice in mind in the development
of multiple assessments for each unit.
