Continuity of Instruction

Chief Cardona gave an overview of the planning for continuity of instruction for students at a school when
a large cohort of their peers are away for an extended period of time. He gave examples of how principals
plan for substitute coverage and curriculum review. There were questions about who funds substitute
teachers for extended trips that last for more than a week. The Committee also questioned whether or not
the Districtwide Advocacy and Fundraising Policy could allow for the funding of substitute teachers.

Growing Great Schools

Committee Chair Chase-Miller opened with remarks about the gravity of the topic and committed to
transparent conversations. Superintendent Armstrong expressed appreciation for the statement.

Staff provided data on current and future enrollment trends and provided some background history of
some of the impacts to previous boundary reconfiguring changes and opening of new schools, like Ockley
Green Middle School. They also shared some lessons learned from previous enrollment and boundary
processes like the Southeast Guiding Coalition (SEGC). Staff provided some past examples of
engagement structures, and the framework analysis for SEGC and District-wide Boundary Review
Advisory Committee (DBRAC), as well as a framework used by Denver Public Schools.

Board members had an opportunity to share some initial thinking about their guiding principles:

    •   Include focus option programs and K-8 schools when making recommendations. For example,
        does a focus option result in a neighborhood being underenrolled.
    •   Protect and ensure strong neighborhood schools.
    •   Use an equity approach in decision making and include consideration of any unintended
        consequences.
    •   Consider if there are disproportionate impacts to lower income neighborhood schools that are
        close enough to a focus option that pulls away families.
    •   Avoid splitting feeder patterns whenever possible.
    •   Consolidate schools over closing them.
    •   Consider building deferred maintenance in a recommendation.
    •   Find out what people want in their schools.
    •   Look at per student costs and move away from inefficient models.
    •   Preference for a whole city approach.
    •   Ensure there are transition plans in place.

There were questions about the lottery system for focus options schools and whether or not families
opted out of Portland Public Schools if they did not get their student into a program that required winning
the lottery to enroll. Board members also asked if there were known reasons for the decline post
pandemic and requested capture rate information, and comparison data for birth rates and kindergarten
enrollment rates five years later. There was also requested middle school transition data, as well as data
on students who enter middle school after choosing other options for elementary. There was also a
request to understand if the change to the policy on early entry into kindergarten has had an impact in
enrollment.

Other Business and Future Agenda Items

The Committee requested that they have an opportunity to understand possible reasons for the decline in
enrollment and wondered if the Portland State University Population Research Center could be at an
upcoming meeting.

Adjournment

The meeting was adjourned at 6:10 pm by Committee Chair Rashelle Chase-Miller.
