Research on Dual Language Immersion points to five conditions that predict program strength — and a whole-school model
is structurally best positioned to deliver all five: 
01 
Schoolwide
Vision & Identity 
A shared vision across
staff, students, and
families is one of the
strongest predictors of
program strength. 
02 
Equity &
Language Status 
Both languages should
carry equal visibility and
status schoolwide. 
03 
Program
Coherence &
Fidelity 
Consistent, schoolwide
implementation is most
tied to stronger
outcomes for English
Learners — exactly what
a full-school model is
built to deliver. 
04 
Staffing &
Resource
Concentration 
Sufficient bilingual staff
and materials parity are
essential, regardless of
model. 
05 
Sustainable K–12
Pathways 
DLI should be sustained
PreK–12 — not
evaluated one school at
a time. 
Takeaway:   This is why PPS is moving toward whole-school DLI — it is the configuration most likely to reliably deliver these
conditions at scale. César Chávez--Moving to a Whole-School Model

Lent has transformed into a successful
whole-school DLI model — integrating
language, curriculum, and a whole-school
approach that has raised the bar for staff
and produced better outcomes for students,
particularly Multilingual Learners and
English Learners, who are showing
significant gains. 
Lent’s trajectory is exactly what the research
conditions and the Collier & Thomas data predict
for a well-implemented whole-school model. 
Whole-School Coherence: 
Every classroom, not just a strand, shares the same language
identity and instructional model — the condition research ties
most closely to stronger outcomes. 
Concentrated Bilingual Staffing: 
Staff plan and collaborate around one shared model instead
of splitting across parallel programs. 
Significant ML/EL Gains: 
Since the shift to whole-school, Lent is seeing measurable gains
for Multilingual Learners and English Learners specifically. Feedback & Transition Planning
