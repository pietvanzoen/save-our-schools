Annual Report Questions

Annual Report Questions
AR1. As you review your progress markers/overall reflection responses and reflect on plan implementation, how do
you see your progress contributing to the Outcomes and Strategies in your plan and your Longitudinal Performance
Growth Targets (LPGT)/Local Optional Metrics (LOM)?

Our 2024–25 implementation has been grounded in a deepening commitment to our academic achievement outcomes,
especially as we begin to operationalize our new District Continuous Improvement Plan (DCIP). The first two quarters of
the year have focused on aligning instructional practices, assessment systems, and intervention strategies with our
Longitudinal Performance Growth Targets (LPGTs). Across schools, instructional leaders and central office teams have
engaged in data cycles and coaching aligned to the PPS Instructional Framework, high-quality curriculum, and student
achievement benchmarks.

What sets this year apart is the formalization of the DCIP process. For the first time, all schools are participating in
structured improvement cycles grounded in common strategies and actions beginning in the 25-26 school year. These
cycles will not only elevate instructional coherence and student-centered planning, but also help teams measure their
impact on focal student groups with greater clarity.

We are also taking deliberate steps to increase data fluency and decision-making at the school level. The combination of
school-based PLC structures and centralized support, such as MAP data analysis, MTSS-aligned intervention planning,
and coaching protocols, is increasing alignment between daily practice and our LPGTs. Preliminary OSAS results show
moderate gains in reading and math overall, with some grade levels showing more profound growth than others.



AR2. Where have you experienced barriers, challenges, or impediments to progress toward your Outcomes and
Strategies in your plan that you could use support with? Discuss at least one Outcome where you have seen challenges
or barriers to implementation.

Several significant system-wide barriers have impacted progress across outcomes this year.

Most notably, the aftermath of the November 2023 work stoppage compressed the instructional calendar and required
additional investment in relationship-building, pacing adjustments, and re-engagement efforts. While the resulting
collective bargaining agreement included important gains in educator compensation and planning time, it also
introduced immediate financial pressure and reduced flexibility across other areas of the system.
Additionally, the evolving demands of enrollment balancing and facilities planning have required significant staff time
and public engagement. While these shifts are important for long-term equity and access, they introduce transitional
complexity that requires careful coordination and communication.

Finally, the uncertainty around federal funding has caused us to have to be more cautious about funding sources and
programs that are funded. This particularly affects academic outcomes for our Multi-lingual Learners.



AR3. 2024-25 Only: Review actual metric rates compared to previously created LPGT and LOM and share reflection on
progress. Describe how activities are supporting progress towards targets and if any shifts in strategy implementation
are planned for the future based upon that current progress. Include specific metrics and target types in your
reflection

At the time of this report, essential data for this response update remains under embargo by the state, limiting what we
are able to share publicly. A report with specific metrics will be available in November, following the release of the
statewide results. In the meantime, PPS continues to strengthen its use of local data to guide instruction and inform
student support strategies. Progress on these data-informed practices is outlined below.

Schools demonstrating the strongest progress toward Longitudinal Performance Growth Targets (LPGTs) are those most
closely aligned with district priorities. These schools consistently implement core curriculum, engage in regular and
meaningful data-driven PLCs, and maintain a strong focus on instructional coherence. Evidence of improvement is seen
through the widespread use of clear learning targets, standards-aligned instruction, and the implementation of
intentional tiered interventions that address academic gaps.

There is a notable shift in how school leaders and instructional teams are leveraging data. Increasingly, principals and
leadership teams are using disaggregated student data to identify groups not yet on track to meet academic growth
targets. This includes multilingual learners, students receiving special education services, and Black and Native
students, among other historically underserved groups. In response, teams are making mid-cycle adjustments to
instruction and interventions to ensure greater alignment with student needs and district outcomes.

While not yet happening systemwide in 24-25, this growing alignment at the school level between progress monitoring
and instructional decision-making reflects a deeper commitment to equity. Schools with the highest growth are using
real-time data to not only track progress but to intentionally identify and address disparities, inform targeted supports,
and accelerate growth among student groups who have been historically underserved.

As we move forward, schools showing slower progress are being supported to strengthen the use of PLC structures,
deepen curriculum alignment, and build capacity around evidence-based tiered supports. These strategic shifts are
designed to ensure all schools are on track to meet their LPGTs, with clear pathways to monitor, adjust, and sustain
improvement over time.

Based on our midyear learning, we are making the following strategic shifts:

    1.​ Tighter integration between central and school-level DCIP/SCIP cycles, with clearer routines for progress
        monitoring and mid-cycle adjustments
    2.​ Refined resource deployment to ensure that interventionists, tutors, and content leads are prioritized for schools
        with the highest need and clearest improvement focus
   3.​ Deeper investment in building data fluency for school teams, especially around interpreting interim results and
       designing responsive instruction

The 2024–25 year marks a shift in how we think about improvement, from isolated programmatic change to a
systemwide culture of evidence-based planning and action. While gaps persist, our progress to date suggests we are
building the conditions for more equitable and sustained improvement over time
