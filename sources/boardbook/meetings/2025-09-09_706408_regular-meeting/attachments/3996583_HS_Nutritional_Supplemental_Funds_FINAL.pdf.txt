    1.​ Overview

Background

An original 1965 grantee, Portland Public Schools Head Start is currently funded for 648 children. Our service
area is PPS school district boundaries, east of the Willamette River in Portland, Oregon. We have 49 full day
classrooms across Portland’s most impacted neighborhoods – North Portland, SE Portland, St. Johns, Cully,
Foster-Powell, and Montavilla – where rates of food insecurity and housing instability are some of highest in
Multnomah County. While USDA Child Nutrition programs provide essential meal support to our program, these
resources do not cover access to local farms, family engagement around healthy eating, or specialized
professional support needed to close persistent nutrition gaps.


Program Need

Our annual Community Needs Assessment highlights growing disparities in food access, rising food insecurity,
and ongoing environmental health risks. The data indicates 1 in 6 children in Multnomah County are food
insecure. Only 85% of those eligible for SNAP are enrolled, with the greatest need concentrated in
neighborhoods within our service area (St. Johns, Cully, Foster-Powell, and Montavilla). National data also
indicates that low-income households – like the families we serve in Head Start – are significantly more likely to
experience food insecurity and rely on calorie-dense, nutrient-poor options (USDA ERS, 2023). Additionally,
many PPS families live in older homes with elevated lead exposure risk – particularly in Kenton, Cully, and Mt.
Tabor — further undermining children’s health and development. Without targeted support, these patterns can
contribute to poor diet quality, higher rates of childhood obesity, and increased risk of chronic disease later in
life.

This is evident in our specific student population. Our 2025 Program Information Report (PIR) Body Mass Index
(BMI) data indicates that 16% of our students are categorized as overweight and 19% categorized as obese, with
the latter reflecting a 5% increase from the previous school year. In addition, 3.3% of enrolled students are

categorized as underweight, indicating that a portion of our population is experiencing undernutrition, which
can negatively affect growth, development, and learning. To normalize BMIs and address both ends of the
malnutrition spectrum, our children need increased opportunities to integrate physical activity, nutrition, and
wellness into daily routines. This includes health literacy on nutrition, physical wellness and movement activities
to equip families with tools to build lifelong healthy habits.

Our families are strong advocates for improving children’s access to nutrient-dense foods and reducing reliance
on ultra-processed items high in added sugars. In March 2024, our Parent Policy Council submitted a formal
letter to PPS Nutrition Services requesting that the program expand fresh fruit and vegetable offerings during
snack and replace packaged, processed foods with whole-food alternatives. Families have also voiced a desire
for nutritious food options at family engagement events, along with workshops focused on nutrition and healthy
eating. At the same time, many parents report barriers to accessing food assistance due to limited availability
outside standard work hours, and the closure of several site-based food pantries has further widened the
nutrition gap. Together, these requests and challenges highlight the urgent need for program-level
improvements that make fresh, healthy foods consistently accessible for children and families while also
equipping parents with the knowledge and resources to support healthier eating habits at home.

While our children need nutrient-rich food and families continue to champion healthy eating, PPS Head Start
faces complex, compounding barriers. Limited access to affordable, high-quality food disproportionately affects
our enrolled families, many of whom live in food deserts or food swamps where convenience stores and
fast-food outlets far outnumber grocery stores with fresh produce. Under-resourced households face financial
strain, long or irregular work hours, and limited transportation, all of which make it harder to prioritize healthy
food purchasing and preparation. The closure of site-based food pantries and reduced access to emergency food
supports further exacerbate these challenges. Additionally, systemic inequities—including the rising cost of
groceries and lack of culturally relevant food options—have widened gaps in access to fresh, healthy food. Taken
together, these barriers create a cycle where children are more likely to consume ultra-processed foods,
increasing their risk of obesity, undernutrition, and long-term health concerns.



    2.​ Plan to expand and improve nutrition services and support for healthy eating

Funding Category: Food Service Upgrades

Goal 1: PPS Head Start will expand long-term access to fresh, healthy, and local food.

        Activity: We will establish vendor contracts with local farms for the procurement of local, seasonally
        fresh food items. Procurement will include transportation costs for delivering seasonally fresh
        ingredients to centers on early release days when we do not provide a snack component to our
        students. Our Registered Dietitian will oversee the quality of local food sources and ensure the fruits
        and vegetables are nutrient dense; the dietitian will also work with nutrition services to integrate



                                                                                                                  2
        ongoing sustainability practices. Our Business Operations Analyst will maintain and document
        procedures to comply with federal procurement guidelines.

        Rationale: Partnering with local farms and distributors through formal vendor agreements increases
        reliable access to fresh, seasonal produce and supports nutrient-rich menus year-round (USDA FNS,
        2024; CDC, 2023). Supplementing processed snacks with fruits, vegetables, and whole grains improves
        nutrient intake, lowers added sugar consumption, and promotes children’s healthy growth while
        modeling positive nutrition habits for families (CDC, 2022; CATCH Program Study, 2013).

Funding Category 2: Materials, Supplies, and Equipment

Goal 2: PPS Head Start will improve the safety and quality of food preparation.

        Activity: We will purchase food safe storage supplies to improve the safety of food preparation and
        quality of family style dining in our classrooms. Our Registered Dietitian will ensure supplies meet
        federal food-safe specifications and our Business Operations Analyst will maintain and document
        procedures to comply with federal procurement guidelines.

        Rationale: Outdated materials can contribute to longer preparation times, inconsistent meal quality,
        reduced food safety, and higher costs, ultimately reducing the efficiency and safety of food service
        operations (National Food Service Management Institute, 2019). Updating kitchen supplies is essential
        to delivering high-quality, nutritious meals that align with USDA Food and Nutrition guidelines and Head
        Start Program Performance Standards.

Goal 3: PPS Head Start will create community gardens at four program sites.

        Activity: We will partner with Growing Gardens, a local nonprofit, to procure materials and supplies to
        install community gardens at 4 of our sites. Growing Gardens has nearly 30 years of experience growing
        foods with families, children, and school communities to build nourishing whole food systems. The
        Growing Gardens contract will include supplies design, installation, and local support of procurement.
        Garden spaces with raised beds, greenhouses, and Indigenous seed hubs; training will also be provided
        to staff to ensure long–term sustainability of gardens and integration of whole food into education
        programming.

        Rationale: Community gardens serve as living classrooms that engage children in hands-on food
        exploration, increase produce consumption, and encourage family participation in healthier eating
        (Journal of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, 2020; Urban Agriculture Review, 2022). By investing
        in sustainable infrastructure — such as garden kits, greenhouse supplies, and Indigenous seed hubs —
        PPS Head Start can embed culturally relevant food education and traditional growing practices into its
        curriculum for long-term impact (Farm to School Network, 2021).

Funding Category 3: Nutrition Education Resources

Goal 4: PPS Head Start will establish staff and family-centered nutrition education programs.

        Activity: We will partner with Greater NW Community Health Collective (GNWCHC) to offer integrated
        health education, resource navigation, and culturally relevant support to our families. GNWCHC will
        offer 4 neighborhood pop-up nutrition fairs that include educational resources and family engagement
        activities. They will also offer 5 nutrition workshops to support long-term positive healthy eating habits
        that empower families, and when applicable, assisting families in enrolling in farmers market vouchers
        to reinforce learning and increase access to healthy food at home. Lastly, the GNWCHC Community
        Health Team will also provide outreach, navigation, and transportation support to families connecting
        them to a variety of essential, healthy food access points (ie grocery stores, farmers markets, food
        pantries, etc.)

        Activity: To extend the impact and ensure the sustainability of our community gardens (see Funding
        Category 2, Goal 3), Growing Gardens will also provide programming to our families, students, and staff.
        Growing Gardens will facilitate 2 workshops per site over 12 months. Workshops will center around
        gardening, garden-based nutrition, and cooking education to build connections. Growing Gardens will
        also enroll at least 8 PPS Head Start Staff into their nationally recognized School Garden Coordinator
        Certificate Training (SGCCT) to increase staff leadership skillsets for making educational connections and
        food production beyond the successful use of one-time funding.

        Rationale: Evidence from nutrition education programs like SNAP-Ed and Cooking Matters programs
        shows that hands-on workshops focused on meal preparation, budget shopping, and healthy cooking
        increase participants’ cooking confidence, food resource management skills, and consumption of fruits
        and vegetables (SNAP-Ed Connection, 2022; Cooking Matters Evaluation, 2021). Healthy nutrition is
        essential for brain development and provides children with the energy needed to stay active and
        focused throughout the Head Start day. Hosting nutrition fairs and workshops will allow PPS Head Start
        families to build practical skills they can immediately home.

        Collaborating with trusted local partners such as Growing Gardens and Greater Northwest Health
        Collective strengthens program reach and ensures that resources reflect community needs. Research
        confirms that partnerships between early learning programs and community-based organizations
        improve family engagement and help reduce disparities in access to health education and support
        services (Annie E. Casey Foundation, 2020).

Funding Category 4: Non-recurring Personnel Fees

Goal 5: PPS Head Start will develop programming to integrate physical activity into children’s daily routines.

        Activity: We will partner with Soccer Shots, a local youth soccer program, to provide 5 weeks of soccer
        programming to our Head Start students at school this year. PPS Head Start established a partnership
        with Soccer Shots in the Spring of 2024 when they offered programming to our students 100% in kind.
        Continuing this programming will deliver life changing opportunities for our kids to be active, to learn
        from coaches, work with their peers, and develop gross motor skills. Soccer Shots and PPS will research
        additional funding sources to continue this model beyond the contract.

        Rationale: Collaborations with organizations like Soccer Shots enable the integration of physical activity
        and wellness into daily routines, complementing nutrition efforts.Our data indicates that our students'
        BMI is a significant program need. Research links combined nutrition and physical activity programs to
        healthier body mass index (BMI), improved motor skills, and overall wellness in early childhood
        (Preventing Chronic Disease, 2020; National Institutes of Health, 2021).

   Financial and Property Management System
   The PPS Head Start Program adheres to the District’s financial management processes and
   procedures which, in turn, are governed by the Oregon Revised Statutes (ORS) and conform to
   Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP). Grant funds are tracked in the District’s
   financial system in a separate fund for ease of visibility and tracking. An annual audit by an
   independent auditor is performed to grant funds of the District to the standards contained in

                                                                                                            10
         Government Auditing Standards issued by the Comptroller General of the United States. All
         grant documents from the Notice of Award to the final SF425 report are kept on file both at the
         Head Start administrative office and at the District’s finance offices. Procurement processes are
         also governed by ORS and by District Board Policy. A record of equipment over $10,000
         purchased with Federal Head Start funds is retained at the District office and is inventoried
         every other year by Head Start staff. Materials and technology inventory is kept at the Head
         Start administrative offices and is also inventoried biennially. All journal entries and reports
         initiated by the Head Start accountant are forwarded to the District accounting office where
         they are reviewed by the District grant accountant and approved by the accounting manager
         prior to posting to the general ledger.

​        Contracting
         The PPS Head Start Program adheres to the District’s contracting guidelines The District’s
         purchasing and contracting activities are governed by Oregon Revised Statutes (primarily ORS
         279A, ORS 279B and ORS 279C) and Board policy in the form of our PPS Public Contracting
         Rules, which are in turn modeled after the Attorney General's Model Public Contracting Rules.

         Indirect Charges
         Indirect charges are calculated at the end of the school district’s fiscal year, once the year is
         closed. This usually happens between May – September. The calculations are submitted to the
         Oregon Department of Education for review and final approval. Once the indirect rates are
         approved, the District is notified of the final rate. This update includes the final state approved
         rate of 4.49% for the 2025–26 school year.
