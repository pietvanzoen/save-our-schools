Introduction
This report fulfills PPS Board Policy 4.50.030-P requiring an annual update to the Board on the
District’s formal complaint process. It covers formal complaints received and decided between
July 1, 2024 and June 30, 2025 (“reporting year”) and summarizes data highlights, trends, and
process improvements.

During the reporting year, families may raise issues or concerns about their students’ schools
or classrooms. Portland Public Schools (PPS) welcomes these expressions as opportunities to
learn, clarify intentions, and drive continuous improvement that benefits all students. We are
committed to working with families to address concerns promptly and fairly.

Most concerns are resolved at the school level (see the Conflict Resolution page for
pathways). The District also provides a Complaint Coordinator, housed within the Office of Civil
Rights, who helps families access information, connect with staff, and navigate resolution
options.

When an issue meets the criteria for a formal complaint under the District’s Formal Complaint
Policy and Oregon Administrative Rule (OAR) 581-022-2370, PPS follows the formal complaint
process.



Types of Complaints
PPS accepts Division 22 formal complaints under Board Policy 4.50.030-P and OAR 581-022-
2370 on the following topics (examples are illustrative, not exhaustive):

   ● Instructional standards and practices
         ○ For example, curriculum, teaching strategies, testing/assessment, counseling,
            class size, instructional materials, and compliance with state standards.
   ● Special education programs (program-level issues; not individual student disputes)
         ○ In most cases, special education complaints are redirected to the Oregon
            Department of Education
         ○ Individual student Individuals with Disabilities in Education Act (IDEA) and/or
            Section 504 disputes follow Oregon Department of Education (ODE) process
            rather than Division 22 guidelines

Concerns Outside of Division 22 Criteria
Most concerns that reach the Complaint Coordinator, whether through the UCF or by email or
phone, do not meet the state’s Division 22 criteria for a formal complaint. That does not mean
they go unanswered. These concerns typically involve individual student supports (special
education or Section 504), transportation, student behavior and discipline, Title IX or other
civil-rights issues, school-based peer conflicts, neighborhood matters around schools (parking,
traffic, community interactions), staff or workplace concerns, records requests, and other site-
specific issues. When a concern falls outside Division 22, we route it to the team best
positioned to help and follow up with the family.

When receiving concerns that fall outside of Division 22 criteria, our process is:

   1. Intake and safety check. We review the concern to understand the issue and to identify
      any school, student, or community safety risks. Urgent safety issues are escalated
      immediately.
   2. Legal review when needed. If the issue involves legal requirements or protected rights,
      we consult with PPS Legal to confirm the correct next steps.
   3. Routing to the responsible team.
          a. For school-based issues, we share the concern with the school principal and the
             Senior Director who oversees that school.
          b. For district program or policy issues, we share the concern with the department
             or subject-matter lead responsible for that topic or program.
          c. For personnel or staff conduct issues, we share the concern with Human
             Resources.

From July 2024 through June 2025, Portland Public Schools received 206 submissions
through the Unified Complaint Form. This figure reflects UCF submissions only; it does not
include concerns received through other channels such as email, phone, staff referrals,
Connect with PPS, Safe Oregon, ParentSquare, or the Title IX reporting form. Because
families can contact us in several ways, we track and respond across all channels; however,
the 206 total is limited to UCF forms. For clarity, this count reflects forms submitted, not
necessarily unique issues, because one form can include more than one topic and multiple
contacts about the same situation may be consolidated during follow-up.



In Summary
Perhaps the most pertinent takeaway from the data above is that PPS continues to receive a
growing count of reported concerns, year-over-year, since the inception of the Unified
Complaint Form in November of 2022. By providing an easy, accessible reporting mechanism,
PPS allows for increased conversation, early resolution, and timely and proper routing. Most
concerns are responded to within two business days. Exceptions rarely occur when concerns
are particularly complex, involving multiple departments and/or stakeholders.

While responsiveness is a priority, so is accuracy to decrease confusion and ensure that
responses are efficiently routed to meet the needs of complainants. Documentation also plays
a critical role in ensuring that there are artifacts for reference in the event that there are
recurring concerns. Taking the time to hear from complainants, meeting them where they are
at, and navigating PPS systems on their behalf to deliver responses to their concerns are an
important part of building trust between the District and the PPS community and ensuring that
complainants receive their due process rights.
